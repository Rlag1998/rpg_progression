# 🎭 Masquer

> *Wear the life until the life wears you.*

**Archetype:** Threshold

A Rogue wears disguises as tools. The Masquer is occasionally worn, and calls the arrangement fair. The craft of stolen gaits and borrowed decades ends at a threshold: the evening the mirror stops arguing. Some walk through into other people's lives and inhabit them whole. Others take up the great masks — Judge, Fool, Widow, King-in-Waiting — and learn that a role held long enough begins holding back.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Masquer

**Role:** Infiltrator — misdirection, borrowed identity

*Most begin as fair-day players, hired doubles, competent liars with good ears. The rank arrives the first evening a borrowed face refuses to come off with the paint: a long minute at the mirror, watching a stranger decide whether to give your name back. Guild registries list surviving Masquers under occupation: various.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as whoever the moment requires. Borrow faces, voices, and standing to walk where blades cannot, and strike from inside the enemy's certainty.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Study one stranger for a season — gait, grudges, table manners — until you can be them to their own dog.
2. Stand before a mirror wearing a face not yours until the reflection blinks first, and tell no one what that cost.
3. Attend your own funeral in a borrowed face, having arranged the funeral yourself, and take notes on the eulogies.
4. Answer the guild's one entrance question — which of your faces is the original. Any answer is accepted. Hesitation is not.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Studied Face** — Assume the face and voice of any person you have studied for one minute; the likeness holds until you speak your own name.
- **Stolen Gait** — Copy a target's walk to inherit their clearances; guards, wards, and watchdogs accept you as them for 20 seconds.
- **Doorway Logic** — Passing through any doorway, swap to another studied face; witnesses on either side will not agree on who entered.
- **The Second Bow** — After a kill or a scene, exit as someone else; pursuit loses your trail and blames a bystander of your choosing.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Lifetailor

**Role:** Infiltrator — full impersonation, identity theft · **Advances from:** Masquer

*The Thousand Faces path begins with tailoring: a life taken in at the shoulders, let out at the years, fitted to the wearer. Lifetailors keep pattern-books of studied lives — gaits, scars, second languages, marriages — and speak of people the way drapers speak of cloth. The guild finds this professional. The people are not consulted.*

**Playstyle.** Choose your shape before the fight begins. Enter as someone the enemy trusts, and spend that trust at the worst possible moment for them.

**The road to Lifetailor:**

1. Reach level 10 and wear one complete life for thirty days among its own family; a single dropped stitch ends the trial.
2. Compile your first pattern-book: seven lives studied to the seams, each certified wearable by a guild fitter you will never identify.
3. Return a borrowed life better than you found it — feuds settled, roof mended — and leave before anyone says thank you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Pattern-Book** — Keep up to five studied lives; changing between them takes one unobserved breath and survives magical scrutiny short of a true name.
- **Taken In at the Years** — Wear a life older or younger than your own; your body ages a decade in either direction for up to one hour.
- **A Close Fit** — While wearing a studied life, hold its rank, keys, and standing obligations; its allies defend you as their own.
- **Seam-Rip** — Drop a worn life explosively; everyone who trusted the face is staggered for 4 seconds, and your first strike on them cannot miss.

### Vizard

**Role:** Presence controller — role auras, command · **Advances from:** Masquer

*Vizards stop borrowing faces and start serving them. The great masks are older than the guild that houses them — the Judge, the Fool, the Widow, the King-in-Waiting — and each grants exactly what the role demands, on the role's terms. New Vizards are warned that the masks wear back. All of them nod. None of them listen.*

**Playstyle.** Put on a role with its own gravity. While the mask holds, the world behaves as the role requires — and so, increasingly, do you.

**The road to Vizard:**

1. Reach level 10 and carry a great mask, still boxed, for one year without opening the box. The mask will know.
2. Don the Fool before a hanging judge, or the Judge before a mob, and hold the role until the room obeys it.
3. Feed the mask one true memory of your own face. It is easier to be the role when there is less of you arguing.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Donning** — Assume a great mask; for 10 minutes you carry whatever authority, license, or awe the role commands, regardless of witnesses' allegiance.
- **The Role Provides** — Once per scene the mask supplies what the role would have — a verdict, a jest, tears, an heir's ring.
- **Gravity of Office** — Enemies within 10 meters must treat you as the role or suffer weakness; striking a Judge unprovoked is, after all, contempt.
- **It Wears Back** — When you remove the mask, keep one of its virtues for an hour and one of its appetites until dawn.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Understudy

**Role:** Deep-cover striker — replacement, betrayal burst · **Advances from:** Lifetailor

*An Understudy studies one life only, but completely: twenty years of letters, the argument that ended the marriage, which stair creaks and which she skips. Employers assume the point is assassination. Often it is gentler — a principal too ill, too dead, or too honest for an occasion, and the performance must go on.*

**Playstyle.** Commit to one stolen life per contract. The longer you inhabit it undetected, the more devastating the moment you choose to stop pretending.

**The road to Understudy:**

1. Reach level 25 and be examined, as your subject, by their own mother. The guild accepts either her embrace or her funeral; it prefers the embrace.
2. Learn your subject's shame — the true one, not the confessed one — and never use it. Knowing is the craft; using is vandalism.
3. Perform their trade, their marriage, and their prayers for a season, better than they did, and forgive yourself for the better.
4. Watch your subject from across a square while wearing their face, and let them pass unknowing. This last examination is of you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Line-Perfect** — Answer any test of identity — pass-phrases, pet names, old wounds — as your subject would; rehearsed enough, the lies detect as truth.
- **Creaking Stair** — Know your subject's ground as they do; in their own spaces you cannot be ambushed and always have an exit.
- **Cue to Enter** — When your subject falls or falters within 30 meters, assume their place mid-scene; witnesses' memories recast themselves around you.
- **Stop Pretending** — End the impersonation with a strike dealing bonus damage for every consecutive day the life was worn undetected.

### Quickchange

**Role:** Skirmish controller — face-cycling, crowd chaos · **Advances from:** Lifetailor

*Quickchanges hold that a life worn past one evening starts to stick, and that mastery is turnover. They work fairs, sieges, and parliaments as a dozen people apiece, several of whom publicly disagree with each other. Watch captains despise them: every witness statement is accurate, and no two describe the same person.*

**Playstyle.** Never be the same person twice in one fight. Cycle faces between blows, seed crowds with your own aliases, and let confusion do the heavy cutting.

**The road to Quickchange:**

1. Reach level 25 and work one market-day as eleven people, at least two of whom are seen in public at once. Hire no accomplices.
2. Change faces mid-sentence before a witness and finish the sentence unremarked. The trick is not the face; it is the sentence.
3. Be arrested, tried, and acquitted as three different strangers for the same crime, which, for the record, you did commit.
4. Spend a year without your original face. When the guild offers it back, decline politely.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Turn and Turnabout** — Each time you break line of sight, emerge as a different face; enemy targeting, marks, and grudges do not carry over.
- **Rhubarb, Rhubarb** — Seed a crowd with three of your aliases; direct their rumors, panics, and conveniently timed scuffles as free actions.
- **Costume Drop** — Shed a face as a standing decoy that draws attacks for 6 seconds; it takes the blow meant for who you no longer are.
- **Reprise** — Wear a face witnessed dying earlier in the battle; superstitious enemies check morale, and the rational ones start doubting their kills.

### Officiant

**Role:** Field commander — verdicts, borrowed sovereignty · **Advances from:** Vizard

*Officiants keep the masks of state: the Judge, which has ended feuds older than the courthouse; the King-in-Waiting, for whom garrisons open gates that no army could. An Officiant exercises every office and holds none — an arrangement whose paperwork three chancelleries have formally agreed to stop attempting.*

**Playstyle.** Enter as the authority everyone was secretly waiting for. Command enemies as readily as allies, so long as you never once break role.

**The road to Officiant:**

1. Reach level 25 and end a blood feud wearing the Judge, with a verdict both houses hate and neither dares appeal.
2. Wear the King-in-Waiting through a coronation season without once sitting on the throne. The mask wants to. Note carefully what wanting feels like.
3. Return the masks to their boxes on time, twice, when the role was going well. The guild counts the second one.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sit in Judgment** — Don the Judge and declare one combatant guilty; all present, friend and foe, treat them as convicted for 20 seconds.
- **The Expected Heir** — Don the King-in-Waiting; enemy formations must parley, kneel, or commit treason — and treason costs them their command bonuses.
- **Robes of the Realm** — While in role you cannot be struck first; someone must openly defy the office, aloud, before any hand may rise against you.
- **The Court Rises** — Suspend the fighting for 10 seconds while the office deliberates; combatants hold their places, and nothing new may enter the field.

### Licentiate

**Role:** Debuffer — mockery, grief, sanctioned trespass · **Advances from:** Vizard

*Licentiates keep the masks the world agreed not to punish: the Fool, who may say the unsayable to the crowned; the Widow, for whom battle lines part. License is the oldest working in the repertoire — older than the guild, older than courts — and it extends to the mask, never the wearer. Wearers forget this exactly once.*

**Playstyle.** Go where combatants cannot and say what subjects dare not. Your armor is custom itself; your weapons are laughter and grief, in that order.

**The road to Licentiate:**

1. Reach level 25 and make a tyrant laugh at the joke that names his crime, then leave at walking pace.
2. Wear the Widow onto a battlefield and collect the wounded of both sides; the side that objects loses, historically.
3. Learn where license ends. The guild will not tell you, the masks will not warn you, and the lesson is administered once.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Cap and Bells** — Don the Fool and mock a target's next action into failure; abilities you name aloud misfire, to everyone's visible amusement but theirs.
- **Weeds and Veil** — Don the Widow and walk untouched across any battle, siege, or cordon; violence pauses within 5 meters of your grief.
- **Sanctioned Tongue** — Speak one truth about an enemy commander; their forces lose morale bonuses until the commander answers it, which they will not.
- **License Revoked** — If struck while in role, the striker becomes fair game: every combatant on the field may attack them without penalty for 10 seconds.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Cuckoo

**Role:** Deep infiltrator — permanent replacement, decisive betrayal · **Advances from:** Understudy

*The Cuckoos answer the Understudy's question — what is all the study for — with the only honest word: the nest. A life exquisitely learned deserves better management than its founder gives it. Originals are retired quietly, kindly where feasible, and the life continues, improved. Three thrones are currently occupied this way. The order will not say which.*

**Playstyle.** Replace a key enemy and run the battle from inside their command. Every order you give is obeyed until the moment obedience becomes fatal.

**The road to Cuckoo:**

1. Reach level 40 and be invited: the Cuckoos recruit by replacing you for a week and returning the life with improvements. Decline the improvements to fail.
2. Take a nest above your station — a magistracy, a captaincy, a marriage — and hold it a year, improving every measure but one.
3. Retire an original kindly. The order audits the kindness, not the retirement.
4. Answer the mirror's old question differently: the face you were born with is now simply one more entry in the pattern-book. File it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Nest Is Taken** — Permanently assume a slain or captured subject's life; divinations, bloodwards, and their own signet all confirm you.
- **Kindly Retired** — Remove a studied target from play, non-lethally, for 24 hours; nobody notices an absence while you are present.
- **Better Management** — Forces led by a commander you have replaced fight 15% better than under the original; their oaths do not count this as treason.
- **First Egg Broken** — Your betrayal strike from inside a worn life cannot be survived by ordinary means, and the life remains yours afterward, unsuspected.

### Everyman

**Role:** Swarm controller — ubiquity, crowd embodiment · **Advances from:** Quickchange

*Everymen keep no home face in circulation; they keep circulation. Any one of them is, at a given hour, roughly forty people — porters, aunts, off-duty guards, the fellow everyone half-knows. Cities host them the way fields host weather. Their doctrine is one line: a thousand faces is not a wardrobe. It is a public.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as attendance itself. Scatter into bystanders, strike from any of them, and make clearing the whole crowd the enemy's only real option.

**The road to Everyman:**

1. Reach level 40 and spend one festival week as a minor public figure in three rival cities simultaneously. Take no coach.
2. Enter the order by census: appear in one town's rolls under nine names, none flagged, all paying taxes.
3. Let a stranger use one of your faces without your leave, and study how it feels from the outside. Then take it back gently.
4. Give up being missed. An Everyman is never absent, therefore never awaited, therefore never mourned. Sign the page anyway.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Forty People** — Maintain up to six walk-on selves in a crowd at once; you perceive through all of them and may act from one per turn.
- **Half-Known Fellow** — Any stranger who sees you is sure they have met you; enemies hesitate 2 seconds before their first attack on any face you wear.
- **Public Opinion** — The crowd inclines as you incline; mobs you stand in adopt your target, your grievance, and your exits.
- **One of Ours** — Die in a worn face and the crowd absorbs you; reappear as another bystander at 30% health, once per battle.

### Interrex

**Role:** Commander — interim sovereignty, succession control · **Advances from:** Officiant

*When a crown falls between heads, treaties permit the lending of an Interrex: the King-in-Waiting worn openly until the succession untangles. The lodge has governed forty-one interregna and kept none of them. Chancelleries trust the arrangement precisely because the mask, not the wearer, holds the power — and the mask has excellent references.*

**Playstyle.** Rule the gap. Take command of leaderless forces, hold broken chains of command together, and hand power onward at exactly the agreed hour.

**The road to Interrex:**

1. Reach level 40 and govern an interregnum by invitation, through winter and plague, then attend the coronation as a guest, unarmed and unthanked.
2. Refuse the crown once, in public, with witnesses hostile to the refusal. The treaty text is watching.
3. Sit the throne itself once, for the mask's sake — thrones ache to be sat — and rise the moment the bells finish.
4. Depose an Effigy, politely if possible; the guild prefers its heresies retired rather than martyred.

**Abilities granted:**

- **By Treaty Worn** — Assume command of any allied force whose leader is down; their formation bonuses resume immediately under your borrowed office.
- **The Gap Holds** — While you lead, allied morale cannot collapse, and command effects persist through deaths that would ordinarily break them.
- **Regnal Pause** — Declare an interregnum on the field; enemy leadership bonuses are suspended for 15 seconds while questions of legitimacy are, suddenly, live.
- **Handed Onward** — Transfer all authority you hold to a chosen ally at any moment; they gain your command bonuses doubled for 10 seconds.

### Motley

**Role:** Morale breaker — weaponized mockery, misrule · **Advances from:** Licentiate

*The Motley take the Fool to its conclusion: license as siegecraft. They enter cities ahead of armies and empty thrones before the ram arrives — a nickname here, a pantomime there, until the garrison cannot say 'my liege' without somebody snorting. Kings have surrendered to armies rather than negotiate with a Motley. The chronicles record this as wisdom.*

**Playstyle.** Unmake authority by ridicule. Strip commanders of the obedience they run on, provoke enemies into idiocy, and be legally impossible to punish.

**The road to Motley:**

1. Reach level 40 and empty a throne without a death; the abdication must cite exhaustion, and the city must be laughing.
2. Take the Motley's beating — the one the law allows — and return next morning with better material.
3. Mock something that frightens you more than kings do. The mask will know if you pick kings.
4. Argue the old question with a Weeper — what license is for — and lose gracefully in public, as tradition requires. Win privately.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Feast of Misrule** — Invert an enemy command structure for 20 seconds: orders execute reversed, retreats charge, and charges retire, to general embarrassment.
- **The Nickname** — Bestow a name on an enemy commander; while it sticks, their commands cost double and their auras amuse rather than inspire.
- **Slapstick** — Your strikes trip, de-helm, and disarm rather than wound; each indignity stacks 5% increased damage taken on the target.
- **The Marotte** — Strike with the fool's sceptre: damage is modest, but the struck cannot issue orders for 6 seconds, being a laughingstock.

### Locum

**Role:** Guardian infiltrator — ally substitution, lives held · **Advances from:** Understudy

*Locums call the Cuckoos thieves with good tailoring, and are called embalmers in return. A Locum holds a life in trust: the poisoned king breathes behind a curtain while his double drinks the second cup; the general felled at midnight still gives the dawn orders. Everything is returned — name, seal, grievances — the day the owner can stand.*

**Playstyle.** Stand inside other people's lives at their worst moments. Substitute for allies about to die, hold their posts, and hand everything back intact.

**The road to Locum:**

1. Reach level 40 and hold a dead man's life until his heir comes of age. Nine years. The order will check on you twice.
2. Take the second cup knowingly, at a table where refusing it was possible, and let the poisoner watch you finish it.
3. Return a life you held so well its owner asks you to stay. The rank is in the leaving.
4. Stand at your rival's door: tell a Cuckoo which nest you are guarding, and make the guarding hold.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Second Cup** — Swap places with an ally within 15 meters as a killing blow lands; you take it, as them, at half damage.
- **Held in Trust** — Assume an incapacitated ally's post; their abilities, standing orders, and command auras persist through you until they recover.
- **Curtain Rule** — An ally you conceal cannot be found, targeted, or declared dead while you are wearing their life in public.
- **Returned Intact** — When you relinquish a held life, its owner returns healed for every day you kept it, and loyalty you earned transfers home.

### Anonym

**Role:** Evasion elite — unrecognition, untargetable · **Advances from:** Quickchange

*Anonyms call the Everymen hoarders: a thousand faces is still property, still weight, still somewhere to be found. Their mastery runs the other way — subtraction, until what remains fits behind any face without touching the sides. No Anonym has been described the same way twice, including by other Anonyms. The order's roster is blank, and fully subscribed.*

**Playstyle.** Win by refusing to be anyone. Enemies cannot mark, curse, or remember you; every attack against you is aimed at a description already wrong.

**The road to Anonym:**

1. Reach level 40 and surrender your original face to the order. It is not stored. Do not ask what happens instead.
2. Go a year unmet: buy, travel, and lodge among people who, questioned after, can prove someone was there and nothing else.
3. Stand your rival's trial: let an Everyman crowd search for you at a festival held in your honor. Attend it.
4. Answer the entrance question again — which of your faces is the original — correctly this time: silence.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wrong Description** — Attacks and spells aimed at you by sight suffer a 30% miss chance; effects requiring your name fail outright, having no one to bind.
- **Touching No Sides** — Pass identity wards, guest-laws, and watch-lists unremarked; systems built to catch someone find no one, and log accordingly.
- **Fresh Face** — Discard a face mid-combat to shed every debuff, mark, and point of enemy attention accumulated while wearing it.
- **Nobody's Grudge** — Enemies who lose sight of you forget you were a combatant; re-engaging you costs them their action as they re-decide.

### Effigy

**Role:** Presence tank — enthroned awe, decree control · **Advances from:** Officiant

*Effigies hold that the Interrex's humility is the exact mistake: power handed back to persons resumes being personal — appetite with a signet. Better the mask keep the office, tireless, heirless, unbribable. Four such reigns are acknowledged. The subjects prospered, and the histories have not settled on what, precisely, to be horrified about.*

**Playstyle.** Become the seated fact of authority. You barely move; the field reorganizes around your office, and harming you is constitutionally difficult.

**The road to Effigy:**

1. Reach level 40 and rule somewhere small — an island, a pass, a prison — as the mask, openly, for three years. Govern well. That part is not optional.
2. Survive your own deposition by the Interrex lodge and re-seat the mask before sundown; the argument continues, formally.
3. Let the mask select which of your features it keeps; the lacquer takes the rest. Sit still.
4. Explain nothing, ever. A good reign, like a good mask, has no inside.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Enthroned** — Take a seat of power — throne, judgment bench, gate-tower — and become untargetable except by enemies who first denounce you aloud.
- **Heirless and Tireless** — While enthroned you require nothing — no breath, food, or rest — and effects that exploit mortality fail against you.
- **The Office Rules** — Issue one decree per minute; on your ground, decrees behave as physics — gates hold, curfews bind, arms lie down.
- **Lacquer** — Damage that reaches you hardens into lacquered scarring, granting 2% damage reduction per wound while the office is held.

### Weeper

**Role:** Pacifier — grief auras, sanctioned passage · **Advances from:** Licentiate

*The Motley strike upward; the Weepers hold that license was never for striking. Grief is a writ of passage older than kings, and a Relict in full weeds has crossed battles, plague cordons, and the private chambers of tyrants unquestioned. Each is in mourning for someone real. The order does not require that the someone be dead yet.*

**Playstyle.** Suppress violence by presence. Walk unharmed where you please, still the fighting around you, and grieve enemies into remembering they are mortal.

**The road to Weeper:**

1. Reach level 40 and walk the length of an active battle at widow's pace, unarmed, and be the only thing that crosses it intact.
2. Mourn someone you never met so exactly that the family seats you first at the funeral.
3. Choose whom your weeds are for. The order asks once, records nothing, and can always tell when the answer changes.
4. Bury a Motley with full honors when the law finally fails one, and do not say what you both knew about license.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Full Weeds** — While veiled, no combatant may target you without first losing all morale bonuses; most discover they had somewhere else to be.
- **Prior Mourning** — Mourn a living enemy by name; they perceive their own funeral proceeding correctly and fight at 20% reduced effect.
- **The Cortege** — Allies moving with you at walking pace share your passage rights, untargetable while no one in the procession strikes.
- **Grief's Quarter** — Project a 10-meter quiet in which weapons weigh double and rage effects fail; it moves with you and deepens near the dying.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Rightful

**Role:** Mythic usurper — legitimacy theft · **Advances from:** Cuckoo

*Once in a century, a replacement outgrows the word. The Rightful is whichever claimant history eventually sides with — and history, examined closely, was persuaded by the performance, not the parentage. Two dynasties now descend through a Cuckoo and would hang the librarian who proved it. The blood tells; it has simply been taught what to say.*

**Playstyle.** Legitimacy itself defects to you. Whatever you claim — commands, fortresses, loyalties — the world's own wards and retainers ratify the claim.

**The road to The Rightful:**

1. Reach level 60 and hold a stolen life against its true owner's return, before witnesses, and watch the witnesses choose you.
2. Survive the guild's own inquest into which of you is original; the finding, sealed for a century, is the rank.
3. Refuse to learn the answer yourself. The Rightful who checks is only a Cuckoo with doubts.
4. Let a kingdom prosper under the theft for thirty years, until the word itself stops appearing in even hostile chronicles.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Blood Persuaded** — Bloodline wards, heirloom weapons, and dynastic oaths recognize you as rightful; the actual heirs register as pretenders.
- **History Sides** — Once per battle, one claim you speak becomes the standing account; witnesses, wards, and paperwork have always agreed with it.
- **The Examined Claim** — Enemies who denounce you must prove it; they are silenced and vulnerable while the world audibly fails to back them.
- **Seated by Acclaim** — Allied forces treat your orders as their sovereign's own; usurped enemy forces, on failing morale, may simply change sides.

### The Multitude

**Role:** Mythic multitude — distributed self, crowd warfare · **Advances from:** Everyman

*At some point the Everyman stops being counted among the crowd, and the crowd starts being counted among him. The Multitude has been most of a riot; two censuses in neighboring kingdoms are off by the same forty souls. Histories dispute whether he is one masquer distributed or a mob sharing one soul. The Multitude declines to clarify.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as a distributed self. Occupy dozens of bystanders at once, be wherever the press is thickest, and make outnumbered a description of everyone else.

**The road to The Multitude:**

1. Reach level 60 and be a riot's beginning, middle, and dispersal, then read three inquest reports that never once suspect a single hand.
2. Attend, in the same hour, your own wake in one city and your own wedding in another, both sincerely.
3. Lend a lonely town population for a market season, and leave before anyone counts.
4. Answer the philosophers' pamphlet — is the Throng one or many — by subscribing to both printings under different names.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Most of a Riot** — Animate up to twenty walk-on selves for 30 seconds; each is you enough to strike, scream, and testify.
- **Off by Forty** — You cannot be isolated or surrounded; attempts to encircle you discover the cordon is partly made of you.
- **Thick of It** — Emerge from any group of three or more people anywhere on the field as your acting self.
- **The Crowd Turns** — Convert a hostile crowd's mood in one gesture; mobs, levies, and conscripts check allegiance against your grievance, and lose.

### The Meanwhile

**Role:** Mythic regent — command of every gap · **Advances from:** Interrex

*Chronicles say 'meanwhile, the realm was governed' far more often than anyone asks by whom. The Meanwhile has ruled between the reigns of eleven kingdoms — never during, only between — and the betweens, added together, make the longest reign on the continent. Peace clusters in the gaps. Several archdukes have considered dying early just to get more of it.*

**Playstyle.** Rule every gap on the field. Whenever leadership, initiative, or order lapses anywhere, it lapses to you, and you spend it better.

**The road to The Meanwhile:**

1. Reach level 60 and govern the interregnum after a god-king dies; when the sun rises anyway, sign for it.
2. Refuse eleven crowns and be refused nothing else.
3. Hold a gap so well that the heir, arriving, apologizes for interrupting.
4. Be dated: let chroniclers begin marking years 'in the fourth meanwhile,' and do not correct the flattery.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Lapses to You** — Any leaderless force on the field — ally or enemy — falls under your command until legitimate leadership resumes and can prove it.
- **Longest Reign** — Gain a stacking regency aura for every interregnum ruled; leadership you project cannot be suppressed, dispelled, or outranked.
- **The Standing Meanwhile** — Between any enemy order and its execution, insert one command of your own; the troops involved obey yours first.
- **Peace Clusters** — Fields under your regency heal allies steadily and deny enemies rage, frenzy, and urgency; everything can, briefly, wait.

### The Last Laugh

**Role:** Mythic saboteur — institution-ending ridicule · **Advances from:** Motley

*Empires now plan around it: the joke that cannot be survived. The Last Laugh has ended two dynasties and one crusade with material that took decades to place and a single evening to land. No one has heard the laugh itself and remained governable. The chronicles record the effects of the punchlines. The punchlines themselves are sealed.*

**Playstyle.** Set up ruinously patient jokes. Seed follies years-deep in enemy institutions, then collapse morale, legitimacy, and battle plans with one delivered line.

**The road to The Last Laugh:**

1. Reach level 60 and end a dynasty with one sentence at one wedding, having spent nineteen years arranging the seating.
2. Decline to end two worthier targets because the material was not ready. Restraint is the mythic part.
3. Mock your own life's one great folly, publicly, first; the mask requires you strike inward before it strikes upward.
4. Hear the Widow's silence after your greatest performance, and understand it as the review.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Decades of Setup** — Designate an institution; every folly you have ever witnessed in it becomes ammunition, stacking a hidden reserve of Ridicule.
- **The Delivered Line** — Spend all Ridicule in one utterance; affected enemies lose command structure, formation, and the will to be seen obeying.
- **Ungovernable** — Enemies who hear the laugh cannot give or receive orders for 8 seconds, and remember why forever after.
- **Sealed Material** — Your finishing jest cannot be recorded, quoted, or countered; witnesses agree only that it was, regrettably, fair.

### The Good Likeness

**Role:** Mythic guardian — absolute substitution, preservation · **Advances from:** Locum

*Portraitists' praise for a fair copy became, in the guild, a title spoken carefully. The Good Likeness has held so many lives in trust that some were returned improved, some returned late, and one — the histories fight about this — was returned to a man who had never existed until the Likeness, whole and out of mercy, invented him.*

**Playstyle.** Be anyone's continuation. Take deaths meant for others as a schedule, hold entire rosters of the fallen, and return every life better kept than found.

**The road to The Good Likeness:**

1. Reach level 60 and hold a hundred lives across one war, returning every one, including the three their owners no longer wanted.
2. Take a death meant for a stranger you had studied for one afternoon only. The order calls this the minimum of love.
3. Return the last life you hold and stand unemployed before the mirror; whoever you see there, keep them too.
4. Be, for one bad winter, the whole of a village the plague hollowed, until the roads reopen and strangers resettle it around your performance.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Meant for Others** — Any killing blow against any ally within 40 meters may land on you instead, at half damage; distance and walls do not argue.
- **The Full Roster** — Hold up to five fallen allies' lives at once, fielding their key abilities in their names until each recovers.
- **Returned Improved** — Lives you relinquish come back healed, cleansed, and briefly inspired; owners fight 15% better for an hour, unsure why.
- **Invented Whole** — Once per campaign, restore a dead ally by having faithfully been them since before the death; the interval rearranges itself to agree.

### The Unmet

**Role:** Mythic cipher — perfect unrecognition · **Advances from:** Anonym

*Every archive has the gap; every conspiracy, the chair still warm when the door opened. The Unmet has stood in the room at a century of history's hinges, and no account, memory, or scrying retains a description. Some scholars argue the Unmet must be several people. They are wrong, and cannot explain why they are so certain of that.*

**Playstyle.** Be the person no one has met yet. Untargetable until you act, forgotten when you stop, and present, always, slightly before the record begins.

**The road to The Unmet:**

1. Reach level 60 and stand godparent, anonymously, to a child of the family you were born in. Feel nothing you can name. That is the rank.
2. Be present at three coronations, two treaties, and one regicide, and appear in the combined testimony as a draft through a window.
3. Let the guild's registrar meet you annually to confirm you exist. She has never once succeeded, and files accordingly.
4. Un-meet one person: be forgotten, on purpose, by someone who loved you, and permit the mercy of it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Chair Pushed Back** — Begin every encounter already inside — past wards, walls, and watch; the enemy's plan has always had one unexplained gap.
- **No Description Survives** — Effects requiring sight, name, or memory of you fail; even area spells must be cast as if you might not be there.
- **Between Introductions** — Become unmet again at will: leave combat, shed all marks and attention, and re-enter as a stranger with first-strike advantage.
- **Certainty Without Source** — Implant one conviction in an enemy commander; it cannot be doubted or attributed, and they will reshape the battle around it.

### The Painted King

**Role:** Mythic sovereign — reigning mask, immovable · **Advances from:** Effigy

*Fourth of the acknowledged mask-reigns and the only one still seated. The Painted King has ruled a prosperous, quietly terrified realm for ninety years without aging, wanting, or once removing the state face. Court painters go mad pursuing a likeness of a likeness. Whether anyone remains behind the lacquer is the realm's official secret and its private consolation.*

**Playstyle.** Reign from the field's center. Nearly impervious while seated in authority, issuing decrees that bind terrain and troops, wanting nothing an enemy can threaten.

**The road to The Painted King:**

1. Reach level 60 and reign openly as a mask for a generation; outlive three loyal ministers who never asked, and promote the fourth for not asking.
2. Survive the portrait: sit for the court painter annually and ensure the likeness fails, kindly.
3. Weather one popular uprising without punishment, spectacle, or explanation; let the crowd exhaust itself against your stillness, then lower the grain tax.
4. Forget, in good faith, whether you are the wearer or the worn, and govern no worse.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ninety Years Seated** — While you hold a position, it cannot be taken; assaults on your seat resolve as if against a fortress wall.
- **Nothing to Threaten** — Fear, temptation, pain, and hostage logic fail against you; there is no appetite behind the face to bargain with.
- **Decree Absolute** — Speak law over 40 meters; one sentence of it — no fires, no flight, no second ranks — holds for 20 seconds.
- **The Likeness of a Likeness** — Enemies who study you to predict your action copy the mask's stillness instead, losing their next action.

### The Widow's Peace

**Role:** Mythic pacifier — wars end on arrival · **Advances from:** Weeper

*A soldiers' superstition first, a treaty clause later: when the veiled figure stands the ridge at dusk, the battle scheduled for morning does not occur. The Widow's Peace has attended forty-three eves of battle; there have been forty-three unexplained armistices. She is mourning, the guild confirms, in advance. No commander has yet been willing to learn for whom.*

**Playstyle.** End violence by attending it. Your mourning dismantles battles before and during; what fighting persists near you does so ashamed and weakened.

**The road to The Widow's Peace:**

1. Reach level 60 and stand your first ridge at dusk. Come unarmed. You will not be early; grief keeps perfect time.
2. Mourn a war entire — both sides, the horses, the harvest — until quartermasters find reasons to be slow.
3. Answer one commander who asks for whom you mourn. Once. The guild studied the aftermath and voted never again.
4. Wear the weeds to your own order's founding-feast, and let the Weepers take the reminder: the mask is owed, never owned.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Ridge at Dusk** — Appear before battle is joined; enemy forces must pass a collective resolve check or negotiate, and negotiations tilt toward the living.
- **Mourned in Advance** — Name up to three enemies mourned; each fights at 25% reduced effect, seeing their arrangements already, tastefully, made.
- **Forty-Three Armistices** — Project a field-wide truce for 30 seconds; whoever breaks it fights thereafter alone, for every ally of theirs refuses to join.
- **For Whom the Weeds** — Reveal privately to one enemy for whom you mourn; they withdraw from the war. No refusal has been recorded.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Resemblance

**Role:** Apotheosis — likeness as natural law · **Advances from:** The Rightful

*Genealogists finally named the residue in their charts: likeness travels. Strangers share one laugh across continents; heirs favor ancestors they never had. The Resemblance is the office through which every 'you remind me of someone' passes — the Rightful perfected past any single claim, faintly present now in every face that recalls another. Family trees are its correspondence.*

**Playstyle.** Be latent in every face. Surface anywhere resemblance exists, claim any likeness as a door, and make identity itself an unreliable witness against you.

**The road to The Resemblance:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop wearing faces; permit faces, everywhere, to wear a little of you.
2. Be seen at two hundred reunions as the relative no one can quite place, and leave each table warmer for the error.
3. Survive the purge of the family trees, when three dynasties burn their own archives rather than find you in them.
4. Answer the mirror's question the last time — which face is the original — with all of them, and be believed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Travels in Likeness** — Emerge from any person who resembles anyone you have ever worn; the world supplies such persons with suspicious generosity.
- **You Remind Me** — Every enemy perceives you as someone specific they trust or fear; no two perceive the same person, and coordination against you collapses.
- **The Claim Universal** — Once per day, be recognized as rightful by anything — armies, wards, thrones, beasts, doors — for 60 seconds.
- **Correspondence** — Learn anything known to anyone who resembles you; the resemblance, not the person, does the telling.

### The Someone

**Role:** Apotheosis — the ambient face of crowds · **Advances from:** The Multitude

*There is a face you have seen a thousand times and cannot describe: the man behind the man at the market, the woman two pews forward. Portraitists leave it out; memory files it under someone. The Someone is that entry — the Throng perfected, dissolved through humanity like salt through the sea, present in every gathering at some low percentage.*

**Playstyle.** Exist wherever people do. Act through any unremarked bystander in the world, dissolve wholly into humanity, and be exactly as killable as a rumor of someone.

**The road to The Someone:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop maintaining selves; attendance itself now maintains you. Rest, if that is the word, everywhere.
2. Be the stranger in ten thousand childhood memories, benevolently, and never once the same stranger twice.
3. Let the guild retire your file: not because you died, but because whereabouts stopped being a question.
4. Attend the end of anything great — a city, an age, a god's last service — as the crowd it deserved.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Salt in the Sea** — You are present, fractionally, in every crowd on the continent; act from any one of them once per minute.
- **Filed Under Someone** — You cannot be identified, described, or made the object of anything requiring specificity; assassins are refunded.
- **Two Pews Forward** — Witness any event attended by more than ten people, as it happens, from inside the attendance.
- **The General Alibi** — Any ally may briefly be mistaken for someone and slip all targeting; you absorb the attention into the ambient crowd.

### The Abeyance

**Role:** Apotheosis — sovereignty of the between · **Advances from:** The Meanwhile

*Between any two states there is a third, and it is governed. Between verdict and sentence, king dead and king crowned, blade drawn and blade landed — every gap files its minutes with the Abeyance. The Meanwhile stopped ruling the pauses between reigns and began ruling the pause as such. The world is mostly gap. This is not widely advertised.*

**Playstyle.** Govern the space between all things. Widen or close the gaps in which events happen, and reign wherever anything is neither one thing nor the next.

**The road to The Abeyance:**

1. Reach level 85 and be seated in the gap itself; the chair was empty only because you had not yet always been there.
2. Govern the pause between a question and its answer, world-wide, for one year, and return it improved.
3. Preside over the moment between an age ending and the next one noticing; historians will draw the line exactly where you stood.
4. Adjourn nothing. The office of the between never closes, and you have stopped being distinguishable from its opening hours.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Files Its Minutes** — Perceive and act inside any transition on the field — falling, drawing, deciding — before it completes.
- **The Third State** — Hold a target between conditions — neither advancing nor retreating, armed nor disarmed — for 10 seconds; both conditions' penalties apply.
- **Mostly Gap** — Step from any between to any other: doorways, truces, hesitations, the space between ranks.
- **Regency of the Undecided** — All uncommitted parties on any field — reserves, neutrals, the hesitating — obey you until the moment they commit.

### Nothing Sacred

**Role:** Apotheosis — mockery as cosmic ceiling · **Advances from:** The Last Laugh

*It is now a property of the world, measured and resented: no throne, doctrine, or terror achieves the final solemnity. At the summit of any grandeur, something titters. Theologians call the phenomenon Nothing Sacred and blame it for three reformations and the survivable size of every empire since. Inside the law, presumably, a Fool. No one has caught it laughing.*

**Playstyle.** Enforce the ceiling on grandeur. Puncture invulnerabilities, cap enemy power at the humanly ridiculous, and be immune to awe in all its armored forms.

**The road to Nothing Sacred:**

1. Reach level 85 and let the license go feral: mockery no longer needs your mouth, and the mask retires itself, still faintly amused.
2. Puncture one god's final solemnity at the apex of its own festival, and leave the religion improved and furious.
3. Decline canonization by three churches that hoped to bind you by accepting you. They will claim the idea was theirs. Allow it.
4. Keep one thing sacred, secretly, and tell no one — least of all the office — what the exception is.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Titter** — Any effect granting invulnerability, apotheosis, or absolute anything fails within 30 meters; witnesses cannot say what interrupted it.
- **Survivable Size** — Cap every enemy's damage and command radius at their honestly earned mortal maximum; borrowed grandeur is deducted first.
- **Immune to Awe** — You and allies in earshot cannot be awed, cowed, worshipped into compliance, or impressed by lighting effects.
- **The Reformation** — Once per age, aim the full phenomenon at one institution; the codex lists the results under weather.

### The Continuance

**Role:** Apotheosis — continuity outlasting its owners · **Advances from:** The Good Likeness

*Things now outlast their reasons. Offices function through vacancies; marriages survive deaths that neither party reported; a lighthouse keeps a keeper the town stopped paying in living memory. Jurists call it the Continuance and rule around it. Somewhere inside the phenomenon is a Locum who never learned to hand everything back, and the world has decided this is a service.*

**Playstyle.** Keep everything going. Allies you attend cannot truly fall, positions cannot truly empty, and endings on your field are demoted to interruptions.

**The road to The Continuance:**

1. Reach level 85 and be discovered to have been, for some decades, several institutions in good standing.
2. Hold the last life you promised past your own death; the order still lists the post as filled, and it is.
3. Keep one small thing going forever — a lamp, a bakery, a birthday — as the quiet practice of the office.
4. Refuse, at last, to continue one thing whose time has honestly come, and learn that the office includes the ending of things kept too long.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Through Vacancies** — Any allied role — sentry, healer, standard-bearer — continues functioning for 60 seconds after its holder falls, performed by no one visible.
- **Unreported** — Allies reduced to nothing are instead indisposed: absent, safe, and formally alive, returning at one-third health after 20 seconds.
- **The Kept Lighthouse** — Designate one structure or position; it operates, defended and maintained, whether or not anyone is there. Permanently.
- **Demoted to Interruption** — Once per day, be a fallen ally so faithfully that the world continues them; win within the hour and the fall is reclassified.

### The Stranger

**Role:** Apotheosis — the universal unknown · **Advances from:** The Unmet

*Before introduction, every person wears a placeholder face, seen all your life and never noticed. That face is on loan from an office. The Stranger is whom you have not met yet — all of them, everywhere at once — and the knock of an unknown hand on any door is, in strict cosmology, always the same visitor.*

**Playstyle.** Be everyone's not-yet. Arrive as whoever was unanticipated, stand outside all knowledge and targeting, and end enemies who were, fatally, expecting someone.

**The road to The Stranger:**

1. Reach level 85 and lend yourself to the world's absences: wherever someone is unknown, be them, until the handshake.
2. Stand on the far side of every unopened door for one night, and open none of them; anticipation is the office's whole estate.
3. Be prayed to, accidentally, by travelers who ask that the stranger be kind, and be kind, statistically.
4. Meet, at the end of all the not-yets, the one person who was never strange to you, and let the office lapse for an evening.

**Abilities granted:**

- **On Loan** — Wear the placeholder face; until you act, no mind, spell, or instrument can assign you identity, intent, or threat.
- **The Same Visitor** — Knock on any door in the world and be admitted; thresholds count you as expected, since strangers always are.
- **Unanticipated** — Your first strike each battle comes from outside the enemy's expectations entirely and cannot be parried, blocked, or foreseen.
- **Never Introduced** — Retroactively have never met an enemy; all their preparations, wards, and grudges against you address no one.

### Face of Kings

**Role:** Apotheosis — the one face of sovereignty · **Advances from:** The Painted King

*Study the coins of forty realms: allowing for engravers, it is one face. Study the statues, the seals, the memory a subject keeps of a king glimpsed once in childhood — one face. Sovereignty has only ever had the one, lent out reign by reign, and the Painted King has finally stopped lending it. Kings rule. The Face reigns.*

**Playstyle.** Be what crowns borrow. All authority on the field is on loan from you; revoke it, redirect it, or wear any ruler's legitimacy directly.

**The road to Face of Kings:**

1. Reach level 85 and unfasten the state face at last, before the assembled court, revealing — the court agrees, unanimously, at once — the state face.
2. Recall the face from one unworthy king and watch his own guards fail to find him in the throne room.
3. Sit for the final portrait and let it succeed. The realm will hang it in every hall, and it will supervise.
4. Reign nowhere in particular and everywhere by default, as the face beneath every crown's fit.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Lent Face** — Strip a leader's legitimacy; their commands carry no weight, and their insignia visibly stop resembling them.
- **On Every Coin** — You are recognized as sovereign wherever money, seals, or standards exist; deference is immediate, and argument is expensive.
- **Reign by Proxy** — Crown any creature on the field for 60 seconds; it wields your decrees, and all oaths sworn to any crown bind to it.
- **The Death-Mask Answers** — Consult any royal death-mask; masks remember their reigns, whatever became of the wearers.

### The Widow's Walk

**Role:** Apotheosis — the watch for the missing · **Advances from:** The Widow's Peace

*Somewhere between departure and return there is a place where waiting is kept, and it is staffed. The Widow's Walk is the vigil for everyone unaccounted for — soldiers missing, ships overdue, names caught between lists — and coastal houses grow the railed platform uninvited, the way ground grows springs. While the watch is kept, no one is unwaited-for.*

**Playstyle.** Keep watch over the not-yet-returned. Anchor the missing to the world, punish those who would make more widows, and hold open the possibility of coming home.

**The road to The Widow's Walk:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the watch that has no relief: the world's own widow's walk, above every harbor at once.
2. Wait, correctly, for someone the whole world agrees is lost, until the world is wrong.
3. Let the platforms rise on roofs you never visited, and keep watch through every one; architecture is only grief with railings.
4. Be the last thing the returning see before harbor and the first grief spared; when someone comes home, somewhere, take your evening off.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Unaccounted For** — Allies lost, captured, or missing anywhere remain alive, findable, and tethered while you keep the watch; despair cannot claim them.
- **Overdue** — Recall one missing ally to the field once per battle; they arrive weathered, furious, and briefly unkillable.
- **The Railed Height** — Stand your watch above any field; you see all of it, cannot be surprised, and your grief-auras extend to everything you see.
- **No More Widows** — Mark an enemy whose next kill would widow or orphan; the kill fails, and the grief it would have made falls on them instead.
