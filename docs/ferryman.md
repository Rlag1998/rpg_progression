# 🛶 Ferryman

> *The fare is named before the pole touches water.*

**Archetype:** Threshold

Every river has a far side, and every far side has a fee. The Ferryman keeps the crossings the world pretends are edges — rivers, borders, the gap between waking and gone — carrying whatever pays across whatever cannot otherwise be crossed. They do not judge the passenger and they do not forgive the fare. The oldest contract has one clause, and they are it.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Ferryman

**Role:** Hybrid support — passage, positioning, price

*Every river-town keeps one, and no town remembers hiring them. A first-rank Ferryman holds a pole, a flat-bottomed boat, and the license to a single modest crossing. The guild's first law is taught before swimming is: name the fare before the pole touches water, and give no one credit.*

**Playstyle.** Move allies where they should be and enemies where they should not, then charge for it. Weak alone in open field; strong at any edge, bank, or line.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Apprentice to a ferry no map marks, and learn to pole standing before you learn to sit.
2. Memorize the fare-table of your crossing: what coin, what kind, and what is never accepted.
3. Carry your first silent passenger at night, and do not ask what the far bank wanted with them.
4. Swear the Bank Oath: the fare before the water, the passenger before the weather, and no credit, ever.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Pole-Plant** — Drive the pole into any surface and swing yourself or a willing ally three paces across a gap, line, or threshold.
- **First Fare** — Name a toll on the ground between you; enemies crossing it uninvited pay in speed, arriving late and winded.
- **Shallow Draft** — You and one passenger ignore difficult ground, water, and marsh; you cannot be slowed while moving toward a bank.
- **Sounding** — Read any water, gap, or border at a glance, learning its depth, its danger, and what it charges to cross.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Oarsworn

**Role:** Mobility support — carries allies across the field · **Advances from:** Ferryman

*The Crossing begins at the shoulder. An Oarsworn has rowed against enough floods to be granted right-of-way: the guild seal that lets a boat pass where passage is refused — spring rivers, contested borders, other people's harbors. Soldiers wave them through, then wonder afterward why they did.*

**Playstyle.** Carry the party: haul allies across gaps, through lines, and out of encirclement. You win engagements by deciding where they happen.

**The road to Oarsworn:**

1. Reach level 10 and row a flooded river alone, upstream, with a passenger who never stops talking; deliver them dry.
2. Present your palms to the guild: the oar's callus must sit deeper than any sword's.
3. Cross a border at war twice in one night, once in each direction, saluted both times.
4. Take the Oar Oath: the crossing does not care whose war it interrupts.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Way-Leave** — Declare a lane across the field; allies moving along it cannot be blocked, slowed, or stopped until they reach the far side.
- **Heavy Weather Stroke** — Row through what should capsize you: while carrying an ally, damage to both of you is reduced until you land.
- **Both Banks** — Swap yourself with a willing ally anywhere in sight, provided a line of water, blood, or spilled drink connects you.
- **Following Sea** — Allies who move where you have just moved gain speed; the path remembers your stroke for six seconds.

### Fareholder

**Role:** Controller — tolls enemy movement and action · **Advances from:** Ferryman

*The Toll begins at the strongbox. A Fareholder keeps every fare in trust until its crossing completes — coin, keepsakes, quieter payments — and learns the fare-law's one rule by heart: everything that crosses pays, and nothing that pays is judged. Passengers trust them the way they trust bridges: completely, and without affection.*

**Playstyle.** Price the battlefield: enemies pay in speed, strength, or steel to act near you. You do little damage; you make everything expensive.

**The road to Fareholder:**

1. Reach level 10 and keep the fare-box of a busy crossing for a season; return every fare whose crossing failed, to the copper.
2. Refuse a king's coin because it was clipped, politely, in front of the king.
3. Accept one fare that is not money, and record honestly what it was worth.
4. Swear the Toll Oath: you are not the price; you only hold it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Turnpike** — Raise an invisible gate across a line; the first enemy through surrenders their swiftest thing until the fight ends.
- **Fare in Hand** — Catch one projectile mid-flight and hold it as a fare; release it along any crossing you own before it expires.
- **Small Fares** — Everything an enemy does within your circle costs slightly more — mana, stamina, footing — and the difference is paid to you.
- **No Credit** — An enemy who cannot pay a toll they triggered is stopped at the line until they can.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Fordwright

**Role:** Terrain support — builds crossings where none exist · **Advances from:** Oarsworn

*Fordwrights make the crossing before they make the trip: stepping-stones raised in a night, fords persuaded through spring floods, a plank across a chasm the map calls final. They do not bend distance — they obtain permission. Engineers hate them; the rivers, for reasons unrecorded, do not.*

**Playstyle.** Build the field's geography in your favor: fords, plank-roads, stepping-stones. Your party crosses what enemies must go around, or into.

**The road to Fordwright:**

1. Reach level 25 and ford a river in flood by building as you cross, one stone ahead of your own feet.
2. Bridge a chasm the guild lists as final; charge the guild full fare to inspect it.
3. Ask a river's permission properly — three nights listening at the bank — and be able to prove it said yes.
4. Leave one crossing unbuilt because the far side asked you to.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Raise Ford** — Persuade a ford up through any water or mire; allies cross it dry, and it dissolves under the first hostile boot.
- **Six Stones** — Set six stones across any gap — air included; each stone bears one crossing and then sinks politely.
- **Gangplank Law** — Extend a plank to any ledge, deck, or battlement in sight; allies boarding by it count as passengers, and passengers arrive.
- **The River Agrees** — Any crossing you build cannot be destroyed while a passenger is on it; the river honors work in progress.

### Palefarer

**Role:** Spirit ferrier — sustains, rescues, carries the dying · **Advances from:** Oarsworn

*Palefarers pole the water that runs between waking and gone. They carry the newly dead outward and, when the fare allows, the nearly dead back — a carrier's errand, not a collector's. Necromancers covet their manifest and are refused; the Palefarer keeps no souls, only appointments.*

**Playstyle.** Ride the line between banks: pull dying allies back across, ship wounded to safety, and make the enemy's dead simply leave.

**The road to Palefarer:**

1. Reach level 25 and row the pale water by night until you can tell, by the sound of boarding, who is coming back.
2. Deliver your first outbound passenger and never look astern; whatever calls from the wake is practicing your voice.
3. Bring one passenger back alive with the fare paid in full beforehand; the guild audits these crossings twice.
4. Refuse a necromancer's charter, at any offered price, and file the refusal.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Back Across** — Take an ally dying within the last six seconds aboard and pole them back; they wake ashore, fare already paid.
- **Outbound Manifest** — Name a corpse or risen dead as your passenger; it boards, and nothing — including its former master — may recall it.
- **Pale Water** — Flood a zone ankle-deep with the between-river; the dying there stop dying, and the dead there start leaving.
- **Coin for the Quiet** — Place a coin on a fallen enemy; they cannot be raised, animated, or questioned, being already mid-crossing.

### Toller

**Role:** Attrition controller — prices enemy action, punishes evasion · **Advances from:** Fareholder

*Tollers keep the fare-law itself: the old schedule of what may cross and what it costs, memorized whole because writing it down has been tried. The name is exact — they collect the toll, and they toll the bell, and seasoned travelers listen carefully to which service they are getting.*

**Playstyle.** Attach prices to everything the enemy wants: movement, casting, retreat. Collect steadily; when a toll is dodged, the bell settles it.

**The road to Toller:**

1. Reach level 25 and recite the schedule of fares entire, in the dark, while the examiners ring wrong bells.
2. Toll the bell once for a crossing that failed, and make it sound different from the bell for one that succeeded.
3. Collect from something that has never paid: a flood, a rumor, a lordling with soldiers. The law does not list exemptions.
4. Set the fare for a crossing no one has ever survived; set it fairly.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Schedule of Fares** — Post prices over the field: each kind of enemy ability costs an added tithe of health when used.
- **Dodger's Bell** — An enemy who crosses your line without paying is tolled: marked, slowed, and audible to your whole party through walls.
- **One for the River** — Collect a dodged toll by force: strike for damage equal to everything the target has avoided paying this fight.
- **Peak Rates** — Declare high water: for ten seconds every toll you have posted doubles, and payment is taken up front.

### Wharfinger

**Role:** Strip specialist — enemies leave things behind · **Advances from:** Fareholder

*Wharfingers keep the near bank's long sheds, where everything that may not cross is left: weapons, grudges, names, one lamed horse of unusual opinions. They decide nothing — the fare-law decides — but they take custody of the law's leavings, and their inventory is the most dangerous room in most provinces.*

**Playstyle.** Disarm by regulation: make enemies shed weapons, wards, and blessings at your lines. What they carry past you, they chose to pay for.

**The road to Wharfinger:**

1. Reach level 25 and inventory the long shed without touching anything twice; several items will try for a second touch.
2. Take custody of a cursed crown, a live grudge, and a sword that weeps; label all three legibly.
3. Return a surrendered thing to its owner after thirty years, unchanged and unasked-about.
4. Refuse to sell one item from the shed to someone with excellent reasons; the shed does not sell.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Leave It at the Bank** — Draw a customs line; the first enemy across sheds one carried enhancement, which sits in your custody until the fight ends.
- **Contraband** — Name one enemy item or effect contraband under the schedule; while it is borne, its bearer takes steady damage and cannot hide.
- **Impound Hook** — Hook a shield, weapon, or familiar out of enemy hands into the shed; reclaiming it requires visiting you.
- **The Long Shed** — Open the shed's door briefly; one item ever surrendered at any wharf is available to your hand for a minute.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Pontifer

**Role:** Mass mobility engine — permanent lanes for many · **Advances from:** Fordwright

*Pontifers hold that a crossing, once made, belongs to everyone forever; they pole the first passage and then pin it open — stone, piles, standing right-of-way. Three churches want the title back and have been invoiced for it. Where a Pontifer has worked, no border is entirely serious again.*

**Playstyle.** Institutionalize movement: standing lanes, party-wide crossings, choke points converted to boulevards. Slow to set up; afterward your whole army travels like rumor.

**The road to Pontifer:**

1. Reach level 40 and pin open a crossing an empire ordered closed; survive the empire's letters.
2. Build a passage a beggar and a king use the same afternoon, at the same fare.
3. Hold a bridgehead against a Ford-Drowner sent to sink your work; convert nothing, bury nothing, simply out-remain them.
4. Answer the churches' final demand for the title in person, by building them a bridge to argue across.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Pin the Crossing** — Make one of your crossings permanent for the battle; it cannot be destroyed, closed, or owned by anyone else.
- **Public Passage** — All allies gain your right-of-way on lanes you have declared, boarding, crossing, and disembarking without triggering attacks.
- **Toll-Free Day** — Suspend your own tolls; for eight seconds every ally ability that moves someone costs nothing and cannot be interrupted.
- **Bridgehead** — Where any lane of yours touches enemy ground, allies within it take reduced damage and cannot be pushed off.

### Consigner

**Role:** Escort bulwark — guarded delivery, unstoppable transit · **Advances from:** Palefarer

*A Consigner's manifest is a promise with a keel under it. Whatever boards, lands: the dead, the dying, the priceless, the pursued. Storms are negotiated with; pirates are billed for the delay. The order's records show no passenger lost in four centuries, and one poler still out, presumed rowing.*

**Playstyle.** Take custody of someone or something and deliver it through anything. While the consignment lives, you are very hard to argue with.

**The road to Consigner:**

1. Reach level 40 and deliver a witness through a city that has voted twice to prevent it.
2. Complete the oldest open consignment on the order's books; the passenger will have grown strange in transit.
3. Refuse the Hulkswains' offer to take an undeliverable off your hands; there is no such word on your manifest.
4. Row four days awake with a fever aboard, and make the landing on the tide you quoted.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Under Consignment** — Take one ally under consignment; harm meant for them arrives to you instead, reduced, until you set them down safely.
- **All Weather** — While escorting, you cannot be slowed, feared, or turned around; the delivery consumes the effect and logs it.
- **Signed On Landing** — On safe delivery, you and the delivered are healed and briefly untouchable while the paperwork completes.
- **Never Lost** — If your consignment would die aboard, the crossing extends instead: both of you vanish midstream and land three seconds later, alive.

### Strickler

**Role:** Precision striker — exact, normalized damage · **Advances from:** Toller

*Stricklers carry the strickle: the plain rod drawn across a grain-measure to level it exact. Their order holds the fare-law's whole authority to live in that gesture — nothing heaped, nothing hollow. A Strickler's strike costs what the schedule says, to the grain, and pity has never once adjusted a measure.*

**Playstyle.** Deal exact sums: strikes that ignore variance, armor, and fortune. The enemy faces the schedule instead of chance, and the schedule does not miss.

**The road to Strickler:**

1. Reach level 40 and level a measure blindfolded to the single grain, three times, while the examiners heap it unfairly.
2. Charge your own order the posted fare for your own investiture, and count the change aloud.
3. Find the crossing where a Kindtaker accepted a memory as fare, and reassess it in grain; deliver the difference, whichever direction it runs.
4. Break your strickle the day it flatters you, and cut the next from wood that has never grown beside a market.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Struck Level** — Draw the strickle: your next strike deals exactly its listed damage, unmodified by armor, wards, luck, or opinion.
- **Heaped Measure** — An enemy who overpays — overextends, overcommits, overcasts — has the excess swept off and delivered to you as vigor.
- **Short Weight** — Detect any enemy carrying more than they paid for; your next hit removes exactly the difference.
- **The Grain** — Once per fight, reduce any incoming blow, however vast, to the smallest unit on the schedule.

### Sequester

**Role:** Custodial tank — impounds harm, holds effects · **Advances from:** Wharfinger

*A Sequester is the law's strongroom given a pulse. Whatever is surrendered at the bank, they hold — unworn, unspent, unread — against the day someone returns with the claim-chit. Their order has waited out dynasties. Undergivers call the sheds a dam across the river's due; Sequesters file the accusation with the rest.*

**Playstyle.** Impound the fight: catch blows, spells, and rages into custody and hold them. Release nothing; outlast everything. Your inventory is your armor.

**The road to Sequester:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand custodian of the long shed through a fire, a flood, and a very persuasive claimant with no chit.
2. Hold a surrendered war-banner through the war that wanted it back; return it afterward to whoever still fits the claim.
3. Learn each shelf blind, because some of what you keep goes quieter when watched.
4. Refuse the Undergivers' annual demand to sink shelf nine; renew, in writing, your offer to hold them too.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Into Custody** — Catch an incoming spell or projectile into the shed; it counts toward nothing and waits there, tagged, until the fight ends.
- **Claim-Chit** — Issue a chit; an ally may instantly reclaim anything of theirs the enemy stripped, dispelled, or stole this fight.
- **Storage Fees** — While holding impounded effects, you gain armor per item held; the shed appreciates being full.
- **Deep Shelf** — Once per battle, impound one enemy — whole — for six seconds; they wait on a shelf, safe, untargetable, and furious.

### Ford-Drowner

**Role:** Infiltration mover — one-way, traceless crossings · **Advances from:** Fordwright

*Every crossing the guild pins open, someone crosses third: the army behind the refugee, the huntsman behind the bride. Ford-Drowners pole you over and give the ford back to the river — drowned, unmapped, gently denied. Pontifers call them misers of passage. Drowners answer that they have never once been followed.*

**Playstyle.** Cross once, cleanly, and unmake the way behind you. Insertions, extractions, and ambushes from banks the enemy is certain are unreachable.

**The road to Ford-Drowner:**

1. Reach level 40 and unbuild, stone by stone in one night, the finest crossing you ever made; leave the river looking uninterrupted.
2. Ferry a bride, her dowry, and her reasons over water no pursuit can name; drink the toast at the wedding under a different name.
3. Sink a Pontifer's newest bridge and attend the inquiry as a helpful witness; contribute nothing but sympathy.
4. Cross your last open crossing; from now on, every way you go closes behind you like water, because it is water.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Drown the Ford** — Collapse any crossing — yours, theirs, natural — into deep water the moment your last passenger steps off.
- **No Second Crossing** — Enemies cannot follow a route your party has used this fight; the way is simply not there for them.
- **Quiet Pole** — Your boat makes no sound, wake, or reflection for one crossing; divination watching the water sees only water.
- **Meet the River** — A pursuer who steps where your ford was meets the river instead, and the river was waiting.

### Hulkswain

**Role:** Zone anchor — shelters and fields the in-between · **Advances from:** Palefarer

*Consigners deliver everything; Hulkswains keep what delivery would break. Off the far bank ride the hulks — roomy, patient vessels for passengers no shore will have: the half-dead, the mid-oath, the interrupted. A Hulkswain feeds them, counts them, and asks nothing. The banks call it a fleet of ghosts. The crew call it home.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor a hulk to the fight: a floating refuge whose strange passengers lend cold hands. Shelter the failing; unsettle the shore.

**The road to Hulkswain:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand the night watch on a hulk alone; by morning, know which sounds are the boat.
2. Take aboard a passenger the Consigners have failed to land three times, and stop trying to land them.
3. Feed the hulk's oldest passenger their remembered meal, correctly, without being told.
4. Sign the standing manifest: you berth what boards, and you do not ask it to finish dying.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Berth the Broken** — Take a crippled, cursed, or dying ally aboard the hulk; aboard, their condition pauses and cannot worsen.
- **Cold Crew** — Your in-between passengers crew the rails; enemies within reach are gripped by hands that hold no opinion of them.
- **Neither Bank** — Stand aboard and be neither living target nor dead one; abilities keyed to either miss you entirely.
- **Deep Berths** — The hulk holds any number of passengers; each aboard slows enemy action nearby, the field disliking so much in-between.

### Kindtaker

**Role:** Exchange specialist — converts anything into passage · **Advances from:** Toller

*Kindtakers accept the fares coin cannot carry: a season of sleep, a mother's name, the color blue. Their order holds that a price is an exchange, and equal is a word with rooms in it. Stricklers call this heresy by weight. Kindtakers call it the schedule, read aloud slowly.*

**Playstyle.** Trade in kind: convert health to speed, an enemy's fury to your fare, a cooldown to a crossing. Everything is currency if named correctly.

**The road to Kindtaker:**

1. Reach level 40 and accept a fare of pure kind — no coin aboard — for one full year of crossings, and end the year solvent.
2. Ferry a passenger who owns nothing, and leave both banks agreeing the fare was paid in full.
3. Refuse to take a fare the passenger will die without, even offered freely; equal is not the same as everything.
4. Sit the Stricklers' audit and balance every strange fare against grain; when the rod comes up level, say nothing satisfying.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Payment in Kind** — Accept a named thing from a willing ally — sight, seconds, warmth — and convert it into any equal boon on your schedule.
- **What It's Worth** — Appraise an enemy's active effect and take it as a fare; they lose it, and you may spend it once.
- **Strange Tender** — Pay any cost of your own abilities with something other than the listed resource; the exchange rate is honest and unkind.
- **Fare of Last Resort** — Once per day, accept a fare coin cannot match — a fear, a first memory — to buy an ally out of a killing blow.

### Undergiver

**Role:** Sacrificial nuker — sinks value for power · **Advances from:** Wharfinger

*Undergivers row the surrendered things to the deep channel and give them under, weighted, with the words said right. The river works, their order teaches, and workers are paid — every shed hoarding the river's share silts a crossing somewhere shut. What the water accepts, it remembers fondly. Sequesters are not invited to launchings.*

**Playstyle.** Feed the river to wield it: sink items, advantages, and your own reserves; the water answers with current, weight, and reach.

**The road to Undergiver:**

1. Reach level 40 and carry your first full cargo to the deep channel alone; whatever asks to stay aboard, nothing stays aboard.
2. Sink a fortune with witnesses and no speech; the river dislikes being thanked on its behalf.
3. Give under one thing you were keeping for yourself. The order will not name it. You will.
4. Watch the drought year end where you sank the crown, and file no claim of credit.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Given Under** — Sink something of value — yours or impounded — into the channel; the river returns it as raw force within ten seconds.
- **The Deep Channel** — Open the channel beneath one enemy; they are pulled waist-deep into water that exists only for them.
- **Ballast Rite** — Bind a sunk offering to a target; they carry its weight, slowing and sinking through whatever they stand on.
- **The Bore** — Sink every fare you hold; a river bore crosses the field, carrying allies forward on its back and enemies underneath it.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Right of Way

**Role:** Unstoppable vanguard — movement as standing law · **Advances from:** Pontifer

*Histories agree something crossed; they disagree on what. The Right of Way is passage with a person somewhere inside it: where they walk, a lane exists, has always existed, and admits whoever follows. Sieges have ended because a supply road politely appeared. Armies do not block them. Armies are, briefly, traffic.*

**Playstyle.** You are the road. Wherever you move becomes lane, and stays lane; your party rides the wake through everything the map calls solid.

**The road to The Right of Way:**

1. Reach level 60 and walk a straight lane through the Shut City, whose charter forbids one; leave the charter amended.
2. Open a crossing so old the river that used it is gone; something else will be using it.
3. Be refused by the mountain wall of Kellwatch, once; return with the paperwork.
4. Grant one army passage and its enemy passage the same day, and collect both fares at the posted rate.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Eminent Passage** — Declare passage through any obstacle — wall, army, mountain-root; the lane opens for your party and remains a public crossing.
- **Give Way** — Everything hostile in your lane must yield: enemies are pressed to the verges and held there while traffic passes.
- **Ancient Easement** — Reveal the easement that always ran through here; treat one interior, fortress, or formation as open ground for a minute.
- **Through Traffic** — Allies moving along your lanes cannot be targeted until they stop; the law protects those in transit.

### The Manifest

**Role:** Mass deliverer — everyone aboard arrives · **Advances from:** Consigner

*Somewhere the full manifest is kept: every passenger every ferry ever promised, and one hand still checking names. The Manifest is that hand, or the list, or both — the order stopped distinguishing. Speak your name near water and it is entered; entered names arrive, eventually, whatever intervenes. Refunds are not a concept.*

**Playstyle.** Board the field: take whole parties into consignment and land them where promised. Nothing you carry can be lost, and you carry everyone.

**The road to The Manifest:**

1. Reach level 60 and find the poler four centuries out; take the oar for the last mile and let them sleep.
2. Deliver a passenger whose destination sank a century ago; the manifest does not accept geography as an excuse.
3. Read the whole manifest once. Do not linger on your own entry, which is long, and still open.
4. Add one name no ferry promised, pay the fare yourself, and never tell them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Boarding Call** — Every ally in earshot boards your protection at once; while aboard, they move with you and share your defenses.
- **Entered Names** — Enter an ally on the manifest; death becomes delay, and they step ashore alive at your next completed crossing.
- **The Long Way Round** — Any ally banished, taken, or teleported against their will lands on your deck instead; abduction is just boarding, mislabeled.
- **Delivered Entire** — Land all passengers at a point you name; each arrives whole, healed, and facing the right direction.

### Dead Reckoning

**Role:** Absolute striker — exact outcomes, no variance · **Advances from:** Strickler

*Navigators reckon dead when no stars serve: position known by speed, heading, and time alone. Dead Reckoning is what the Stricklers' exactness became when it stopped needing instruments — a person who knows precisely where everything is, what everything costs, and how far each living thing stands from its far bank. They are never consulted twice.*

**Playstyle.** Remove chance from the engagement: your strikes land for known sums, enemy variance is struck level, and every outcome you quote occurs to the grain.

**The road to Dead Reckoning:**

1. Reach level 60 and cross an ocean of fog with no star, no coast, and no error you are willing to discuss.
2. Quote a war's cost to both crowns beforehand, to the man; decline to round.
3. Measure the far bank's distance from yourself, and carry the number without behaving differently.
4. Break your last instrument on the gunwale; the reckoning is you now, and it does not drift.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Reckoned Position** — Know every enemy's exact position always; stealth, illusion, and distance are bookkeeping errors, corrected.
- **Quoted Sum** — Announce the damage of your next strike; it deals exactly that, through anything, and cannot be a different number.
- **Strickle Sweep** — Sweep the field with the strickle: every temporary advantage, heap, and hollow on both sides is struck level at once.
- **Distance to the Bank** — Once per day, measure what stands between a target and the far bank, and remove exactly that much.

### The Left Bank

**Role:** Eternal custodian — impounds without limit · **Advances from:** Sequester

*Below the sheds the shelves continue, and the Left Bank walks them: keeper of everything no one returned for — abdicated crowns, unfinished vows, one war both sides surrendered and neither collected. The office holds all of it in perfect trust. Any valid chit is honored instantly. None has ever been presented.*

**Playstyle.** Hold the fight itself in custody: impound damage, effects, even outcomes, and shelve them indefinitely. You do not win engagements; you retain them.

**The road to The Left Bank:**

1. Reach level 60 and walk past the last numbered shelf; number what you find, and do not read anything that asks you to.
2. Take custody of a thing that is still happening.
3. Refuse a claimant with every reason and no chit; grieve afterward, off the premises.
4. Outwait something immortal that wants shelf nine opened. Bring nothing to read. It will blink.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Custody Without End** — Impound any effect on the field — hostile, friendly, ongoing — indefinitely; it resumes only if lawfully claimed, which is unlikely.
- **Shelf of Wars** — Shelve an entire enemy assault for ten seconds: projectiles, charges, and spells wait in the stacks, tagged.
- **Unfinished Things** — Wield one unclaimed item from the deep shelves each battle; it is always, unsettlingly, exactly what was needed.
- **No Valid Chit** — Damage that would kill you is impounded instead, pending a claim against your life; claims are processed slowly.

### The Midnight Ford

**Role:** Covert mover — crossings that never happened · **Advances from:** Ford-Drowner

*Somewhere there is a ford that exists only while it is being crossed, at an hour that is not quite on the clock, and the Ford-Drowners' last rank is its keeper — or the ford itself; Drowners decline to clarify. Fugitives find it. Pursuers find the river. Histories mention it only in retractions.*

**Playstyle.** Move the party without evidence: crossings that close, tracks the water takes back, arrivals no scout can explain. Ambush is your commute.

**The road to The Midnight Ford:**

1. Reach level 60 and cross the same border every night for a year without the border ever having been crossed.
2. Drown your own ferry's name and take no new one; the ford does not answer to names.
3. Carry a hunted family over, then drink in the hunters' camp while they describe the river that stopped them.
4. Be seen mid-crossing once, by one child, and become the story that keeps the ford fed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Never Crossed** — Move your party across any water or border; all traces, memories, and divinations of the crossing drown behind you.
- **The Ford Opens** — At night, open the midnight ford between any two banks you have seen; it exists only underfoot, and only once.
- **Take the Tracks Back** — The river reclaims your party's tracks, scents, and dropped evidence across the last minute; pursuit reverts to guessing.
- **Wrong Bank** — A pursuer who follows your crossing arrives at the wrong bank: alone, reversed, and wet in a place with no name.

### The Mooring

**Role:** Sanctuary anchor — harbors the unlandable · **Advances from:** Hulkswain

*Downriver of everywhere, the hulks ride at permanent anchor, and The Mooring is what they are tied to. Half-dead kings, unfinished ghosts, plague ships no port will name — everything the banks refuse rides there in good order, fed, counted, and unjudged. The banks call it a menace. The banks are not asked.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor the hulks to the battlefield: a floating sanctuary that shelters allies, holds the in-between, and slowly unnerves everything hostile ashore.

**The road to The Mooring:**

1. Reach level 60 and take the oldest hulk's watch for a decade; learn every passenger's name and no passenger's story.
2. Turn no one away for a hundred years. The hundred-first will test the record. Turn them not away either.
3. Hold anchor through the fleet-burning at Corvel Reach; take aboard both admirals, in-between and furious, in adjacent berths.
4. Refuse the Consigners' final audit: nothing aboard is undelivered; everything aboard is home.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Make Fast** — Anchor the Mooring in any terrain; allies aboard cannot be targeted, and dying allies stabilize at the waterline.
- **Strange Crew** — The in-between crew your rails; enemies boarding uninvited are handled by passengers the banks never learned to fight.
- **No Port Asked** — Take aboard anything mid-effect — a cursed ally, a burning charge, an unexploded spell; aboard, it simply waits.
- **Slip Moorings** — Weigh anchor and relocate the whole sanctuary, crew and sheltered included, to any water in sight.

### The Going Rate

**Role:** Reality broker — anything traded for anything · **Advances from:** Kindtaker

*Ask what a thing truly costs, and somewhere the answer settles. The Going Rate is that settling, walking: once a Kindtaker, now the living equivalence between all things — a bridge priced in sons, mercy quoted in rain. Markets drift when they pass and correct when they leave. No one audits why.*

**Playstyle.** Exchange anything for anything: health for distance, fury for weather, an advantage for its opposite. Every fight is a market only you can read.

**The road to The Going Rate:**

1. Reach level 60 and price something the guild swears is priceless, and be neither struck down nor haggled with.
2. Sell nothing for a year while every market on the river quietly obeys you.
3. Quote the rate between two lives once, to no one, over water, and let the river carry it away unheard.
4. Hold the scale steady through a famine and a festival without once flattering either.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Settle the Price** — Fix the cost of one action battlefield-wide; anyone — ally or enemy — performing it pays your posted rate.
- **Equivalence** — Convert one effect into any other of equal worth: a wall into healing, a curse into distance, at once.
- **Market Correction** — When an enemy gains something unpaid-for, the field corrects: the gain transfers to whoever, by your reading, covered its cost.
- **The Rate for Ruin** — Quote the price of destroying you aloud; attackers pay it up front, per attempt, in whatever they value most.

### The River's Share

**Role:** Catastrophe channel — the river collects · **Advances from:** Undergiver

*Every river takes a share — a boot, a bridge, some years, occasionally a town. The River's Share is the hand it collects with: once an Undergiver, now the water's own reach, deciding nothing and collecting everything on schedule. Where the share is paid, floods arrive polite. Elsewhere, the rate is the rate.*

**Playstyle.** Channel the river's collections: drownings, bores, whole currents redirected through dry land. Sink what you hold for power that arrives like weather.

**The road to The River's Share:**

1. Reach level 60 and row out at high water with everything you own; row back with the boat.
2. Negotiate nothing with the flood at Marren Weir; walk beside it instead, pointing out what is spoken for.
3. Give under the thing the order kept back for three centuries; attend the silence afterward.
4. When the river hands you something in return — it will choose the hour — take it without counting.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Collection Day** — The river rises through the field to collect: every enemy loses their heaviest advantage to the water at once.
- **Undercurrent** — Run a current through solid ground; enemies standing in its path are swept, tumbled, and deposited where you choose.
- **The Polite Flood** — Call a flood that parts around allies and property whose share is paid, which is to say, yours.
- **Drowning Wages** — Sink your own health into the channel; the river returns it during the fight as force, threefold, on its own schedule.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Causeway

**Role:** World-road — passage as permanent law · **Advances from:** The Right of Way

*Some offices are roads. The Causeway runs over every water at once — the standing right of passage the world keeps where a Ferryman used to be. No map shows it whole; every ferryman poles a mile of it. Empires legislate against it and then, quietly, pave their bit.*

**Playstyle.** Exist as infrastructure. Allies anywhere travel your spans; enemies discover that every road they hold has always, legally, been yours.

**The road to The Causeway:**

1. Reach level 85 and lay the span nobody commissioned, between banks nobody agreed were banks; collect the fare anyway, forever.
2. Let a war end because both armies used you to go home.
3. Be paved, cursed, taxed, and renamed by six empires; outlast the empires and keep the tolls posted in the old amounts.
4. When the last river dries, remain: some crossings outlive their water.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Every Water at Once** — Open crossings over all waters on the field simultaneously; your party treats the map as a suggestion with bridges.
- **The Standing Mile** — Raise a mile of causeway anywhere, instantly, permanently; it counts as your ground, and your ground has rules.
- **Public Works** — Any lane an ally has ever crossed with you re-opens on command, wherever it was, whatever replaced it.
- **The World Yields** — Once per day, everything between your party and a destination becomes crossing: armies, seas, walls, weather — all briefly lane.

### The Far Shore

**Role:** Arrival incarnate — every landing is theirs · **Advances from:** The Manifest

*Arrival is not an event; it is a place all events borrow — and the place is kept. The Far Shore is what the Manifest became when the last name was delivered: the standing guarantee that crossings end, that decks give way to ground. Sailors thank the wrong saints for it. The saints forward it on.*

**Playstyle.** Guarantee arrival: allies land where intended, always. Everything hostile that travels also arrives — exactly where your manifest says, which is the difference.

**The road to The Far Shore:**

1. Reach level 85 and deliver the manifest's final name, then keep the office open; arrival does not retire.
2. Stand on every shore at once for one tide, and be the reason wrecks wash up findable.
3. Receive a fleet, a plague, and a prophecy in the same harbor week; land all three correctly.
4. When something without a destination finally comes across the water, be its far shore too. Everything lands. That is the office.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Everything Arrives** — No movement near you can be interrupted, reversed, or lost midway; all of it completes, at destinations you administer.
- **Assigned Landing** — Assign every enemy arrival — charge, teleport, reinforcement — a landing of your choosing; they come ashore there instead, on schedule.
- **Safe Ashore** — Allies who reach ground you stand on are healed whole and cannot be moved from it against their will.
- **The Last Delivery** — Once per day, declare any single journey complete immediately: the traveler, missile, or army arrives now, at the destination the fare actually covered.

### The Fixed Price

**Role:** Cost incarnate — everything charges exactly · **Advances from:** Dead Reckoning

*Under all haggling there is one true cost per thing, and the world checks it against something. The Fixed Price is what it checks against: the office the strickle was always pointing at. Near them, nothing arrives free — no spell, no crown, no rescue. Gods pay retail. This is resented, and exact.*

**Playstyle.** Enforce true cost: every hostile action charges its full price in real time, no exemptions. You rarely strike; the fight simply stops being affordable.

**The road to The Fixed Price:**

1. Reach level 85 and price the act of pricing; pay it once, in full, and never speak of the denomination.
2. Audit a miracle to the grain and send no report; the numbers were correct, which was the miracle.
3. Hold the rate steady while an age of wonders begs for discounts; wonders can afford it. That is what makes them wonders.
4. Become the reason nothing in the world is free, and accept how rarely you are thanked at the posted rate.

**Abilities granted:**

- **True Cost** — Every enemy ability now costs what it actually costs — health, years, memory included; the schedule is universal and posted.
- **No Exemptions** — Immunities, invulnerabilities, and free actions are repriced; using one charges its full listed rate, backdated to the field's start.
- **The Fare Before the Water** — Anything attempting to affect you must pay first; unpaid effects wait at the line indefinitely.
- **Exact World** — Once per day, level the entire engagement to the schedule: all heaped advantages on every side are struck to true measure.

### The Strand

**Role:** Shore of custody — everything lost, kept · **Advances from:** The Left Bank

*Everything the world loses washes up somewhere, and the somewhere is kept. The Strand is that shore and its keeper, no longer separable — the long beach where drowned fleets, dropped names, and misplaced centuries lie tagged above the tideline. Nothing rots there; nothing is sold. Beachcombers who find it once spend their lives failing to find it twice.*

**Playstyle.** Fight from custody: everything ever lost is briefly yours to field. Impound what threatens, retrieve what saves, and shelve entire assaults above the tideline.

**The road to The Strand:**

1. Reach level 85 and walk the beach past where your lamp ends; file what you find there under things that found you first.
2. Re-tag the oldest item yourself; the old tag goes on a shelf, tagged.
3. Return one thing at last to a valid chit, and learn why the office celebrates quietly and only once a century.
4. When the sea itself asks for its dead back, show it the schedule of claims and the window's hours.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Above the Tideline** — Move an entire hostile force's momentum above the tideline; the assault lies inert on the sand until lawfully claimed.
- **Tagged and Waiting** — Produce any object ever lost by anyone; it is in your custody, catalogued, and briefly willing to help.
- **The Wreck Returns** — Raise a drowned ship, bridge, or fortress from your inventory as terrain, positioned as suits the crossing.
- **Lost with All Hands** — Declare an enemy formation lost at sea; they wash up on your strand, disarmed, dazed, and thoroughly catalogued.

### The Fog Bank

**Role:** Weather of crossings — concealed passage everywhere · **Advances from:** The Midnight Ford

*Rivers keep one weather for their own business. The Fog Bank is it: the low white hour in which crossings happen off the record — held, the way offices are held, by whatever the Midnight Ford became when even a ford was too much evidence. Sailors call it a fog bank. The river knows which bank it means.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive as weather. Inside your fog, allies move unseen and unrecorded; enemies navigate by memory, which stayed on the other bank.

**The road to The Fog Bank:**

1. Reach level 85 and rise off the water for good; leave the pole standing upright midstream for the next rumor to find.
2. Settle over a war for nine nights until both armies, unable to find it, go home; take credit as weather does — never.
3. Be cursed by every navy and blessed by every smuggler in the same season, and roll in regardless of either.
4. When someone crosses inside you and forgets they ever left, keep their crossing safe until they need it never to have happened.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Roll In** — Settle over the field as fog; all allied movement inside is invisible, silent, and deniable afterward, including to diviners.
- **Off the Record** — Events inside your fog leave no evidence: no tracks, no witnesses' certainty, no scrying purchase, no history.
- **The White Hour** — For ten seconds, every ally may cross to anywhere else inside the fog, each crossing unobserved even by the others.
- **Lost in the Bank** — An enemy who enters the fog uninvited exits somewhere reasonable and wrong, having paid for a crossing they never booked.

### The Harborage

**Role:** Refuge eternal — shelter for the unlandable · **Advances from:** The Mooring

*Beyond the last chart there is anchorage for everything no port will take, and the world keeps it staffed. The Harborage is that office: the Mooring grown to the size of its manifest — plague fleets, half-gods, cities that sank still arguing. Storms detour around it. Nothing has ever been turned away, and the light is always lit.*

**Playstyle.** Be the safe water: berth allies, casualties, and unstable powers alike. What shelters with you cannot be reached; what attacks you is becalmed first.

**The road to The Harborage:**

1. Reach level 85 and drop anchor beyond the last chart; send back one buoy so the desperate can be lucky.
2. Berth the fleet that burned at Corvel Reach, every deck, both admirals still furious, and post quiet hours.
3. Grant anchorage to something the gods themselves misplaced overboard, and list it under general cargo.
4. Keep the light lit through the age when nothing comes; the office is the lit light, not the traffic.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Open Berths** — Shelter any number of allies in numbered berths; berthed allies heal, cannot be targeted, and disembark when they choose.
- **Becalm** — Still the water and air around hostile forces; their momentum, projectiles, and morale hang slack in the calm.
- **Harbor Light** — Any ally, anywhere, may spend their movement arriving at you instead; the light is always lit.
- **Take the Unstable Aboard** — Berth a catastrophe in progress — a detonation, a rift, a dying god — and let it ride at anchor, indefinitely.

### The First Question

**Role:** Threshold law — every price, named first · **Advances from:** The Going Rate

*Before any threshold anywhere is crossed, there is an instant in which the price is named. The First Question is that instant, held as an office. It was a Kindtaker once; now it is the pause on every doorstep, the breath before every bargain, the question the pole asks the water. Nothing crosses unasked. Nothing ever has.*

**Playstyle.** Interpose the question: every hostile act must first settle what it costs, with you. The fight proceeds only as fast as its answers.

**The road to The First Question:**

1. Reach level 85 and be present at every naming of every fare at once; keep each one straight, and keep none of them.
2. Ask the first question again — the one the first river asked the first bank — and this time, wait for the whole answer.
3. Stand in the pause before a god's decision until the god, uncomfortable, names their price like everyone else.
4. Never answer for anyone. The office is the question. It has always only been the question.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Name the Price First** — No enemy ability begins until its price is named through you; complicated things take longer to price, and hostility is complicated.
- **Asked and Answered** — An enemy who accepts their named price acts freely but pays it; one who refuses is still standing at the threshold.
- **The Pause** — Extend the instant before one crossing — a blow, a spell, a betrayal — indefinitely; the world waits with surprising grace.
- **Free Crossing** — Waive one ally's every cost for six seconds; the First Question asks them nothing, which the wise find more frightening.

### The High Water

**Role:** Flood sovereign — collection at world scale · **Advances from:** The River's Share

*Rivers rise on a schedule older than the towns that complain about it. The High Water is that schedule, kept past the need for a person: where it stands, valleys remember being riverbed and behave accordingly. The share is collected entire, unhurried, and without opinion. Lawsuits are filed against it annually. It banks them.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as the flood season: raise the field's water until the enemy position is riverbed in denial. Slow, total, indifferent to walls.

**The road to The High Water:**

1. Reach level 85 and rise once past every recorded mark, gently, collecting only what was always the river's; the argument will outlive the towns.
2. Spend one year as low water so complete that even the share goes uncollected; return to find the banks planted with thanks and higher fences.
3. Collect a tyrant's bridge and a village's footlog in the same rise, at the same rate, and be sung about entirely wrong both times.
4. When the world needs reminding where the water reaches, be the mark.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Riverbed Memory** — Remind any ground it was river once; it floods from below, and structures on it recall floating, briefly.
- **The Crest** — Arrive as the flood's crest across the field; enemies are swept, allies are lifted, and the fare is collected en route.
- **Spoken For** — Mark allies and their works as spoken for; no water of yours touches them, at any height.
- **The Share, Entire** — Once per day, collect a battlefield's share in one rise: every hostile structure, mount, and advantage goes to the river.
