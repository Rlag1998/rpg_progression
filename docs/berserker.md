# 🪓 Berserker

> *What winter could not kill, it armed.*

**Archetype:** Might

The marches breed them: fighters raised where winter kills more than war does, taught that pain is fuel and fear a language they never learned. A Berserker does not hold formation; formation holds its breath. Whether they invite a beast-spirit under the skin or simply refuse to stop moving, they end fights by making restraint irrelevant.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Berserker

**Role:** Melee bruiser — reckless sustained damage

*Born to the frozen marches, where winter culls the timid and the rage-fire is banked in every hearth-tale. The berserker learns early that pain is a door, not a wall. Villages feed them, fear them, and set them loose when the wolves, or worse, come down from the ice.*

**Playstyle.** Trade defense for damage. Build fury by taking and dealing hits, then spend it in short, terrifying windows where nothing else on the field matters.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Survive a winter the elders call a culling year, and come out of it angrier than you went in.
2. Take your first true wound and stand back up before the count of ten.
3. Let the fury take you once in front of witnesses, and learn its cost when you wake.
4. Be turned away from the war-band's fire, then follow them anyway until they stop turning you away.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Red Veil** — Enter a fury that raises all damage dealt and taken for twelve seconds; you cannot be feared or slowed while it lasts.
- **Blood Price** — Sacrifice a tenth of your current health to make the next strike unblockable and critical.
- **Howl of the Marches** — A shout that strips enemy courage effects and staggers everything in a cone before you.
- **Deaf to Fear** — Passive: fear, panic, and morale effects are ignored; gain fury whenever one is attempted against you.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Skinbearer

**Role:** Feral skirmisher — pursuit and bleed damage · **Advances from:** Berserker

*Some berserkers hear a second heartbeat inside the fury and go looking for its owner. Skinbearers keep the old bargains: meat left at treelines, blood given at solstice, a pelt worn until it stops being a garment. The spirit that answers moves in, and it is always hungry.*

**Playstyle.** Fast, low, and feral. Track wounded targets by scent, open bleeds that punish movement, and let the guest spirit sharpen every sense past human limits.

**The road to Skinbearer:**

1. Reach level 10 and carry a killed beast home whole, wasting nothing; the spirit watches how you treat its kin.
2. Sleep three nights in an abandoned den without fire, until you dream in smells instead of words.
3. Cut your palm on a solstice altar and speak the invitation; if something answers, do not flinch.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Guest Beneath the Skin** — Invite the beast-spirit in: attack speed and movement rise sharply, but you cannot disengage from wounded prey.
- **Borrowed Fangs** — A bite attack that opens a bleed which deepens each time the target moves.
- **Second Scent** — Detect hidden, invisible, and fleeing enemies within sixty paces; bleeding targets cannot break your tracking.
- **Winter Coat** — Grow a dense hide that halves cold damage and shortens every bleed laid on you.

### Bloodwader

**Role:** Frontline breaker — damage soak, forward pressure · **Advances from:** Berserker

*The marches remember warriors who walked into spear-hedges and came out the far side, annoyed. Bloodwaders court that memory. They learn to drink pain like mead, to keep moving when muscle says stop, and to make of their own body a weight the enemy cannot brace against.*

**Playstyle.** Walk forward through everything. Absorb hits to build momentum, ignore wounds until the fight ends, and be exactly where the enemy least wants you standing.

**The road to Bloodwader:**

1. Reach level 10 and stand in the practice line while five spearmen strike true; walk the field's length before you fall.
2. Take a wound the healers call mortal, and make them wrong by morning.
3. Carry a wounded shield-brother out of a rout, walking, without once looking back.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wade the Spears** — Advance at full speed through enemy reach; each hit taken while moving forward grants fury.
- **Shrug the Grave** — Once per battle, reduce a killing blow to a flesh wound and keep swinging.
- **Drunk on Hurt** — Passive: damage taken converts to a stacking damage bonus that decays only outside combat.
- **Landslide Step** — A slow, unstoppable advance that shoves enemies back and cannot be halted by roots, bodies, or fear.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Vargr

**Role:** Pursuit striker — single-target attrition hunter · **Advances from:** Skinbearer

*Named for the outlaw-wolves of the old tongue, Vargr leave the war-band for the long trail. They hunt what others flee: man-eaters, deserter kings, things that walk out of the ice. In three provinces, a Vargr's declaration of hunt is treated as a death already recorded.*

**Playstyle.** Choose one target and end them. Stack pursuit bonuses over days of chase, deny escape and healing, and strike when exhaustion has done half the work.

**The road to Vargr:**

1. Reach level 25 and follow a quarry through one full turn of the moon, eating only what the trail gives.
2. Refuse a hunt once — for coin, for cruelty, for a wrong scent — and be known for the refusal.
3. Run down something faster than you, and understand that speed was never the point.
4. Return with proof of the kill that the elders look at once and never again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Speak the Quarry** — Name a single target; against them you ignore armor thresholds and always know their direction.
- **Tireless Lope** — Travel without rest for days equal to your fury, and arrive at full strength.
- **No Den Deep Enough** — Your quarry gains nothing from stealth, sanctuary, or door wards while you are within a mile.
- **Winter's Patience** — Each day the hunt continues, your first strike against the quarry grows stronger, without cap.

### Urskin

**Role:** Shapeshift bruiser — claws, hide, raw grapple · **Advances from:** Skinbearer

*The bear-shirt is the oldest rite the marches keep. Urskin let the guest spirit past the skin and into the bone: hands broaden, hide thickens, voice drops below language. They are welcome at fires in the wary way a tame avalanche would be welcome.*

**Playstyle.** Slow, huge, and horribly durable. Shift deeper into beast-shape as the fight lengthens, trading finesse for claws, hide, and a grip nothing slips.

**The road to Urskin:**

1. Reach level 25 and wear the raw pelt through a winter, until you cannot say where it ends.
2. Let the spirit take your hands for one fight, and win it without a weapon.
3. Winter in a den beside a true bear and leave in spring, unchallenged.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Bear-Shirt** — Transform: gain claw attacks, doubled carrying strength, and hide that treats all weapons as blunt.
- **Winterhide** — Passive: regenerate steadily in combat; the rate doubles below half health.
- **Red Maul** — An overhead claw blow that crushes shields, breaks grapples, and flattens man-sized foes.
- **Below Language** — While transformed, mind-affecting magic finds nothing in you it can speak to.

### Woundtaker

**Role:** Attrition bruiser — converts damage to power · **Advances from:** Bloodwader

*Where others bind wounds, Woundtakers open accounts. Every cut is entered, remembered, and spent later with interest. Old soldiers recognize the look — a fighter glancing at a fresh wound the way a merchant checks a purse. It unsettles men more than the axe does.*

**Playstyle.** Deliberate attrition. Invite hits you can afford, bank the pain as fury, and out-economy enemies who believe damage dealt means they are winning.

**The road to Woundtaker:**

1. Reach level 25 and name every scar you carry, in order, without touching them.
2. Accept a wound in a duel you could have dodged, and win with what it paid.
3. Go a full campaign without letting a healer close anything you can spend.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Open the Ledger** — Begin recording: a portion of all damage taken is stored as spendable fury.
- **Spend the Scar** — Consume stored pain to drive a strike beyond your weapon's normal ceiling.
- **Ask for the Knife** — Willingly take a small wound to end any bleed, poison, or curse; the pain crowds it out.
- **Second Heartbeat** — Passive: below half health, fury generation doubles and your hands stop shaking.

### Breachwright

**Role:** Line-breaker — charge, knockdown, formation collapse · **Advances from:** Bloodwader

*Sappers dig for weeks; a Breachwright arrives. They study walls the way duelists study opponents — where a gate hinges, where a shield-line breathes — then apply themselves like falling masonry. Siege captains list them in inventories, somewhere between rams and reasons to surrender.*

**Playstyle.** Momentum management. Build speed and mass across the field, spend both on walls, gates, and formations, and never fight standing still.

**The road to Breachwright:**

1. Reach level 25 and put a shield wall on the ground — the whole wall — before your war-band closes.
2. Carry the old gate beam from the quarry road to the walls without setting it down.
3. Walk through a burning granary because it was the shorter way, and be seen doing it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Gathering Slope** — Each stride in a straight line stacks momentum, raising damage and knockdown force.
- **Keystone Blow** — Strike a structure's weak point; walls, gates, and shield formations all count as structures.
- **Doorless** — Passive: doors, barricades, and lines of pikemen do not end your charge, merely tax it.
- **Press the Rout** — Enemies you knock down rise slower; enemies who flee you spread fear as they go.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Fangthane

**Role:** Elite hunter — marked-prey execution · **Advances from:** Vargr

*A thane of the long hunt holds no hall. Their oath-torc hangs with a fang from every hunt completed, and no Fangthane's torc has a gap. Kingdoms retain them against horrors, then quietly pray never to earn a place on the torc themselves.*

**Playstyle.** The endgame of pursuit. Bind yourself by oath to impossible quarry, turn their fear into a leash, and close hunts other trackers died beginning.

**The road to Fangthane:**

1. Reach level 40 and close a hunt that outlasted a season, alone, across a border war.
2. Have your torc weighed at the gathering of hunters and found without a gap.
3. Decline a king's price for a hunt, then take the hunt for a reason he cannot buy.
4. Kill something that had killed a Fangthane, and hang both their fangs together.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Oathmark** — Swear a hunt: you cannot die of wounds while the quarry lives, and it knows you are coming.
- **Fear Is a Scent** — Frightened enemies are revealed, slowed, and take doubled bleed damage from you.
- **Culling Silence** — Your approach mutes sound and warning; sentries notice you only as the strike lands.
- **The Last Mile** — When the quarry falls below quarter strength, gain unstoppable speed and immunity to everything but the quarry itself.

### Ironpelt

**Role:** Immovable shapeshifter — anchor tank, grapples · **Advances from:** Urskin

*An Ironpelt has stopped shifting all the way back. The claws fold, mostly; the hide stays. They den apart from villages that nonetheless leave meat and grievances at the treeline, because an Ironpelt settles both. Whole raiding cultures have rerouted around a single known den.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor the field. Grapple what cannot be cut, endure what cannot be dodged, and regenerate through punishment that erases shield-lines around you.

**The road to Ironpelt:**

1. Reach level 40 and winter in the open den, sleeping the true sleep, and wake with the pelt grown inward.
2. Answer a village's grievance without being asked twice, and take payment in meat alone.
3. Hold a bridge through a cavalry charge, moving nothing but your arms.
4. Out-grapple the thing the spirit sends to test you, and let it leave alive.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Pelt of Iron Winters** — Passive: your hide counts as heavy armor that regenerates and cannot be sundered.
- **Denfather's Grip** — Seize a foe up to horse-size; while held, they take your regeneration as damage.
- **The Long Sleep** — Out of combat, sleep to heal any injury, regrow what was lost, and shed one curse.
- **Treeline Law** — Mark ground as den; within it your grip cannot be broken and your allies cannot be moved.

### Flayed Saint

**Role:** Martyr tank — damage transfer and aura · **Advances from:** Woundtaker

*The order keeps no relics but skin. Each Saint's scars are read like scripture, a wound-history of battles that should have had no survivors. Soldiers touch their hems for luck and flinch doing it. The Saints bless shield-lines by standing in front of them.*

**Playstyle.** A martyr in the tactical sense. Intercept harm meant for others, convert it to power, and hold ground that has no business holding.

**The road to Flayed Saint:**

1. Reach level 40 and let the order read your skin, scar by scar, omitting nothing.
2. Stand before a shield-line as the volley lands, and take what was meant for all of them.
3. Refuse the healers three times in one campaign, and outlive the campaign.
4. Bless a coward with a scar he asked for, and watch him hold the line for it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Give Me Yours** — Transfer any ally's wounds onto your own body; the pain converts to fury at double rate.
- **Litany of Scars** — Recite your wound-history aloud; allies in earshot cannot fear or rout while it continues.
- **Red Absolution** — A blow that grows stronger for every wound currently open on your body.
- **Standing Scripture** — Passive: while you are below half health, allies behind you take less damage and deal more.

### Towerbane

**Role:** Siege striker — structure and formation destruction · **Advances from:** Breachwright

*Fortresses keep two sets of plans: one for war, one for a Towerbane. The elite of the breach-trade end sieges by arriving; garrisons that see the banner — a black keystone on grey — are permitted by long custom to open the gates first.*

**Playstyle.** A one-man siege. Convert momentum into structural collapse, walk out of the rubble, and turn every fallen wall into a road for the army behind you.

**The road to Towerbane:**

1. Reach level 40 and end a siege that had lasted a year, between one dawn and the next.
2. Take a standing tower down with nothing you did not carry in your two hands.
3. Be buried in a collapse of your own making, and let them hear you knocking from inside.
4. Accept the black keystone banner from a Towerbane grown too old to lift it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Toll the Gate** — Announce yourself with a single blow to the gate; wood, iron, and defender morale all take it.
- **Rubble Stride** — Collapsing structures deal you nothing; you emerge from debris at full momentum.
- **Read the Masonry** — See every load-bearing weakness in a structure or formation at a glance.
- **Bring Down the Sky** — Topple a tower, arch, or gatehouse onto a chosen area after three uninterrupted blows.

### Quietus

**Role:** Elite hunter — undeclared single-strike kills · **Advances from:** Vargr

*The Fangthanes swear their hunts aloud; the Quietus finds that vanity, and worse, cruelty. Fear sours a quarry like panic sours meat. This order kills without declaration, without pursuit the prey ever notices, without a moment of dread. Shepherds pray for them by name. Their quarry never learns to.*

**Playstyle.** Stalk without ever being detected, keep the quarry calm and unaware, and end the hunt in one strike that lands before fear can.

**The road to Quietus:**

1. Reach level 40 and take a quarry a Fangthane has already sworn aloud, finishing it before the oath is a day old — unwitnessed, unfeared, unclaimed.
2. Sit at your quarry's fire as a guest, share its bread, and leave without doing the work; learn to wait for the kind hour.
3. Kill a man-eater in the middle of its sleeping pack without waking one of them.
4. Be credited by nobody. The order confirms your first true kill by finding no story of it anywhere.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Unspoken Hunt** — Choose a quarry in secret; it gains no warning instincts, omens, or premonitions of you while unaware of the hunt.
- **Calm the Herd** — Your presence soothes animals and sentries into inattention; alarms within sixty paces of you simply fail to be raised.
- **One Clean Breath** — A single strike against an unafraid target deals quadruple damage and kills without sound, struggle, or pain.
- **Spoiled Meat** — Passive: your damage drops against frightened targets, but all your bonuses double against targets that feel entirely safe.

### Bearward

**Role:** Shapeshift duelist — commanded transformation, controlled fury · **Advances from:** Urskin

*Where the Ironpelt surrendered and denned apart, the Bearward broke the guest to harness and stayed. They sit at the fire with human eyes, and the village never quite relaxes. The spirit hates the bit, obeys it, and waits. Every Bearward knows exactly how the arrangement ends, and wears it anyway.*

**Playstyle.** Precision shapeshifting. Don the beast for exact windows, spend its strength without its appetites, and pay for control with a spirit that fights the harness.

**The road to Bearward:**

1. Reach level 40 and win the second wrestling — the one inside — pinning the guest until it accepts the bit.
2. Shift to the bear and back nine times between one bell and the next, ending each turn with clean hands and a full memory.
3. Sit through a village feast transformed, breaking nothing, frightening no one old enough to know better.
4. Refuse an Ironpelt's den-peace to their face, and explain why, and leave on speaking terms with everything but the spirit.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Broken to Harness** — Transform instantly, fully, and on command; the shape never lingers, slips, or acts without your order.
- **The Bit** — While transformed you keep speech, judgment, and craft; frenzy, bloodlust, and provocation effects find no purchase.
- **Let It Slip** — Loose the harness for six seconds of the spirit's full hatred, doubling all damage; regaining control costs health.
- **Fireside Manners** — Passive: you may halt any transformation mid-motion; beasts and beast-spirits within sight must acknowledge you before acting against allies.

### Weregild

**Role:** Retaliation bruiser — instant wound-for-wound reprisal · **Advances from:** Woundtaker

*The marches never wrote their law; they remember it in weregild, the price paid for every hurt. This order made the price immediate. Where a Flayed Saint hoards wounds like scripture, a Weregild carries none: every blow is returned in kind before the blood cools, and the skin heals smooth, keeping nothing.*

**Playstyle.** Counter-attrition. Answer every hit with its equal in the same breath, close your own wounds without trace, and make striking you a losing trade.

**The road to Weregild:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand a full judicial duel returning only what you are given, blow for blow, and walk away unmarked while your opponent is carried.
2. Recite the weregild table of the marches from memory, then correct the elder reciting after you.
3. Let a Flayed Saint read your skin and find nothing — no scar, no history, no scripture — and hold their eye while they call it obscene.
4. Return a wound to a warlord across a battlefield, publicly, so the marches remember why the price exists.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Paid in Kind** — Every wound you take appears identically on its maker within a heartbeat, bypassing armor, wards, and distance.
- **Sealed Skin** — Passive: your wounds close without scarring once their price is collected; nothing lingers, bleeds, or festers on you.
- **The Set Price** — Name aloud what a blow against you costs; any enemy who strikes you regardless pays it doubled.
- **Whole and Even** — While you carry no open wounds, you cannot be slowed, weakened, or marked by any lingering effect.

### Leveller

**Role:** Siege zealot — total demolition, area denial · **Advances from:** Breachwright

*The Towerbanes end sieges; the Levellers end places. Their creed is short — what can fall, must — and their work is never partial: walls, then foundations, then the memory of foundations. Captains hire them reluctantly, because a Leveller does not stop at the terms of surrender. Or, strictly speaking, at all.*

**Playstyle.** Methodical annihilation. Grind structures past rubble into ground, deny enemies anything to rebuild or rally behind, and grow stronger standing in what you have flattened.

**The road to Leveller:**

1. Reach level 40 and finish what a Towerbane started: return to a breached fortress after the treaty and take it down to the root-stone.
2. Refuse the black keystone banner when it is offered; carry no banner at all, because banners are things that stand.
3. Sleep one full night on ground you levelled, in the open, and wake certain you did a kindness.
4. Grind a milestone to powder with your hands while the road's owner watches, as an estimate.

**Abilities granted:**

- **What Can Fall, Must** — Passive: your blows against structures continue working after you move on; anything you crack keeps cracking until it is ground.
- **Down to the Root-Stone** — Destroy foundations, cellars, and wards anchored in them; ground you clear cannot support new construction or fortification magic.
- **Open Ground** — On ground bearing no standing structure, your damage, speed, and fury generation all rise by half.
- **The Creed Recited** — Speak the creed before a fortification; defenders inside fight at a penalty, already believing the walls temporary.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### Wolf of the Long Night

**Role:** Apex predator — inevitable single-target death · **Advances from:** Fangthane

*Histories argue whether there has been one or many; the torc suggests one. When the Wolf speaks a name, chroniclers write the death first and the date after. Border tales say winter follows them out of courtesy, and that they have not slept since the oath.*

**Playstyle.** Play inevitability itself. Declared prey cannot heal, hide, or bargain a way out; everything and everyone else on the field is merely in the way.

**The road to Wolf of the Long Night:**

1. Reach level 60 and accept the hunt no gathering will speak aloud.
2. Follow the quarry past the map's last honest line, and keep the trail through places with no ground.
3. Kill what history says cannot be killed, and let the chroniclers strike its name.
4. Come back to the fires, and say nothing, and be believed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Doom Spoken Once** — Name a living thing; it can no longer be healed, resurrected, or hidden by any power.
- **The Long Night** — Draw unnatural dusk across a mile; within it you see everything and your quarry sees only you.
- **The Hunt Remembers** — Inherit the tracking knowledge of every hunter who ever failed against your quarry.
- **No Last Door** — Wards, gates, and other planes do not bar your pursuit, and deaths you deliver cannot be undone.

### First Beast

**Role:** Primal avatar — escalating transformation, zone control · **Advances from:** Ironpelt

*The eldest spirit — the one that taught wolves hunger and bears winter — does not take guests. It takes a body. Once in an age, an Urskin walks into the high tundra, and what walks out answers no human name, though it still, for its own reasons, defends the fires.*

**Playstyle.** A permanent, deepening transformation. Cycle primal aspects mid-fight, hold ground through sheer presence, and grow stronger the longer the battle forgets to end.

**The road to First Beast:**

1. Reach level 60 and climb past the treeline carrying no iron, wearing every pelt you have earned.
2. Offer the eldest spirit your name, and let it be eaten without asking what remains.
3. Walk back down in spring on feet that no longer print as human.
4. Defend one fire without being asked, so the marches know what came back is still theirs.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Shape Before Names** — Cycle three primal aspects — fang, hide, antler — without pause; none can be dispelled or dismissed.
- **The Old Growl** — A subsonic warning: beasts obey, mounts throw riders, and lesser courage simply leaves the field.
- **Blood of the First Winter** — Your blood freezes to armor where it spills; every wound taken thickens it.
- **Den of the World** — All wild ground counts as your den: regeneration for you, fearlessness for allies who stand on it.

### Agonarch

**Role:** Immortal attrition — banked-damage burst · **Advances from:** Flayed Saint

*The Agonarch has died before witnesses — the annals record where, and how often — and returned each time angrier. Pain no longer visits; it pays tribute. Veterans say a battlefield goes quiet when the Agonarch begins counting scars aloud, because everyone present knows a new entry is coming.*

**Playstyle.** Immortal attrition. Bank every point of damage taken across an entire battle, then repay the whole ledger at a moment of your choosing.

**The road to Agonarch:**

1. Reach level 60 and read your full ledger of scars aloud to the order, one origin at a time, forgetting nothing.
2. Die before a hundred witnesses, and stand up while they are still deciding to grieve.
3. Forgive one debt — a single scar, freely — and learn what that mercy costs you.
4. Walk unarmored into a war and end it by demonstrating what its best blow is worth.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Throne of Scars** — Damage taken is banked instead of killing you; only the ledger's full total can end you.
- **All Debts Called** — Release the banked pain as a single strike or a field-wide shockwave of equal total.
- **Tribute** — Attackers take a share of the pain they deal you, doubled if they flee afterward.
- **The Wound That Rules** — Passive: your wounds close on their own, faster the more enemies can see them.

### Walking Ruin

**Role:** Catastrophe engine — army-scale momentum damage · **Advances from:** Towerbane

*Chronicles class them with floods and fire-mountains rather than soldiers, and the insurance canons of three cities agree. A Walking Ruin does not besiege; it schedules. The last one to cross the marches is the reason two duchies now share one very wide road.*

**Playstyle.** Terminal momentum. Accumulate unstoppable mass and speed over an entire battle, and let geography, architecture, and armies resolve themselves around your path.

**The road to Walking Ruin:**

1. Reach level 60 and cross a kingdom that refused you passage, in a straight line.
2. Have a fortress surrender to your name while you are still two days away.
3. End a war by attending it, once, briefly.
4. Leave one wall standing, deliberately, so the histories know it was a choice.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Name Arrives First** — Enemies who learn you are coming begin the battle already shaken; some simply do not attend.
- **Nothing Holds** — Passive: no wall, ward, or formation counts as an obstacle, only as material.
- **Momentum of Ages** — Your momentum no longer decays; each battle begins at the force the last one ended.
- **Terminus** — Stop moving, once per war, and release all gathered momentum as a collapse felt for miles.

### Winterkill

**Role:** Mythic hunter — deaths indistinguishable from nature · **Advances from:** Quietus

*Chroniclers have no entry for the Winterkill, which the order considers the only proof required. Tyrants die of age early; horrors starve in good years. Every march keeps a count of quiet deaths no one questions, and the elders keep a second count, of which ones the winter cannot honestly claim.*

**Playstyle.** Erase the hunt itself. Kills read as accident, age, or weather; no alarm, oath, or vengeance can attach to a death that was never noticed as one.

**The road to Winterkill:**

1. Reach level 60 and cull a name from the world so cleanly that its own kin light no vengeance-fires, only mourning ones.
2. Take a tyrant in his guarded tower between heartbeats, and let the succession proceed grieving, unrobbed of even one night's sleep.
3. Refuse the gathering's torc when it is offered; the order's honor is that no gathering can prove you exist to honor.
4. Walk through the marches in a culling year and leave the count of quiet deaths exactly one short of what the elders feared.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Death of Natural Causes** — Your kills leave no wound, mark, or magical trace; divination reads them as misfortune and cannot name you.
- **The Lean Season** — Mark a region for culling; within it, your chosen quarry weaken daily as if wintering badly, and only you know why.
- **No Story** — Passive: witnesses, wards, and recording magics fail to retain you; memory of your passing settles into something ordinary.
- **Mercy of Predators** — Targets that never feared you die instantly below a third of their health, painlessly, regardless of remaining defenses.

### The Yoke

**Role:** Mythic spirit-breaker — commands what possesses others · **Advances from:** Bearward

*Beasts have hearth-tales of their own, told in scent and hackle, and the Yoke is what they frighten their young with. Somewhere below the tundra a thing older than hunger wears a bit, and carries a rider. Spirits that once took bodies now ask first, in case the answer is listening.*

**Playstyle.** Ride the broken eldest thing into battle. Command every beast-shape at once, conscript hostile spirits mid-fight, and spend their hatred as fuel.

**The road to The Yoke:**

1. Reach level 60 and go below the tundra where the eldest spirits winter, carrying a harness you forged yourself, and do not come up alone.
2. Break a thing that taught the teaching spirits, and hold it pinned until it speaks your name as an owner's.
3. Ride it once through the gathering of hunters, slowly, so that every order revises its opinion of what can be tamed.
4. Dismount at your village fire and hang the harness where children can reach it, because the point was never the riding.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Eldest, Bitted** — Call up the thing you broke: a primal spirit given flesh, mountain-shouldered, obedient, and furious; nothing it charges holds formation.
- **Ask First** — Possessing spirits, curses, and riding entities must petition you before touching anyone in your warband; you may refuse, or redirect them.
- **Harness of the Wild** — Any beast or beast-spirit you strike must serve you for a hundred heartbeats before it may flee or fight again.
- **Hatred as Harness** — Passive: hostile fury, fear auras, and dominating effects aimed at you are seized as fury for you and your mount.

### The Unwounded

**Role:** Mythic retaliator — unwoundable, war-ending reciprocity · **Advances from:** Weregild

*Histories dispute whether the Unwounded has ever been hurt. Veterans know better: every wound it ever took is still out there, walking around in the bodies that made them. Armies now settle terms rather than engage, having done the arithmetic. Its skin is the only unmarked thing the marches have produced.*

**Playstyle.** Absolute reciprocity. Damage cannot rest on you — it returns doubled to its maker, and whole formations learn that harming you is simply harming themselves slower.

**The road to The Unwounded:**

1. Reach level 60 and receive an army's opening volley alone; end the battle before your side arrives, without striking one blow of your own.
2. Find the man who scarred you first, before the order, before the law, and give it back grown thirty years older.
3. Be struck by something no law can price — a god, a season, a grief — and send even that home again.
4. Outlive the last chronicler who claims you were ever wounded, unmarked, and attend the funeral politely.

**Abilities granted:**

- **No Purchase** — Passive: wounds cannot remain on your body; each returns to its origin within three heartbeats, doubled if delivered from ambush.
- **Up the Chain** — Cross a battlefield untouched; any volley, spell, or charge aimed at you resolves against those who ordered it.
- **Even the Old Scores** — Touch a scarred ally; their oldest wound leaves them and finds whoever dealt it, wherever that maker now hides.
- **The Arithmetic** — Reveal to all enemies present exactly what attacking you will cost them; those who can read futures rout first.

### The Fallow

**Role:** Mythic eraser — permanent razing, battlefield subtraction · **Advances from:** Leveller

*Three cities appear on old maps and no new ones, and the cartographers' guild lists the cause, in careful hand, as the Fallow. Where it finishes, ground refuses masonry the way scorched fields refuse seed. Scholars argue whether this is a person or a condition. The marches see no difference worth arguing.*

**Playstyle.** Erase fortification from the war entirely. Unmake structures at a glance's patience, salt ground against rebuilding, and fight best where nothing stands at all.

**The road to The Fallow:**

1. Reach level 60 and finish a city the wars began, alone, over a patient year, leaving standing water and good pasture.
2. Turn down a crown's ransom to spare a citadel, then take the citadel apart for nothing, so the creed stays clean.
3. Stand in the last archway of a place you have ended and let it fall on you, and walk out the far side of the dust.
4. Watch farmers plow your first great razing without knowing a city fed the soil, and tell no one.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Let the Ground Rest** — Declare a razed site fallow; for a generation, no wall, ward, or siegework can be raised upon it.
- **Condition, Not Person** — Passive: while any structure you damaged still stands, you cannot be killed, bound, or turned away from it.
- **Harvest of Walls** — Every structure that falls within a mile feeds you fury; a city yields a war's worth.
- **Struck from the Maps** — Finish a place completely and it fades from maps, songs, and claims; armies cannot rally to defend what has no name.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### Midwinter

**Role:** Cosmic hunter — the year's dark made lethal · **Advances from:** Wolf of the Long Night

*The marches no longer say the Wolf hunts in winter; they say winter is the hunting. On the year's longest night, doors are barred not against cold but against appointments, for every doom ever spoken falls due at Midwinter, and the office that keeps the calendar has never been late.*

**Playstyle.** Hunt as a season hunts. Dooms you speak ripen on a calendar no power can appeal, and the world's dark hours are all your ground.

**The road to Midwinter:**

1. Reach level 85 and hunt the year itself through its shortest days, until the calendar concedes and gives its longest night your name.
2. Let the Wolf of the Long Night die in the histories — mourned, disputed, complete — and answer to the season instead.
3. Keep the calendar for one full year: every doom spoken anywhere falls due on your night, and none may come early.
4. Spare one door on the longest night, unknocked, so the marches keep lighting fires instead of despairing of them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Calendar of Teeth** — Speak a doom with a date; when it arrives, the target dies wherever and whatever they are, no save, no appeal.
- **All Nights Are One Night** — Step from any darkness into any other darkness you have ever hunted through, arriving mid-stride.
- **Season of the Wolf** — Passive: in every winter everywhere, your speed, senses, and inevitability effects act at double strength.
- **Bar the Door Anyway** — Nothing barred, warded, or hidden counts as shelter from you; entering costs you only the courtesy of knocking once.

### Hearthlaw

**Role:** Primal warden — instinct-deep protection of the fires · **Advances from:** First Beast

*Wolves are born knowing not to cross firelight. Nothing taught them; something set it there, and the marches know what. When the First Beast finished defending the fires one by one, the defending settled into every wild thing's blood as law. Hearthlaw is what remains: a rank, an instinct, a boundary the dark obeys.*

**Playstyle.** Hold every fire at once. Project den-law across whole regions, command the instincts of wild things, and be present wherever firelight meets the dark.

**The road to Hearthlaw:**

1. Reach level 85 and defend one fire each night of a whole winter, no two fires alike, until the dark stops testing and starts remembering.
2. Give the eldest spirit back its shape and keep only the duty, so that what walks to the fires now is yours alone.
3. Be present at two fires at once, verifiably, on a night when both needed you.
4. Watch a wolf refuse to cross firelight, and know the refusal by name, because it is yours.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Every Fire a Den** — Any lit fire counts as your den: allies within its light cannot be moved, feared, or hunted.
- **Bred in the Bone** — Passive: beasts and beast-spirits obey you as instinct, not command; compulsion cannot make them cross you.
- **Answer Every Fire** — Step out of any hearthfire within the marches when something threatens the people keeping it.
- **The Old Shape at the Edge** — Things from beyond the firelight must challenge you before touching anyone inside it; most, remembering, decline.

### The Old Ache

**Role:** Cosmic attrition — the world's pain answers to one · **Advances from:** Agonarch

*Old scars ache when weather turns. Healers call it humors; the marches know the truth: something passes, taking a census. The Agonarch's ledger outgrew one body, and now every healed wound in the world reports to the office that survived it. Three faiths define damnation as a fraction of what it carries.*

**Playstyle.** Carry all pain that was ever survived. Draw on a world's worth of banked hurt, wake old wounds in enemies, and be past killing.

**The road to The Old Ache:**

1. Reach level 85 and take on the ache of every scar you have ever caused, all at once, and remain standing while it settles.
2. Let the Agonarch's annals close with a death the witnesses agree on, and keep walking under a name only weather uses.
3. Answer one old soldier's aching knee in person, at his fire, and take the ache with you when you go.
4. Hold the world's whole hurt through one long night without spending any of it on anyone, to prove you keep it and not the reverse.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Census of Scars** — Sense every scarred creature in a region, know each wound's story, and draw fury from the sum.
- **Every Wound Remembers** — Reopen any healed wound an enemy has ever taken; veterans face you carrying their whole careers.
- **Ache Before the Storm** — Passive: every scarred creature within a day's ride feels you coming, and chooses its side early.
- **Past Killing** — Passive: you cannot die of damage; anything that would kill you becomes pain, and pain has nowhere left to go but out.

### The Glacier

**Role:** Geologic catastrophe — unstoppable mass on world scale · **Advances from:** Walking Ruin

*The chronicles classed the Walking Ruin with floods and fire-mountains; the classification matured. A Glacier is a catastrophe with a calendar: it publishes its route generations ahead, and mountains begin negotiating. Nothing about it hurries and nothing about it stops, and the insurance canons of eleven cities simply cite the map.*

**Playstyle.** Momentum as geology. Advance at any pace and remain unstoppable at all of them, carry battles in your wake, and reshape maps by walking.

**The road to The Glacier:**

1. Reach level 85 and keep walking after the war, the employer, and the century that hired you have all ended.
2. Publish your route for the next hundred years and honor it exactly, through whatever builds there in the meantime.
3. Let a kingdom found a city in your path on a bet, and arrive on schedule, and be nobody's fault but the founder's.
4. Grind one mountain pass wide enough for two duchies to share, as an apology or a joke; the histories may decide.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Published Route** — Declare your path years ahead; along it your momentum never decays, and everything knows better than to be there.
- **Moraine** — Ground you cross is permanently altered: walls cannot stand, roads run wide, and armies cannot form lines.
- **Geologic Patience** — Passive: slows, stuns, and stops convert into stored momentum, released the instant you resume.
- **The Calving** — Shed accumulated mass as an avalanche across the field; once per age, shed all of it.

### The Good Death

**Role:** Cosmic cull — painless ending as standing office · **Advances from:** Winterkill

*Some deaths hurt, and some arrive like sleep after harvest, and the marches long ago stopped believing the second kind is luck. The Good Death is an office: it culls what winter would only torment, takes the suffering out of endings that were coming anyway, and has never once been witnessed working.*

**Playstyle.** End what must end, gently. Unseen, unfeared, striking only the strikes that finish; the field's dying simply stops hurting where you walk.

**The road to The Good Death:**

1. Reach level 85 and take over the quiet deaths of one entire province for a year, so that no one in it dies afraid.
2. Let the Winterkill's count end even, and the elders' second count end empty, and be the reason for both.
3. Refuse a king who wants his enemy to suffer, and grant his enemy the gentlest ending the office keeps, as instruction.
4. Sit with something dying that you may not touch — a friend, say — and keep the office's hands still all night.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sleep After Harvest** — Any creature you end feels no pain, no fear, and no regret; nothing can make your kills cruel.
- **The Herd's Winter** — Cull a battlefield: everything already dying dies now, gently, and everything that can be saved stops bleeding.
- **Never Witnessed Working** — Passive: you cannot be perceived, recorded, or remembered while doing the office's work, by anything.
- **Not Yet** — Refuse a death: once each night, something that should die under your hand instead recovers completely.

### The Wain

**Role:** Celestial rider — heaven's beast under harness · **Advances from:** The Yoke

*Ask an astronomer why the Great Bear is also called the Wain, and watch the profession change the subject. Something went up the sky's long hill with a harness, and now the constellation hauls: seasons arrive on time, drawn. Every ox that consents to a yoke is honoring a precedent set overhead.*

**Playstyle.** Drive the sky's own beast. Haul seasons, tides of battle, and the unwilling wild wherever you point the pole, at the pace of stars.

**The road to The Wain:**

1. Reach level 85 and drive your bitted eldest up the long hill of the sky, and do not stop for the protests of astronomers.
2. Yoke it to something that must be pulled and cannot be — the year, say — and lean in until it moves.
3. Let the marches rename the stars for your cart while you are still using it, which is the only fame the office permits.
4. Come down once a year in deep winter to haul firewood for the village that fed you, because the point was never the sky.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Point the Pole** — Turn the constellation; night creatures, migrations, and lost armies all follow it, because they always have.
- **Haul the Season** — Drag a season into arriving early or staying late across a region; winter, notably, comes when carted.
- **Precedent of the Yoke** — Passive: anything that has ever worn harness — beast, spirit, or siege engine — obeys you first and its driver second.
- **Ride Down the Sky** — Once per war, drive the Wain through a battlefield at ground level; what survives the crossing surrenders.

### The Cost

**Role:** Universal reciprocity — violence priced, collected in person · **Advances from:** The Unwounded

*Old captains tell recruits to count the cost before drawing. They no longer mean it as figure of speech. Since the Unwounded's skin outlasted its chroniclers, every blow struck anywhere carries a price that is somewhere, patiently, being counted — and marshals who plan wars now leave one chair empty at the table.*

**Playstyle.** Make violence expensive. Every hostile act near you rebounds by law rather than reflex, and entire campaigns stall when the arithmetic is shown.

**The road to The Cost:**

1. Reach level 85 and stand between two armies at the charge, and let both learn at the first exchange what the morning will cost.
2. Collect the price of a massacre three generations cold, from the house that profited, in full view of the histories.
3. Take a blow meant for no one — spite, spent on a wall — and return it anyway, so the law admits no exceptions.
4. Sit in the empty chair at a war council, once, and watch the war be quietly cancelled.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Empty Chair** — Attend any council of war uninvited; plans made in your presence honestly price their own harms, aloud.
- **Law, Not Reflex** — Passive: within a mile of you, all damage dealt is simultaneously dealt to its dealer; no speed, ward, or god exempts.
- **Collect in Person** — Name a slaughter that went unpaid, however old; its price transfers to you to deliver, with your hands.
- **Waive Nothing** — You cannot be bribed, blessed, or argued out of the collection; effects that would compel mercy fail, expensively.

### The Common

**Role:** World-law — ground unclaimable, walls impossible · **Advances from:** The Fallow

*Where the Fallow finished, grass came back; where the Common walks, ownership does not. Ground it has crossed can never again be walled, deeded, or held — it becomes common land by a law no crown remembers passing and none has survived contesting. Shepherds graze three former empires and pay no one.*

**Playstyle.** Abolish the fortified world. Walls fail near you on principle, claims lapse, and battle becomes what it was first: bodies on open ground.

**The road to The Common:**

1. Reach level 85 and walk the borders of every land you ever levelled; where your feet pass, the fences forget which side is whose.
2. Outlast the last deed that names your first razing, and be present, politely, when the archive's wall comes down.
3. Refuse a throne offered by people who misunderstood you completely, and level nothing in the refusing.
4. Watch shepherds graze the ground where the citadel stood, arguing about weather, owing nothing, and go unrecognized among them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **No Fences** — Passive: walls, wards, and borders within a mile of you stop functioning as barriers; they remain, embarrassed, as suggestions.
- **Common Ground** — Declare a battlefield common land; neither side may fortify, entrench, or hold it, and sieges there simply lapse.
- **The Grazing Right** — Allies on unowned ground heal, regain fury, and cannot be dispossessed of their footing by any force.
- **Older Than Crowns** — Laws, decrees, and magical claims of ownership have no hold on you or anything you stand upon.
