# 🌙 Witch

> *Power grown slow, cut fresh, and owed to no one.*

**Archetype:** Esoteric

The witch's power is a garden, not a grant: planted in hedgerows, brewed in kettles, buried under thresholds and dug up stronger. She learned no grammar and signed no compact — the craft is folklore, which works anyway. Villages fear her, consult her by the back door, and would riot if she left. Every remedy has its hour; every hex has its root.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Witch

**Role:** Versatile caster — hexes, remedies, slow control

*Every village keeps one, at the lane's end where the hedge grows strange. She was somebody's apprentice once — swept the floor, learned which mushroom forgives and which does not. The craft came with the cottage: charms against fever, words for warts, a garden nobody robs twice. The parish disapproves of her weekly, and consults her nightly.*

**Playstyle.** Preparation and patience: brew beforehand, hex early, and let workings ripen mid-fight. Weak in a fair trade of blows, so never offer one.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Be the child who watches where the old woman buries things, and have the sense not to dig.
2. Learn the garden's two rows: what mends taken at dawn, what kills taken at dusk, and that they are the same row.
3. Nurse one fever through a bad winter with nothing but what the hedge gave you.
4. Inherit the cottage, the kettle, and the visitors who knock at the back door after dark.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Kettle Cure** — Brew a rough draught that restores health over a minute; its potency doubles if it was bottled before the fight began.
- **Small Spite** — Lay a minor curse that saps a target's next three attacks; it itches worse each time they mention it.
- **Rowan and Red Thread** — Knot a charm that turns aside the first blow aimed at its wearer in every fight.
- **Besom Line** — Sweep a threshold across the ground that spirits and strangers hesitate to cross; the first who does loses their footing.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Wortcunner

**Role:** Hybrid support — poisons and mending brews · **Advances from:** Witch

*Wortcunners commit to the green half of the craft: simples, tinctures, and the exact hour a leaf turns from cure to killer. They keep gardens the parish calls kitchen plots and the knowing call arsenals. Farmwives fetch them for birthings; poisoners' victims fetch them after. Both leave payment on the same sill.*

**Playstyle.** Manage a satchel of brews with strict hours: dawn-picked heals, dusk-picked harms. Match the dose to the moment; the wrong hour helps the enemy.

**The road to Wortcunner:**

1. Reach level 10 and keep a garden whose every row can mend or murder, depending on the hour you cut it.
2. Sit a season with a dying herbalist and take dictation; the last page is yours when she no longer needs it.
3. Cure a poisoning you were once paid to arrange, and return the first fee.
4. Taste everything you grow, once, in the smallest measure; write down what you learn while your hands still obey.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Dawn Picking** — Salves from dawn-cut herbs mend allies over time and close bleeding wounds on the second application.
- **Dusk Picking** — The same herb, cut at dusk: coat a blade or a cup, and the target weakens with every passing hour.
- **Nightshade Measure** — Dose a target precisely — too little to kill, exactly enough that their strongest ability fails when they reach for it.
- **Stillroom Garden** — Plant a small standing garden on the field; allies who pluck from it receive your dawn brews, enemies your dusk ones.

### Nightgoer

**Role:** Pet-and-ritual caster — familiar synergy, shared rites · **Advances from:** Witch

*Nightgoers have been fetched to their first meeting: a lantern at the crossroads, a round of strangers who all knew her name. The coven shares work the way farms share harvest — no grammar, no compacts, just many hands. Somewhere on the walk home, a small creature falls in step and does not leave.*

**Playstyle.** Never work alone: split labor with a fledgling familiar and draw on the round's pooled strength for slow, communal castings. Alone, you are half of something.

**The road to Nightgoer:**

1. Reach level 10 and answer the knock that comes at the dark of the month; bring nothing but your own name.
2. Dance the round widdershins and sunwise both, and mind which one your feet prefer; the coven minds too.
3. Share out a working among thirteen hands and take your thirteenth of the cost without complaint.
4. Walk home alone the long way, and greet politely whatever small thing chooses to walk with you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Fall In Step** — A small spirit in a borrowed body fights beside you; it is clumsy yet, but it harries one enemy and watches your back.
- **Round-Strength** — Channel the coven from afar: your next working casts as though three of you had spoken it.
- **Crossroads Lantern** — Set a lantern marking a meeting-ground; allies inside it share a portion of every heal any one of them receives.
- **By Night** — Travel unremarked between dusk and cockcrow; hostile eyes slide past you while you carry the coven's errands.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Eyebiter

**Role:** Ranged debuffer — wasting curses, slow kills · **Advances from:** Wortcunner

*Eyebiters specialize in the hex that takes root: a look over a fence, a name said backward, a wax figure sweating by the fire. Their curses are planted things — slow, seasonal, patient as bindweed. Assize courts acquit them regularly, there being no law against looking. Juries nonetheless prefer to convict someone else.*

**Playstyle.** Plant curses early and tend them: each hex ripens through stages if left uncleansed. Kill slowly, from ambush of time, and never twice the same way.

**The road to Eyebiter:**

1. Reach level 25 and grow one curse a full year, from seed-spite to harvest, without once touching your target.
2. Stand trial for witchcraft and be acquitted on the evidence; leave the jury certain and the record clean.
3. Learn the nine ways to say a name backward, and the one occasion proper to each.
4. Sour a strong man's luck so gently he blames the weather. Collect nothing. This one was practice.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Overlook** — Bewitch whatever you can see from where you stand; the target's luck sours, and sour luck compounds daily.
- **Bindweed Hex** — Plant a curse that roots and spreads; each day untreated, it claims the strength of one more limb.
- **Poppet** — Bind a wax likeness to a target; harm done the doll arrives on the body, discreetly, at an hour of your choosing.
- **Said Backward** — Unsay a target's name in their hearing; their own wards mistake them for a stranger and briefly stand aside.

### Pellar

**Role:** Healer — cures, cleanses, costly restorations · **Advances from:** Wortcunner

*Pellars are fetched when the curse is already in the walls: the herd dry, the child fading, the churn refusing butter. They unwitch — lift hexes, draw elf-shot, turn workings home — and they cure, though every pellar's cure has a price stated plainly at the door. Nothing mends for free. The good ones say so first.*

**Playstyle.** Diagnose, then trade: every cleanse and cure lands harder when its stated cost is paid up front. Triage is the whole art; charity is a dosage error.

**The road to Pellar:**

1. Reach level 25 and lift a hex you cannot see from a child you cannot lose, and charge the family one hen.
2. Trace a curse to its planter across three parishes by the taste of the well-water alone.
3. Set a price you hate, once, and hold to it; a pellar who discounts is a pellar who lies.
4. Carry what you have drawn out of others until you learn where it can safely be put down. There is no such place. Carry it anyway.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Unwitching** — Lift any curse, hex, or ill-working from an ally; lifted workings do not vanish — they wait in your satchel.
- **Drawing the Shot** — Draw elf-shot, venom, or fever out through the skin as a small stone; keep the stones. They are useful later.
- **Stated Price** — Name a cure's cost aloud before casting; if the patient accepts, the healing cannot fail and cannot be dispelled.
- **Turning Home** — Send a curse back up its own root to whoever planted it, along with everything it learned on the way.

### Gyre-Carline

**Role:** Group amplifier — shared power, ritual zones · **Advances from:** Nightgoer

*The gyre is the coven's turning round, and the carline is who calls it. The title means old woman and is conferred without regard to age or sex; the gyre does not care, and the villages find it simpler. A carline keeps the calendar of meetings, the tally of shared work, and the peace between thirteen strong opinions.*

**Playstyle.** Conduct group workings: pool allies' strength into single greater castings and share it back out as blessing. Keep the round turning under fire.

**The road to Gyre-Carline:**

1. Reach level 25 and be handed the calendar by a carline who has watched you dance for seven years.
2. Hold the round steady through a working that fails; a circle that scatters once will scatter twice.
3. Settle a feud between two witches without losing either; the gyre turns on such hinges.
4. Learn every step of both turnings, sunwise and widdershins, well enough to teach the one you did not choose.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Call the Round** — Gather allies into a turning circle; while it holds, their smaller abilities are braided and cast as one greater working.
- **Thirteenth Share** — Divide a working's cost among every willing ally; divided costs lighten, and no one carries the whole.
- **Kept Calendar** — Rites cast on their proper night — full, dark, or quarter — hold a full night longer than they should.
- **Peace of the Round** — Enemies caught inside your circle must observe its courtesy: only one may act against you at a time.

### Impwife

**Role:** Pet controller — spirit familiar, split attention · **Advances from:** Nightgoer

*Impwife is a trade name, like alewife: one who keeps spirits the way others keep bees. Her familiar is no pet and no servant — a bound colleague wearing a small warm body, paid in milk, blood, and civility. The arrangement is employment, not borrowing; she owes it wages, never herself.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as a firm of two: the familiar scouts, carries hexes, and works your will at distance while you hold the kettle steady.

**The road to Impwife:**

1. Reach level 25 and keep one spirit fed, housed, and unresentful for a year and a day.
2. Learn its true errand-name, and never once use it to compel; compulsion is the lender's habit, not yours.
3. Negotiate its wages annually. Expect to lose the negotiation. Budget for this.
4. Sit up with it through its bad night, whatever a spirit's bad night is. Do not ask. Bring milk.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wages** — Feed your familiar a drop of blood; it works at double vigor until sunrise, and it remembers generosity.
- **Small Errand** — Send the familiar to deliver any touch-range working across the field; it travels low, quick, and unremarked.
- **The Small Watch** — See through your familiar's eyes at will; while you watch from there, your abandoned body keeps its wards doubled.
- **Civility** — A familiar treated well interposes once per fight, taking a killing blow in its small body and re-forming by dawn.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Overlooker

**Role:** Single-target executioner — earned, measured dooms · **Advances from:** Eyebiter

*The Overlookers hold that a hex is a verdict, and verdicts must be earned. They look long before they look hard: weeks of watching, the wrong confirmed twice, the sentence measured to the crime and not a scruple past it. Blightsowers call them squeamish. Overlookers reply that frost has no jury, and they are not frost.*

**Playstyle.** Build a case against one target: observed wrongs become marks, marks become sentence. Devastating against the guilty; nearly harmless to the innocent, which is the point.

**The road to Overlooker:**

1. Reach level 40 and watch a bad man for a season without acting; act on the day the case is complete, not before.
2. Curse the guiltier of two brothers and be right. The order checks.
3. Refuse a rich commission because the target had not earned it; refuse the explanation too.
4. Stand a year under another Overlooker's watching, and give her nothing to sentence.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Long Watching** — Study a target to reveal their wrongs as marks; each mark deepens every hex you land on them.
- **Measured Doom** — Sentence a marked target: damage equal to the wrongs witnessed, delivered over as many nights.
- **Not One Scruple** — Your curses cannot spread, splash, or err onto bystanders; in exchange, the deserving cannot resist them.
- **Seen It Done** — Once per day, declare a wrong committed in your sight; every mark on its doer ripens at once.

### Ague-Eater

**Role:** Tank-healer — absorbs every ailment into self · **Advances from:** Pellar

*Ague-Eaters end sickness by eating it: the fever crosses a shared cup and burns in the witch instead. Their order keeps its saints in bed and its funerals private. Painsetters call the practice gluttony dressed as mercy. Ague-Eaters, between chills, observe that mercy is the only thing worth being sick with.*

**Playstyle.** Pull every wound, ailment, and curse in the party into your own body, then outlast your collection. A bulwark whose armor is appetite.

**The road to Ague-Eater:**

1. Reach level 40 and eat a village's whole fever-season, house by house, and be seen upright at the end of it.
2. Learn which of your carried ills keeps the others in order, and keep it.
3. Refuse the Painsetter's arithmetic to her face, politely; you will work beside her again.
4. Write your will early and update it annually; the order finds this steadies the hand.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Shared Cup** — Drink an ally's affliction out of them; it afflicts you at half strength, and you have had practice.
- **Fever-Hoard** — Each ailment you carry thickens your hide; at five carried ills, new hostile workings cannot find room in you.
- **Sickbed Authority** — Allies near you cannot worsen: bleeds, poisons, and curses pause while you are watching them.
- **Sweat It Out** — Purge everything you carry as one contagious exhalation; nearby enemies catch what you were kind enough to take.

### Deasil

**Role:** Party engine — blessings, regeneration, compounding luck · **Advances from:** Gyre-Carline

*Deasil is the old word for sunwise, and the Deasil Rounds dance with the day: their gyres knit flesh, sweeten wells, call rain in season, and lay luck on cradles by the dozen. They hold that many hands exist to fill, not empty. A shire with a Deasil Round wants for little, and denies having one with suspicious firmness.*

**Playstyle.** Turn the circle toward increase: layered blessings, compounding regeneration, luck bent generously. You win by making allies unkillable and harvests boring.

**The road to Deasil:**

1. Reach level 40 and turn a starving parish green in one year using nothing traceable to miracle.
2. Dance the sun up on the year's longest day with your whole round, and feel it arrive early by a breath.
3. Bless three cradles of families who spat at you; the turning keeps no score.
4. Break one widdershins working gently, and invite its caster to dinner. Rivalry is not feud.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sunwise Turn** — While your round turns with the sun, all healing received by allies in the circle increases for each ally dancing.
- **Cradle-Luck** — Lay luck on an ally at dawn; the day's first three killing blows against them miss, narrowly and deniably.
- **Sweetened Well** — Bless a point of ground; allies who drink, rest, or rally there shed one affliction and keep the taste.
- **Rain in Season** — Call the rain the season intended; allies mend beneath it, fires fail, and the enemy fights in mud of your scheduling.

### Toadstone

**Role:** Pet bulwark — housed god, guarded ground · **Advances from:** Impwife

*Toadstones keep the great in the small: a drowned mill's genius in a toad, a forgotten harvest god in a one-eyed cat. The order holds that a familiar is a guest, never a tool — you do not ride what is older than your language. Their cottages are quiet, well-swept, and regarded by local gods as reputable lodging.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor ground around a housed power: your familiar barely moves, and barely needs to. Defense, awe, and the slow patience of something ancient.

**The road to Toadstone:**

1. Reach level 40 and offer lodging to something the last century evicted, and keep every promise made at the door.
2. Keep a god through its winter sulk without flattery and without fear. Firewood helps.
3. Turn away three kings who come to hire what lives in your cat.
4. Arrange your succession early; a house of this keeping must never stand empty at the keeper's death.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Reputable Lodging** — Your familiar houses a far greater spirit; enemies who strike the small body answer to the guest.
- **Swept Ground** — Ground within the familiar's sight refuses violence done to sleepers, the wounded, and guests; breakers of the peace are struck lame.
- **Stone in the Head** — Draw the jewel from your familiar's brow once per day; it cures any one poison or reveals any one lie.
- **The Latch** — Lift the latch on the small body for one breath; whatever looks out ends the argument.

### Blightsower

**Role:** Area attrition — spreading agricultural ruin · **Advances from:** Eyebiter

*Blightsowers hold that a hex aimed is a hex wasted: proper cursing is husbandry, sown broadcast and reaped by whatever it finds. Murrain, rust, smut, and want follow their satchels the way gulls follow the plow. Overlookers call them indiscriminate. Blightsowers agree — so is winter, and nobody tries the frost at assize.*

**Playstyle.** Seed zones with slow agricultural ruin and let time do the killing. Wide, patient, amoral pressure; you own every field the fight lasts long enough to reach.

**The road to Blightsower:**

1. Reach level 40 and sow a hunger, then watch it feed where it will; if you flinch, sow again until you do not.
2. Follow your own blight through a winter and bury whom it starves; the order requires attendance, not remorse.
3. Out-curse an Overlooker's measured doom with plain weather, and let the survivors judge which was kinder.
4. Keep one seed of every ruin you have raised, against the day a worse one is wanted.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Broadcast Sowing** — Scatter curse-seed in a wide arc; every enemy it touches hosts a blight that ripens by the third minute.
- **Murrain** — Sicken the enemy's beasts, mounts, and summoned things first; cavalry becomes infantry, and infantry becomes tired.
- **Rust and Smut** — Crops, ropes, leather, and siegeworks in your fields decay at a season per second.
- **Blight Harvest** — Harvest your ripened blights; each afflicted enemy feeds the potency of your next working.

### Painsetter

**Role:** Tactical healer — relocates harm at a price · **Advances from:** Pellar

*Painsetters cure by relocation: the fever goes into the pig, the madness into the sea, the wound into the man who gave it. Their law is conservation — nothing mends for free, and the cost must land where the setter says. Ague-Eaters call them heartless. Painsetters note, mildly, which of the two orders still makes house calls at ninety.*

**Playstyle.** Heal by redirecting harm into vessels you choose: beasts, stones, enemies, volunteers. Cold arithmetic with warm hands; the art is choosing where the cost lands.

**The road to Painsetter:**

1. Reach level 40 and empty a plague-house into a herd of swine, then drive the herd off the cliff yourself; delegation is for the squeamish.
2. Post your prices at the door and honor them in famine and in plague-year alike.
3. Set a dying stranger's wound into your own arm once, to know the weight; never make a habit of knowing it.
4. Answer the Ague-Eater's sermon with your census of the living. Bring numbers, not feelings.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Set Elsewhere** — Move any wound, ailment, or curse from an ally into a vessel within sight; vessels object at their own pace.
- **Into the Pig** — Standing livestock, summoned things, and pets can each carry two afflictions; the pig's opinion is noted and filed.
- **To Its Author** — Set a harm into whoever caused it; harms delivered to their authors arrive doubled and cannot be set again.
- **Stone-Sink** — Sink one affliction per day into bedrock, permanently; the field develops a reputation.

### Widdershins

**Role:** Group dismantler — mass unraveling and stripping · **Advances from:** Gyre-Carline

*The Widdershins Rounds dance against the day. Their gyres unmake: knots untie, walls unmortar, oaths unswear, armies remember they are farmers. They hold that increase without undoing is only hoarding — some things must be turned backward out of the world for the year to breathe. Deasil shires sleep better for never asking who unwound the last tyrant.*

**Playstyle.** Turn the circle against things: strip enchantments, formations, and certainty in widening spirals. Little raw damage; everything the enemy counted on stops being countable.

**The road to Widdershins:**

1. Reach level 40 and unwind something the parish agrees needed unwinding; accept no payment and no thanks.
2. Dance a dead king's statue off its plinth with nine turnings, and leave the plinth for the pigeons.
3. Hold your own name steady while the round turns; those who forget themselves mid-gyre stay forgotten.
4. Break one deasil blessing gently, and accept the dinner invitation that follows. Rivalry is not feud.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Widdershins Turn** — While your round turns against the sun, every rotation strips one blessing, ward, or resolve from all enemies inside.
- **Unknotting** — Untie one bound thing: a summoned creature from its caster, a shield-wall from its discipline, a rider from her saddle.
- **Backward Dance** — Enemies caught in the gyre act in reverse order of their choosing; plans execute last step first.
- **Thirteen Turnings** — Complete thirteen rotations around a target; it is escorted out of the fight for thirteen seconds, unreachable and unmissed.

### Fetch-Candle

**Role:** Dual-body skirmisher — self in two places · **Advances from:** Impwife

*Fetch-Candles keep no lodger; they keep themselves twice. The fetch is the witch's own spirit sent out in a small coat — a wren, a hare, a pale light over the marsh — while the body minds the kettle. Toadstones call it vanity. Fetch-Candles answer that at least they know their tenant by heart, and have never once been surprised by it.*

**Playstyle.** Operate two bodies with one attention: the small one scouts, hexes, and dies cheaply; the large one works safely from the hearth-line. Positioning, squared.

**The road to Fetch-Candle:**

1. Reach level 40 and spend a market-day as a wren; return before your kettle boils over, and know what the miller hides.
2. Be seen in two parishes at the same hour, and let the magistrates ruin each other's testimony.
3. Die once in the small coat, calmly; panic buried three promising Fetch-Candles last decade.
4. Choose which body ages. The order recommends deciding young.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Small Coat** — Send your spirit out dressed as a wren, hare, or marsh-light; your body stands warded but vacant meanwhile.
- **Spare Coat** — If the small coat is destroyed, you snap home dazed but whole; the coat re-knits itself by moonrise.
- **Marsh-Light** — As the pale light, lead enemies a mile wrong; followers arrive tired, lost, and conveniently single-file.
- **One Witch Twice** — For ten seconds both bodies work at full strength; the headache arrives tomorrow, in whichever head you keep.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Third Look

**Role:** Verdict caster — inevitable single-target dooms · **Advances from:** Overlooker

*Folklore counts a witch's looks: the first notices, the second knows, the third concludes. The Third Look is that arithmetic made person. Histories dispute her — three duchies record her execution, each on a different date, and all three ruling houses are nonetheless extinct. Survivors of her attention agree on one detail: she seemed sorry.*

**Playstyle.** You do not cast at targets; you conclude them. Long observation arms instant verdicts, and one glance kills whatever has been thoroughly seen.

**The road to The Third Look:**

1. Reach level 60 and survive your own execution three times, in three duchies, without once interrupting the proceedings.
2. Look once at a tyrant across a tournament field, and let the second look wait a year. Timing is the mercy.
3. Spare one deserving man because the case, on its third reading, held a doubt.
4. Train your successor by letting her watch you decline to look.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Look** — Notice a target: their resistances, wrongs, and escape routes are revealed to you and dimmed for everyone else.
- **Second Look** — Know a target: their next three actions are disclosed to you before they choose them.
- **Third Look** — Conclude a target you have noticed and known; no ward answers, no armor applies. Twice a day, and never in haste.
- **Averted Gaze** — While your eyes are shut you cannot be targeted; the world returns the courtesy.

### The Pesthouse

**Role:** Immortal absorber — carries every affliction · **Advances from:** Ague-Eater

*There is one Pesthouse a century, and the century argues about her. Cities that once walled up their sick now send them up her lane; the lane sends back cured strangers and no explanations. What she carries by now — the hoarded agues of a hundred parishes — has a mass the learned decline to calculate. She bakes on Thursdays.*

**Playstyle.** Absorb without limit and refuse to fall: everything hostile becomes something you carry, and everything you carry becomes wall. Attrition surrenders first.

**The road to The Pesthouse:**

1. Reach level 60 and take in the epidemic no city would name, all of it, and answer your door the same week.
2. Count your collection every full moon; what you can still count, you can still carry.
3. Cure a Painsetter of something she could not set elsewhere; keep her secret and her friendship.
4. Outlive your physicians, your biographers, and the theory that you must be dead by now.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Door Never Barred** — Any affliction on any ally you can name transfers to you at any distance; distance was never the hard part.
- **The Collection** — Each carried ill is armor and authority; hostile casters feel, briefly and vividly, what you are hosting, and reconsider.
- **Quarantine** — Draw one enemy into quarantine with you; nothing enters or leaves until one of you is cured of the other.
- **Thursday Bread** — Share loaves carrying a crumb of your immunity; allies who eat are proof against the next plague they meet.

### The Fat Year

**Role:** Aura support — regional plenty, bent luck · **Advances from:** Deasil

*Some years everything comes through: the twins, the bridge, the barley. Almanacs mark them fat years and assume weather. Three shires each swear she wintered there, and none can prove it; all three have skipped their famines for a lifetime. Folk leave the first plate of every feast out, just in case. The Lean Year, asked about her, only smiles.*

**Playstyle.** Play the region, not the fight: standing luck, ambient mending, and hostile plans that quietly fail to come together. Your presence is the intervention.

**The road to The Fat Year:**

1. Reach level 60 and be a shire's whole difference for ten years without once being thanked to your face.
2. Turn a famine away at the border by arrangements nobody afterward can find in writing.
3. Let three shires argue over which of them you love best; a fat year is anyone's, which is the trick of it.
4. Refuse sainthood twice; the petitions die in committee both times, which was also your doing.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Plate** — Households that set you a portion fall under your table-peace; violence within them fails like damp powder.
- **The Bridge Holds** — Within your shire-wide aura, allied structures, mounts, and promises do not break at the worst moment; the worst moment is rescheduled.
- **Safe Delivery** — Once per day, an ally who should die instead arrives somewhere safe, confused, and hungry.
- **Just In Case** — Your workings leave no signature; divination about you returns only fair weather and a faint smell of bread.

### Ark of Small Gods

**Role:** Pet pantheon — many small housed powers · **Advances from:** Toadstone

*When rivers are dammed and groves are milled, their spirits walk, and the Ark is where they end up — nothing summoned, everything arrived on its own feet. Her household has been numbered at nine, thirteen, and one, depending on the theologian and how the cat felt about being counted. Nothing in her cottage is only what it looks like. Latterly, including the cottage.*

**Playstyle.** Convene a congregation of housed powers, each small body keeping an old dominion. You do not wield them — you chair them, and the meeting is armed.

**The road to Ark of Small Gods:**

1. Reach level 60 and be, for one winter, the only roof between twelve homeless divinities and the reformers' bonfires.
2. Keep the peace at a table where flood sits across from drought; seat thunder below the salt and survive dinner.
3. Bury the god that chooses to end, properly, in a grave dug by hand; keeping includes the last part.
4. Decline godhead when the household offers to pool for it. Someone must keep the house.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Roll Call** — Call the household to order: every housed spirit performs one act of its old office — flood, harvest, thunder, silence.
- **Always Room** — House a defeated enemy spirit, summoned thing, or small god on the spot; it arrives furious and settles in by winter.
- **The Cat Abstains** — One household member always refuses your plan; its refusal reveals the plan's actual flaw.
- **Festival Right** — Host a guest's working at its ancient scale; it casts through you, once per its own feast-day.

### The Lean Year

**Role:** Field-scale attrition — scarcity as weapon · **Advances from:** Blightsower

*Some years the harvest simply fails, and some years it is failed. Chronicles cannot settle whether the Lean Year was a woman or a weather: she is recorded burning in two centuries, marrying in a third, and gleaning, always gleaning, at the edge of every account. Where she walks in autumn, granaries audit themselves and come up short.*

**Playstyle.** Impose scarcity as a field condition: enemy mana, ammunition, morale, and reinforcements all arrive short. You do not defeat armies; you ration them into surrender.

**The road to The Lean Year:**

1. Reach level 60 and starve a warlord's campaign to a treaty with no battle fought and no child's winter touched.
2. Glean behind every reaper in a bad shire for a season, and take only what was truly dropped.
3. Be burned in effigy at a harvest festival and attend it; judge the straw likeness on craft alone.
4. Keep one granary secretly full against the year after you; even the Lean Year sets seed aside.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Short Harvest** — All enemy resource generation on the field is cut by a share that grows each minute; the share is called hers.
- **Gleaner's Right** — Whatever enemies waste — missed arrows, overkill damage, spilled mana — you gather and spend as your own.
- **Seed Corn** — Force the choice: enemies who spend their last reserve draw your whole attention; those who hoard it starve slower.
- **Early Frost** — End one growing thing before its time — a summoned creature, a rally, a hope; it does not return this fight.

### The Henwife

**Role:** Cost sovereign — sets the price of everything · **Advances from:** Painsetter

*Every district remembers its henwife, who cured what mattered and charged what she liked. This one sets every price and is subject to none. Emperors have bid provinces for her hour; she has taken a hen, an apology, a promise kept before witnesses. Her ledger, recovered blank, hangs in a museum. Histories dispute whether anyone ever paid her in full.*

**Playstyle.** Reprice everything: wounds, deaths, miracles, mistakes. Nothing on your field resolves until its cost is met — by them, at the door, never on credit.

**The road to The Henwife:**

1. Reach level 60 and hear the highest offer your century can make, and take a hen instead, and mean it.
2. Post your prices where kings queue, and charge beggar and king by the same unreadable scale.
3. Set the one price you will never collect, and outlive everyone who thinks that makes it negotiable.
4. Keep your ledger blank by settling everything at the door; a witch in credit is already half a warlock.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Stated at the Door** — Declare the cost of harming each ally — pain, mana, memory; enemies pay it on the spot or their blows refuse.
- **Taken in Kind** — Convert incoming harm into stated goods: the blow lands as lost speed, spilled mana, or confessed secrets instead of blood.
- **Never on Account** — Nothing may be taken from you now and paid for later; effects that would cost you in advance decline to occur.
- **Priceless** — Once per day, declare one ally beyond price; until dawn no effect, oath, or bargain can spend, trade, or claim them.

### The Raveling

**Role:** Mass disabler — unravels armies and workings · **Advances from:** Widdershins

*Ravel means knit and unknit both; the old word never chose, and neither does she. Histories dispute what the Raveling undid — a dynasty, a doctrine, a war of forty years recalled now only as a rumor of mud. Where her round has danced, chroniclers find the chapter missing and the index unable to say what for.*

**Playstyle.** Unmake at the scale of institutions: armies, enchantments, allegiances, and sieges come apart in widening turns. Nothing dies; things stop being organized enough to fight.

**The road to The Raveling:**

1. Reach level 60 and unwind a war so thoroughly that both armies march home certain they never mustered.
2. Dance alone one turning that used to need thirteen, and stop while your name still fits you.
3. Reknit one thing you were paid to leave unmade, because the old word never chose, and neither may you.
4. Leave every chronicle one thread; a past unraveled entirely is a kindness the living cannot afford.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wide Gyre** — Your unraveling turns span a battlefield; each rotation strips armies of one layer — wards, orders, formation, cause.
- **Knit One** — The old word cuts both ways: reknit one unmade thing — a wall, a ward, a friendship — as easily as you unmade it.
- **Thread's End** — Find the one thread a great work hangs by; pull it, and the work has an hour to make its peace.
- **Recalled as Rumor** — Loosen a past event's grip on the present: oaths sworn upon it release, and grudges rooted in it forget their root.

### The Fetch

**Role:** True dual presence — two whole bodies · **Advances from:** Fetch-Candle

*At some point the question reversed: not whether her fetch had grown too real, but which of the two had been real first. The parish buried one of them in the year of the comet; attendance, the register notes, was two. Histories dispute which walked away. The Fetch, asked directly, answers that she gets that a lot.*

**Playstyle.** Two complete witches, one biography: both bodies cast, walk, and die independently, and neither is the spare. Lose one, and the other files the paperwork.

**The road to The Fetch:**

1. Reach level 60 and attend the burial of one of you; carry the front left corner, and do not clarify which you are.
2. Live one year as the wren while the woman keeps house; the village must not notice the difference.
3. Meet yourself on the marsh road at dusk, which is fatal, and disagree about who dies.
4. Answer honestly, once, which of you was first. Practice the honest answer until it is true.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Both of Her** — Operate two full bodies at full strength anywhere on the field; their abilities cool down separately and alibi each other.
- **Which Was Buried** — When one body dies, prove it was the other one; the death is reassigned retroactively, with witnesses.
- **Seen Before Arriving** — Send your image a day ahead; wherever it was seen, you may later step in as though you had already been there.
- **Coat for a Friend** — Knit a dying ally a small coat; they persist as their own fetch until a body can be argued about later.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Regard

**Role:** Ambient executioner — judgment as standing law · **Advances from:** The Third Look

*The Third Look concluded persons; something now concludes in general. The Regard is the standing fact of being seen: every fence-line wrong, every unwitnessed cruelty, is witnessed after all, and the files accumulate. Nations with clean consciences call it conscience. The others have, in recent centuries, taken heavily to curtains, which has not helped.*

**Playstyle.** You are ambient. Guilt, anywhere, is an address your verdicts already know; judgment arrives as weather does, unhurried and unanswerable. Play at the tempo of consequence.

**The road to The Regard:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop looking at anything in particular; particularity was the last limit.
2. Be cited in law codes you never wrote — as under the Regard, the clerks put it — and let the citation do the policing.
3. Answer no petitions; the knowledge that you saw is the entire service.
4. When two empires wrong each other in secret, be the reason neither believes it got away. Peace by mutual certainty.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Under Sky** — Every wrong done beneath the sky in your dominion is recorded to you at the moment of doing, with names.
- **The Curtains Fail** — No ward, roof, lie, or distance obstructs your seeing; the attempt itself is entered as evidence.
- **Season of Consequence** — Ripen every recorded wrong in a region at once; each doer meets their accumulated case as a single event.
- **Stay of Doom** — Withhold a deserved conclusion indefinitely; the deferral is felt, and entire dynasties have reformed inside it.

### The Remission

**Role:** Absolute sustain — concludes afflictions everywhere · **Advances from:** The Pesthouse

*Plagues end. Physicians take credit, priests take tithes, and the fever, asked where it went, would answer: to her. The Remission is the office sickness retires into — the standing mercy by which afflictions, having raged, abate. The Pesthouse carried what she took; the Remission is where taking goes. Fevers break at her name, embarrassed.*

**Playstyle.** End categories of harm rather than instances: not this wound healed but this wound concluded, here, for everyone. Support at the scale of a century's health.

**The road to The Remission:**

1. Reach level 85 and end a plague by accepting its retirement; grant the pension personally.
2. Inherit the Pesthouse's whole collection and file it where nothing relapses — in the office, not the woman.
3. Let physicians publish your work under their own names for a hundred years; correct only the dosages.
4. Remain the reason fevers break at three in the morning; accept no shrine larger than a kept garden.

**Abilities granted:**

- **General Abatement** — Declare one affliction concluded field-wide; every instance on every ally ends, and new castings of it fail politely.
- **The Corner Turned** — Any ally at death's threshold instead turns the corner; the turn is audible, like weather clearing.
- **Retired Plague** — Take a named disease out of the world into your keeping; it is henceforth folklore, and folklore is yours.
- **No Relapse** — Ground you bless mends: all healing there compounds daily, and nothing may relapse inside your fences.

### The Ingathering

**Role:** Cosmic support — brings all things home · **Advances from:** The Fat Year

*Harvests come home; so, eventually, does everything else. The Ingathering is the office by which strays return: lost ships, last sheaves, prodigal sons, seed thought extinct since the burning years. The Deasil Rounds danced increase; she is where increase was always headed. Autumn is her paperwork. The feast at the end of it sets, in every land at once, one extra place.*

**Playstyle.** Bring things home as a combat verb: allies to safety, strength to the weary, the lost to the found. Attrition cannot beat a harvest that recovers everything.

**The road to The Ingathering:**

1. Reach level 85 and become the direction home lies in; compasses will not show it, but feet know.
2. Bring in a harvest from fields that burned in spring; where you glean, the year relents.
3. Set the feast at which every empty chair is filled by whoever it was kept empty for.
4. When the last stray of an age comes up the lane, be on the step, with the lamp, unsurprised.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Called Home** — Recall any ally from anywhere — battle, capture, error — to your table, mended and fed, once each per day.
- **Last Sheaf** — Nothing allied is left behind; the fallen, the dropped, and the routed are gathered standing at your side by dusk.
- **Laid for One More** — Any stranger who joins your side mid-fight arrives blessed; there was always a place set for them.
- **Harvest Home** — Once per season, everything ever taken from those under your roof — years, strength, hope — comes home ripened.

### The Household

**Role:** Spirit host — houses the world's powers · **Advances from:** Ark of Small Gods

*The world keeps a Household for powers with nowhere to be: retired gods, dammed rivers, faiths down to their last believer. It is not a place; it is a keeping, and someone holds the keys. Theology files the Household under metaphor. The metaphor's chimney smokes. Applicants knock at the small door, which is any door, after dark.*

**Playstyle.** Host at world scale: every power you house is a room, and any room can be opened onto any battlefield. You never fight alone; you fight as a full house.

**The road to The Household:**

1. Reach level 85 and accept the keys from the Ark's estate; the will names no heir, only whoever kept the house.
2. House something no roof has ever agreed to hold, and lose no sleep you had not already scheduled to lose.
3. Evict exactly one guest in your whole tenure, for cause, in winter; be right, and never say what it did.
4. Keep the chimney smoking through the death of the metaphor; theology will re-file you eventually. Be home when it does.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Any Door After Dark** — Enter or leave through any door in the world after dusk; guests and allies may be conducted through with you.
- **Open House** — Throw one housed power's room open around you — the drowned mill's flood, the harvest god's noon — for a minute.
- **Beneath This Roof** — Declare ground your roof's shadow; violence there requires your leave, and your leave has conditions.
- **Re-Keyed** — When you die, the Household simply re-keys; you resume at the hearth by the next dark of the month, keys still in your apron.

### The Pruning

**Role:** World gardener — cuts powers down to size · **Advances from:** The Lean Year

*Everything grows rank if nothing cuts it back: orchards, dynasties, pantheons. The Pruning is the world's standing horticulture — the office by which what has overgrown its portion is cut to live, and what will not be cut is cut anyway. Farmers bless her rotation. Empires appeal it. The appeals are heard in the order received, in pruning season.*

**Playstyle.** Impose limits as an absolute: powers, armies, and miracles you designate are cut back to a size you choose. You do not break enemies; you trim them, renewably.

**The road to The Pruning:**

1. Reach level 85 and cut back a war that had run three generations; size the wound so the fourth is born bored of it.
2. Prune a god over its objections and wake it kinder; take no credit for the improvement.
3. Let your own garden lie unworked one year in seven, publicly; a law that exempts the lawgiver is a hex.
4. When the world itself grows thin, schedule its rest against every empire's appeal. Hear the appeals fully. Grant none.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Cut Back** — Prune one enemy power at its source — a god's favor, a legion's discipline, a mage's talent; it regrows smaller.
- **Fallow Strip** — Divide the field into strips and rest one at a time; nothing in a resting strip may act, suffer, or scheme.
- **Exhausted Soil** — Ground that has hosted too much war refuses further armies; they sink to the knee in its refusal.
- **Seventh Year** — Every seventh working you witness is canceled for rest, including — the office is strict — your own.

### The Crossroads

**Role:** Bargain sovereign — every exchange passes through her · **Advances from:** The Henwife

*Bargains want a witness; forks want a resident. The Crossroads is both: every bargain struck where roads meet is struck in her hearing, and holds. Witches were buried at crossroads to confuse their walking; it worked backward — now every crossroads is her, and the traffic in promises pays its respects. She carries no one anywhere; she only witnesses what they trade to go.*

**Playstyle.** Preside over exchange itself: every trade, oath, and bargain on the field routes through you, and you may amend the terms at the fork.

**The road to The Crossroads:**

1. Reach level 85 and be buried at the crossroads with full ceremony; be waiting at the next fork the mourners reach.
2. Witness the oldest bargain still running and decline to say between whom; some traffic predates the roads.
3. Untangle the junction where seven kingdoms' roads and treaties knot, and charge each kingdom one hen.
4. Keep faith with the buried: any witch interred at any fork wakes rested in your jurisdiction, and owes nothing for it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Struck in Your Hearing** — Every bargain made within your dominion is witnessed by you and binds only as deeply as you countersign.
- **Fork the Road** — Place a crossroads under an enemy mid-stride; they must choose a road, and both were built by you.
- **Amended at the Fork** — Amend any active bargain, blessing, or compact that crosses your ground; warlock compacts arrive particularly amendable.
- **Buried Standing** — Having been buried at your own office, death is a road like the others; take it in both directions, once per season.

### The Unwinding

**Role:** Entropy sovereign — gentle, total disassembly · **Advances from:** The Raveling

*What is wound must someday unwind — springs, oaths, dynasties, the year itself. The Unwinding is that law with a name on it: the standing office by which the world is taken apart gently enough to survive it. The Raveling unwound kingdoms; the Unwinding is why unwinding works at all. Nothing she attends comes apart screaming. That is the whole mercy, and it suffices.*

**Playstyle.** Operate as entropy with manners: designate what comes apart, in what order, and how gently. You never destroy; you conclude assemblies, and everything is an assembly.

**The road to The Unwinding:**

1. Reach level 85 and unwind one age of the world so gently its people call it history instead of catastrophe.
2. Take apart a war-engine of ten thousand parts and lay them out in a meadow in the order of their making.
3. When something beloved must end, attend it personally; the office exists so that endings are kept kind.
4. Remember alone what the world was spared; carry the list without sharing it, even deasil, even at dinner.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Gently Apart** — Disassemble any construct, formation, spell, or siege into harmless parts; the parts remember being whole, fondly.
- **Wound Down** — Slow one enemy's mainspring: each action costs them more than the last, until stillness is the cheapest choice left.
- **Order of Parting** — Choose the sequence in which an enemy's powers, weapons, and resolve depart; they watch the schedule keep itself.
- **Comes Apart Kindly** — Whatever you unmake ends without pain, terror, or ghosts; the mercy is famous, and famously demoralizing.

### The Glimpse

**Role:** Omnipresent haunt — seen everywhere, held nowhere · **Advances from:** The Fetch

*Everyone has seen her: the figure at the edge of the eye, the reflection half a step behind, the shape at the window of an empty house. The Fetch was two; the Glimpse is distributed. She is the world's spare presence — kept, like all good myths, just out of focus. Turning to look is the one prayer she always answers, with absence.*

**Playstyle.** Exist peripherally everywhere at once: strike from the corner of the eye, vanish under direct attention. Untargetable by anything that must look at what it kills.

**The road to The Glimpse:**

1. Reach level 85 and let go of the second body, then the first; the glimpse of you is body enough now.
2. Be seen on the same dusk by a sailor, a queen, and a child in a mirror, and be all three sightings.
3. Visit every window of every empty house once a generation; the houses stay empty out of respect.
4. Never be looked at directly again; grant the world instead the certainty that you could be, which keeps it kind.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Corner of the Eye** — Act from anyone's peripheral vision anywhere; direct observation ends your presence there and begins it somewhere less convenient.
- **Half-Step Behind** — Attach to an enemy's reflection; everything they do arrives half a step late, and the lateness accumulates.
- **The Window Figure** — Be seen, once, deliberately, in full; the seer must sit down for a while. Usable sparingly. Remembered always.
- **Answered With Absence** — When targeted, be elsewhere retroactively; the attack finds the place you are famous for having just left.
