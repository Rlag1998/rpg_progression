# ⚔️ Warrior

> *Steel forgives nothing; the drill-yard rewards everything.*

**Archetype:** Might

The Warrior asks for nothing you were born with. No bloodline, no pact, no whispered patron — only a body, a weapon, and the willingness to repeat one motion until it stops being a motion and becomes you. Warriors fill the world's garrisons and free companies, and from their mud-floored drill-yards every road of Might begins.

## The Tree

```text
Warrior (Lv 1)
├── Drillsworn (Lv 10)
│   ├── Halfsword (Lv 25) → Doomfencer (Lv 40) → The Unanswered (Lv 60)
│   └── Edgewright (Lv 25) → Severant (Lv 40) → Hundredth Form (Lv 60)
└── Bannerhand (Lv 10)
    ├── Linebreaker (Lv 25) → Forlorn Captain (Lv 40) → Ruin of Gates (Lv 60)
    └── Ironmuster (Lv 25) → Standfast (Lv 40) → Sovereign of Hosts (Lv 60)
```

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Warrior

**Role:** Frontline melee — versatile and durable

*Every garrison keeps them: farm sons and gutter orphans who traded whatever came before for a spear and a bunk. Most die unremembered. The ones who survive their first winter learn what the drill-yards teach — that steel forgives nothing and rewards everything, and that a body, patiently punished, can be re-forged into an instrument.*

**Playstyle.** Straightforward frontline melee. Manage stamina, hold your ground, and trade blows with unglamorous efficiency while deciding which road calls louder — the perfected self, or the company.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Present yourself at any muster, garrison, or free company; bring nothing but two working hands.
2. Survive your first winter of drills — the yard at dawn, the wall at dusk, blisters into callus.
3. Stand your first real fight without breaking. What you do after the fear arrives decides everything.
4. Bury a comrade, then report for drill the next morning.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Measured Cut** — A drilled strike that cannot glance; deals full weapon damage even against a raised guard.
- **Set the Feet** — Brace for one round: knockbacks, trips, and shoves against you fail, and blocked damage is halved.
- **Soldier's Wind** — Once per fight, recover a burst of stamina and shake off one bleed or daze.
- **Yard Callus** — Passive: each week of campaigning grants small permanent gains to carry weight and fatigue resistance.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Drillsworn

**Role:** Melee striker — precision single-target · **Advances from:** Warrior

*Some soldiers discover the company was only ever a scabbard for the self. The Drillsworn take a private vow: ten thousand repetitions of every cut before they may claim its name. Officers find them in the yard at midnight, alone, correcting a wrist angle that no one else alive can see.*

**Playstyle.** Precise single-target melee. Build tempo through repeated engagement; feints and drilled counters punish anything predictable. Weaker amid chaos, lethal wherever the fight narrows to one.

**The road to Drillsworn:**

1. Reach level 10 and swear the Drill-Vow before a witness who has themselves sworn it.
2. Perform one cut ten thousand times, counted honestly. The count restarts the first time you lie.
3. Defeat your own drill instructor using the weapon they taught you.
4. Fight one full campaign without a wasted motion — every strike thrown must land or teach.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ten-Thousandth Repetition** — Passive: consecutive strikes against one target each land truer than the last, stacking until you switch targets.
- **Feint in Tempo** — Sell a false opening; if the enemy takes it, your true strike ignores their armor.
- **Single Combat** — Mark one foe: you read them fully, gaining counters against them while dropping guard against all others.
- **Edge Discipline** — Passive: your weapon never dulls, jams, or slips, and disarm attempts against you all but fail.

### Bannerhand

**Role:** Frontline support — orders and morale · **Advances from:** Warrior

*When the standard-bearer falls, someone catches the pole before it touches the mud. That reflex — reaching for the weight of everyone else — is what quartermasters watch for. A Bannerhand holds no commission at first; they hold the thing itself, the cloth a company follows after its officers are dead.*

**Playstyle.** Frontline support and control. Plant the standard, anchor allies to its shadow, and spend your own guard freely to keep the line's shape from breaking.

**The road to Bannerhand:**

1. Reach level 10 and carry the company standard through one engagement without letting it touch the ground.
2. Learn every soldier's name in your file, and the name each one calls out in their sleep.
3. Rally a broken line using only your voice. If they re-form, the rank is yours.
4. Refuse a promotion that would take you off the line.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Raise the Standard** — Plant your banner: allies within its shadow resist fear and strike steadier while it stands.
- **The Voice That Carries** — Shout an order over any din; one ally immediately moves or strikes out of turn.
- **Shoulder the Line** — Redirect a portion of the damage dealt to any adjacent ally onto your own guard.
- **Forced March** — Your company covers a further span each day and delays all fatigue penalties until camp.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Halfsword

**Role:** Duelist — bind, control, armor-piercing · **Advances from:** Drillsworn

*Duels in the border towns are settled close: gauntlet on the blade, pommel raised, breath shared. Halfswords study the crossing itself — that grinding half-second where two weapons argue. They are hired to end quarrels the courts cannot, and they walk out of rooms that other fighters are carried from.*

**Playstyle.** Close-range duelist. Bind the enemy's weapon, deny every disengage, and win the grapple-distance argument with armor-slipping thrusts and pommel strokes. Helpless against numbers, near-certain alone.

**The road to Halfsword:**

1. Reach level 25 and win seven witnessed duels without taking a wound past the first.
2. Grip your own blade barehanded and drive it through a breastplate. Learn what the edge permits.
3. Study one opponent for a month before fighting them, then end the duel inside three exchanges.
4. Decline a duel you would have won, because the cause was rotten. The refusal is the lesson.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Grip the Blade** — A halfsword thrust at grappling range that slides through visor slits and joint gaps, ignoring plate entirely.
- **The Bind** — Lock weapons with a foe; neither of you may disengage, and you win every contested crossing.
- **Murder-Stroke** — Reverse the blade and swing the pommel; against a helmeted foe this dazes straight through the steel.
- **Read the Shoulder** — Passive: enemies telegraph to you; the first attack in each exchange grants you a free counter-thrust.

### Edgewright

**Role:** Burst striker — single decisive cuts · **Advances from:** Drillsworn

*In the high passes stand shrines of split stones, each cloven by a single stroke and signed with a maker's mark. Edgewrights leave them. They pursue the one honest cut — the motion with nothing extra in it — and grade their entire lives against falling silk and standing water.*

**Playstyle.** Deliberate burst striker. Long setups and ruinous single strikes; draw-cuts from the scabbard that resolve whole fights in one motion. Patience is the true resource.

**The road to Edgewright:**

1. Reach level 25 and cut a hanging silk scarf so cleanly that both halves settle before the wind notices.
2. Forge or commission a blade fitted to your hand alone, and never lend it.
3. Spend a season cutting only air, until an Edgewright of standing certifies your motion holds nothing wasted.
4. Split a standing stone with one stroke, and sign it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Draw-Cut** — Strike from the scabbard in one motion; against an unwounded target it always lands first and cannot be parried.
- **One Motion** — Merge parry and cut: a successful defense this round is simultaneously a full-damage riposte.
- **Cut the Falling Leaf** — A strike so clean the target feels nothing for a breath; the delayed wound resolves doubled.
- **Whetstone Rite** — An hour's ritual sharpening: your next three cuts sever material one grade harder than steel should allow.

### Linebreaker

**Role:** Assault leader — breach and momentum · **Advances from:** Bannerhand

*Every siege has a moment when the wall opens and someone must be first into the smoke. Linebreakers volunteer twice. They command the worst thirty yards in the world — the breach, the gate, the ladder-top — and free companies bid against each other for a muster-roll carrying such a name.*

**Playstyle.** Momentum-driven assault leader. Charge first, hit hardest at the point of entry, and drag your squad through the gap you tore before it closes.

**The road to Linebreaker:**

1. Reach level 25 and be first through a defended breach three times. Volunteering counts only if you live.
2. Carry a wounded soldier back out through the gap you led them into.
3. Take a gate from the inside with fewer than ten at your back.
4. Have your name written first on a storming-party's roll — by someone else's hand.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Through** — Entering a space held by three or more enemies grants you a surge of guard and a free strike.
- **Weight of the Charge** — Your first strike's damage scales with every yard you crossed to deliver it.
- **On Me** — Allies who follow your charge this round share your speed and half your momentum bonus.
- **Doorbreaker** — Your strikes deal quadruple damage to gates, barricades, shields, and anything else pretending to be a wall.

### Ironmuster

**Role:** Line commander — discipline and attrition · **Advances from:** Bannerhand

*Armies are not raised; they are manufactured, and Ironmusters run the forge. They hammer levied plowboys into ranks that hold, count arrows and bread with the same cold arithmetic, and hold that glory is a rumor started by people who never smelled a supply train burn.*

**Playstyle.** Defensive line commander. Position allies precisely, ration everyone's stamina including the enemy's, and grind battles down into arithmetic your side was built to win.

**The road to Ironmuster:**

1. Reach level 25 and train one hundred raw recruits to hold formation under a cavalry feint.
2. March a company two hundred miles and arrive with more discipline than you left with.
3. Hold ground you were ordered to hold until the order changes, whatever crosses the field.
4. Court-martial a friend fairly. The muster is owed the truth more than you are owed comfort.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Dress the Line** — Reposition any allies in earshot by one rank; the corrected line gains guard until it breaks formation.
- **Attrition Doctrine** — Passive: enemies attacking your formation spend double stamina; your ranks spend half.
- **Quartermaster's Eye** — Passive: your company consumes fewer supplies and shrugs off weather, hunger, and forced-march fatigue.
- **No Step Back** — Allies adjacent to you cannot be pushed, routed, or frightened out of position.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Doomfencer

**Role:** Execution duelist — sanctioned single combat · **Advances from:** Halfsword

*When a verdict must lie beyond appeal, crowns send for a Doomfencer. They fight judicial duels as officers of the court, in black quilting, before witnesses the law requires to watch. Perhaps forty hold the office across the known kingdoms. Their arrival in a city settles most lawsuits by itself.*

**Playstyle.** Sanctioned execution duelist. Isolate one opponent under formal challenge, convert their every error into mounting counters, and close with strokes the law itself has blessed.

**The road to Doomfencer:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand judicial champion in a capital trial, a kingdom's law resting on your point.
2. Kill a Doomfencer in office, or be named successor by one retiring — the office admits no third door.
3. Fight one duel you believe unjust, and let it teach you exactly what the office costs.
4. Swear before the bench to accept every lawful challenge, forever, regardless of who offers it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sentence of Steel** — Formally challenge one foe: both of you are bound to the duel until one yields, falls, or dies.
- **Court of One** — While your duel stands, no outside blow can reach either duelist; the world is reduced to witness.
- **Weighed and Wanting** — Passive: every miss your duel opponent makes grants you an escalating counter that cannot itself miss.
- **Black Verdict** — Against a duel opponent below a quarter of their health, deliver the sanctioned stroke: it kills, cleanly, always.

### Severant

**Role:** Nova striker — one prepared, absolute cut · **Advances from:** Edgewright

*High on the scarps lives an order that has cut everything else away — property, names, speech on certain days. Severants hold that every problem has a grain, like wood, and that a sufficiently honest edge will find it. Lowlanders climb for months to ask them for one stroke. Sometimes they descend.*

**Playstyle.** Ascetic nova striker. Fights last one motion or none; everything before the cut is positioning, breathing, and an absolute refusal to be hurried.

**The road to Severant:**

1. Reach level 40, climb to the Severed Order's scarp, and leave your possessions at the treeline.
2. Keep silence for a hundred days, drilling the same cut at dawn and at dusk.
3. Sever a falling waterfall so the stream stutters. The elders will hear it and come down.
4. Cut away one thing you love. The order does not say which. You will know.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sever the Question** — One prepared stroke that ignores all armor, guard, and mitigation; preparing it costs an entire round of stillness.
- **Cut Water** — Your strike passes through parries and blocks as if through current; only distance defends against it.
- **Nothing Wasted** — Passive: any cut that kills refunds its full cost; a finished fight leaves you unspent.
- **The Pared Self** — Passive: feints, taunts, illusions, and pain cannot move you; your tempo belongs to you alone.

### Forlorn Captain

**Role:** Vanguard commander — first-wave shock and rally · **Advances from:** Linebreaker

*The forlorn hope is the first wave into a breach — soldiers counted dead at signing. A Forlorn Captain has led it and lived, more than once, which armies regard as something between skill and blasphemy. Veterans wear their captains' names inked on their arms: a roll of the saved.*

**Playstyle.** Vanguard commander. Open every battle personally, convert the first minutes of violence into a beachhead, and make desperate allies fight like cornered kings.

**The road to Forlorn Captain:**

1. Reach level 40 and lead a forlorn hope through the wall, bringing more than half your roll out alive.
2. Be given up for dead by your own army, then report for duty.
3. Turn down the general's tent. Captains of the hope sleep where the ladders are stacked.
4. Learn your letters if you lack them — someone must write to the families afterward, and it should be you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Hope of the Doomed** — Allies below half health within your sight deal heavier damage and cannot flee while you still advance.
- **First Wave** — In an engagement's opening rounds, you and every soldier who signed your roll strike twice.
- **Carry the Fallen** — Drag a downed ally at full speed while fighting one-handed, without penalty.
- **The Breach Holds** — A gap you open in an enemy line stays open; enemies cannot re-form ranks around you.

### Standfast

**Role:** Bulwark commander — immovable defense · **Advances from:** Ironmuster

*Some ground cannot be lost, so the crowns keep the Standfast: a small order sworn to hold named places — a bridge, a pass, a stair — until relieved or dead. Their muster-book lists positions, not people. In four centuries it records eleven retreats, each one ordered, none of them chosen.*

**Playstyle.** Immovable bulwark commander. Fortify a position, rotate your line like a machine, and outlast anything the enemy can afford to keep sending at it.

**The road to Standfast:**

1. Reach level 40 and hold a named position through a siege of no fewer than forty days.
2. Recite the muster-book's eleven retreats from memory before the order's eldest, and say what each one cost.
3. Refuse relief once, because the relieving column was needed elsewhere more.
4. Add one position to the book, and be the first name sworn to it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **This Ground Is Ours** — Name the ground you hold: allies standing on it take reduced damage and cannot fail morale.
- **Rotation Drill** — Swap fatigued front-rank allies with rested rear ranks instantly, without ever opening the wall.
- **Outlast** — Passive: each hour a battle continues, your formation's guard and stamina recovery climb.
- **The Wall Speaks** — A single shout halts a rout; fleeing allies within earshot stop, turn, and re-form on you.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Unanswered

**Role:** Apex duelist — perfect counters · **Advances from:** Doomfencer

*Histories disagree about everything except the count: no riposte has ever landed. The Unanswered is less a person than a standing question — dueling culture across three kingdoms is one long argument about a fighter most living people have never seen draw, because the draw is the end of it.*

**Playstyle.** Perfect counter-duelist. You act inside your opponent's hesitations; fights are decided before observers understand they have begun. Numbers do not matter — there is only ever one opponent at a time.

**The road to The Unanswered:**

1. Reach level 60 bearing no recorded wound from single combat since your Doomfencer oath.
2. Answer one hundred lawful challenges, and let none of them become a second exchange.
3. Duel a sitting Doomfencer before every crowned witness alive; leave them living, unmarked, and finished with swords.
4. Refuse the last challenge ever offered you, and let the silence that follows stand as your successor.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Final Tempo** — Passive: you act in the instant between an enemy's decision and their motion; you always move first.
- **No Second Exchange** — Your riposte resolves the duel: a landed counter ends the target's capacity to continue, one way or another.
- **Perfect Measure** — Passive: you dictate the exact distance of every engagement; enemies cannot flank, crowd, or reach you unwilled.
- **The Question** — An enemy who watches you draw must pass a test of will or decline to fight you at all.

### Hundredth Form

**Role:** Apex striker — one absolute stroke · **Advances from:** Severant

*The order teaches ninety-nine forms and admits a hundredth exists: unteachable, arrived at only by exhausting the others. Once in a generation, someone stops drilling mid-motion and does not resume, because they are finished. Mountainsides where such people have cut carry weather-smooth scars. Farmers plow around them without being told why.*

**Playstyle.** Apex single-strike fighter. One drawn breath, one motion, one ended thing per encounter; everything else is the walking between cuts.

**The road to Hundredth Form:**

1. Reach level 60 and perform all ninety-nine forms before the assembled order without one correction offered.
2. Cut something the order agrees cannot be cut, in a single stroke, with the elders watching.
3. Give away the blade fitted to your hand alone, and cut as well with whatever is handed to you.
4. Perform the hundredth form once. There is no second performance; the order will raise your shrine from the pieces.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Hundredth Form** — Once per day, the unteachable cut: one target, structure, or obstacle simply ends. No roll is made.
- **Ten Thousand Days** — Passive: your techniques cost no stamina; a lifetime of drilling has paid every bill in advance.
- **Stillness Before** — While your blade is sheathed you cannot be surprised, hastened, or made to move first.
- **Cut the Uncuttable** — Your edge severs what edges cannot: stone, siege chain, tempered plate, and the momentum of a charge.

### Ruin of Gates

**Role:** Apex assault — siege-ending offense · **Advances from:** Forlorn Captain

*Certain cities surrender when a particular banner crests the hill, reasoning that their walls are already gone in every way that matters. The Ruin of Gates has ended more sieges by arriving than by fighting, and the masons of six kingdoms curse the name professionally, the way sailors curse a reef.*

**Playstyle.** Siege-ending assault apex. Collapse fortifications personally, chain-rally whole armies into the gap, and convert one breach into a general surrender within the hour.

**The road to Ruin of Gates:**

1. Reach level 60 and take a city sung as untakeable, its gate opened by your own hands.
2. Survive your hundredth forlorn hope, until the muster-clerks stop entering your name among the dead.
3. Accept a fortress's surrender offered to your banner alone, before your army has arrived.
4. Rebuild one city you broke, stone by stone, and garrison it with the soldiers you led through its wall.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Gate Remembers** — Passive: your melee strikes deal siege-engine damage; gates, walls, and towers count as valid single targets.
- **Follow Me or Mourn Me** — A battle-wide rally: every ally who can see your banner joins the charge carrying your momentum bonuses.
- **Hundredth Hope** — Once per battle, a blow that would kill you while advancing simply fails.
- **Terms at the Wall** — Offer surrender mid-assault; a shaken garrison must genuinely weigh it, checking morale at heavy penalty.

### Sovereign of Hosts

**Role:** Apex commander — army-scale command · **Advances from:** Standfast

*When the muster-horns pass a certain name down the valleys, farms empty by choice. The Sovereign of Hosts commands no standing army; they are owed one — by veterans, by veterans' sons, by kingdoms that remember. Historians have taken to measuring wars by whether this person was in them.*

**Playstyle.** Army-scale apex commander. Personally modest in the melee, overwhelming in aggregate: every ally fights as if drilled by your hand, because most of them were.

**The road to Sovereign of Hosts:**

1. Reach level 60 and raise ten thousand soldiers with no coin offered — only your name on the summons.
2. Win a war the historians agree was unwinnable, then disband the army when they beg you to keep it.
3. Train the sergeant who trains the sergeants of three kingdoms' armies.
4. Speak once on a field where two hosts stand ready, and send both home unblooded.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ten Thousand Answer** — Sound the muster: within days, veteran companies arrive owing you service no coin could buy.
- **The Field Entire** — Passive: you perceive the whole battlefield as a single shape; all allies act on your initiative.
- **Worth a Legion** — An enemy force that learns you command must check morale before offering battle at all.
- **One Voice** — Your orders reach every ally on the field regardless of distance, din, or dying.
