# 🪶 Shaman

> *Every ford has a god; every god has a price.*

**Archetype:** Spirit

The Shaman stands where the living end and everything else begins, speaking for both sides. Ancestors ride willingly in the marrow, lending skill and counsel; the small gods of ford, hearth, and herd accept the proper gifts at the proper times. Nothing is compelled and nothing is raised. Power is a relationship, and the Shaman keeps every one of ten thousand in good repair.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Shaman

**Role:** Hybrid support — small favors, small gods

*Every district keeps one: the child who left barley at the ford unasked, who heard the hearth mutter and answered politely. The old speaker-for takes such children walking, teaching names, payments, and the dates that must never be missed. The pay is poor. The neighbors are ten thousand, and watching.*

**Playstyle.** Trade small courtesies for small interventions; your strength is local and relational, rising wherever you have paid attention, and paid on time.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Notice what your village leaves at the ford, the sill, and the first furrow, and begin leaving your own.
2. Be caught at it by the district's speaker-for, and accept the walking apprenticeship no one ever applies for.
3. Learn the names, payments, and feast-days of every small god within a day's walk, and miss none.
4. Hear an ancestor clear its throat behind your ear, and do not turn around until invited.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Barley at the Ford** — Gift the nearest water-spirit; one enemy crossing, footing, or retreat fails at the worst possible moment.
- **Grandmother's Steadying** — A forebear rests a hand on yours; reroll one failed action as she would have done it.
- **Salt on the Sill** — Scatter a proper ward; minor spirits, curses, and ill dreams decline to enter for a night.
- **Ask the Resident** — Question a place's small god politely; learn what passed here, what waits, and what its help would cost.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Kinhost

**Role:** Versatile melee — borrowed skill, ancestral counsel · **Advances from:** Shaman

*At the rite of the opened door, the apprentice kneels at the family stones and invites. Some forebear always volunteers — a spear-dame, a midwife, a drowned rigger — lending what a lifetime taught. Nothing is summoned and nothing is bound; the Kinhost keeps guests, and guests, well kept, work.*

**Playstyle.** Hand the reins to whichever guest the moment needs — a duelist's parry, a rigger's knots — and thank them aloud while fighting on.

**The road to Kinhost:**

1. Reach level 10 and kneel three nights at your line's stones, asking nothing, until one of the dead asks after you.
2. Host your first guest through a full day's labor, and credit them by name to everyone you meet.
3. Learn the full names and standing grievances of five generations, so no guest is ever greeted wrongly.
4. Part with a guest on good terms, and feel the door stay open behind them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Opened Door** — Invite a willing forebear in; gain their trade's full proficiency until they take their leave.
- **Spear-Dame's Parry** — A war-dead guest turns aside a blow you never saw; negate one melee strike each round.
- **Borrowed Callus** — Your hands remember work you never did; wield any tool, weapon, or instrument as one long practiced.
- **Eyes of the Line** — The dead keep watch behind you; you cannot be flanked or surprised while hosting a guest.

### Handselman

**Role:** Utility caster — offerings, favors, spirit bargains · **Advances from:** Shaman

*The other road leads to market — the one held at fords and hilltops, where nothing has hands and everything has terms. The Handselman learns each small god's preferred payment: silver for the mill-spirit, a song for the well, first catch for the pike-god. The opening gift matters most. Openings always do.*

**Playstyle.** Prepare the field with gifts and standing favors, then call them due mid-fight — trips, dousings, warnings — one courtesy at a time.

**The road to Handselman:**

1. Reach level 10 and give a year of offerings without one missed date, through fever, flood, and mourning.
2. Learn what the sour well was owed a century ago, pay it in full, and drink first.
3. Be greeted by name at a crossroads by something with no mouth, and return the greeting properly.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Handsel** — Offer the opening gift; a spirit or place becomes willing to treat with you, whatever its prior mood.
- **Preferred Payment** — Know at a glance what any spirit wants; your offerings count double and are never refused as wrong.
- **Favor at the Ford** — Call in a small local kindness: a foe trips, a torch drowns, a door sticks, a whisper warns you.
- **Etiquette of the Unseen** — Your courtesy is armor; hostile spirits must parley with you a full round before they may do harm.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Bonechoir

**Role:** Adaptive striker — a different master each breath · **Advances from:** Kinhost

*Some Kinhosts keep table for a standing choir rather than one guest — a duelist dead a century, a wrestler, a poacher, a siege-cook, each lending mastery in turn. Garrison clerks despair of one soldier qualified for six trades. The choir votes on nothing. It volunteers, precisely, one lifetime at a time.*

**Playstyle.** Cycle presiding ancestors like stances; answer every problem with the specialist who solved it in life, and keep the rotation courteous.

**The road to Bonechoir:**

1. Reach level 25 and win a formal duel in a style extinct for a hundred years, crediting your teacher truthfully at the salute.
2. Finish a craft-guest's unfinished masterwork with your hands and their patience, and sign it with their name, not yours.
3. Keep nine guests through one battle without once addressing the wrong one.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Turn at the Reins** — Change presiding guest as a free action; each grants a distinct weapon style, stance, or craft.
- **Six Lifetimes' Practice** — No weapon, lock, knot, or saddle is unfamiliar; someone in the choir handled one professionally.
- **The Poacher's Quiet** — A hunting guest presides; your steps make no sound and your first strike from stillness bleeds.
- **Deathbed Lessons** — When a foe wounds you, the choir studies the technique; gain rising accuracy against that foe.

### Yearteller

**Role:** Support controller — precedent, counsel, the long view · **Advances from:** Kinhost

*Every line keeps one door open for the Yearteller, who carries nine generations of births, oaths, feuds, and weather, attested by the witnesses themselves. Magistrates consult them and regret the completeness. They answer every question the same way: with what happened last time, told by someone who was there.*

**Playstyle.** Fight with hindsight as a weapon; declare precedents that blunt enemy tricks, and lend allies the benefit of every mistake already made.

**The road to Yearteller:**

1. Reach level 25 and recite your line nine generations back before the assembled stones, and be corrected on nothing.
2. Settle a boundary feud with the sworn testimony of the dead, accepted by both farms in daylight.
3. Receive a dying village's whole memory in one winter, and carry it out through the snow.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Answered Before** — Negate a spell or maneuver an ancestor has witnessed; the counter is recited aloud, with attribution.
- **Nine Generations Deep** — Recall any detail a forebear witnessed — routes, weaknesses, weddings, weather — as though you stood there.
- **The Grandmothers Confer** — A round of whispered counsel; each ally may reposition or reroll one action as advised.
- **Blood-Remembered** — Know on sight anyone whose line has dealt with yours, and exactly how that went.

### Totem-Carver

**Role:** Controller — standing totems, beast-god tenancies · **Advances from:** Handselman

*Where the Handselman deals in favors, the Totem-Carver deals in lodgings: poles cut and carved to a beast-god's taste — cave-bear, river-pike, carrion-crane — and let for a season's offerings. No contract compels the tenant; good quarters and better manners do. Summoners find the arrangement appallingly informal. It has never once been broken.*

**Playstyle.** Shape the field with planted totems — fear here, vigor there, watchfulness behind — then fight inside the architecture your tenants provide.

**The road to Totem-Carver:**

1. Reach level 25 and carve your first lodging from a lightning-felled tree, with tools you made and manners you were taught.
2. Be shown the shape the god prefers in a dream, and carve what you saw, not what you wanted.
3. Keep a totem tenanted one full year with no missed offering, and be recommended by your tenant to its kin.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Plant the Lodging** — Set a carved totem; its tenant god's aura claims the ground around it until unplanted.
- **Bear-Cave Bellow** — The bear totem roars; enemies on its ground are shaken, staggered, and driven from it.
- **Crane's Watch** — The crane totem misses nothing; allies on its ground cannot be surprised, and hidden things are pointed out.
- **Pike-Still Water** — The pike totem hushes its ground; enemies entering are slowed, and the first to linger is seized.

### Good Neighbor

**Role:** Territory support — the land takes your side · **Advances from:** Handselman

*Some Handselmen settle into a circuit and become what farmers carefully call a Good Neighbor — one who knows every small god between two market towns by name, preference, and grudge. Where they walk, wells stay sweet and fires keep their tempers. Nothing is owed. Everything is, more dangerously, appreciated.*

**Playstyle.** Prepare ground before trouble finds it; on land whose spirits know you, you are formidable — elsewhere, begin introductions immediately.

**The road to Good Neighbor:**

1. Reach level 25 and walk your circuit greeting every spirit and well between two towns, omitting none; the omitted remember.
2. Win back a soured well by finding what it was owed a century ago and paying it without haggling.
3. Spend a night as the honored guest of a place that kills trespassers, and leave thanked.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Names of the Land** — On arrival, learn the local spirits and their preferences; small favors become available within the hour.
- **Sweetwater Camp** — Your campsite is kept: sleep is safe, wounds knit, and nothing approaches unannounced.
- **The Ground Objects** — On befriended land, enemy footing fails, gates stick, bowstrings damp, and every shortcut lies.
- **Hearth-Peace** — Under any roof whose hearth you have greeted, the first blow aimed at you each round goes astray.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Twicelived

**Role:** Elite duelist — two minds, one body · **Advances from:** Bonechoir

*The order of the second name holds that hosting is a marriage: one forebear, chosen and choosing, for life. The pair share the body thereafter — one presiding, one watching — and the order's rolls count each member twice. Of the Doorless Hall they say only: a crowd is not company.*

**Playstyle.** Fight with doubled attention — parry while casting, watch while sleeping — and lean on one dead lifetime's mastery made second instinct.

**The road to Twicelived:**

1. Reach level 40 and choose your one guest from the whole assembled line, and be chosen back, before witnesses living and dead.
2. Exchange second names at the stones, knowing the rite has no rite of undoing.
3. Give the guest a full day of your body each season — their walks, their songs, their grave-visits — and begrudge none of it.
4. Decline the Doorless Hall's open door at their own table, courteously, and be toasted for it anyway.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Second Name** — Your guest is permanent; keep two reactions each round, one theirs, one yours.
- **Four Hands on the Haft** — Feints, disarms, and blindside strikes fail against you; one of you has always seen it before.
- **Divided Watch** — One of you is always awake; you cannot be ambushed, and exhaustion accrues at half weight.
- **The Elder's Turn** — Once per battle your forebear seizes the moment: take a full extra action outside your turn.

### Mindhoard

**Role:** Elite controller — total recall, nothing lost · **Advances from:** Yearteller

*The Mindhoards swore one oath: nothing entrusted is ever let go. They keep dead cities street by street, treaties with the signatures' hesitations, famines to the last shared crust. Kings find their completeness unflattering. Of Sin-Eaters they say: a people that prunes its memory bleeds out slowly, smiling.*

**Playstyle.** Turn the battle into precedent as it happens; nothing works on you twice, and your allies inherit every lesson instantly.

**The road to Mindhoard:**

1. Reach level 40 and receive a dead city entire — streets, songs, censuses — from the dead who keep it.
2. Correct a king's chronicle to his face, and survive because every word held.
3. Take the Long Count: a year reciting winters backward to the first your line remembers, arriving without a gap.
4. Sit across from a Sin-Eater at a wake and keep what they swallow, so that somewhere, it is still true.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Winters Count** — Each round of battle joins the hoard; gain rising power as the fight becomes precedent.
- **Not Twice** — Any spell or maneuver used against you this battle fails outright on its second attempt.
- **Unflattering Account** — Recite a foe's true record aloud; their followers waver and your allies strike them truer.
- **In the Hoard** — Allies' skills, memories, and oaths cannot be drained, altered, or forgotten in your presence.

### Bellwether

**Role:** Warding leader — the fold behind the bell · **Advances from:** Totem-Carver

*The Bellwethers keep the old compacts of fold and byre — herd-gods, hound-gods, the patient gods of hives. Their rule is simple: the bell walks first into every danger. No valley records what it owes them; the herds know. Of the Redgifts they say only: whoever feeds wolves is raising wolves.*

**Playstyle.** Name your companions a fold and walk point; while you lead from the front, the covenant hardens everyone walking behind.

**The road to Bellwether:**

1. Reach level 40 and bring a starving valley's herds through a wolf-winter, losing none you did not name beforehand.
2. Carry the bell first through a gate everyone present knows is wrong, and ring it going in.
3. Stand between raiders and the fold with nothing but the compact, and watch their horses refuse.
4. Refuse a Redgift's offered peace with the wolves, and pay for your winter the long way.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Bell Goes First** — While you are foremost in danger, allies behind you take reduced harm and cannot rout.
- **Compact of the Fold** — Name your companions a fold; they gain herd-god endurance, and the scattered always know the way back.
- **Hound-Gods' Circuit** — Spirit hounds circle the fold; the first flanker each round is intercepted, loudly.
- **Milk and Firstwool** — Make the fold's offering; everyone in your bound is mended and cleansed of one affliction.

### Guestright

**Role:** Sanctuary controller — courtesy as fortification · **Advances from:** Good Neighbor

*In four centuries the order of Guestright has never compelled, tricked, or missed a date, and the unseen know it. Their answer to a god gone bad is withdrawal: no offering, no name, no notice, until the ford weeps and treats. Godsreeves, they observe, are one bad day from being Necromancers of the air.*

**Playstyle.** Win by standing; declare truce-ground, call freely given aid, and let land that likes you refuse your enemies everything.

**The road to Guestright:**

1. Reach level 40 and keep every date for seven years; the order counts, and so does everything else.
2. Be welcomed as a guest by something that has eaten every guest before you, and leave it a better host.
3. Starve a murdering ford-god by courtesy alone — village moved, name unspoken — until it weeps and treats.
4. Turn a Godsreeve from a door you both know they could break, armed with nothing but the custom, and be obeyed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Perfect Guest** — Traps, wards, and hostile ground decline to harm you; you have standing wherever custom has been kept.
- **Declared Hearth** — Draw a circle and name it hearth; within it, no spirit or spell may do violence while parley holds.
- **Withheld Custom** — Name a hostile spirit's offense; every gift, aura, and power it grants others fails while it stands accused.
- **Ten Thousand Invitations** — Once per day, ask one great favor of the gods you have kept faith with; one always says yes.

### Doorless Hall

**Role:** Elite generalist — a specialist for every moment · **Advances from:** Bonechoir

*They took their doors off the hinges at the founding, and mean it: any decent dead may lodge, a night or a decade. A different master answers every need, and none is asked to stay. Of the Twicelived they say: the dead are a nation, not a spouse, and depth is hoarding.*

**Playstyle.** Carry the broadest toolkit on any field; open each fight with the right three guests and trade them freely as problems change.

**The road to Doorless Hall:**

1. Reach level 40 and unhang your door before the line's stones, and burn the hinges.
2. Host nine strangers' dead in nine nights, learning each name, trade, and favorite chair.
3. Attend a Twicelived wedding-rite and hold your tongue; then go home and take in the forebear nobody chose.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Hall Is Open** — Begin each battle hosting three volunteers of your choosing; each grants a full mastery.
- **Right Guest, Right Moment** — Once per round, exchange one guest for another better suited to the immediate problem.
- **Crowded Rafters** — Many watchers: you cannot be surprised, and every lie told near you is recognized by whoever coined it.
- **The Whole Hall Answers** — Once per battle every lodger lends one heartbeat at once: a single action of overwhelming, borrowed skill.

### Sin-Eater

**Role:** Absorbing support — eats what ails others · **Advances from:** Yearteller

*When two villages must share a well but cannot share what they did to each other, a Sin-Eater is sent for. Bread is passed over the dead; the worst is eaten; the living get on. The church keeps no rite for this, and no objection it cares to own. Mindhoards call it mutilation of the record. Sin-Eaters call it surgery.*

**Playstyle.** Eat what is hurting your allies — curses, terrors, griefs — and carry it visibly; you are strongest when heaviest.

**The road to Sin-Eater:**

1. Reach level 40 and eat your first great sin at a stranger's wake, and keep it down, and keep it.
2. Let two villages that murdered each other share a market again, because only you remember why they could not.
3. Be cursed by a Mindhoard for the mercy of it, and thank them for keeping what you now carry.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Funeral Bread** — Eat an affliction off an ally — curse, fear, madness, poison — taking it into yourself at half weight.
- **Swallowed Grudge** — Consume an enemy's vengeance; whatever power their hatred, oath, or grief was feeding, it starves.
- **Strong Stomach** — What you carry cannot kill you; carried afflictions never stack past bearable, merely past pleasant.
- **The Table of Ills** — Once per battle, eat every hostile effect on your companions in one grim sitting.

### Redgift

**Role:** Blood-paying striker — lent fangs, honest fees · **Advances from:** Totem-Carver

*The Redgifts keep the other compact: the predators' one. Wolf-gods, bear-gods, the carrion-cranes — all fed on schedule, all from the shaman's own arm, and in return the wild hunts beside the village instead of through it. Bellwethers call them wolf-feeders. The Redgifts answer: the fold stands because the wolves agree it may.*

**Playstyle.** Pay power's fee in your own blood; every invocation costs, and every cost buys teeth that something else must answer.

**The road to Redgift:**

1. Reach level 40 and open your arm at the wolf-stone on midwinter, and be answered before the blood cools.
2. Keep the red calendar one full year — every date, your blood, nobody else's.
3. Sleep a night in the den as a guest whose fee is current, and wake unmarked among the yearlings.
4. Let a Bellwether watch you pay, and answer their look with the count of lambs alive because you do.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Wolf's Share** — Open your arm; the pack-god strikes one foe as lent fangs, savage in proportion to what you paid.
- **Carrion Terms** — Promise the crane-gods first pick of the field; they harry your enemies, eyes first, until it is theirs.
- **Old Bear Rising** — Pay heavily; a bear-god fights beside you for a scene — uncompelled, well fed, and thorough.
- **Fees Current** — While your payments stand current, no beast or beast-god will harm you; their strikes refuse at the last.

### Godsreeve

**Role:** Spirit-hunter — sanctions gods that break terms · **Advances from:** Good Neighbor

*Custom binds both parties, or it is superstition. When a hearth sours births or a ford drowns past its due, the Godsreeve serves notice under the old custom, which the small gods are held by more deeply than any spell. Guestright calls it bullying. The Reeves speak for the drowned children, who call it late.*

**Playstyle.** Drag the unseen into the open and prosecute; strip a god's gifts, evict its tenancy, and make the field inhospitable to spirits.

**The road to Godsreeve:**

1. Reach level 40 and prosecute your first god — the ford that took the miller's daughters — and carry the verdict home dry-shod.
2. Post the year-and-a-day watch on an evicted haunt, all of it, awake.
3. Break a door a Guestright would have knocked at, and answer for it at their table without lowering your eyes.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Serve Notice** — Name a spirit's breach aloud; it must manifest visibly for a scene, and may be struck.
- **Distrainment** — Seize a hostile god's gathered offerings; wield its stolen strength against it for the rest of the battle.
- **Eviction** — Cast a possessor or haunt out of body, house, or field; it may not return for a year and a day.
- **Custom Has Teeth** — Any spirit that harms one under your protection takes the same harm back, as the old custom provides.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Second Skeleton

**Role:** Mythic duelist — two lifetimes, load-bearing · **Advances from:** Twicelived

*Carried long enough, a guest stops being carried. The histories name a handful in whom the seam closed entirely — one continuous person across two lifetimes, the elder bones bearing weight the younger never could. Asking which of the two is alive is considered rude by the order and unanswerable by the church.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as one person with two lifetimes' instincts; wounds, watches, and burdens are shared across a frame built double.

**The road to The Second Skeleton:**

1. Reach level 60 and be addressed by the stones as one name, and answer without deciding which of you speaks.
2. Host your guest's own forebear for a season, and learn what your marriage looks like from above.
3. Let the physicians declare you dead and the genealogists declare you living, and correct neither.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Load-Bearing** — Blows that would fell you are taken up by the elder frame; you remain standing past mortal arithmetic.
- **Two Lifetimes at Once** — Mastery of everything either life ever practiced, held simultaneously, without switching.
- **No Vacancy** — Possession, domination, and beguilement fail outright; the house is full.
- **The Elder Hour** — Once per battle the forebear presides entirely: for one round, act with a dead master's full prime.

### The Unforgotten

**Role:** Mythic archive — fallen nations, standing account · **Advances from:** Mindhoard

*When the last speaker of a tongue dies, one of these wakes fluent, because the dead ask to be kept somewhere warm. The Unforgotten carry extinguished nations entire — law, lullaby, grievance. Empires have sent armies to court or kill them, and found the attempt already entered in the account, dated.*

**Playstyle.** Fight with the skills of extinct peoples and the foresight of complete records; nothing about your enemy is new to everyone you carry.

**The road to The Unforgotten:**

1. Reach level 60 and wake speaking a drowned tongue, and go find the last stones that grieve in it.
2. Attend the burning of a library and walk out carrying it, because its keepers died volunteering.
3. Recite a king's inherited crimes from his own scaffold until the crowd unbuilds it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Account Includes You** — Your dead have met your foe's people, always; begin every fight knowing their habits, oaths, and fears.
- **Extinct Techniques** — Strike in styles no living school counters; defenses trained against the present fail against the departed.
- **As It Is Kept** — Near you, glamours, forgeries, and false histories fail; things revert to what the witnesses attest.
- **Nowhere Lost** — Allies' minds and skills cannot be erased, drained, or taken in your presence.

### The Fold

**Role:** Mythic protector — nothing preys within the bounds · **Advances from:** Bellwether

*Where the Fold stands, the compact holds absolutely: within their bounds nothing preys, and predator and prey wait out the weather together, embarrassed. Refugee columns have crossed war-winters inside that walking peace. Histories dispute whether the great wolves obey the person or the office. The wolves decline to clarify.*

**Playstyle.** Walk your bounds through the worst of it; inside, allies cannot be preyed upon, and hunger of every kind waits outside.

**The road to The Fold:**

1. Reach level 60 and walk a fleeing people through a war so that the war never quite finds them.
2. Pen a battle: step between two armies, declare the field fold, and be obeyed by the hungrier side first.
3. Winter with the wolves inside your own bounds, and share the same fire without one sheep's worth of doubt.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Within the Wattle** — Declare your bounds; inside them, ambush, sniping, and every advantage of predator over prey simply fails.
- **No Wolf Here** — Stalking, terror, and the singling-out of the weak cannot function inside your bounds.
- **Gathered In** — Once per battle, every scattered or fleeing ally is drawn safely to your bounds and steadied.
- **Counted and Kept** — Name your fold at dusk; those named cannot fall below one breath while your bounds hold.

### The Laid Table

**Role:** Mythic diplomat — sanctuary wherever they sit · **Advances from:** Guestright

*Wherever they sit becomes the table, and the table is always laid, and the unseen come to dinner. Feuds between river-gods have ended over that bread. Historians dispute whether the table is wherever they are, or they are wherever the table is; the guests consider the question poor manners.*

**Playstyle.** Turn any ground into sanctuary and any enemy of the unseen into a dinner guest; battles end at your table or not at all.

**The road to The Laid Table:**

1. Reach level 60 and lay the table between two armies, seating the hearth-gods of both homelands, while the soldiers watch.
2. Host the feud of two rivers until it ends in the only place it could: over your bread.
3. Eat alone one night a year, so you remember what the table is for.

**Abilities granted:**

- **A Seat for the Unseen** — Invite any spirit or god to sit; seated, it does no harm and grants one boon, having come for dinner.
- **Peace of the Board** — While your table stands, violence within its light falters; blows soften, spells hesitate, parley holds.
- **What the House Provides** — Food, shelter, and mending appear as ten thousand small hosts repay old courtesies at once.
- **The Great Favor** — Once per day, call in the largest kindness you are owed; some small god intervenes decisively.

### The Thronged

**Role:** Mythic multitude — a nation in one frame · **Advances from:** Doorless Hall

*Host enough guests for long enough and the census becomes a matter of opinion. The Thronged walk into town as one person and are remembered as a procession. Tyrants dislike them professionally: a people that was scattered, burned, and forbidden is walking around inside one polite visitor, intact.*

**Playstyle.** Bring a nation to a fistfight; meet every task with its best dead practitioner and every hardship with a thousand steadying hands.

**The road to The Thronged:**

1. Reach level 60 and carry an entire fallen clan through their last feast-day, and be their hall, and wash up after.
2. Enter a tyrant's census as one name and be counted eleven hundred, none of them incorrectly.
3. Be refused a room at an inn for the noise, having sat silent all evening, and pay for the damages the dead insist they caused.

**Abilities granted:**

- **A People Presiding** — Every action is taken by whichever of your thousands did it best in life.
- **Steadied by Thousands** — Wounds, terror, and despair are shared across the throng; what would break one person thins across many.
- **Ten Hundred Witnesses** — No deed done near you can be denied afterward; oath-breakers are known to every line you carry.
- **The Riding Out** — Once per battle the whole throng surges through your limbs: one act of numberless, unanimous skill.

### The Fed Silence

**Role:** Mythic sink — catastrophe eaten, kept down · **Advances from:** Sin-Eater

*In certain provinces there is a quiet that must be fed, and everyone knows the house it lives in. Villages bring it their worst year; kingdoms, discreetly, their worst century. Where it dwells, old horrors stay down — not bound, not banished. Digested. It is a very calm neighbor.*

**Playstyle.** Consume what the field cannot survive — curses, cataclysms, names — and grow heavier, calmer, and harder to move with each swallow.

**The road to The Fed Silence:**

1. Reach level 60 and eat the name of a feud so old that both houses believe they were always friends, and carry the truth alone.
2. Be fed a reign by the heir who cannot rule beside it, and bow to the better king you made.
3. Refuse, once, to eat, and make the village look at what it brought you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Fed and Sleeping** — What you have eaten stays eaten; no art, god, or grief can restore a thing you swallowed.
- **Bring It Your Worst** — Once per battle, eat one ongoing catastrophe — a curse-storm, a rout, a burning — whole.
- **Heavy with Years** — Attacks on your mind and soul sink into the stored silence and do not come back out.
- **The Spared Seat** — Folk leave your seat empty by instinct; enemies must overcome dread merely to target you.

### The Open Vein

**Role:** Mythic provider — hunger's fee, personally met · **Advances from:** Redgift

*Famine-winters end at their arm. The Open Vein pays the wild's whole fee personally, and has for years, and the gods of hunger know their taste the way herdsmen know a bell. Nothing that eats will take another while they stand offering. What this costs is not discussed in front of them.*

**Playstyle.** Bleed on purpose and buy the extraordinary; the deeper the cut, the greater the answering hunt, and you decide what you can spare.

**The road to The Open Vein:**

1. Reach level 60 and end a famine-winter at the wolf-stone with one arm and the whole of a season's nights.
2. Learn what your blood is worth on the far side, and never once renegotiate.
3. Watch the pack lay a winter's kills at your door, unasked, and understand it as thanks.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Great Fee** — Spend any amount of your own blood; the predator-gods answer in strict, generous proportion.
- **Known to Every Hunger** — Devouring things — beasts, famine-gods, drainings — will not take another creature while you stand offering.
- **Sworn on the Vein** — Terms sealed in your blood have never been broken by the other side; invoke one such term each battle.
- **Everything Left** — At death's edge, offer everything left; every god you ever fed answers at once, and they carry the fight.

### The Quiet Court

**Role:** Mythic magistrate — the unseen bring their disputes · **Advances from:** Godsreeve

*Where they sit, court is quietly in session, and the small gods arrive of their own accord — plaintiff wells, defendant winds, a hearth seeking custody of a family. Findings are enforced by the custom itself, which has no officers and needs none. The living mostly notice better weather.*

**Playstyle.** Preside rather than fight; call the unseen to session, strip the convicted of station, and let outlawry do your pursuing.

**The road to The Quiet Court:**

1. Reach level 60 and try a river in spate, and have the finding honored by the water while it is still angry.
2. Hear the case the wind has been bringing back for a hundred years, and close it.
3. Recuse yourself once, publicly, and let the unseen watch what the law does without you in it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Session** — Declare court; every spirit on the field must appear plainly, state its business, and abide the finding.
- **Stripped of Station** — Convict a hostile god; its powers, grounds, and offerings are forfeit for a year.
- **Contempt** — A spirit that flees your court is outlawed; every ward bars it and every hearth refuses it shelter.
- **The Custom Provides** — Invoke any lesser ruling ever rendered under the old custom; precedent applies, immediately.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Eldest

**Role:** Apotheosis — forebear of record to all lines · **Advances from:** The Second Skeleton

*Trace any family honestly and far enough, and the lines converge on an office rather than a person. The Eldest is everyone's forebear of record: every stone answers them, every line's dead defer, and each holder, dying, joins the guests of the next. The office has never once been vacant. Genealogists have stopped checking.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as the head of every family at once; any willing dead of any line may preside, and all of them are willing.

**The road to The Eldest:**

1. Reach level 85 and watch three unrelated houses trace their lines honestly, and arrive, all three, at you.
2. Hold the winter feast at which every family's dead attend one table, and carve.
3. Greet the previous Eldest among your own guests, and understand, at last, where Eldests go.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Forebear of Record** — Host any willing dead of any lineage on earth; none has ever declined the Eldest.
- **The Stones Answer** — Anywhere you stand, the local dead treat you as their own; their ground, memory, and standing are yours.
- **Seniority** — Bloodline curses, house oaths, and powers of inheritance bend to your ruling; you outrank the ancestor they cite.
- **While Any Line Lives** — You cannot be unmade while anyone living has ancestors; the office declines to lapse.

### The Long Memory

**Role:** Apotheosis — the world's act of remembering · **Advances from:** The Unforgotten

*Above every archive there is a memory that does not burn, and it is awake. The Long Memory is the office by which the world recalls itself: what it holds cannot be made never to have been. Conquerors erase; a generation later every child somehow knows. Clerks call it providence. The dead call it work.*

**Playstyle.** Know everything the dead ever witnessed and lend it freely; near you the past is present tense and erasure runs backward.

**The road to The Long Memory:**

1. Reach level 85 and receive the memory of the first speaker-for, passed holder to holder since before the word for it, and add one winter.
2. Attend the burning of a murdered people's last archive, and let the murderers discover, in a generation, that every child knows.
3. Be asked by a Sin-Eater's heir to surrender one memory, and refuse, gently, and keep also the asking.

**Abilities granted:**

- **On Good Authority** — Anything ever seen by anyone now dead is available to you on request, with attribution.
- **Erasure Fails** — Workings of forgetting, unmaking, and false history collapse near you; what was hidden is re-attested.
- **Remembered Valor** — Once per battle, the field recalls its finest hour; allies fight with the skill of everyone who ever held this ground.
- **Beyond Slander** — You cannot be falsified, glamoured, or misquoted; every attempt reports its author to you.

### Worldfold

**Role:** Apotheosis — the world penned safe at night · **Advances from:** The Fold

*The old songs are literal: the world is a fold, the dark outside is full, and something walks the wattle-line at night. The office is why the wild suffers villages to exist, and why what circles beyond the edge stays beyond it. Shepherds everywhere set aside a fleece at shearing. None could say who told them to.*

**Playstyle.** Hold the bound of the world itself; declare ground inside the fold and nothing may prey there, whatever world it came from.

**The road to Worldfold:**

1. Reach level 85 and walk the world's bound once around, greeting what patrols the far side of the wattle, and renew the terms.
2. Fetch back the lamb that got out, whatever it had become by the time you found it.
3. Stand at the edge on the long night, and be the reason the world has an edge.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Bounds Walked** — Declare any ground within the world's fold; nothing may prey upon it, including things from outside the world.
- **Outside the Wattle** — Put one enemy out; it is exiled beyond the world's edge, alone with what circles there, until you relent.
- **First Through the Dark** — You take the first harm of any new horror personally; having taken it, your fold is proof against it after.
- **The Flock of Nations** — Every settlement knows the office; their dogs, herds, hearths, and dead rally to you unasked.

### The Welcome

**Role:** Apotheosis — hospitality as a standing force · **Advances from:** The Laid Table

*Wherever a stranger is fed, the office is present; wherever a guest is harmed, the weather takes it personally. The Welcome is hospitality made a fact of the world: fires burn taller for the offered seat, and the guest-killer finds that roofs leak, roads lengthen, and fords deepen for him, everywhere, forever.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive anywhere custom is kept, shelter whomever you name, and unmake enemies not with blows but with a world that stops hosting them.

**The road to The Welcome:**

1. Reach level 85 and let midwinter find you at every hearth that set a place for the unseen, and leave each one fuller.
2. Pass judgment on a king who killed a guest, and let his fires go out to say so.
3. Be, at the last, the knock at the door in every story where the stranger turns out to matter.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wherever Bread Is Broken** — Step from any kept hearth to any other, anywhere, arriving as an expected guest.
- **The World Recuses** — Judge a violator of guest or host; fire, water, roof, and road refuse them from this day on.
- **Sanctuary Absolute** — Declare a hearth; within its light no violence of any kind, by anyone, succeeds until the embers die.
- **The Owed World** — Ten thousand small gods keep accounts in your name; once per day the world itself does you a favor.

### The Folkmoot

**Role:** Apotheosis — the dead in permanent assembly · **Advances from:** The Thronged

*The dead outnumber the living and have opinions, and somewhere the moot of all of them is permanently in session. The office carries it. No age is decided by the living alone anymore: wars have ended because the fallen of both sides voted, and were, for the first time anyone could recall, counted.*

**Playstyle.** Bring the weight of every dead voice to the field; contests of will become votes you cannot lose, and the fallen of both sides attend.

**The road to The Folkmoot:**

1. Reach level 85 and convene the first full sitting in nine ages, and read the roll until the stars change.
2. Overrule a living empire's declaration of war with the recorded refusal of its own dead, spoken into the wind.
3. Take the chair that has stood empty nine ages, and learn that the office was never yours; you are its.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Standing of the Dead** — In any battle, the dead of both sides may take a side; those who listen to the dead are preferred.
- **Counted Among** — Each ally fights bolstered by everyone who ever loved them; the dead make surprisingly good seconds.
- **The Objection** — Once per round the moot objects; one enemy action is suspended while the matter is heard, and the matter takes a while.
- **Recess** — Once per battle the moot withdraws its consent from the violence; enemy resolve, formations, and borrowed powers collapse.

### The Amnesty

**Role:** Apotheosis — the past pardoned, struck, void · **Advances from:** The Fed Silence

*Beyond forgetting there is a standing pardon, and someone holds it. What the Amnesty swallows is not hidden but void: the feud unhappened, the blood-guilt lifted from children not yet born. Custom in three mountain provinces keeps a saying no elder remembers coining: what has been eaten shall not be inherited.*

**Playstyle.** Annul rather than defeat; eat curses, feuds, and inherited dooms out of the world entirely, and walk lighter than you have any right to.

**The road to The Amnesty:**

1. Reach level 85 and eat the founding crime of an empire, and watch it survive being innocent.
2. Hold the amnesty of an age: one night when anything may be brought to your door, and everything is.
3. Teach your successor to eat, and offer them, first and freely, everything you carry.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Voided** — Consume a curse, oath, feud, or doom; it never binds anyone again, anywhere, beginning with today.
- **Eaten Whole** — Swallow one hostile working outright, however large; it is not dispelled but digested, and cannot be made again.
- **General Pardon** — Once per day, annul every affliction, geas, and inherited penalty binding your companions, in one sitting.
- **Nothing to Avenge** — Powers fueled by vengeance, grievance, or old wrongs find no fuel near you; you have eaten their reasons.

### The Portion

**Role:** Apotheosis — the share the unseen are owed · **Advances from:** The Open Vein

*From every harvest something goes missing, and no one asks, because the missing share is the arrangement. The Portion is that share, walking: the office through which the world's ten thousand hungers are fed on schedule and therefore mind their manners. Bad years happen where it has not lately been. It travels constantly.*

**Playstyle.** Stand at the center of every exchange; all power spent near you pays you a share, and everything you feed fights for you.

**The road to The Portion:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the portion of a whole age's harvest in one night, and deliver it, shrine by shrine, before dawn.
2. Stand in the granary of a starving city and be the reason the hunger takes its share from the war instead.
3. Go unfed yourself one year in ten, so the office remembers whom it serves.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Taken First** — The first harm of every battle is taken by you and converted whole into payment for your gods.
- **The Set-Aside** — Every enemy spell, strike, and blessing pays you a share as it happens; you grow strong on their effort.
- **No Hunger Unfed** — Starvation, drain, and devouring effects fail near you; every appetite present has already been paid.
- **Call the Fed** — Once per day, everything the office has ever fed answers at once. Stand somewhere dry.

### The Old Custom

**Role:** Apotheosis — the law beneath the gods · **Advances from:** The Quiet Court

*Older than the gods' names is the way things were first agreed, and it is not a document; it is an office, walking. Fords take only their due because the Old Custom holds. Its holder is forgotten by name in their own lifetime and addressed by title at every threshold on earth. The gods stand when it enters.*

**Playstyle.** Preside over the terms of the world's oldest handshake; spirits behave as first agreed near you, and your rulings become how things are.

**The road to The Old Custom:**

1. Reach level 85 and be forgotten by name in your own lifetime, and answered to by title at every ford on earth.
2. Reopen the first case — the original breach between the living and the unseen — and settle it at last, out of court.
3. Amend the custom once in an age, and spend the rest of the age paying for it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **As First Agreed** — Revert any god, spirit, or bargained place to its founding terms; corruption, usurpation, and bad amendments fall away.
- **Older Than Names** — No spirit or god may act against its own terms in your presence; each behaves as it originally promised.
- **The Custom Walks** — Every ford, hearth, and threshold where custom was ever kept is adjacent to you; arrive by any of them.
- **The Old Ruling** — Once per day, rule on anything; the world enforces the ruling as though it had always been custom.
