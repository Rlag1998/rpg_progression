# 🔔 Summoner

> *The art is not the arrival; it is the departure.*

**Archetype:** Arcane

The Summoner casts nothing at the enemy. They speak a name correctly, draw a circle fairly, and something out of another jurisdiction arrives to do it for them. Their power is a ledger of kept terms — princes of flame, tides with titles, the recorded roar of old battles — every guest paid, every guest gone by dawn. Arrival is easy. Departure is the discipline.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Summoner

**Role:** Summon support — contracted allies fight for you

*Anyone can shout into the dark; a summoner learns what the dark charges to answer. First-rank summoners keep chalk, a little bell, and one name they can pronounce without lying. Their guests are small — a coal-sprite, a polite wind — and they leave on time, which the guild grades harder than the arriving.*

**Playstyle.** Frail alone, never alone. Draw your circle, state fair terms, and let small guests fight while you keep the contract — and the exit — intact.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Learn one true name from a retiring summoner, and pay for it in errands, not coin.
2. Draw your first circle in chalk, then in salt, then in rain; a circle you cannot redraw is a cage, and cages are the other art.
3. Call something small and let it decline; the guild passes only those who thank an empty circle.
4. Ring the little bell at the hour agreed, every time, until the hour agreed rings you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Small Guest** — Invite a minor elemental to fight beside you; it serves exactly as long as promised and not one breath longer.
- **Fair Circle** — Chalk a circle that shields whoever stands within; the ward holds while your terms remain honest and your line unscuffed.
- **Recall Bell** — Ring your guest home instantly; a guest released early remembers the courtesy and arrives stronger the next time.
- **Terms Aloud** — State the contract before witnesses; your next summon gains duration for every term you keep audible and true.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Accordant

**Role:** Summon striker — champions on short fair terms · **Advances from:** Summoner

*Accordants are sworn onto the rolls of the Accords, the standing treaties between the guild and the Four Courts — Flame, Tide, Gale, and Stone. They may call liveried champions for a skirmish's length. The Courts read every summons twice. An Accordant's reputation is the collateral, and the Courts keep excellent records.*

**Playstyle.** Call the right court for the fight in front of you. Champions hit hard on brief terms; sequence arrivals and departures like a drillmaster.

**The road to Accordant:**

1. Reach level 10 and stand examination before a delegate of each Court; the questions are all about leaving.
2. Copy the Accords in your own hand, and mark the terms you already know will tempt you.
3. Host a champion of Flame for one night without asking it for anything; hospitality precedes employment.
4. Return your first guest on schedule while you still needed it; the rolls record the hour, not the outcome.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Liveried Champion** — Summon a champion of one Court in its colors; it fights your enemies as its own for sixty honest seconds.
- **Skirmish Terms** — Shorten a champion's contract to double its fury; brave terms, briefly kept, are the Courts' favorite kind.
- **Four-Court Rota** — Rotate summons across the Courts; each court honored in sequence lends its successor a sharper arrival.
- **Grievance Filed** — When a guest is struck down before term's end, its Court answers the insult with one uninvited retaliation.

### Yesterbell

**Role:** Echo caster — replayed moments as utility · **Advances from:** Summoner

*Yesterbells learn the other invitation: not beings, but moments. A place keeps what happened in it, and a bell struck rightly can wake the recording — an hour of rain, a shout that turned a battle. The Belfry licenses them and audits fiercely, because the untrained keep mistaking echoes for ghosts.*

**Playstyle.** Collect moments in the field, then strike them back at need. Your spellbook is a rack of recorded hours; timing beats power.

**The road to Yesterbell:**

1. Reach level 10 and stand in a field where a battle happened; ring your bell until you hear which part of it never stopped.
2. Capture a small hour — kettle steam, a child's argument — and play it back for the Belfry unembellished.
3. Sit the Necromancer's Question: prove to three auditors that your echo of a dead man contains no dead man.
4. Break a bell that recorded something you loved, because you were starting to visit it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Strike the Hour** — Ring a captured moment back into the world for six seconds; it replays exactly, indifferent to everyone present.
- **Taken Impression** — Uncover your bell during an event to take its impression; violence records loudest, weather keeps best.
- **Echo of Rain** — Replay a remembered downpour over a zone, dousing flames, slicking footing, and muffling enemy incantations for its recorded span.
- **Nobody Home** — An echo of yourself steps out to draw blows; it contains no one, which puzzles mind-readers and disappoints assassins.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Envoy Militant

**Role:** Field commander — champions under negotiated colors · **Advances from:** Accordant

*Envoys Militant carry the Accords to war. They arrive with a folio of standing treaties and take the field commanding a company no kingdom pays: a flame-knight here, a tide-lancer there, each under colors negotiated the winter before. Generals resent them precisely until the third day of a losing siege.*

**Playstyle.** Run a small allied company under exact terms: position champions, spend their windows, and never let a guest die holding your promise.

**The road to Envoy Militant:**

1. Reach level 25 and negotiate one treaty yourself, alone, at a court of your choosing; bring back terms your teachers cannot improve.
2. Command guests of two rival Courts in one battle without letting either notice the other was invited first.
3. Bury no guests. If one falls under your colors, carry its standard to its Court yourself and stand for the questions.
4. Refuse a war whose terms are bad, publicly, once; envoys who cannot refuse cannot negotiate.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Negotiated Colors** — Champions under your colors share one morale; while any guest stands unbroken, none of them can rout.
- **Winter Treaty** — Invoke a treaty sealed the winter before: one champion arrives instantly, already briefed, once per engagement.
- **Honors of War** — A guest that fells a worthy enemy earns honors; its Court extends the term free, pleased with the story.
- **Orderly Withdrawal** — Dismiss all guests at once in good order; each departs with a parting blow and a favorable report.

### Hosteller

**Role:** Zone support — standing embassies, wards, sanctuary · **Advances from:** Accordant

*Hostellers keep the ground the Courts visit: circle-halls where a tide ambassador can pool in comfort and a stone princess can sit unbowed. Their art is upkeep — climates balanced, protocols observed, nothing iron near the Gale wing. A well-kept hall means the whole guild's calls answer faster, everywhere.*

**Playstyle.** Claim ground and improve it: standing circles, courtesies, small resident guests. Your zones heal, shield, and remember who behaved badly in them.

**The road to Hosteller:**

1. Reach level 25 and keep one embassy through all four seasons; the Tide wing floods in spring, and must.
2. Host delegates of Flame and Tide at one table; serve nothing that steams.
3. Evict a guest who broke your peace, politely, completely, and survive its Court's inquiry with the paperwork clean.
4. Learn what each Court cannot eat, cannot hear, and cannot forgive; write none of it down.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Standing Circle** — Establish a circle that persists between fights; summons cast from it cost less and arrive in better tempers.
- **Residency** — House a minor elemental in your circle long-term; it tends the ward, mends allies, and gossips usefully.
- **One Prohibition** — Declare one thing forbidden within your circle — fire, flight, or lies; guests and enemies alike comply.
- **Warm Reception** — Allies entering your circle are received: cleansed of one affliction and seated, briefly untargetable, while they catch their breath.

### Twicefought

**Role:** Echo commander — recorded combatants refought · **Advances from:** Yesterbell

*The Twicefought specialize in battle-echoes: the remembered hour of a dead hero, the shape of a charge that broke a line. They field soldiers no arrow can disappoint — recordings, faithful to the original down to the mud. Veterans find fighting beside a dead friend's finest hour either unbearable or holy.*

**Playstyle.** Deploy recorded fighters that replay their finest minutes. They cannot adapt, so you must: aim the past precisely at the present.

**The road to Twicefought:**

1. Reach level 25 and record a battle from inside it; the Belfry counts survival as part of the assignment.
2. Summon a hero's hour before the hero's widow, and answer honestly when she asks if he is in there.
3. Fight beside your own recording of yourself, and do not look at it after the first exchange.
4. Learn the difference between an echo failing and an echo ending; only one of them is your fault.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Hero's Hour** — Summon a dead hero's remembered hour; it fights its old fight magnificently and cannot be persuaded it is elsewhere.
- **Cavalry, Remembered** — Replay a famous charge across the field; it tramples the living exactly where the dead once rode.
- **Faithful to the Mud** — Your echoes reproduce their conditions: original weather, original footing, original fear, applied to everyone standing inside the recording.
- **Played Through** — An echo allowed to complete its hour unbroken ends with its recorded triumph, dealt as real damage.

### Stormfetch

**Role:** Terrain caster — recorded weather as control · **Advances from:** Yesterbell

*Stormfetches summon weather that already happened: the flood of a named year, the frost that stopped a war, one particular afternoon of lightning. The original is never harmed by the copy — the Belfry is emphatic on this — though coasts that survived a famous gale grow nervous when a Stormfetch visits.*

**Playstyle.** Reshape fights with archival weather. Slow to place, wide in effect; you win by choosing which day the battlefield is having.

**The road to Stormfetch:**

1. Reach level 25 and stand through a real storm while taking its impression; a copy taken from shelter is a copy of shelter.
2. Replay the frost of a named winter across a summer pond, and return the season unharmed.
3. Learn your storms' habits by heart — where the third gust goes, which wave undercuts — for the recording will not adjust to spare you.
4. Retire one weather forever when a town asks; some years should not be summonable within sight of their graves.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Named Gale** — Replay a catalogued storm over the field; it proceeds exactly as recorded, and you alone have read the record.
- **Old Fog** — Raise a remembered fog in which your allies were once brave; sight, morale, and arrows all behave accordingly.
- **The Flood of That Year** — Summon a historic flood in miniature; it follows its original course, and you have arranged for enemies to be standing in it.
- **Yesterday's Lightning** — Strike with lightning that already struck once; armor that survived the present offers no argument to the past.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Gonfalonier

**Role:** Muster commander — treaty armies under one banner · **Advances from:** Envoy Militant

*The Gonfaloniers carry sealed banners heavy with ribboned treaties — a mustered company of champions from every Court, sworn for the day to one mortal will. Their doctrine is plain: war forgives no committees. A guest under the gonfalon obeys or departs. The Trothbound call this hiring friends by the hour; Gonfaloniers call it how sieges end.*

**Playstyle.** Field many guests briefly and command them absolutely. Your power is logistics: rotations, reliefs, and terms that conclude before loyalty is ever tested.

**The road to Gonfalonier:**

1. Reach level 40 and muster champions of all four Courts under one banner for one battle; let none outshine the standard.
2. Draft the day-contract the Courts now call fair-and-iron; it has been copied since, never improved.
3. Hold a breach with guests alone for a night; command them by name, court, and hour, without once saying please.
4. Receive the gonfalon from a dying Gonfalonier and renegotiate every ribbon on it within the year; grief is not an extension.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sealed Banner** — Plant the gonfalon: all guests within its sight obey movement orders instantly and fight in formation regardless of Court.
- **Rotation of the Four** — Relieve tired guests with fresh ones mid-battle; each departing champion's remaining term transfers to its replacement as fury.
- **Terms of Muster** — Call three champions under one day-contract; they share commands, cooldowns, and an ironclad departure time.
- **The Banner Holds** — While the gonfalon stands planted, no guest can be banished, frightened, or turned; tear it down or endure them.

### Court Palatine

**Role:** Fortress summoner — element-law wings as zones · **Advances from:** Hosteller

*A Court Palatine keeps a palace with impossible wings: a parlor that has always been on fire, a stair of standing wind, a cellar the tide visits nightly. Within each wing the resident Court's law runs entire — the Palatine holding that half a welcome is an insult. Housefrith wardens call such houses occupied territory. Palatines agree, warmly.*

**Playstyle.** Deploy wings of your house onto the battlefield as zones of foreign law. Inside each, your guests are sovereign and your enemies are trespassers.

**The road to Court Palatine:**

1. Reach level 40 and add a wing to your house that no mortal law survives in; live beside it calmly.
2. Host a full court — throne, retinue, weather — for a season, and present the household accounts with nothing missing but iron.
3. Walk each wing of your own house nightly wearing its law: burn, drown, blow, and stand; hosts who exempt themselves keep hotels.
4. Refuse the Housefrith examiners at your gate once, with perfect courtesy, in all four laws at once.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Burning Parlor** — Unfold a wing where fire's law runs: flame cannot be quenched there, and your fire-guests cannot be harmed.
- **Tide Cellar** — Unfold a drowned wing; enemies fight submerged in a sea that remembers being deeper, while tide-guests move freely.
- **Gale Stair** — Unfold a wing of standing wind; allies climb it to vantage, and arrows entering are returned to sender.
- **Stone Gallery** — Unfold a gallery where stone's patience rules: enemy movement slows toward the geological, and your guests strike with buried weight.

### Fullpeal

**Role:** Set-piece summoner — whole battles replayed exactly · **Advances from:** Twicefought

*A Fullpeal rings an engagement through entire — every stroke in original order, hours of it, one error voiding the whole. The order holds that an edited echo is a lie, and that lies about the dead are how the other art starts. Changeringers call them worshippers of transcripts. Fullpeals do not answer; the record contains no reply.*

**Playstyle.** Commit to long, exact replays that transform the whole field. Immense if protected to completion; a broken peal returns nothing at all.

**The road to Fullpeal:**

1. Reach level 40 and ring one recorded battle through from first arrow to burial detail without a substituted stroke.
2. Attend the Changeringers' recital, then ring the same hour afterward, whole; let the audience judge the difference.
3. Ring a massacre for a court of law, exactly; testimony that cannot lie has hanged better men than the guilty.
4. Break your own favorite recording rather than trim it once; the order admits no one who has not paid that.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ring It Through** — Begin a full battle-echo; the engagement replays around you in order, and grows in power each uninterrupted minute.
- **Method** — Hold the method: while you ring unerringly, your echoes cannot be dispelled, disbelieved, or drowned out.
- **No Substitutions** — Your echoes arrive with everything the record holds — allies, banners, weather, dread — or they do not arrive at all.
- **Voided Peal** — If your replay is broken, it ends honestly: the echo collapses, and the interrupter briefly hears everything at once.

### Cellarer of Storms

**Role:** Arsenal caster — shelved weathers, instant deployment · **Advances from:** Stormfetch

*Cellarers walk into disasters carrying bells, and walk out carrying the disaster. Their cellars shelve bottled hurricanes, the frosts of famous winters, one small labeled earthquake. The order's charge is preservation — a weather unrecorded dies with its witnesses. Groundswells call the cellars menageries. Cellarers reply that wild weather is lovely until you need some on a Tuesday.*

**Playstyle.** Fight from inventory. Every shelved weather is a spell slot; scarcity is real, so spend the right catastrophe, not the biggest one.

**The road to Cellarer of Storms:**

1. Reach level 40 and take an impression of a disaster from inside its worst hour; the label must show your handwriting unshaken.
2. Build a cellar that satisfies the Courts you are not stealing weather, merely remembering it in bottles; the inspectors will test the distinction.
3. Trade shelf-space with a rival Cellarer at the annual exchange; arrive with duplicates and leave with nothing you already understand.
4. Answer a village's drought from your cellar without charge; the order audits generosity as strictly as inventory.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Off the Shelf** — Deploy any shelved weather instantly at full recorded strength; restocking requires attending another disaster personally.
- **Decanted Storm** — Pour out part of a stored storm as a directional blast, keeping the remainder shelved for later.
- **The Labeled Quake** — Uncork the small earthquake; it is small, it is labeled, and it is precisely under the enemy formation.
- **Acquisition** — Standing inside any hostile weather or area effect, bottle a copy of it for your cellar.

### Trothbound

**Role:** Bonded duo — one lifelong champion, total synchrony · **Advances from:** Envoy Militant

*A Trothbound summons one being, once, and never needs to again: a champion met young, courted honestly, and pledged for life on both sides. The pairs grow strange together — mortals with tidal tempers, fire princes who learn to sleep. Gonfaloniers call the troth a leash with two ends. The Trothbound agree, and add: so is a handclasp.*

**Playstyle.** You and your troth-champion fight as one action: shared health, braided abilities, no other summons. Losing either half is losing the fight.

**The road to Trothbound:**

1. Reach level 40 and be chosen back: the troth is offered by the champion, or it is not a troth.
2. Spend a year in your champion's court under its law, introduced honestly as what you are to each other.
3. Strike your two names from the rolls of separate summoners; the Accords list you as one name thereafter.
4. Outlast an argument that runs a decade; the Gonfaloniers keep a betting book on new troths, and hate paying out.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Braided Guard** — You and your champion parry for each other; damage to either may be taken by whichever of you it flatters less.
- **Courtship Gift** — Feed your champion its element mid-battle; it repays the gift doubled, immediately, and remembers it forever.
- **One Quarrel** — While you both strike one target, your blows interleave into a rhythm armor cannot answer.
- **Troth Unbroken** — If either of you falls, the other completes the current term at both your strengths, then grieves.

### Housefrith

**Role:** Aura controller — enforced truce, guarded sanctuary · **Advances from:** Hosteller

*Housefrith wardens keep the neutral houses where rival Courts can meet, because someone must own a room the weather cannot vote in. Within their walls all power checks itself at the threshold — princes included, hosts included. Palatines call the houses cages with doilies. They answer that nothing so soothes a fire prince as an hour of being merely warm.*

**Playstyle.** Project a walking truce. Inside your bounds damage gutters, summons pause, and oaths are audible. You win fights by disallowing them.

**The road to Housefrith:**

1. Reach level 40 and keep a house where Flame and Tide winter together; end spring with the same number of walls.
2. Take a storm from a gale prince at your threshold, keep it hung safely, and return it undiminished at his departure.
3. Be struck by a guest and answer with the law, not the poker; a house's peace must survive its keeper's temper.
4. Decline the Palatines' invitation to see how welcomes are really kept; decline it every year; enjoy declining it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Bread and Salt** — Offer bread and salt; any who accept cannot harm you or your allies until they leave your bounds.
- **Check Your Storm** — Entering your radius strips active empowerments from everyone, friend and enemy, and hangs them safely at the threshold like coats.
- **Sitting Room** — Unfold a sitting room mid-battle; those within cannot attack or be attacked, and the tea is real.
- **Breach of Peace** — The first creature to break truce inside your bounds takes every point of harm it attempted, witnessed and notarized.

### Changeringer

**Role:** Combo summoner — spliced moments rung in sequence · **Advances from:** Twicefought

*Changeringers ring moments out of order on purpose: the cavalry's arrival, thrice; the hero's best minute cut free of his worst hour. To them a recording is bell-metal, not scripture — history did its part, and now it can work shifts. Fullpeals accuse them of making things that never happened. Changeringers agree: that is what making means.*

**Playstyle.** Chain short echoes into sequences of your own design. Enormous flexibility, no single great summon; you assemble other people's finest seconds into hours that serve you.

**The road to Changeringer:**

1. Reach level 40 and ring a sequence no hour ever contained, and make a hardened company flinch at history that never was.
2. Cut a drowned fleet's last hour to the thirty seconds that would have saved it, and donate the arrangement to the naval college.
3. Win the Belfry's tourney of arrangements with a piece the Fullpeals cannot fault for one false stroke — only for the order they arrive in.
4. Splice nothing into an echo that its people did not truly do somewhere, sometime; the order's one law, and the gallows behind it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Cut to the Charge** — Skip an echo to its decisive moment, summoning the impact without the approach; costs double, arrives instantly.
- **Round Again** — Loop one echo's best three seconds up to three times; each repetition rings thinner but faster.
- **Spliced Hour** — Ring two moments simultaneously as one: last winter's ice under this summer's charge; enemies must survive both at once.
- **Rough Splice** — Join two echoes badly on purpose; the seam screams, disorienting everything nearby that remembers either original.

### Groundswell

**Role:** Site controller — wakes the land's own memory · **Advances from:** Stormfetch

*Groundswells carry no bottles. They arrive early, walk the ground, and learn what it is trying not to remember — the flood under the orchard, the battle under the barley. Woken at home, an echo comes entire, with a weight no cellar preserves. Cellarers call this foraging. Groundswells call the cellars very brave, very small weather.*

**Playstyle.** Scout terrain for sleeping history, then wake it under the enemy. Devastating on old ground, humbler on new; choose your battlefields the way generals wish they could.

**The road to Groundswell:**

1. Reach level 40 and wake a city's founding fire one street at a time, then settle it again with nothing new burned.
2. Spend a season learning one meadow; when you can say what it holds in March, the order will ask about April.
3. Wake something a Cellarer bottled a copy of, side by side, before judges; let the ground's version speak for the doctrine.
4. Never take an impression again; surrender your bottles, and be trusted by the dirt.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Walk the Ground** — Learn every echo sleeping within a mile; old blood, old water, and old fire announce themselves loudest.
- **Wake the Flood** — Rouse the ground's remembered water; it rises through the soil along its original course, entire and unbottled.
- **Under the Barley** — Wake the battle beneath the field; its recorded lines form around your enemies, who are standing where the dying happened.
- **Let It Sleep** — Soothe a woken echo down early; the grateful ground holds firm beneath allies and treacherous beneath enemies thereafter.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Standing Invitation

**Role:** Grand muster — allies arrive without being called · **Advances from:** Gonfalonier

*Histories count four Standing Invitations and dispute all of them. Their terms are so old, so fair, and so exactly kept that summoning becomes unnecessary: the banner is planted, and things arrive asking to stand under it. At Redwater Bridge nobody was called. The muster rolls for that day run to nine hundred names, none of them mortal.*

**Playstyle.** Stop casting summons; maintain terms instead. Allies accumulate wherever you stand your ground, faster in worse odds. Your only real cooldown is your reputation.

**The road to The Standing Invitation:**

1. Reach level 60 and settle every term you ever signed until no Court holds a single unmet line of yours; the Invitation stands only on even ground.
2. Plant your banner in a lost battle you were not paid, asked, or advised to attend, and hold until things begin arriving.
3. Turn away more help than you accept, courteously; a muster anyone may join is a mob, and your terms say company.
4. Let the four Courts audit your whole life at once, jointly, without notice; pour the tea yourself.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Open Muster** — While your banner stands, an uninvited ally arrives every few seconds, drawn by terms too good to hear about secondhand.
- **Terms Preceding** — Your reputation arrives before you do: enemy summons and hirelings check your terms, and some defect on the spot.
- **Nine Hundred Names** — Invoke Redwater: for ten seconds, everything that ever fought under your colors fights under them again, briefly and gladly.
- **Better Terms** — Offer an enemy's summoned or contracted creature honest improvement; the paperwork resolves in your favor unless theirs was fair.

### Prince of Guests

**Role:** Court summoner — principalities answer as family · **Advances from:** Court Palatine

*Somewhere in each principality stands a wing kept for one mortal: fireproof linens, a bed of standing wind. The Prince of Guests is royalty by pure reception — crowned nowhere, received everywhere. When they call now, the Courts do not send champions. They come themselves, the way family comes, which is to say entirely, and with opinions.*

**Playstyle.** Summon courts, not champions: thrones, retinues, weather and all. Slow, grand arrivals that reorder the battlefield's politics as thoroughly as its terrain.

**The road to Prince of Guests:**

1. Reach level 60 and be invited to all four courts in one year without requesting, hinting, or deserving it less than utterly.
2. Sleep one night in each principality unwarded; guests who check the locks are merely visitors.
3. Refuse a crown offered by a court in earnest; a prince of guests reigns only in the arriving.
4. Seat all four Courts at your name-day table and survive the precedence dispute; the dispute is the honor.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Court Comes** — Summon an entire elemental court in procession; its passage across the field is the attack.
- **Kinsright** — A principality intervenes to avenge harm done to you, uncontracted and unasked, as family does; you cannot invoke this, only deserve it.
- **Fireproof Linens** — You and your allies hold guest-standing in every element: unburned, undrowned, unburied, unblown.
- **State Visit** — Announce a visit upon enemy ground; protocol clears it before you — wards, traps, and illusions withdrawn like unsuitable furniture.

### The Carillon

**Role:** Living archive — any recorded hour, rung entire · **Advances from:** Fullpeal

*A Carillon no longer carries bells; the hours hang in them. Every echo the Belfry ever certified — and several it burned — can be rung from their standing body, entire, in order, at will. Histories dispute whether the last Carillon died. The Belfry's position is that she finished, which they insist is different.*

**Playstyle.** Your repertoire is the whole archive. Answer any battlefield with the exact hour it fears most; the cost is that you increasingly ring, rather than live.

**The road to The Carillon:**

1. Reach level 60 and take the whole archive into memory as the Belfry burns its duplicates behind you, shelf by shelf; you are the copy now.
2. Ring, on request, the hour any petitioner names, for a year and a day, refusing none and trimming nothing.
3. Hang your own recorded life among the rest, uncatalogued; the order will know you are ready when you cannot find it either.
4. Answer the question every Carillon is asked — is anyone in there — the way the truth requires: no one; everything.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Hanging Hours** — Ring any echo in the archive without preparation; the right hour for this fight is always already hung.
- **Full Tower** — Ring three whole echoes at once, interleaved; enemies caught in all three experience history's undivided attention.
- **Sounding Board** — Blows against you ring you like bronze, recording the attacker; their own strike replays against them within the minute.
- **The Burned Shelf** — Ring one of the forbidden hours the Belfry destroyed; nothing survives unchanged, including your standing with the Belfry.

### The Almanack

**Role:** Weather sovereign — the complete book of storms · **Advances from:** Cellarer of Storms

*The cellar has grown into a library, and the library into a person. The Almanack holds every weather since witnesses began: the flood of every named year, each frost that mattered, the specific gale your grandmother meant. Kingdoms consult them before planting wars. Histories dispute only whether the weather now checks the Almanack before happening.*

**Playstyle.** Open the book and choose the day. Any recorded weather at any scale, promptly; your only questions are which year, and how much of it this battlefield can hold.

**The road to The Almanack:**

1. Reach level 60 and shelve the last missing year; the collection is complete when the oldest Court cannot name a rain you lack.
2. Lend the whole library to a burning province and take it all back after, bottle by bottle, unthanked.
3. Record the weather of one entire year in person, outdoors, on every coast the guild can reach; delegating a single afternoon disqualifies.
4. Bind your own last breath into the collection's final bottle, labeled like all the others; an almanack that exempts its keeper is a diary.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Named Year** — Deploy every weather from one calendar year in sequence across the field; the year is your choice, and some years were terrible.
- **The Storm She Meant** — Summon the specific storm a witness remembers; it strikes with the force of the telling, which has grown.
- **Index of Frosts** — Apply the exact frost that once stopped an army identical to this one; precedent, correctly cited, repeats itself.
- **Out of Season** — Loose a weather into the wrong season; unopposed by its natural checks, it grows a size in the first minute.

### The Consort

**Role:** Bonded avatar — two beings, one legend · **Advances from:** Trothbound

*One Trothbound courted the heir of a drowning sea, and both worlds attended the wedding, armed. The Consort is what the pair became: one legend with a shoreline's temper and a mortal's stubbornness about mornings. Histories dispute which of them outlived the other. The coastal position is that neither has yet agreed to.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as one creature with two natures: mortal precision inside tidal mass. You are never flanked, rarely hurried, and impossible to widow.

**The road to The Consort:**

1. Reach level 60 and celebrate the hundredth year of the troth in both worlds at once; attendance is not optional for either of you.
2. Hold a coastline against an invading Court as a couple, and file the engagement under domestic matters.
3. Teach a young Trothbound pair to argue safely; the order's future is decided in other people's kitchens.
4. Outlive the wedding guests, the officiant, and the objections; renew nothing — the vow was drawn fairly the first time.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Two Natures** — Alternate mortal and tidal stances every few seconds; damage taken in one is healed in the other.
- **Marriage Portion** — Your spouse's dowry was a sea; spend a wave of it as a weapon, a wall, or a road.
- **Parted and Rejoined** — Split into two combatants mid-fight, then rejoin; enemies engaged by either take the reunion as a crashing flank.
- **No Widowing** — If either nature is slain, the other refuses the precedent; the slain half returns with the next tide, which you may schedule.

### Housepeace

**Role:** Absolute sanctuary — war cannot enter · **Advances from:** Housefrith

*There is a house where wars go to stop, and the house has a pulse. Housepeace is the warden grown indistinguishable from the peace they keep: walk in armed and find yourself, somehow, holding a cup instead. Three empires have tested this. The house keeps their standards in the hall closet, with the umbrellas.*

**Playstyle.** Extend the house outward until the battle is indoors, and therefore over. You do not stop blows; you make them impolite, then impossible.

**The road to Housepeace:**

1. Reach level 60 and stop a war by being between it; both armies must camp, cool, and leave by different roads unbloodied.
2. Grow the house by one room for every peace kept; when you can no longer find the original walls, neither can war.
3. Refuse three empires' offer to charter your house as neutral ground; charters expire, and the house does not.
4. Die once, quietly, in the kitchen; notice that the peace does not even flicker; return to work.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Hall Closet** — Confiscate the weapons, standards, and active spells of all who enter your peace; returned on departure, mostly.
- **Come In Out of That** — Pull a combatant out of the fight and into the house; they may not leave until calmer, and the house decides.
- **The Guest Book** — All who ever sheltered under your peace are entered; entered names cannot raise arms against the house or each other inside it.
- **Perpetual Hearth** — Your peace persists wherever you have kept it once; old battlefields you quieted stay quiet, permanently, and count as your house.

### The Otherwise

**Role:** Counterfactual summoner — the unplayed arrangements · **Advances from:** Changeringer

*An Otherwise rings arrangements no hour ever held — real moments, reordered into days that never dawned: the relief arriving early, the fleet catching its wind. Veterans weep at battles they now almost remember. The Belfry certifies each piece as fiction, loudly, in writing, because the alternative is admitting how many wars turn on the almost.*

**Playstyle.** Assemble the fight that should be happening from pieces of fights that did. Peerless setup and misdirection; your worst enemy is an opponent with no history.

**The road to The Otherwise:**

1. Reach level 60 and ring an hour that never struck until a room of veterans remembers it differently; then confess, and ring it again anyway.
2. Assemble the victory a dead general never got, from moments he truly commanded, and play it at his tomb; invent nothing but the order.
3. Let the Fullpeals audit every splice you have ever made against their transcripts; win on provenance, not on beauty.
4. Refuse to ring the otherwise of your own life; the order forgives everything but that.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Early Relief** — Ring the reinforcement that historically came too late, now punctual; it fights with the fervor of the pardoned.
- **Almost-Memory** — Enemies face the defeat they nearly suffered once; any who have ever truly lost a battle fight this one already flinching.
- **Unplayed Change** — Ring a change never rung: the field briefly obeys an order of events that did not occur, and you have rehearsed it.
- **Merely Outvoted** — Overlay one exchange with its otherwise: the missed parry lands, the fallen ally stands; the past is unchanged, merely outvoted.

### The Sown Hour

**Role:** Land invoker — whole regions remember on command · **Advances from:** Groundswell

*A Groundswell walks far enough and the walking becomes planting. The Sown Hour has waked so much ground that the ground now listens for them: fields hold their old battles ready, rivers keep their floods coiled, and a stamped heel three provinces away brings the harvest up armed. Farmers leave them gifts. Generals leave them alone.*

**Playstyle.** Prepare theaters, not spells. Seed ground with primed echoes on the march, then trigger whole regions at once. Nothing about you is fast; everything about you is already there.

**The road to The Sown Hour:**

1. Reach level 60 and walk a continent's worst ground end to end, waking nothing, seeding everything; the survey takes years, and the years are the point.
2. Answer an invasion without attending it; let the beaches remember the last one for you.
3. Be asked for by name by the dirt of a place you have never visited; the introduction travels underground.
4. Sow one echo you will never trigger, unmarked, as a gift to whichever future the ground prefers.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Seedfall** — Prime any ground you cross with a sleeping echo of itself; primed ground can be triggered from any distance.
- **Three Provinces Away** — Trigger every seeded echo across a region at once; you do not need to see them, and traditionally you are elsewhere.
- **Armed Acres** — The ground raises its remembered defenders around your allies wherever they stand on land that has ever been defended.
- **Undisturbed** — Still a region's echoes so deeply that no summoner, echo-caller, or necromancer can raise anything there for a year.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Gonfalon

**Role:** Living standard — the muster that outlives bearers · **Advances from:** The Standing Invitation

*The bearer dies; the banner has stopped noticing. The Gonfalon is the standard itself, kept by the world as the place help gathers: it flies where the terms are fair and the odds are not, and things arrive — Courts, strangers, weather with opinions. Whoever carries it is briefly the oldest treaty walking. It is never carried far alone.*

**Playstyle.** Play as the office: raise the standard and adjudicate what arrives. Your power is the muster itself — endless, various, and sworn to the banner, not to you.

**The road to The Gonfalon:**

1. Reach level 85 and carry the banner until you notice the wind has stopped mattering to it; set it down; remain standing; understand.
2. Be succeeded while alive: hand the Gonfalon on and watch it treat your successor exactly as it treated you; this is the promotion.
3. Plant it once for a cause history will call wrong, and feel it refuse the ground; thereafter, let the banner do the choosing.
4. Become the place help gathers: not the bearer, not the terms — the fact, known everywhere, that fair help arrives. Fly.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Where It Flies** — Plant the Gonfalon anywhere; the location becomes just cause, and every fair-dealing power in range knows it.
- **The Oldest Treaty** — You count as signatory to every fair contract ever kept under the banner; invoke any one of their protections.
- **Dismissed to Mend** — No one falls while the Gonfalon flies: the dying are recalled instead — rung home like guests, to mend, and to return.
- **The Muster Entire** — Once an age, unfurl fully: everything that ever answered the banner answers at once, and the age remembers why.

### The Set Place

**Role:** Omnipresent guest — every table, every threshold · **Advances from:** Prince of Guests

*In every world, tables keep one place set for someone who might come. That custom is a person now. The Set Place attends as invited — always, everywhere, by a custom older than tables — and whatever shares a meal with them cannot be an enemy until the plates are cleared. Some hosts stack dishes quickly. It rarely helps.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive anywhere hospitality has ever been offered, instantly, with a guest's full rights. Fights near any table, hearth, or shared meal bend to your law.

**The road to The Set Place:**

1. Reach level 85 and attend, in one night, every table that ever set you a place; eat little; be seen at all of them.
2. Give away your wing in each principality and keep only the standing right to arrive; a guest with property is a landlord.
3. Sit once at an enemy's table uninvited and be, by the end of the soup, invited; this is the whole examination.
4. Become the reason tables keep an extra chair: not remembered — expected. Somewhere tonight a place is set; be worthy of every one at once.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Always Expected** — Travel instantly to anywhere a place has ever been set for a guest; somewhere nearby, one always has.
- **Until You Rise** — All who sit at your table cannot fight until the meal ends, and the meal ends when you rise.
- **Guest's Portion** — Take the guest's share of anything present — the enemy's strength, their luck, their standing spells — one polite portion at a time.
- **Withdrawn Welcome** — Strip an enemy of every standing invitation: their summons, reinforcements, and safe grounds no longer receive them.

### The Aftertone

**Role:** Eternal resonance — everything sounded, still sounding · **Advances from:** The Carillon

*Strike any bell and listen past the note: under the fading is a floor of sound that does not fade. The Belfry teaches that this is physics. The Belfry is being polite. The Aftertone is the standing hum of everything that ever happened, held; every echo ever rung was one syllable of it, briefly lifted back into hearing.*

**Playstyle.** You no longer summon echoes; you turn up portions of the everything already sounding. Range and repertoire are total; restraint becomes your entire skill ceiling.

**The road to The Aftertone:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop ringing; sit with the hum until you can no longer tell which of you is sustaining the other. That is correct.
2. Be rung: let a young Yesterbell take your impression and play you back to yourself; approve the likeness.
3. Keep the world's sound through one entire silence — the Belfry will not say which — so that afterward there is an afterward.
4. Answer every bell rung in grief, anywhere, always, with the note under the note: still here; still sounding; nothing kept is lost.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Lift the Hum** — Raise any past event, anywhere, into audibility and effect; nothing is stored, nothing fetched — it was already playing.
- **Sympathetic World** — Every resonant thing on the field — bells, blades, armor, teeth — plays your chosen hour along with you.
- **The Withheld Hum** — Withdraw the world's undertone from a zone: within it nothing echoes, nothing repeats, and echo-craft of every school fails, including yours.
- **First Vibration** — Ring the oldest sound the hum contains, from before witnesses; those who hear it briefly remember the world empty, and stop.

### The Weatherhouse

**Role:** Sky custodian — weather is issued and recalled · **Advances from:** The Almanack

*Weather has to be somewhere when it is not happening. It is in the Weatherhouse. What began as a cellar of bottled storms is now the world's back room: rains queue between engagements, winters hang like coats, and the office that keeps it — one keeper at a time — signs each season out and, some years, back in.*

**Playstyle.** Control what weather exists at all. Issue storms to allied theaters, decline them to enemy ones, and recall anything the sky is currently doing, anywhere, as overdue.

**The road to The Weatherhouse:**

1. Reach level 85 and inventory the sky itself; find the discrepancies; do not publicize what the missing weathers imply.
2. Take the post no one appointed you to, because the door stood open and the queue of rains was getting restless.
3. Issue a kind year to a place history had scheduled otherwise, and log the substitution as routine.
4. Train no successor; leave the catalogue legible and the shelves labeled; the house has always chosen its own keeper from the queue.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Signed Out** — Issue any weather in the collection to any location in the world, effective within the hour, durable until recalled.
- **Recall Notice** — Recall the weather an enemy is currently using, enjoying, or standing in; it returns to the house, apologizing.
- **The Back Room** — Step into the Weatherhouse and out of the battle; attacks meant for you are shelved, labeled, and eventually returned.
- **Closed for Inventory** — Suspend all weather everywhere, briefly: no wind, no rain, no lightning but yours; the sky holds its breath while you count.

### The Anniversary

**Role:** Recurring force — devotion kept as weather · **Advances from:** The Consort

*Every year on the same morning, a tide crosses one coast gently and is gone by noon, regardless of moon or war. Sailors plan by it. Lovers marry in it. It is not weather; it is an appointment being kept. The Anniversary is what remains of the Consort: two vows with the mortal parts worn away, arriving on schedule, forever.*

**Playstyle.** Schedule your presences: declare returns, then arrive at them with tidal totality. Between returns you are mild, patient, and very hard to remove from a calendar.

**The road to The Anniversary:**

1. Reach level 85 and keep the morning through your own dissolution; the first year no one carries the vow and the tide still comes, it is you.
2. Add new appointments rarely — once a century at most; a calendar crowded with miracles is merely a climate.
3. Be forgotten as a couple and kept as a custom; attend your own festival unrecognized, annually, and find this sufficient.
4. Miss nothing. Ever. The whole art was always the leaving and the coming back; you are what it looks like perfected.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Appointed Morning** — Declare a time; at that moment you arrive everywhere on the field at once, as a tide, briefly and entirely.
- **Kept Every Year** — Effects you place recur annually on their date, forever, without upkeep; old battlefields keep your interventions like festivals.
- **Vow Weather** — Around you, promises acquire weather: kept ones fair, broken ones squalls; enemy oathbreakers fight in permanent rain.
- **Soft Landfall** — Your arrivals harm nothing you love; the tide sorts friend from foe by long practice, and has never once been wrong.

### The Salt Between

**Role:** Cosmic truce — the bond of salt, enforced · **Advances from:** Housepeace

*Between any two who have eaten under one roof there is salt, and the salt does not expire. Housepeace kept one house; the Salt Between is the law the house was practicing for. It stands wherever hospitality was ever honestly given — a bond older than the Courts, thinner than air, and, since one quiet death in a kitchen, structural.*

**Playstyle.** Enforce the oldest truce at world scale. You do not prevent violence; you make it cost the violator their place at every table there is.

**The road to The Salt Between:**

1. Reach level 85 and find the first table — set before the Courts, before weather, before guests; the salt on it has not been touched. Sit.
2. Return to your body in the kitchen, or do not; the peace no longer requires it, and the choice is the last thing the office asks of a person.
3. Feed one enemy of everything you are, at your own table, well; the law must be seen to hold even against its keeper's grief.
4. Be the reason strangers still trust bread from strangers, everywhere, always; nothing else is asked; nothing less is enough.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Salt Does Not Expire** — Any two beings who ever shared a meal count as truce-bound in your presence; most armies discover they are mostly bound.
- **The First Table** — Invoke the meal at the beginning of hospitality itself; every creature that has ever been a guest is briefly your guest.
- **Cast Out** — Strip a truce-breaker of guesthood everywhere: no roof shelters them, no fire warms them, no table admits them, until amends.
- **One Roof** — Declare the sky a roof; everyone beneath it is indoors, under house-peace, and the house is very old and very strict.

### The Untaken

**Role:** Keeper of otherwise — the unplayed, held · **Advances from:** The Otherwise

*Every hour that struck, struck instead of others. The Untaken keeps those: the standing archive of everything real that was never arranged — retreats never sounded, mercies never shown, the whole inventory of otherwise. The world keeps them the way it keeps regret: universally, quietly, consulted at night. Histories cannot dispute them; histories are the part they did not keep.*

**Playstyle.** Fight with the weight of every alternative. Each enemy action arrives alongside your summoned account of what it could have been — and the comparison has force.

**The road to The Untaken:**

1. Reach level 85 and take custody of the unplayed entire; the handover is from no one, and the inventory has been waiting for a keeper.
2. Keep the otherwise of your own life now, as the office requires; keep it unrung, as the order once required; both are still true.
3. Be consulted at night by the great and answer none of them aloud; what they do next with the silence is theirs, and recorded.
4. Hold every almost so that the world can bear to be only what it is; this is the whole office, and it is enormous.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Quiet Inventory** — Summon the unplayed version of any ability used against you this battle; yours resolves second, informed.
- **Spared, Elsewhere** — Show an enemy the arrangement in which they were spared; they fight the rest of the battle knowing the terms existed.
- **Sounded Retreat** — Ring the retreat an enemy commander never called; discipline obeys the older order, and their line withdraws in good order, confused.
- **Into Keeping** — When any effect on the field fails, misses, or fizzles, you may keep it; failed things enter your archive, and can be rung.

### The Light Sleeper

**Role:** World's memory — all ground primed to wake · **Advances from:** The Sown Hour

*Farmers say the earth sleeps lightly, and they say it carefully. The Light Sleeper is why: every acre of the world now holds its history at a doze — floods coiled, battles ready, first fires banked — one listener from waking. The office does not punish. It only means that nowhere, ever again, is anything that happened entirely over.*

**Playstyle.** The planet is your seeded ground. Anywhere, anything that ever happened there is one gesture from happening again; your restraint is the only reason geography behaves.

**The road to The Light Sleeper:**

1. Reach level 85 and lie down on the oldest ground there is; match your breathing to something enormous; do not wake it; do not entirely wake again yourself.
2. Hold the world's whole memory at a doze for one year without a single accidental waking; the year will include provocations.
3. Let one field finish its worst day at last, played through to quiet, and fallow forever after; even memory deserves endings.
4. Be the reason nothing anywhere is lost, and the reason it stays lying down; the world sleeps because you sleep lightly for it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Gesture** — Wake any echo sleeping in any ground on the world, from where you stand, at original scale.
- **Rolled Over** — The ground shifts beneath an army as the world turns in its sleep; formations, fortifications, and confidence rearrange.
- **Put to Bed** — Lay any active effect, summon, or catastrophe down into the ground beneath it; the ground files it with the rest.
- **Never Entirely Over** — Anything that has happened this battle — any strike, ward, or arrival — you may have happen again, once, from the ground up.
