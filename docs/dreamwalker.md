# 🌀 Dreamwalker

> *Sleep is a country, and someone must walk its roads.*

**Archetype:** Threshold

Nightly the world crosses, unknowing, into the one country every border shares. The Dreamwalker goes on purpose. Sleep is a place; places have roads; roads want walking. Some learn the soft country's geography and carry words no checkpoint can stop. Others keep its fauna, breaking and pasturing nightmares as drier lands keep hawks and herds. The dreaming is no omen, no afterlife — a country, and the Dreamwalker its honest traffic.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Dreamwalker

**Role:** Skirmisher-scout — lulls foes, slips between

*Some children never learn the difference between falling asleep and leaving. Most have it taught out of them. The rest wake with road-dust on their feet, are found sooner or later by the walking trade, and learn the first law of the soft country: it is a place, you are in it, and places can be crossed.*

**Playstyle.** Fight light and half-elsewhere; drowse the dangerous, slip through nearby sleep to reposition, and remember your sleeping body is the only door an enemy needs.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Fall asleep somewhere wrong — a battlefield, a birthday, a border — and wake on a road that was not there yesterday.
2. Walk the road instead of waking, and answer the stranger at the first crossing when they ask whose dream this is: no one's.
3. Learn to sleep in rain, in grief, and at noon, until the door opens the moment you knock.
4. Deliver one small true word to a sleeping friend and hear them repeat it at breakfast, wondering.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Practiced Drowse** — Drop into true sleep in three breaths, anywhere, and rise from it without grogginess or delay.
- **Pinch of Sand** — Flick dream-sand at a foe's eyes; their next action drags as sleep tugs the lids.
- **The Near Hem** — Sense every sleeper within a bowshot and the rough weather of what they dream.
- **Step Through Sleep** — Where someone nearby sleeps, slip through their dream and out again, emerging several paces from where you stood.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Sleepfarer

**Role:** Infiltrator-scout — long travel, soft entry · **Advances from:** Dreamwalker

*The trade rates a Sleepfarer by what they can cross: a river, a siege line, a quarantine. They travel the shallows of a thousand strangers' sleep, polite as any guest, and step out the far side of geography with the news still warm. Waking checkpoints have begun, uselessly, to inspect pillows.*

**Playstyle.** Go where walls say you cannot: enter through nearby sleep, scout the soft country's shallows, and come back carrying routes, weaknesses, and unbought truth.

**The road to Sleepfarer:**

1. Reach level 10 and cross your first sealed border asleep, carrying a word the checkpoint would have hanged you for.
2. Sleep in the same inn as a stranger and walk their dream from hearth to hedge without once being noticed.
3. Map the shallows of a village in one night: who dreams of the sea, who of fire, who of the door upstairs.
4. Return from the soft country with mud on your boots that no waking road could have put there.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Borrowed Bed** — Enter the dream of any sleeper you can see or touch, without waking or harming them.
- **Softcountry Stride** — Cross the shallows at the pace of a night's rest; leagues of waking country pass in an hour of walking.
- **Thin Hour** — Find the hour and place where waking and sleep sit closest, easing your whole party briefly across.
- **Guest Manners** — Dreams do not turn on you; their weather, monsters, and doors treat you as furniture until you act.

### Maregroom

**Role:** Beast handler — one leashed terror, harried foes · **Advances from:** Dreamwalker

*Every district has bad nights, and someone must muck them out. Maregrooms are called for the child who screams at moonrise and the garrison that has stopped sleeping; they net the mare at the bedside, halter it, and lead it away calm. The sleeper wakes lighter. What the groom leads home is now the groom's problem.*

**Playstyle.** Manage a stabled terror like a working animal; loose it to harry and horrify, recall it before it forgets whose leash it wears.

**The road to Maregroom:**

1. Reach level 10 and clear a haunted nursery alone, leading the mare out by dawn without waking the child.
2. Keep your first terror stabled a full season: fed little, worked often, and never once named.
3. Stand stable-watch through a screaming winter and learn which fears bite because they are cruel and which because they are cold.
4. Lose a mare you had grown fond of, and learn what the halter is for.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Bedside Net** — Net a feeding nightmare out of a sleeper's dream; the sleeper wakes rested and unaware.
- **Halter of Sighs** — Bind a caught terror with woven breath; it cannot flee, feed, or turn on you while haltered.
- **Loose the Mare** — Slip your terror at a foe; it rides them with dread, souring aim, nerve, and footing.
- **Stable Temper** — Read a terror's mood at a glance and settle it; your beasts recover fast and spook never.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Nightpost

**Role:** Mobile skirmisher — courier speed, strike and relocate · **Advances from:** Sleepfarer

*Couriers of the walking trade, sworn to the delivery and to nothing else. A Nightpost carries what cannot cross awake — treaties, confessions, a warning running ahead of an army — through the sleep of whoever lies between. Paid on arrival, never on departure. Kings distrust them precisely as much as kings use them, which is constantly.*

**Playstyle.** Move first and often; dart through sleeping ground to flank, deliver each blow like a sealed letter, and be three dreams away before the reply.

**The road to Nightpost:**

1. Reach level 25 and run the Long Round: nine cities, nine nights, nine words delivered, none read.
2. Outpace a purge; carry the proscription list to the names on it and arrive before the knives do.
3. Refuse your first fortune for the opening of one seal, and report the offer to nobody.
4. Wear out three pairs of boots on roads that do not exist.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sealed Word** — Carry a message none can read, coax, or cut out of you; it exists only at its address.
- **Relay of Beds** — Chain crossings between nearby sleepers to cross a battlefield in heartbeats, arriving behind whoever felt safest.
- **Outrun the Rider** — Once per night, declare a destination; nothing traveling by waking roads arrives there before you.
- **Cold Delivery** — Your first strike against an unaware foe lands with a whole journey's momentum behind it.

### Legate of the Shallows

**Role:** Support controller — parley fields, pacification · **Advances from:** Sleepfarer

*Diplomacy's quiet way around itself. A Legate arranges meetings that cannot exist: enemies asleep in their own camps, seated at one table in a borrowed dream. The Legate keeps the venue and the protocol — who may speak, what may follow the guests out — and bills both sides, which is traditional.*

**Playstyle.** Impose parley on chaos; lull hostilities, draw combatants briefly into the shallows to talk or falter, and reposition friend and foe like place-settings.

**The road to Legate of the Shallows:**

1. Reach level 25 and host your first impossible meeting: two men who have each paid for the other's funeral, sharing salt.
2. Raise a meeting-house from a stranger's borrowed comfort, with chairs enough for every grudge invited.
3. Hold protocol when a guest draws steel in your shallows, and let what happens to him be instructive.
4. Be paid by both sides, thanked by neither, and mentioned in no account of the peace.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Borrowed Table** — Pull willing or sleeping minds into a shared shallow dream where you set the room and the rules.
- **The Floor Is Given** — Name one speaker in the shallows; all others find tongue and limbs heavy while they interrupt.
- **Nothing Follows You Out** — Wounds, oaths, and workings taken inside your parley stay inside it unless you permit otherwise.
- **Seating Plan** — Rearrange everyone within your shallows; the meeting adjourns with each guest standing where you preferred them.

### Fewterer

**Role:** Beastmaster striker — leashed terrors slipped at quarry · **Advances from:** Maregroom

*Grooms turned hunters. A Fewterer keeps a brace of broken terrors leashed at the wrist and slips them at whatever the contract names: deserters, rogue mares, the occasional waking monster that has never yet been afraid. The pay is good, the kennel-work constant, and the leash-hand always cold.*

**Playstyle.** Hunt in tandem; slip your terrors to run down and unman the quarry, then close in yourself while its nerve is elsewhere.

**The road to Fewterer:**

1. Reach level 25 and break your first wild-caught terror to the leash without dulling its teeth.
2. Course a quarry across three nights' dreaming and take it at dawn, awake and too tired to be afraid.
3. Learn the kennel-law: feed them before yourself, walk them before you sleep, and never let them taste you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Slipped Brace** — Loose two leashed terrors at one quarry; they harry it from either side of sleep.
- **Cold Leash** — Your terrors return to heel from any distance, dragging a mouthful of the quarry's courage with them.
- **Hounded Sleepless** — Quarry your terrors have touched cannot hide, rest, or profit from sleep until you call off the hunt.

### Mareherd

**Role:** Flock controller — area dread, herd management · **Advances from:** Maregroom

*Where a groom keeps one beast, a Mareherd runs the valley's whole flock of fears: folding the small ones at dusk, thinning the biters, driving the herd off a town that cannot stand another bad week. Landholders pay them the way they pay against weather — grudgingly, and every year.*

**Playstyle.** Fight with the flock; blanket ground in grazing dread, cut one terror out to bite, and herd enemies toward where their courage is thinnest.

**The road to Mareherd:**

1. Reach level 25 and take a valley's contract: one year, every bedroom, no deaths by dreaming.
2. Cull your first man-eater in front of the flock, so the flock understands the terms of employment.
3. Drive a hundred small fears across a river of sleep without losing one to the deeps.
4. Winter the flock on your own sleep when the grazing runs out, and wake each morning having paid for it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Dusk Folding** — Gather every minor fear on the field into your fold; enemies lose their dreads, and you keep them.
- **Cut One Out** — Send a chosen terror from the flock at a single foe; it knows exactly which fear fits.
- **Drive the Herd** — Stampede the flock across an area; all caught in it are hazed, scattered, and moved your way.
- **Shepherd's Count** — Know the number, size, and appetite of every fear within a night's walk.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Causewright

**Role:** Battlefield engineer — lanes, footing, mass mobility · **Advances from:** Nightpost

*Half of what the Post split over, two centuries back. Causewrights hold that even a soft country owes its travelers footing, and pave accordingly: staked fords, raised causeways, waystones that keep their place from dream to dream. Any trained sleeper can walk a made road. Deepstrays call this farming. Causewrights note whose mail arrives.*

**Playstyle.** Lay road under the fight; allies on your ground move faster, stand surer, and withdraw in good order down ways no enemy can find.

**The road to Causewright:**

1. Reach level 40 and drive a causeway through the deeps that a frightened stranger could cross alone; then watch one do it.
2. Set a waystone in the dream of a city that hates your guild, and maintain it for a year of nights.
3. Survey the shallows of a coming war: where the routs will run, where the widows will walk. Pave accordingly.
4. Accept a Deepstray's apology when your road saves their delivery, without smiling.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Waystone** — Plant a stone that exists on both sides of sleep; allies can always find, and rally at, your stones.
- **Raised Causeway** — Firm ground unrolls ahead of your party — over mire, rubble, or panic — granting speed and certain footing.
- **Camber and Drain** — Hostile ground-effects run off your road like rainwater; your lanes cannot be cursed, mired, or frightened.
- **The Road Holds** — Once per battle, an ally's forced movement, fall, or rout simply does not happen while they stand on your road.

### Chatelain of the Hem

**Role:** Sanctuary warder — inviolable ground, guest-law · **Advances from:** Legate of the Shallows

*Keepers of the great peace-houses standing just past the hem of sleep. A Chatelain's law is short: under this roof, nothing wounds, nothing binds, nothing follows you home. Break it and you are put out — not from the house, from the dreaming. Snarehosts find the whole arrangement naive. Chatelains keep a room ready for them.*

**Playstyle.** Hold an inviolable house around your allies; harm must knock, guests answer to the salt, and those who force the door answer to the hem.

**The road to Chatelain of the Hem:**

1. Reach level 40 and hold the door of a peace-house while a war tries to come in; keep it a war outside.
2. Give the room ready to your worst enemy on a bad night, and charge the usual rate.
3. Put out a king. Readmit him when he apologizes to the scullery.
4. Learn every hinge, joist, and kindness of your house until it knows you asleep or waking.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Roof of the Hem** — Raise the house around your allies; attacks, curses, and terrors must knock, and you keep the door.
- **Guest-Salt** — Any who accept your salt cannot raise a hand against your other guests while the house stands.
- **Put Out** — Eject one offender from the dreaming; they wake alone, exhausted, and barred from sleep's country for days.
- **The Room Ready** — Once per battle, an ally struck down is instead found abed in your house, stable and sleeping.

### Cruelhood

**Role:** Beast tyrant — one apex terror, absolute command · **Advances from:** Fewterer

*The elder doctrine of the mews: a terror is tackle, and tackle gets no name. Cruelhoods break the great fears to hood and jess by being the worst thing in any room. Asked whether a broken terror loves them, they answer that the leash does not care. The name is no accusation; it is the tool on their belt.*

**Playstyle.** Field one apex terror as a living siege engine; it strikes, screens, and horrifies on command, and its obedience is never in question.

**The road to Cruelhood:**

1. Reach level 40 and break a named fear — one with a district to its reputation — without help, sleep, or sentiment.
2. Hood the thing that emptied Marrowdown in a single night, and walk it to heel through the survivors' camp.
3. Refuse to name it. Refuse twice. The second refusal is the one the order watches for.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Hood Comes Off** — Unhood your apex terror at a chosen foe; it attacks with perfect focus and cannot be diverted.
- **Jess-Law** — Your terror cannot be charmed, turned, stolen, or frightened; its will is not in the room.
- **Worst Thing in the Room** — Enemy beasts and terrors that watch you correct your own lose their nerve; yours has none left to lose.
- **Broken to the Fist** — Recall your terror from any distance or danger instantly; whatever held it keeps nothing.

### Drownsman

**Role:** Terror-executioner — anti-fear, anti-beast purges · **Advances from:** Mareherd

*The cull doctrine, plainly practiced. Drownsmen hold that the only safe night is an empty one: they run drowning-drives through infested sleep, thin the spring litters, and keep honest tallies of what a county's peace cost. Graziers call them famine-makers. Drownsmen answer that nobody they serve has ever been eaten.*

**Playstyle.** Purge the field; every fear, terror, and conjured horror is quarry, and each one destroyed leaves the night around you harder to haunt.

**The road to Drownsman:**

1. Reach level 40 and empty a market town's nights inside one month, leaving a tally the mayor can read.
2. Drown a litter you had the skill to break and sell, because the doctrine is not about you.
3. Walk a Grazier's pasture at lambing with your hands in your pockets; the feud has rules.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Drowning Fold** — Drag a nightmare or summoned horror under the surface of sleep; it does not come back up.
- **Vermin Tally** — Mark every fear-born and conjured thing on the field; your strikes against marked quarry cut double.
- **Empty Night** — Clear all dread, terror, and panic from an area; for a while, nothing frightening can enter it.
- **Cold Arithmetic** — Each quarry destroyed steadies you; fear finds no purchase on a Drownsman in the middle of a count.

### Deepstray

**Role:** Evasive striker — trackless, arrives from nowhere · **Advances from:** Nightpost

*The other half of the split. Deepstrays hold that a road through a living country is a leash on it: they deliver by drifting, never the same way twice, and unpick abandoned causeways like snares. Causewrights call them vagrants. Deepstrays reply that the mail arrives anyway, and that the country, notably, still lets them through.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive by being lost correctly; no route, no schedule, no tracks — strike from angles the country invents for you, and unravel the ways behind you.

**The road to Deepstray:**

1. Reach level 40 and deliver to a city the roads have lost, by losing yourself better than the roads did.
2. Unpick a dead causeway before something learns to wait beside it, and leave the waystones to the moss.
3. Cross the deeps without a heading and let the country hand you to your destination like a parcel.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Never Twice** — No path you take exists afterward; pursuit finds meadow, wall, or water where your road was.
- **Drifting Delivery** — Cross any distance in the dreaming by surrendering your heading; you arrive when the country decides, which is early.
- **Unpicked Road** — Unravel a stretch of made road or known ground; those standing on it are strewn where it wasn't.
- **Snare-Wise** — You cannot be ambushed along a way; anything waiting finds you already behind it.

### Snarehost

**Role:** Zone manipulator — hospitality as ambush · **Advances from:** Legate of the Shallows

*The Legates' other answer. Snarehosts observe that a dream is always somebody's house and neutrality a curtain somebody hung, so they hang it themselves: venues that negotiate, doors that favor, chairs that soften resolve, weather with a position on the agenda. Every Chatelain house, they note, has a best chair. Someone decides who sits in it.*

**Playstyle.** Prepare the ground like a dinner; enemies arrive into seating, sightlines, and moods you chose, and the trap is indistinguishable from courtesy.

**The road to Snarehost:**

1. Reach level 40 and host a summit where the terrain does the negotiating and your client never speaks.
2. Seat a delegation of seven so that their alliance dissolves by dessert, blamelessly.
3. Attend a Chatelain's peace-house as a courteous guest, break no law, and leave the doubt behind you like a coat.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Prepared Venue** — Battles you begin happen in a room you dressed; you place cover, exits, and mood before anything is drawn.
- **The Best Chair** — One enemy is seated — comfortable, flattered, and unable to act while anyone is speaking to them.
- **Weather With a Position** — The venue's weather takes your side; rain, gloom, and warmth apply themselves to your agenda.
- **Doors That Favor** — Exits open where you prefer; an enemy's retreat delivers them to the smaller room you had in mind.

### Quarrysworn

**Role:** Pack skirmisher — willing terrors, shared hunt · **Advances from:** Fewterer

*The mews' heresy. A Quarrysworn takes no net and no hood: they walk into the deeps, let a terror hunt them, and survive it until the hunt curdles into something older, and it follows them out. Their beasts fly free and come back. Cruelhoods say a loved terror is spoiled. Quarrysworn answer that fear knows how to slip a jess.*

**Playstyle.** Run with the pack; your terrors fight unleashed and choose their moments, and they grow stronger for every scar you take in front of them.

**The road to Quarrysworn:**

1. Reach level 40 and go into the deeps unarmed; be hunted by something with a name, and come out walking.
2. Sleep three nights in its territory afterward, uneaten. On the fourth it will lie down within reach. Do not touch it yet.
3. Take a wound meant for it, in front of it. This cannot be staged; it will know.
4. Never call it. The day it comes anyway, you are Quarrysworn.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Survived You** — Terrors that have hunted you obey no one else and cannot be turned against you by any craft.
- **Free Cast** — Your beasts range unleashed and strike at their own judgment; their instincts outrank your orders, correctly.
- **Taste Given Freely** — Feed a terror your own fear before battle; when you falter, it returns the strength doubled.
- **The Pack Answers** — When you fall below half your strength, the nearest terror you have earned arrives, invited or not.

### Grazier of the Small Hours

**Role:** Attrition controller — dosed dread, managed pasture · **Advances from:** Mareherd

*The feeding doctrine. A Grazier runs fear on rotation, dosing towns with small survivable nightmares the way fields are dosed with ash — because fear unfed does not die. It fasts, and its appetite comes back famine-sized. Drownsmen call this farming wolves. Graziers point to the quiet counties, and wait.*

**Playstyle.** Bleed the enemy by installment; graze small dreads across their line, harvest the panic they compound into, and starve nothing you may need later.

**The road to Grazier of the Small Hours:**

1. Reach level 40 and take a county on rotation: every town dreams badly once a season, mildly, on schedule.
2. Explain the schedule to a mayor who demands perfect sleep, be refused, and leave the rotation running anyway.
3. Fold a famine-sized fear back down to lamb-size by feeding it properly for a year.
4. Attend a Drownsman's tally-reading in polite silence, and leave a lamb on the doorstep.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Measured Dose** — Afflict a foe with a small survivable dread each round; the doses compound quietly until the whole herd arrives at once.
- **Rotation** — Move your grazing dreads between foes at will; fresh pasture makes them fatter, faster.
- **Fear Unfed** — An enemy who resists your dreads is saving them up; when one finally lands, it lands famine-sized.
- **Small Hours** — Your flock feeds strongest after midnight; night battles begin with your herds already fat.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Prior Road

**Role:** Strategic mover — armies travel by sleep · **Advances from:** Causewright

*The histories quarrel politely over whether the last great Causewright built the road or uncovered it: the causeway somehow always beneath the others, the one survey-lines bend to agree with. Where the Prior Road walks, ways align — fords shallow, gates stand unbarred, and cities pave, not knowing why, along lines first walked asleep.*

**Playstyle.** Carry whole companies down your road through sleep; arrival, retreat, and supply are yours to schedule, and the ground keeps your itinerary.

**The road to The Prior Road:**

1. Reach level 60 and walk a road so old it has no first map, and recognize your own staking-marks on it.
2. Move a beaten army home asleep in one night, and let the enemy chronicle call it weather.
3. Refuse to pave the way to somewhere that should stay far.

**Abilities granted:**

- **March of the Sleeping Column** — Lead any number of sleepers — an army encamped — down your road; they wake where you decided, rested.
- **Alignment** — Roads, bridges, and doors within a day's walk agree with your route; travel against it sours.
- **The Road Was Here First** — Walls, wards, and fortifications standing across your road count as already breached; you walk through where it ran.
- **Milestones of the Deep** — Your waystones stand in every sleeper's dreaming; any ally asleep anywhere may step onto your road.

### The Hundred Beds

**Role:** Living sanctuary — war-stopping hospitality · **Advances from:** Chatelain of the Hem

*Past the hem stands an inn with a bed for every quarrel, and no waking door; you arrive by lying down angry somewhere else. Wars end there: opposing marshals keep waking rested, fed, and too embarrassed to order the assault. Two empires date their peace to a breakfast. Whether the Hundred Beds is keeper or house, guests never ask.*

**Playstyle.** Make the battle your house; combatants inside your walls become guests whether they meant to or not, and guest-law does the fighting.

**The road to The Hundred Beds:**

1. Reach level 60 and host a war entire — both camps, asleep, under one roof — and serve breakfast until it is a former war.
2. Refuse a god a room, politely, on grounds of prior conduct.
3. Wake one morning unsure whether you built the inn or the inn grew you, and decline to check.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Come In Out of the War** — Extend the house over the field; all within are guests, and violence indoors must argue with the walls first.
- **The Long Table** — Seat enemies at your table; while any bread remains on it, none may rise in anger.
- **Every Room Ready** — Fallen allies anywhere wake abed in the inn, tended, their wounds left at the door.

### The Empty Mews

**Role:** Terror-marshal — the night itself flies for you · **Advances from:** Cruelhood

*There is a mews at the roof of the deeps, and it stands empty, which is the whole horror of it: everything the Empty Mews ever broke is aloft. Always. Fears across seven kingdoms fly patrol-patterns and stoop to a whistle heard once a generation. Historians dispute whether the falconer still lives. The fears do not dispute anything.*

**Playstyle.** Command the sky of sleep; call broken terrors down anywhere night falls, stack their stoops, and keep nothing on the glove.

**The road to The Empty Mews:**

1. Reach level 60 and empty your mews for the last time; from now on, recall is a courtesy you extend, not a need.
2. Break a terror no Fewterer could course — one that hunted the hunters — and add its beat to the patrol.
3. Hang the hoods on the wall in size order. You will not need them. Oil them anyway; doctrine is doctrine.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Everything Is Aloft** — Your broken terrors patrol wherever darkness or sleep exists; call any of them down, each round.
- **The Stoop** — A called terror dives one target, arriving with the weight of every night it has patrolled.
- **Heard Once** — Whistle: every fear on the field — yours, wild, or enemy-kept — checks its flight and looks to you.

### The Quiet County

**Role:** Purge incarnate — dread cannot exist near you · **Advances from:** Drownsman

*There is a county where no bad dream has crossed a sill in thirty years, and the Drownsman who emptied it is now spoken of as a place. Historians argue the cost; the county sleeps through the argument. No one asks what the Quiet County carries instead.*

**Playstyle.** Extinguish; where you stand, fear-craft fails, horrors drown, and the night goes empty for miles — including, sometimes, of things your allies wanted kept.

**The road to The Quiet County:**

1. Reach level 60 and empty your county — however long it takes, however small the doors, however often the spring litters return.
2. Decline the statue. Suggest, instead, better shutters.
3. Keep the tallies in your head from now on. Some numbers should not be teachable.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Thirty Years' Practice** — Destroy any single fear, terror, or conjured horror outright; there is no size exception anymore.
- **County Line** — Draw the line; within it no dread can be born, summoned, loosed, or fed. It stays drawn.
- **What It Carries Instead** — Take a fear meant for an ally into yourself; it is never seen again, and neither is your comment.

### The Drift

**Role:** Untrackable assassin — arrives by no route · **Advances from:** Deepstray

*Everyone knows the drift — the sideways slide at the hem, when the room seems to move. The trade has stopped pretending it is not somebody. The Drift is never where it was; what it carries arrives by no route, at addresses the maps gave up on. Causewrights concede, privately, that some country only the lost can cross.*

**Playstyle.** Be unfindable and inevitable; approach by no route, strike from the deep side of things, and leave pursuit holding a map of nowhere.

**The road to The Drift:**

1. Reach level 60 and deliver to an address that exists on no map of either country, and let the letter be your proof.
2. Be hunted by both halves of the Post at once, be found by neither, and deliver both their mails that night.
3. Forget your own way home, and find you no longer mind; the country keeps what travels light.

**Abilities granted:**

- **No Route Taken** — Arrive anywhere you have never been; wards, walls, and watchers cover every approach except yours.
- **Heading Unmade** — Enemies who pursue or aim at you lose their bearing; shots wander, and trackers find their own prints.
- **The Current Keeps It** — Drop a foe into the drift; they surface moments later, elsewhere, facing the wrong way, missing their advantage.

### The Parlor

**Role:** Arch-manipulator — the room that negotiates · **Advances from:** Snarehost

*The age's great settlements were all reached in one parlor, though no two of its doors stand in the same country. The chairs are comfortable; the fire agrees with you; the tea is, somehow, your favorite. Guests leave having conceded what they arrived sworn to keep — grateful, and planning to return. Everyone walks in eventually.*

**Playstyle.** Fight in a room you own; enemies act under your hospitality, their choices gently pre-arranged, their exits subject to negotiation.

**The road to The Parlor:**

1. Reach level 60 and settle a succession war over tea, with neither claimant noticing the settlement until home.
2. Furnish the parlor with one honest chair, for your own use, and never let a guest sit in it.
3. Keep the kettle warm for whatever war arrives next; something always does.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Will You Walk In** — Draw a foe into the parlor mid-battle; they return a moment later having agreed to something.
- **The Furniture Votes** — Terrain, cover, and footing within your influence favor whichever side you are currently fondest of.
- **Your Favorite** — Offer a foe the exact comfort they are starving for; refusing costs them will, accepting costs more.

### The Followed

**Role:** Pack legend — ancient terrors fight unbid · **Advances from:** Quarrysworn

*Leashed nothing; earned everything. The Followed walks with a following nobody summoned: terrors old enough to have names in dead tongues, keeping to heel out of something older than fear. Towns that see them coming argue whether to bar the gates or bake. The wise do both.*

**Playstyle.** Give no orders and need none; your following guards, hunts, and avenges by its own ancient judgment, and it judges quickly.

**The road to The Followed:**

1. Reach level 60 and come up out of the deeps followed by something no living Fewterer could name. Do not comment.
2. Let three towns call your arrival an omen, and disappoint all three; you are only walking.
3. Sleep once, deeply, in the middle of them all, and wake to find they kept the watch in shifts.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Old Company** — Your earned terrors are always somewhere near; each battle, those best suited step forward unasked.
- **Older Than Fear** — Allies near you cannot be frightened; your following has standards about who does the frightening.
- **It Will Know** — Any hand raised against you is marked; somewhere in the following, something takes it personally.
- **Walk Unhunted** — Nothing in either country hunts you anymore; predators recognize the company you keep.

### The Wandering Fold

**Role:** Continental grazier — fear arrives as weather · **Advances from:** Grazier of the Small Hours

*Each autumn a tide of small bad dreams crosses the continent, gentle as drizzle, gone by winter. Scholars file it under weather. Shepherds know a drive when they sleep through one. The Wandering Fold walks ahead of it — one keeper, ten thousand fears on rotation, fat, docile, and spoken for.*

**Playstyle.** Deploy dread at landscape scale; your flock arrives as weather, grazes a battle into exhaustion, and moves on before anything starves.

**The road to The Wandering Fold:**

1. Reach level 60 and take the continent on rotation: no town twice in a season, no fear unfed in a year.
2. Walk the drive through a war so that both armies oversleep an appointment with a massacre.
3. Refuse to sell the flock to a king who wants a weapon, and explain about famine until he stops wanting it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Autumn Drive** — Bring the migration through the battlefield; every enemy is grazed small each round, gently, comprehensively.
- **Folded In** — Wild and enemy fears entering the field are folded into the flock; your rotation has room.
- **Gentle as Drizzle** — Your dreads cannot be noticed landing; victims count the cost only once it is prohibitive.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Way Through

**Role:** World-road — every dreamt way is one way · **Advances from:** The Prior Road

*Every dream keeps ground underfoot and somewhere to walk. Sleepers assume this; it is not a law of nature. It is an office, and it is held. All dreamt roads are one road now, and everyone on any of them — the lost, the fleeing, the six-year-old crossing a nightmare — is on the Way Through, and known there.*

**Playstyle.** Be the way itself; anywhere anyone dreams you are already underfoot — arrive, carry, and deny passage as the ground pleases.

**The road to The Way Through:**

1. Reach level 85 and be walked on by the whole world for a year without once making yourself felt.
2. Keep the ground steady under a frightened child's nightmare, and under her mother's, the same hour, countries apart.
3. Let the Post argue for a century over which half of it you vindicate, and bear both their traffic without comment.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Already Underfoot** — Step out of any dream's road anywhere in the world; you were, technically, always there.
- **Known Traffic** — Learn who walks any dreamt road and whether they run toward or from; the fleeing may be delivered to you.
- **No Way for You** — Withdraw the road from one enemy everywhere; in dreams they stand in trackless dark, and waking journeys misgive.
- **Carried the Whole Way** — Any number of sleepers anywhere may be brought to one place by morning; the ground moves under them.

### The Spare Room

**Role:** Sanctuary absolute — the room always there · **Advances from:** The Hundred Beds

*Every house anyone dreams has one room more than it should. It stands at the top of the stairs, unlocked, and nothing hostile has ever crossed its sill. Hunted sleepers find it without being told. Architects, counting doors in the morning, have learned to stop at the number they built. The room is always made up for company.*

**Playstyle.** Be shelter itself; anyone, anywhere, on either side of sleep can be brought inside you, and inside has never once been breached.

**The road to The Spare Room:**

1. Reach level 85 and be the room for a stranger you dislike, exactly as warmly; the office does not ask.
2. Shelter the assassin and the target on the same night, and let them leave by different mornings.
3. Wait, made up and empty, through the good years when no one is hunted, without once wishing for guests.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Top of the Stairs** — Any creature fleeing may find your door; inside, no harm, hunger, or fear applies. You decide when morning comes.
- **Never Once** — The room cannot be breached, scried, burned, or believed away; sieges against it age poorly.
- **Made Up for Company** — Guests leave healed, rested, and quietly braver; the linens have opinions about despair.

### The Recall

**Role:** End of nightmares — all fear answers home · **Advances from:** The Empty Mews

*Nightmares end. Sleepers assume the terror finishes with them; the truth is that something whistles it home. Every fear in either country — wild, kept, newborn, ancient — is subject to the Recall, knows it, and files the knowledge under obedience. The fist they return to is an office now. The glove is always worn.*

**Playstyle.** Command fear categorically; enemy terrors are yours mid-flight, panics disperse on a word, and anything frightening can be summoned to the glove.

**The road to The Recall:**

1. Reach level 85 and call home a fear that has run wild since before the trade had a name, and hang its hood.
2. Whistle once over a burning city and grant ten thousand sleepers one unhaunted night; take nothing for it.
3. Keep the mews empty and the hoods oiled; doctrine, at this altitude, is gentleness that keeps its gloves on.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Come to Heel** — End any fear, nightmare, or terror within your notice; it returns to the glove, spent and civil.
- **Mid-Flight** — Terrors loosed by others are redirected in the air; they complete their stoop for you instead.
- **The Glove Is Worn** — You cannot be frightened; whatever tries learns it has a falconer now.
- **General Recall** — Once per battle, whistle every fear in the region home; for one breath, all sleep nearby goes unhaunted.

### The Lull

**Role:** The empty night — nothing hunts at all · **Advances from:** The Quiet County

*Now and then a night comes when nothing hunts, anywhere: no mare crosses a sill, no terror stoops, every tally in the trade reads zero. Farmers call it good weather. The trade knows an office when it sleeps through one. The Lull is that emptiness held open on purpose — rest made a standing appointment the world cannot cancel.*

**Playstyle.** Impose the empty night; within your lull nothing hostile can act from fear, dark, or dream, and the stillness itself restores your own.

**The road to The Lull:**

1. Reach level 85 and give the world one whole empty night — every sill, every camp, every cell — and take no credit.
2. Hold the lull over a besieged city so that, for one night, being surrounded is only geography.
3. Keep the gate of the empty night yourself; what was drowned to buy it stays drowned, and stays yours.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Reads Zero** — Within your lull, no fear, dread, or nightmare can exist, arrive, or be made. None.
- **Standing Appointment** — Declare tomorrow night empty across a region; all who sleep it heal double and wake unhauntable for a day.
- **Unplanted** — Ground you walk cannot grow ambushes, workings, or horrors for a season; it is resting.

### The Uncharted

**Role:** Horizon of sleep — the unmapped, keeping itself · **Advances from:** The Drift

*The soft country cannot be finished. Surveys agree to within a night's walk of the edge, then stop agreeing; the blank quarters stay blank not for want of walkers but because something keeps them, the way a forest is kept. The Uncharted is why there is always a farther. Mapmakers of sleep leave their margins open now, on advice.*

**Playstyle.** Fight from off the map; you and yours act from country no chart contains, where nothing can follow, predict, or prepare.

**The road to The Uncharted:**

1. Reach level 85 and walk off every map of the dreaming at once, including the one you kept in your boot.
2. Keep one small blank for a young Sleepfarer to find, and be far away when they do, and be glad.
3. Decline, forever, to say what is out there — not because it is terrible, but because it is not yours to finish.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Off the Edge** — You and chosen allies occupy unmapped country; targeting, scrying, and pursuit fail for lack of a where.
- **The Receding Farther** — Enemies advancing on you find the distance has opinions; approaches lengthen until intent is abandoned.
- **Unsurveyed** — Declare ground unsurveyed; effects that require knowing the terrain — traps, wards, ambushes — never knew it.
- **Somewhere New** — Once per battle, relocate the fight entire to country no one, including you, has ever seen.

### The Rooftree

**Role:** Owner of indoors — all sleep one house · **Advances from:** The Parlor

*There is no outdoors in sleep. The moors and seas and skies of the dreaming are rooms — spacious ones, handsomely kept — and rooms imply a ridge-beam, and every dreamt roof, followed up far enough, meets the same one. The Parlor's settlements never named a host. The Rooftree never needed naming; you were always under it.*

**Playstyle.** Fight indoors, always; walls arrive where you want them, doors open where you decide, and eviction is a question only your enemies face.

**The road to The Rooftree:**

1. Reach level 85 and open a door in the last place everyone was certain was outdoors, and step in, not out.
2. Host the age's peace without once appearing in it, as before, as always.
3. Leave the Spare Room alone. It was there before the beam. This is the house's one humility; now it is yours.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Under This Roof** — Declare one behavior forbidden indoors — flight, spellcraft, the harming of your guests — and indoors is everywhere.
- **Rearrangement** — Walls, stairs, and doors of the field move to your preference each round; guests adapt or are adapted.
- **Shown the Door** — Remove an enemy from the house; there being no outdoors, where they go is a question they now face.
- **Always Inside** — You cannot be exiled, exposed, or caught in the open; every somewhere is a room under your beam.

### The Truce of Teeth

**Role:** Fear's covenant — one throat forsworn · **Advances from:** The Followed

*Every terror is born already knowing one throat it will not take. None can say whose; the knowledge simply has teeth of its own. This is the Truce of Teeth, older than the hood and the halter both, and it is not a law. It is a person, walking, and the agreement leaks generously onto whoever walks beside them.*

**Playstyle.** Walk untouchable through fear's whole kingdom; terrors guard you as the truce itself, those beside you inherit its shade, and breaking it is the one thing that frightens fear.

**The road to The Truce of Teeth:**

1. Reach level 85 and walk the deeps end to end unarmed, and be escorted, and pretend not to notice the escort.
2. Spend a season among the unbroken as neither hunter, prey, nor groom; the truce wants a witness, not a hand.
3. Never learn why it is you. The truce holds because nobody knows, and you are nobody's exception but its own.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Forsworn Throat** — No fear, terror, or nightmare can harm you; the prohibition is congenital and not negotiable.
- **Shade of the Truce** — Allies beside you are covered; terrors treat them as kin of the throat and hunt elsewhere.
- **What Frightens Fear** — Threaten the truce's breaking; every terror present will do anything asked to prevent it.
- **Born to the Glove** — Terrors newly made or summoned arrive already yours in the one way that matters; their maker learns this late.

### The Pastoral

**Role:** Season of fear — dread arrives survivable, forever · **Advances from:** The Wandering Fold

*Fear arrives in season now — small in spring, grazed close by harvest, resting after — and this is not nature. It is husbandry so old and total it reads as nature. The Pastoral is the arrangement itself: the office by which dread feeds without famine, forever, on schedule. Scholars still file the autumn dreams under weather. The filing is finally correct.*

**Playstyle.** Set the season of the battle; meter every heart's dread — enemies dosed toward collapse, allies kept fallow — and let the arrangement do the fighting.

**The road to The Pastoral:**

1. Reach level 85 and take all sleep everywhere onto the rotation, and let no one notice anything but easier winters.
2. Send the Quiet County a flock small enough to live with, and receive, in time, an empty tally-book as thanks.
3. Answer the child who asks why there are nightmares at all with the truth — so that there are never worse — then go check the fences.

**Abilities granted:**

- **In Season** — Declare the season; your enemies' fears mature on your schedule, all at once if you like.
- **Kept Fallow** — Allies' hearts lie fallow; nothing frightening takes root in them while your season stands.
- **The Arrangement** — Any fear anywhere feeding outside your rotation is trespassing; it is folded, fattened, and put to work.
- **Famine Withheld** — Once per battle, show one enemy the unhusbanded alternative — every fear at once, famine-sized, briefly.
