# 🕳️ Umbralist

> *Darkness is not absence; it is material, and it remembers.*

**Archetype:** Threshold

Most people mistake darkness for absence — the room with the lamp out, the space behind the door. The Umbralist knows better: shadow is a material, older than light and more patient, and in trained hands it holds edges, bears weight, and remembers shapes. Rogues hide in the dark. An Umbralist puts it on the anvil and asks what it wants to be.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Umbralist

**Role:** Hybrid conjurer — improvised shadow tools and arms

*Every city has a few: apprentices who noticed that the dark behind the woodpile pushed back. A first-rank Umbralist can chill a candle's shadow until it holds a shape — a hook, a cup, a clumsy knife. Neighbors call it a trick of the light. It is the opposite of that.*

**Playstyle.** Shape small tools and weapons from ambient shadow. Stronger near darkness, weaker at noon; carry your material with you and spend it deliberately.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Sit with an unlit room until you stop calling it empty.
2. Fold your own shadow three times against the grain; if it creases, you have the hands for this.
3. Carry water in a cup of shadow from the well to the door. Spill nothing. Tell no one.
4. Find a teacher who works after dark, or an after-dark that teaches; the guild insists there is a difference.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Coldshaping** — Press ambient shadow between your palms until it takes a simple shape; the object lasts one minute or one use.
- **Lampwane** — Dim every light source nearby by half, gathering the difference as raw shadow-stuff in your hands.
- **Duskhide** — Harden the shadow you stand in into a hide across your skin, absorbing one blow before it flakes away.
- **First Heft** — Give a shadow weight enough to lean on; a plank, a step, a brace that holds until sunrise.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Duskwright

**Role:** Melee conjurer — solid-shadow arms and barriers · **Advances from:** Umbralist

*Duskwrights take the material seriously: they apprentice to it, keep its hours, learn what it will and will not bear. Their benches hold tongs no light has touched. Village smiths resent them slightly — a Duskwright's blade is folded from the unlit and never needs quenching, only night.*

**Playstyle.** Forge weapons, shields, and simple structures mid-fight. Everything you make is real while it lasts; managing durability and darkness is the whole craft.

**The road to Duskwright:**

1. Reach level 10 and forge one object of solid shadow that survives a full noon in the open.
2. Serve a season at the guild bench, working only by the light you have failed to remove.
3. Break your first three blades learning that shadow folded against its grain holds grudges.
4. Present a masterpiece the examiners can hold: weight, edge, and no apologies.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Fold the Unlit** — Hammer ambient darkness into a one-handed weapon of your choosing; it keeps its edge until dawn touches it.
- **Shadow Tongs** — Extend a gripping hand of solid shadow that holds a foe, a beam, or a falling ally at ten paces.
- **Blackglass Buckler** — Raise a small shield of hardened dusk; it shatters after three blocks into blinding slivers of dark.
- **Journeyman's Gloom** — Store a pocket of worked shadow in your coat; draw it later as raw material where light leaves none.

### Penumbrant

**Role:** Skirmish controller — passage and thinned presence · **Advances from:** Umbralist

*Penumbrants stop treating shadow as stock and start treating it as a door left open. They learn to lean into darkness the way swimmers lean into rivers — first a hand, then the wrist, then whole minutes spent as something thin against a wall. Their families notice the candles burning brighter.*

**Playstyle.** Slip through connected darkness and thin your body against harm. High mobility, low durability in light; you control range by controlling where dark is.

**The road to Penumbrant:**

1. Reach level 10 and put your arm into a shadow to the shoulder, then bring it back still yours.
2. Spend a night as the stain beside the wardrobe in a room where someone is dying. Touch nothing. Learn what the dark is for.
3. Cross a guarded bridge by the shadow under it, and leave the guards their pride.
4. Return fully. The guild examines the edges of you for fraying, and passes what it can still count.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Through the Unlit** — Enter one shadow and leave by another within sight; the dark between counts you as itself.
- **Go Thin** — Flatten your body to a hand's depth of dusk for three seconds; blades pass through what is barely there.
- **Second Silhouette** — Peel your shadow upright to stand watch or take a blow meant for you; it reforms at your heel by dusk.
- **Dimmet Sense** — Know the position of every shadow deep enough to admit you within a hundred paces.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Gloamsmith

**Role:** Weapon conjurer — arms for self and allies · **Advances from:** Duskwright

*Gloamsmiths make arms and nothing else, and make them the way cathedrals were built: to specification, to outlast doubt. A Gloamsmith's sword weighs what conviction weighs; their spears fly true in pitch blackness because the dark recognizes its own work. Armies quarter them well back and ask nicely.*

**Playstyle.** Forge and hurl weapons of hardened gloam, and arm allies with edges of dark. Damage scales with ambient darkness and with your patience between forgings.

**The road to Gloamsmith:**

1. Reach level 25 and forge a blade of gloam that a stranger, blindfolded, cannot tell from steel until it sings.
2. Arm a hopeless garrison with shadow and stay through the siege to maintain the edges.
3. Refuse a king's commission once, politely, to learn what your work is worth.
4. Quench nothing for a year. The dark tempers by patience, and so will you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Gloamsteel** — Temper shadow into a weapon a full rank finer than any you could carry here; it dissolves if it leaves your sight.
- **Nail of Night** — Drive a spike of compacted dark through armor; the wound stays cold and refuses light-based healing.
- **Rack of Dusk** — Stand seven shadow-weapons upright in the earth around you; allies who draw one fight with your craft for a minute.
- **Evening Whet** — Sharpen an existing blade against raw shadow; it cuts through shields for ten strikes, leaving unlit grooves.

### Murkmason

**Role:** Battlefield architect — walls, spans, and cover · **Advances from:** Duskwright

*Murkmasons build. Bridges over gorges no quarry could span, stairs up sheer cliff-dark, barracks pitched in an hour from the shade of a standing army. Their guild-mark is a keystone with no arch, because the arch is night itself. Engineers hate them; sappers hate them considerably more.*

**Playstyle.** Raise cover, spans, and chokepoints from massed shadow. You win by making the ground argue for your side; direct damage is someone else's trade.

**The road to Murkmason:**

1. Reach level 25 and bridge the Grey Gorge at midnight with shadow; walk across last, carrying the lantern.
2. Build a poorhouse of night in a city that outlawed you, and leave it standing when they repeal.
3. Hold your wall against a sapper company until they submit their resignation to the dark.
4. Set one keystone of pure unlit in a daylight arch, where the guild can check your nerve.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Mortarless** — Raise a wall of set shadow along a line you walk; it stands until dawn or demolition, whichever is ruder.
- **Gloomspan** — Throw a bridge of solid dusk across any gap you can see both sides of; it bears a column of cavalry.
- **Bastion Shade** — Pitch an instant redoubt around allies: crenellated, roofed, and dark enough inside to rearm your whole craft.
- **Undershoring** — Brace a failing structure — or a collapsing morale line — with columns of night that refuse, on principle, to buckle.

### Nightswimmer

**Role:** Mobile flanker — repositions self and allies · **Advances from:** Penumbrant

*Nightswimmers hold that all darkness is one water: the shadow of a cradle touches the dark under mountains, and a trained body can swim the distance between. They surface where they are needed, dripping dusk. Harbor towns keep a lamp unlit at the pier for them, the way others keep one burning.*

**Playstyle.** Dive through connected shadow to flank, carry allies short distances, and drag enemies under. Range grows with darkness; noon beaches you.

**The road to Nightswimmer:**

1. Reach level 25 and swim from your own shadow to the far bank of the Sorrows in one held breath of dark.
2. Carry a stranger through the dark without losing them; the guild counts arrivals, not departures.
3. Map a shadow-road no one has survived, and post the depths where swimmers pray.
4. Learn where not to swim. Something older keeps the deepest dark, and courtesy is survival.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Deep Stroke** — Swim the dark between two shadows within a hundred paces, surfacing with momentum your first blow spends.
- **Drag Under** — Pull one enemy waist-deep into their own shadow for four seconds; they can shout, and usually do.
- **Wake of Dusk** — Leave a trailing current of dark behind you; allies who follow it move as fast as rumor.
- **Surface Tension** — The film between light and dark hardens at your word: nothing else enters or leaves shadow nearby for six seconds.

### Shadeworn

**Role:** Evasion tank — thinned, persistent, hard to hold · **Advances from:** Penumbrant

*The Shadeworn have been worn thin by passage, and made the thinness a discipline. Daylight finds less of them each year to press on: scars gone grey, footprints optional, a silhouette that arrives a little before they do. They stand in shield-lines as gaps that bite. Spears find wall instead.*

**Playstyle.** Hold ground by being barely there: attacks slide through your thinned self while your unlit hands stay solid enough to answer.

**The road to Shadeworn:**

1. Reach level 25 and let a firing line volley through you at dusk; sign the report they file in disbelief.
2. Fast from light one full winter, taking sun only through curtains, until your shadow forgets which of you is which.
3. Hold a breach as the thin place in it. What passes through you must be permitted first.
4. Keep one solid thing — a hand, a habit, a name — and defend it. The rank is for those who stop thinning on purpose.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Worn Thin** — Passive: a portion of every blow passes through you and hits nothing; the portion grows as light dims.
- **Solid on One Side** — Your strikes are matter and their answers are not, for five seconds; duelists file complaints posthumously.
- **Become the Stain** — Flatten against any surface as a stain; you cannot act or be harmed, and you hear everything the wall does.
- **Grey Increment** — Each wound you ignore thins you further: harder to hit, lighter of step, one shade closer to the wall.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Nightanvil

**Role:** Arsenal artillery — persistent conjured armaments · **Advances from:** Gloamsmith

*The Nightanvils number their works and expect them found in barrows a thousand years on. Shadow, they hold, is the one metal that never rusts — fix it once, correctly, and you have armed the future. Their vault-forges open only at new moon. Each smith's masterwork is entered against their name, permanently.*

**Playstyle.** Build a persistent arsenal across engagements: standing weapons, mounted engines, armed allies. Slow to start, compounding forever; your old work fights beside your new.

**The road to Nightanvil:**

1. Reach level 40 and forge a work you will never see finished cooling; the vault tempers by decades.
2. Recover Work Nine from the barrow that stole it, and apologize to the blade for the wait.
3. Arm your own funeral guard in advance; the order considers this ordinary prudence.
4. Debate a Quickshade before the assembled forges and do not raise your voice; permanence does not hurry.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Fixed in the Dark** — Your conjured weapons no longer expire; each battlefield keeps what you forged there, loyal to your banner.
- **Moonless Temper** — Quench a weapon in true moonless night; it gains an edge that ignores armor forged in daylight.
- **Ballista of the Vault** — Assemble a siege engine of set shadow over ten seconds; it fires spears of night until dismantled by dawn or axes.
- **Numbered Work** — Speak the catalogue number of anything you ever forged; it flies to your hand from wherever the years shelved it.

### Evening Spanwright

**Role:** Logistics support — spans, roads, mass movement · **Advances from:** Murkmason

*The Causewrights build with borrowed shade — the honest dark that towers and mountains lend at evening — and return every plank of it by dawn. Their spans have carried harvests, armies, and one entire fleeing city. The lending, they insist, is the craft: darkness trusts those who give it back.*

**Playstyle.** Move whole formations: causeways, ramps, gates of dusk. Your works expire at dawn by design, so schedule the war accordingly.

**The road to Evening Spanwright:**

1. Reach level 40 and carry a fleeing city across a strait on borrowed shade, and return every shadow unbruised by dawn.
2. Note what you borrow and from whom; a Causewright who forgets a mountain's name builds no more.
3. Build one span for no one, somewhere remote and beautiful, and let dawn take it unseen; the guild calls this the true examination.
4. Refuse to build a bridge meant to burn; the dark is a lender, not an accomplice.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Borrowed Span** — Lift a mountain's evening shadow into a causeway; it bears any weight that intends to cross, and no weight that intends to hold it.
- **Dusk Gate** — Stand two shadows upright as posts; for a minute, entering one is leaving the other, luggage included.
- **Return by Dawn** — Dismiss all your standing works at once; enemies on them are returned to the ground, from their current altitude.
- **Evening Freight** — Sink supplies into a shadow at dusk and appoint the shadow where they surface; quartermasters weep with relief.

### Nightporter

**Role:** Mass transporter — carries companies through darkness · **Advances from:** Nightswimmer

*Nightporters carry: refugees, regiments, grain in famine years, once a hospital with its lamps still lit. The one dark is their warehouse and every shadow a loading door. They are sworn to deliver whatever they lift, which is why they lift so carefully. Bandits avoid harming them; everyone eventually needs porting.*

**Playstyle.** Reposition allies at company scale through connected shadow. You are the supply line, the retreat, and the flanking maneuver; guard your own candle accordingly.

**The road to Nightporter:**

1. Reach level 40 and carry a hundred strangers through the one dark in a single night, and count the same hundred out.
2. Deliver to an address that no longer exists; the dark remembers where it was.
3. Refuse to port a weapon aimed at those you carried; the oath has teeth, and they are yours.
4. Meet what keeps the deepest dark, and be found polite; the guild has no advice beyond this.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Loading Dark** — Open a shadow as a freight door; allies and cargo step through to any dark you have personally stood in.
- **Handed Through** — Pass one ally through the dark mid-blow; the blow arrives punctually at where they no longer are.
- **Convoy of Dusk** — Chain up to twenty travelers behind you through the deep dark; none may let go, and none ever has.
- **Night Freight** — Once per night, carry something impossible — a wall, a ship, a wounded giant — one shadow closer to where it must be.

### Evenstain

**Role:** Elusive attrition — flattened presence, creeping harm · **Advances from:** Shadeworn

*Evenstains perfected the wall. They live flattened by preference — a discoloration in the plaster, a long shadow with no owner on file — and armies break on rooms that hate them. Where an Evenstain has soaked in, iron dulls, torches gutter, and courage develops a draft. Scrubbing, famously, spreads them.*

**Playstyle.** Soak into surfaces and let the field sicken around you: auras of dulling, guttering, and dread. Nearly unkillable, never fast; patience is the weapon.

**The road to Evenstain:**

1. Reach level 40 and hold a fortress alone by soaking into it; accept the garrison's surrender through the walls.
2. Be painted over, plastered, and consecrated against, and remain; the order accepts the parish's complaint as your credential.
3. Spread across a battlefield by dusk and be its weather by midnight.
4. Contract to a single drop once a year, to prove you still can; vanity is for the solid.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Soak In** — Merge with floor, wall, or standing dark across a wide area; while soaked, you are terrain, and terrain is angry.
- **Dull the Edges** — Every weapon inside your stain loses its temper — the metallurgical kind first, the other kind shortly after.
- **Drink the Torchlight** — Torches, lanterns, and lesser suns within the stain dim to embers, feeding your spread.
- **Setting Stain** — Enemies who linger in your area carry a patch of you when they leave, dulled and dimming, until true noon scours it.

### Quickshade

**Role:** Reactive duelist — improvised arms each instant · **Advances from:** Gloamsmith

*Quickshades hold that a fixed shadow is a stuffed hawk: accurate, and dead. Shadow lives in the moment of casting, so the weapon must be born mid-stroke — blade for this parry, hammer for this door, gone before gratitude. They own nothing, sign nothing, and beat Nightanvils in every duel either order admits to.*

**Playstyle.** Conjure the exactly right weapon each beat and discard it spent. Enormous versatility, nothing persistent; your power resets to zero and doesn't mind.

**The road to Quickshade:**

1. Reach level 40 and empty your hands for good; the order's initiation confiscates everything, including sentiment.
2. Win a smiths' fair with a blade that no longer exists by the judging.
3. Fight one battle armed only with what each second offers, and want for nothing.
4. Hold a dying friend with hands and no hilt in them, and learn why the order keeps nothing that can be lost.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Born Drawn** — Every attack you make is a new weapon, shaped mid-swing to whatever the target's guard forgot to expect.
- **The Correct Tool** — Instantly counter a weapon by conjuring its one bad match-up; the dark has opinions about every armory.
- **Unburdened** — You move faster for each thing you are not carrying; a true Quickshade blurs.
- **Let Go** — Release a conjured weapon mid-arc to burst into night; blinding, brief, and impossible to parry twice.

### Pitchmason

**Role:** Fortress controller — permanent bastions of first dark · **Advances from:** Murkmason

*Pitchmasons kept the guild name and abandoned the guild manners. They quarry the First Dark — the unlit that was never cast by anything, older than dawn — and what they build with it does not come down. Causewrights call them tomb-makers. Pitchmasons reply that someone must build for the nights with no morning after.*

**Playstyle.** Erect permanent fortifications of never-cast dark: keeps, ring-walls, cells. Immensely durable, utterly immobile; you win sieges by having already won them.

**The road to Pitchmason:**

1. Reach level 40 and descend to where the dark was never cast; quarry one block and carry it up the long stair alone.
2. Build a keep with no door and live in it a year; visitors will find the lesson instructive.
3. Let a Causewright inspect your walls and say tomb aloud; thank them, and point out that it is empty.
4. Stand your masterwork against dawn itself: if sunrise moves one shadow of it, begin again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Quarry** — Cut a block of the dark from before dawn existed; structures built of it ignore sunlight, sappers, and centuries.
- **Ringwall Pitch** — Raise a circling wall of set pitch-dark around a position; it has no gate, which the order considers a feature.
- **Undervault** — Open a cell of solid dark beneath one enemy; it closes over them gently and opens for no argument but yours.
- **Nightfooting** — Found your works on the dark under the world; nothing built on it can be undermined, by shovel or by doubt.

### Gloamhasp

**Role:** Anti-passage warden — seals shadow against traffic · **Advances from:** Nightswimmer

*The Gloamhasps hold that every passage wounds the dark and every wound lets something look through. They shut what swimmers open: hasping shadows closed, standing watch inside the medium itself, dragging trespassers out by the collar regardless of errand. Nightporters call them jailers of a country nobody owns. Hasps reply: exactly.*

**Playstyle.** Deny movement: seal shadows, cancel teleports and shadow-passage, and hold the dark as guarded ground. You are the wall inside the medium.

**The road to Gloamhasp:**

1. Reach level 40 and seal one city's every shadow for a night; let the morning count what failed to arrive.
2. Catch a Nightporter mid-carry and escort them out with their cargo intact; the rivalry has manners.
3. Stand watch inside the dark for a season and log what pressed against the far side; submit the log sealed.
4. Close one passage forever. You will know which one when it asks you not to.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Hasp the Dark** — Seal every shadow in a wide radius against entry and exit; the seals hold until dawn or your death.
- **Collar the Swimmer** — Reach into any shadow-passage in progress and haul the traveler out at your feet, itinerary cancelled.
- **Stitched Shut** — Sew two shadows together so neither admits anyone; the thread is visible only to those it disappoints.
- **Inward Bolt** — Bolt yourself inside the medium; while you keep watch there, nothing else may so much as thin.

### Nightbrace

**Role:** Immovable tank — dense shadow, load-bearing bulk · **Advances from:** Shadeworn

*The Caryatids took the opposite lesson: if shadow bears weight, become the weight it bears. They pack their unlit selves denser than basalt — person-shaped night that floors complain about — and stand where collapse is scheduled. Temples hire them as pillars during earthquakes. Evenstains call them furniture. The furniture outlasts the argument.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor the line as compacted night: taunt, absorb, and hold up whatever is falling — ceilings, gates, morale. Slowest thing on the field, and the last.

**The road to Nightbrace:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand under a temple roof through the earthquake; when it ends, be the reason it didn't.
2. Gain a hundredweight of night each season for a year, and give none of it back.
3. Be mistaken for statuary by an occupying army, and choose the moment to disagree.
4. Hold something no one asked you to hold, forever. The order's founder is still under the mountain, and the mountain is grateful.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Compaction** — Pack your shadow-self denser each second you stand still; damage reduction and weight climb without ceiling.
- **Take the Roof** — Catch any collapsing structure or descending blow on your shoulders; it stays caught until you decide otherwise.
- **Basalt Habit** — You cannot be moved, thrown, or knocked down while you can see something worth holding up.
- **Let It All Down** — Release every stone of gathered weight in one stamp; the field staggers, and you are briefly, dangerously light.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Nightforge

**Role:** Mythic armorer — arsenal at army scale · **Advances from:** Nightanvil

*Histories agree the Nightforge exists and agree on little else. An armory with a pulse; a smith who is also the smithy; the place all shadow goes to be given purpose. Kingdoms that found one wintering in a barn have retroactively declared the barn a citadel. Its works turn up centuries early.*

**Playstyle.** Arm everything: allies, militias, the ground itself. Your body is a working forge; standing near you upgrades steel and resolve alike.

**The road to The Nightforge:**

1. Reach level 60 and take the vault-forge into yourself: every numbered work, every bench, the dark between them. Inventory hereafter is introspection.
2. Arm a war on both sides with work so fine the war is called off for appraisal.
3. Forge one thing shadow was never meant to hold — a bell's note, a promise — and shelve it unnumbered.
4. Let the guild stop naming you smith. Smithies are places; be one.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Open Forge** — Allies who touch you draw a masterwork of night suited exactly to their need, discovered rather than requested.
- **All Night Ringing** — Your forge works while you fight: every ten seconds, a finished weapon rises from your shadow and picks an ally.
- **Arm the Field** — Edge every shadow on the battlefield; enemy footing becomes a matter for surgeons.
- **Centuries Early** — Draw a legendary work you have not forged yet; the future files no objection it can enforce.

### The Evening Road

**Role:** Strategic mobility — army-scale roads of dusk · **Advances from:** Evening Spanwright

*Somewhere between sunset and true dark there is a road, and histories cannot agree where it has been. Down it, granaries reach famines and reinforcements arrive the night before the histories say they could have. It lends itself only whole: take the Evening Road, and every traveler on it that night travels with you.*

**Playstyle.** Redeploy armies nightly across any distance. Your presence is infrastructure; battles are won by who you deliver, rested, where they were impossible.

**The road to The Evening Road:**

1. Reach level 60 and lay a road from the war to the harvest and back, and let both arrive in time.
2. Carry a beaten army home by dusk-roads so gently that its ballads survive; defeat travels, too.
3. Be declared impossible by three royal surveys and keep your schedule.
4. Return the last shadow you ever borrowed better than lent: a cooler evening, earlier stars.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Nightly Alignment** — Each dusk, declare where the Road runs; any friendly force that marches into shadow marches out at your destination.
- **Waystation Dark** — Plant rest-houses along your span; allies passing through shed exhaustion, wounds, and up to one regret.
- **The Road Rises** — Lift your span above ambush height; what happens on the Road is between the Road and the sky.
- **Wrong Exit** — Enemy columns that force their way onto your dark exit where you prefer, which is rarely where they were going.

### The Open Dark

**Role:** Rescue support — extraction anywhere night touches · **Advances from:** Nightporter

*While an Open Dark lives, no massacre is airtight. Cellars breathe. Locked holds arrive empty at port, their cargo walking free three kingdoms away with bread in hand. Histories dispute whether it is one person or a relay of them; the rescued dispute nothing, and light candles facing away from the window.*

**Playstyle.** Extract anyone from anywhere the dark reaches, at growing scale. Weak in a stand-up fight; the fight standing up is your cue to leave with everyone.

**The road to The Open Dark:**

1. Reach level 60 and empty a prison the size of a province in one night; alphabetize nothing, free everything.
2. Be in two rescues at once and let the theologians divide you; they need the exercise.
3. Leave every dark you pass through wider than you found it.
4. Teach the dark to do it without you once; retirement is a rumor you will deserve.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Nowhere Is Sealed** — Any dark large enough for a hand is a gate you may operate from either side.
- **Exodus Stroke** — Swim an entire crowd through the one dark in a single held breath; head-counts match, always.
- **The Bread Rule** — Everyone you extract arrives fed, shod, and unpursued for a day; the dark provisions its guests.
- **Kept Open** — Designate one shadow permanently passable to a person you name; heirs inherit the privilege, which has started dynasties.

### The Unlit

**Role:** Untargetable attrition — presence without substance · **Advances from:** Evenstain

*Thinning has a far shore, and the Unlit have waded past it. What remains is authored absence: a figure present in every under-lit room at once, thin as the difference between shut eyes and dark. Histories dispute whether the Unlit persist or merely recur. Rooms they favor stay favored for centuries.*

**Playstyle.** Exist as ambient dark: unhittable, everywhere shade is, wearing enemies down by increments they only notice in aggregate. Light is your one appointment.

**The road to The Unlit:**

1. Reach level 60 and be nowhere in particular for a year without missing an engagement.
2. Attend your own memorial as its poor lighting; leave before the eulogy improves.
3. Thin until the dark stops noticing where you end, then keep the one solid habit you swore at twenty-five.
4. Answer, when the wall is asked whether it is you: sometimes.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ubiquity of Shade** — Occupy all shadow in the region simultaneously; area attacks hit a fraction of you, which forgives nothing.
- **Increment** — Each second an enemy spends in any shade, they are slightly less: slower, dimmer, more agreeable.
- **Absence of Address** — Targeted effects require a location; you have stopped keeping one.
- **Recur** — If destroyed, you happen again at the next nightfall, minus one memory you volunteer.

### The Eclipse

**Role:** Burst conjurer — total armament from nothing · **Advances from:** Quickshade

*Night falls gradually, except where it doesn't. The Eclipse is the exception with a name: a person who arrives as a dusk with no evening before it, armed in the instant with everything the moment requires. Chroniclers dispute each sighting, since witnesses report only that it was bright, and then it was decided.*

**Playstyle.** Do nothing, then everything: no sustained damage, only complete instants in which you are every weapon at once. Timing is the entire class.

**The road to The Eclipse:**

1. Reach level 60 and fall on a battle as night falls on a candle: once, entirely, and without preamble.
2. Be three sightings in three provinces in one hour, and let the chroniclers ruin each other.
3. Decline to exist between engagements; the order's roll lists you as pending.
4. Arrive, once, before you were needed instead of when — and learn to apologize for the only mistake you make.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Noon Interrupted** — Drop instant midnight over the field for six seconds; within it, your improvisation has no upper bound.
- **Everything at Once** — For one second, conjure simultaneously every weapon the fight has made you imagine; each finds its own argument.
- **No Evening Before** — Your arrivals cannot be foreseen by scrying, scheduling, or instinct; even prophecy files you under weather.
- **Decided** — The engagement's loudest threat is disarmed, unhorsed, and seated, in that order, faster than the eye files grievances.

### The Sunless Keep

**Role:** Living fortress — bastion of the first dark · **Advances from:** Pitchmason

*Sieges have a word for the wall that does not fall, and the word has an address. The Sunless Keep is a Pitchmason who finished the last wall from inside: a fortress with a temper, quarried entirely from the First Dark. Three empires list it as terrain. Refugees list it as mercy with battlements.*

**Playstyle.** Be the objective: garrison allies inside your dark and break assaults on walls that predate dawn. You relocate rarely, and always overnight.

**The road to The Sunless Keep:**

1. Reach level 60 and mortar yourself into your masterwork as its final block; the guild sends flowers and a chisel, as tradition requires.
2. Outlast a siege so long the besiegers' descendants apply for citizenship; grant it.
3. Shelter something the whole daylight world wants dead, and win the correspondence.
4. Grow one new wing per century, and never explain the floor plan.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Let In** — Your walls admit the hunted from any shadow they flee through; their pursuers meet the masonry's other opinion.
- **Curtain of First Dark** — Your outer wall predates light; projectiles, spells, and dawns break on it in that order of embarrassment.
- **Sortie of Pillars** — March your walls outward course by course until the siege discovers it is indoors, where the rules are yours.
- **No Morning Inside** — Within you it is always the hour you choose; garrisons rest, wounds wait, and stores never spoil.

### The Shut Night

**Role:** Absolute denial — nothing crosses the dark · **Advances from:** Gloamhasp

*Some nights nothing moves — no couriers, no armies, no things between walls — and veterans check the calendar for a Shut Night's passage. The office is disputed by every history and respected by every smuggler: one warden, sworn inward, against whom the whole traffic of darkness files and fails. The dark sleeps soundly those years.*

**Playstyle.** Lock the entire medium: no shadow-travel, no summons through dark, no reinforcements after dusk. You barely fight; you make the night a held breath.

**The road to The Shut Night:**

1. Reach level 60 and hold a continent's darkness shut for a year of nights; every history will blame the weather, correctly.
2. Refuse an emperor's convoy and an orphan's escape in the same hour, and stay whole; the office is a door that does not choose.
3. Learn each thing that waits outside by the pattern of its patience; name none of them aloud.
4. Open, once, for one reason you will keep to yourself, and shut again before the histories notice; even the office blinks.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Night Is Shut** — Declare darkness closed across the region; every passage, gate, and summoning that routes through shadow is refused.
- **One Warden Wide** — You occupy the whole medium at once; anything entering the dark anywhere finds you already there, unamused.
- **Evicted Whole** — Expel every foreign presence from the dark simultaneously; they surface where they entered, however long ago that was.
- **The Far Side Waits** — The things beyond the dark cannot press through while you hold; they wait, and you feel them waiting.

### The Black Plinth

**Role:** Immovable anchor — the field's fixed point · **Advances from:** Nightbrace

*Every age has a weight it cannot set down, and hires accordingly. The Black Plinth is compaction ended in myth: a figure of night dense enough to anchor bridges, treaties, and the occasional mountain that had plans. Armies break formation around it like water. Nothing it has agreed to hold has ever fallen.*

**Playstyle.** Be the fulcrum: absolute immovability, gravitational threat, and the power to declare things held. The war bends around where you decided to stand.

**The road to The Black Plinth:**

1. Reach level 60 and relieve the founder under the mountain; carry their watch until another Caryatid grows patient enough.
2. Anchor a peace treaty by standing on it — literally, in the square, for a decade.
3. Let sculptors finish you; wear the pigeons with grace.
4. Decline to move for a god. Cite precedent.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Declared Held** — Name a structure, line, or promise held; it cannot break while you stand, and you are still standing.
- **Nightmass** — Your density bends the fight: projectiles curve to you, charges arrive exhausted, and orbits form among the reckless.
- **Underbear** — Slide beneath any falling thing — citadel, glacier, dynasty — and receive it; setting it back is a separate negotiation.
- **The Field Parts** — Enemy formations part around you and lose each other; cohesion is for battlefields without a Plinth.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Tempered Night

**Role:** Cosmic armorer — night itself bears your edge · **Advances from:** The Nightforge

*Smiths temper metal; something must temper the dark, or it would arrive soft. The Tempered Night is that office: since its founding, every shadow has held its shape, every night has borne weight somewhere, and winter dark has come down harder than summer's. Smiths' guilds keep a cold anvil for it, faced away from the hearth.*

**Playstyle.** Work at world-scale: the night is your stock, dawn your deadline, and every dark thing anywhere sharpens when you attend to your trade.

**The road to The Tempered Night:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the apprenticeship the dark offers you in return: it has been watching your hands for sixty years.
2. Temper one whole night so finely that no one anywhere dies of the dark in it; file it as a masterpiece, unnumbered.
3. Reforge the world's oldest shadow where it has worn thin, and do not sign the work.
4. When smiths swear by the dark between hammer-falls, be what the oath lands on.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Universal Temper** — Every shadow on the field is your finished work: edged, load-bearing, and loyal to your side of the argument.
- **Dawn Deadline** — Whatever you forge before sunrise is permanent; the sun has learned not to inspect your bench.
- **Winter Issue** — Arm an entire front from one long night; the equipment is standard, the standard is yours.
- **The Cold Anvil** — Strike the night itself once; everything forged of shadow anywhere rings, and its bearers know who made them.

### The Shade at Noon

**Role:** World infrastructure — shelter-shade as standing mercy · **Advances from:** The Evening Road

*The Causewrights' lending, perfected past personhood: an office that answers for every shade that shelters anyone. The cool under the fig tree, the awning that holds through the riot, the cellar dark that hides the hunted — all of it is one span now, maintained. Deserts negotiate with it. Noon has never won.*

**Playstyle.** Protect at planetary scale: every shadow allies touch is your fortification, your road, and your infirmary. You are terrain now — the merciful kind.

**The road to The Shade at Noon:**

1. Reach level 85 and lend yourself whole: become the shade of everything at once, and return yourself each dawn, on time, forever.
2. Hold the awnings of a burning city until everyone under them is elsewhere.
3. Shade one traveler across the Glass Waste personally; offices need the exercise.
4. Outlast the sun's one recorded attempt to set forever, and talk it back up; shade without noon is just night, and you have colleagues for that.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Span Now** — All shade everywhere is a single structure you maintain; damage to any of it may be referred to the sun.
- **Cool Standing** — Allies in any shade cannot overheat, starve, or despair; the office considers all three the same failure.
- **Awning Law** — Declare one shadow inviolable; violence beneath it simply fails to occur, and has since before you held the office.
- **Noon Repealed** — Cancel daylight's advantages across a battlefield; the sun retains ceremonial duties.

### The Night Entire

**Role:** Universal passage — all darkness is one place · **Advances from:** The Open Dark

*The Open Dark opened it all the way. The Night Entire is the standing fact that darkness is one country with one weather and no interior borders — an office, a geography, and a kindness, depending on which history is consulted. Every shadow is its capital. The rescued of ten centuries hold dual citizenship.*

**Playstyle.** Treat every dark point as adjacent: deploy, extract, and reinforce anywhere instantly. Wars against you happen wherever you prefer, in whatever order.

**The road to The Night Entire:**

1. Reach level 85 and dissolve the last border inside darkness: the one between where you are and where you are needed.
2. Hold a census of the dark and lose count on purpose; countries that know their size defend it.
3. Grant asylum to the shadows of things that no longer exist; they have nowhere else.
4. Be the reason no night anywhere is foreign; travelers will never thank you, feeling merely at home. Accept this.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Country** — All shadow is one location you administer; you and your allies are always, technically, already there.
- **Border Abolition** — Enemy zones of control cannot partition darkness; their perimeters hold light and little else.
- **Citizenry of the Saved** — Anyone ever rescued through the dark, descendants included, may step to your side when called; gratitude at that scale is an army.
- **Capital Everywhere** — Fight in every shadow at once when you must; distance has resigned its commission.

### The Halflight

**Role:** Ambient sovereign — the gradient between lit and dark · **Advances from:** The Unlit

*No light ends at an edge; it fails politely, through a margin of grey. That margin is an office now. The Halflight is the Unlit spread thinner than recurrence — present wherever light gives out, which is everywhere, eventually. Painters curse it. Dying men report it kind. Both reviews are kept on file.*

**Playstyle.** Exist in every gradient at once. You cannot be excluded by any light or found by any dark; attrition without address, mercy without witness.

**The road to The Halflight:**

1. Reach level 85 and thin past recurrence into constancy: stop happening, and simply obtain.
2. Be the last thing ten thousand candles do; be gentle each time.
3. Sit for every portrait painted at dusk; sign none of them.
4. When the first light asks where its edges went, keep them; edges were always yours.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Where Light Gives Out** — You are present in the falloff of every light source; wards must be perfect to exclude you, and none are.
- **Polite Failure** — Dim every hostile light by exactly the amount its bearer will not notice; repeat until relevant.
- **Kind to the Fading** — Allies at death's edge slip into your grey and are held there, beyond harm, until help or morning.
- **Neither Nor** — You are neither lit nor dark; abilities that target either category have filed you, incorrectly, under both.

### Nightfall

**Role:** Recurring event — dusk arrives as a combatant · **Advances from:** The Eclipse

*Somewhere along the line, the office and the evening merged. Nightfall is now performed: each dusk, somewhere, a figure lowers the dark over the world like a smith laying down a finished blade, differently every time. Astronomers model it as rotation. The order keeps the truth, and the truth keeps the schedule.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive as the day's end: one entrance per engagement, absolute and improvised. Everything before your arrival is prologue and knows it.

**The road to Nightfall:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the evening shift permanently; there is no other applicant, and there never was.
2. Bring down one nightfall so gentle a sleeping child is the only witness; bring down one so total a war is misplaced. Alternate.
3. Miss no dusk in a century, and let the one dawn you attend remain unexplained.
4. When they ask what you were before you were evening, answer honestly: brighter.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Day Ends Here** — Bring dusk to the engagement regardless of hour; every advantage of light is adjourned.
- **Performed Fresh** — No two of your nightfalls share a weakness; counters prepared against your last arrival find the repertoire has moved on.
- **Prologue's End** — On arrival, cancel every effect staged before you came; the fight restarts at the interesting part.
- **Early Tonight** — Once a season, night falls the moment you are needed; the sun apologizes to the astronomers.

### The First Dark

**Role:** Primordial bastion — the dark beneath everything · **Advances from:** The Sunless Keep

*The quarry and the mason have concluded their long argument by merging. The First Dark — the unlit that preceded light — is now also its own final fortress, and somewhere in it the Keep that walked in is load-bearing. Everything ever built rests, eventually, on dark that was never cast. It holds. That is the whole doctrine: it holds.*

**Playstyle.** Defend from beneath existence: foundations, deep cellars, the underside of the world are all interior to you. Sieges against you besiege geology.

**The road to The First Dark:**

1. Reach level 85 and open your gates onto the dark beneath the world, and find your own oldest wall already standing there, expecting you.
2. Bear the weight of everything built by everyone, and mention it to no one.
3. Refuse the light's first and only request: to see what you are keeping down there.
4. Hold. The rest of the doctrine was commentary.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Founded On You** — Any structure allies hold is revealed to rest on your dark; it gains your walls' patience and your doctrine's stubbornness.
- **Older Than Light** — Attacks of radiance, dawn, and revelation fail against you on grounds of seniority.
- **The Deep Interior** — Swallow a battlefield's underside; enemy earthworks, mines, and cellars open into you, and you are not a corridor.
- **It Holds** — Once per age, something that must not fall — a city, a sky, a last hope — simply does not.

### The Curfew

**Role:** Cosmic seal — the law that closes the night · **Advances from:** The Shut Night

*In every town the bell rings dark, and the practice remembers why. The Curfew is the law the Shut Night became: the nightly closing of the world's one dark against what presses on its far side. It has no text, only observance. The bell-ropes of ten thousand towns are its signature, renewed each evening.*

**Playstyle.** Enforce closure at world scale: end incursions, lock the medium, and hold the far side back. Your presence is the difference between night and breach.

**The road to The Curfew:**

1. Reach level 85 and replace your oath with a ringing: when every dusk bell in the world sounds, that is you, arriving.
2. Keep the far side out for a century in which nobody learns there is a far side; the office grades on ignorance.
3. Forgive the one passage you once opened; laws that remember being people last longer.
4. Teach the bells to ring without ropes on the night you are busiest; delegation is survival now.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Rings Dark** — Toll the unheard bell; every passage through shadow worldwide closes mid-step, travelers deposited at the nearest honest ground.
- **Observance** — While any creature anywhere keeps your curfew — shutters a window, bars a door at dusk — you cannot be destroyed.
- **The Far Side Kept** — What waits beyond darkness is held to its side of the skin; breaches close at the rate of your attention.
- **Last Bell** — Once, ever: close the dark entirely, to everything, including yourself; the histories disagree about whether this has already happened.

### The Nadir

**Role:** World anchor — the point everything rests on · **Advances from:** The Black Plinth

*Astronomers define the nadir as the point beneath all feet, and assume it is a direction. It is a posting. The Black Plinth that never resurfaced holds the world's underside the way a keystone holds an arch — from below, in the dark, without relief. Plumb-lines point to it. So, at the end, does everything else.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor existence: absolute stability radiating upward through everything standing. What you hold cannot fall; what you drop was warned.

**The road to The Nadir:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the posting beneath the world; the Plinth you relieved has somewhere to be, and has waited politely.
2. Hold everything for one full turning of the sky without once being thanked; the job is the monument.
3. Learn what the world rests on rested on before you; do not tell the philosophers, who are happier arguing.
4. Be down. Be what down means. When anything falls anywhere, be where it was always going.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Beneath All Feet** — You support whatever you choose worldwide; chosen structures, lines, and causes cannot collapse while you keep the posting.
- **Plumb Truth** — All falling redirects to you; avalanches, sieges, and thrown gods arrive at your shoulders, pre-sorted.
- **Without Relief** — You do not tire, waver, or rotate out; the concept of shift-change breaks against you and apologizes.
- **Let One Thing Fall** — Withdraw your support from a single thing; gravity remembers its old enthusiasm.
