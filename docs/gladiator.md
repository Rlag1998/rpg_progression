# ⛓️ Gladiator

> *The pit keeps count; the crowd keeps gods.*

**Archetype:** Might

Nobody chooses the pit. It buys its fighters, sentences them, or accepts the rare fool who volunteers, and it teaches them all the same double lesson: survival is arithmetic — reach, footing, the cost of every swing — and the crowd is a second opponent, one that can be won. Some gladiators fight the thing in front of them. The great ones fight the ten thousand watching.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Gladiator

**Role:** Melee striker — momentum and showmanship

*Sold, sentenced, or foolish enough to volunteer — the pit takes all kinds and issues them the same sand. A new gladiator owns nothing; even the sword is counted back at the gate. The first lesson is not swordwork. It is that ten thousand strangers are watching, and that this can be used.*

**Playstyle.** Fight loud. Showy strikes and taken risks feed Acclaim, and Acclaim feeds you; a quiet, careful bout is a losing one.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Enter by any of the three doors — sold, sentenced, or volunteered — and understand that the sand does not care which.
2. Survive your first bout, and hear one stranger in the stands say your name to another.
3. Learn the gate's arithmetic: the sword is counted out and counted back, and you are a smaller entry in the same tally.
4. Bleed once where everyone can see, and notice what the roar does to the pain.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Face the Stands** — Deal 10% more damage while any creature other than your target can see you.
- **First Blood Flourish** — Drawing first blood in an encounter grants Acclaim and 15% attack speed for 8 seconds.
- **Sand in the Eyes** — Kick grit into a foe's face, blinding them for 3 seconds; the crowd approves of dirty fighting done crisply.
- **Counted Steel** — You take no penalty fighting with borrowed, broken, or improvised weapons; the pit rarely issues better.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Crowdheart

**Role:** Acclaim striker — crowd-fed surges · **Advances from:** Gladiator

*A Crowdheart is a fighter who has stopped hearing the roar as noise and started hearing it as rations. They fight facing the expensive seats, take wounds where wounds show, and finish bouts the way the stands want them finished. Promoters bill them early; the crowd bills them forever.*

**Playstyle.** Court the stands with risk and flourish to build Acclaim, then spend it in surges of strength the quiet fighter never gets.

**The road to Crowdheart:**

1. Reach level 10 and top a bill for the first time; the promoter will spell your name wrong, and the crowd will correct him.
2. Fight one full bout facing the stands more often than the opponent, and win it.
3. Take a wound you could have avoided because the third tier could not see the fight, and make it worth their fare.
4. Refuse the quick kill at the crowd's whistle, and give them the long one they paid for.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Drink the Roar** — Gain Acclaim whenever the crowd reacts — first blood, a near miss, a flourish; at full Acclaim your strikes deal 30% more.
- **Play to the Gods** — Salute the cheapest seats; for 10 seconds every landed attack counts as a performance, generating double Acclaim.
- **Wound Them Pretty** — Your bleed effects are visible from any seat and demoralize enemies who can see them, reducing their damage 10%.
- **The Second Opponent** — You always know the crowd's mood; while it favors you, fear and taunt effects against you fail.

### Undercard

**Role:** Survivalist skirmisher — scavenged arms, control · **Advances from:** Gladiator

*The undercard fights at noon, when the seats are half empty and the beasts are half starved. No one bets much and no one is buried well. What the Undercard learns instead of applause: chain reaches farther than pride, nets beat pedigree, and everything on the sand is a weapon, including the sand.*

**Playstyle.** Win ugly. Scavenge weapons mid-fight, trip and tangle, spend nothing you cannot recover, and let better-armed fighters tire themselves on your patience.

**The road to Undercard:**

1. Reach level 10 by staying alive through a season of noon slaughters, which is the only promotion the undercard offers.
2. Kill something with the weapon it broke off you.
3. Learn the tells of every beast in the menagerie by feeding them, cleaning them, and twice nearly being them.
4. Be the last fighter of your intake still breathing, and drink once for each of the others.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Pit Provides** — Any object you lift counts as a weapon you are proficient with; improvised arms in your hands strike 20% harder before breaking.
- **Length of Chain** — Sweep a three-meter chain to trip every enemy in an arc; downed foes lose their next action.
- **Cheap Blood** — Kills made with scavenged or improvised weapons restore 10% of your health; what the pit left lying around costs nothing.
- **Gate-Read** — Study the gate before it opens; learn what is released two seconds before your enemies do.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Ovant

**Role:** Morale tank — adoration as armor · **Advances from:** Crowdheart

*An Ovant walks out to a wall of sound and wears it. The crowd's love is real and it is load-bearing: blades turn on it, fear breaks against it, and every chant of the name the stands invented adds a course of stone. An Ovant unloved is just meat, and trains accordingly.*

**Playstyle.** Hold the middle of the sand and be adored. Acclaim converts to mitigation; your work is to remain magnificent while everything hits you.

**The road to Ovant:**

1. Reach level 25 and earn a name you did not choose; you will know it is yours when the stands teach it to strangers.
2. Fight a full bout blindfolded, guarded only by the crowd shouting the blows as they come, and take no serious wound.
3. Stand in the sand after a loss and be cheered anyway; adoration that only attends victory is a wage, not armor.
4. Visit the market unarmored and count how many hands touch you for luck. Above a hundred, you are ready.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Worn Ovation** — Gain 1% damage reduction per stack of Acclaim, to a maximum of 40%; the reduction is audible.
- **They Came for This** — Taunt every enemy that can see you; while Acclaim is above half, taunted foes cannot target anyone else.
- **The Chant Holds** — While the crowd chants your name, you cannot be staggered, feared, or knocked from your feet.
- **Blindfold Trust** — Fight blind or in darkness without penalty; the stands call the blows as they come, and you have learned to listen.

### Sandpainter

**Role:** Burst striker — composed executions · **Advances from:** Crowdheart

*Sandpainters have the arena raked flat before their bouts, the way a canvas is stretched. They fight in arranged strokes — a wound here, a fall there — building toward a finish the connoisseurs in the good seats will describe for years. The kill is the picture; everything before it is underdrawing.*

**Playstyle.** Compose the bout. Place wounds deliberately to build the picture, then deliver the finishing stroke you designed, when the light is right.

**The road to Sandpainter:**

1. Reach level 25 and finish a bout so cleanly that the good seats stay seated after, discussing it.
2. Take a patron's commission for a specific ending and deliver it exactly, to the wound.
3. Halt a winnable bout once because the light was wrong, and accept the fine without argument.
4. See a rival copy your finish in another city, and take it as the compliment it is.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Raked Canvas** — Your first exchange marks the foe as your subject; your critical chance against the subject rises 5% each round.
- **Placed Wounds** — Wounds you deal to a new location — brow, thigh, sword-arm — each grant a Composition stack; stacks empower your finisher.
- **The Stroke They Came For** — Consume all Composition stacks in one execute; below 35% health the subject dies if you hold three or more stacks.
- **Red Perspective** — Enemies who witness your finished work are shaken, losing 15% accuracy until they leave the field or the memory.

### Trammel

**Role:** Controller — nets, chains, subtraction · **Advances from:** Undercard

*A Trammel's kit is a jailer's: net, hook, weighted cord, three ells of chain. The doctrine is subtraction — first the weapon, then the footing, then the air — until what remains of the opponent is a decision rather than a fight. Swordsmen call it cowardice, usually from the ground.*

**Playstyle.** Strip choices. Disarm, entangle, and drag until the enemy has nothing left to fight with, then collect whatever the bout was for.

**The road to Trammel:**

1. Reach level 25 and take a champion swordsman's blade, feet, and surrender, in that order, without giving him a wound worth stitching.
2. Net something that flies before it lands, and hold it down while it argues.
3. Fight one bout with nothing you brought: win with what the pit provides or do not come back for your kit.
4. Learn the eleven knots from a retired jailer, and pay whatever is asked; the price is famous and worth it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Cast** — Throw a weighted net over a foe within 10 meters, immobilizing them for 4 seconds; strength shortens this, cunning does not.
- **Confiscation** — Rip a weapon from an enemy's grip; disarmed foes take 25% more damage from you and know it.
- **Short Leash** — Hook a tangled enemy and drag them to any point within your chain's reach, including through their allies.
- **Ells of Iron** — Your chain parries as a shield and strikes as a flail; nothing inside its circle keeps its footing for long.

### Remainderman

**Role:** Attrition fighter — outlasting, cost control · **Advances from:** Undercard

*The pit's clerks coined the title as a joke: when a bout's estate settles — beasts fed, wagers paid, sand raked — the Remainderman is what is left standing to inherit. They study the gate the way farmers study weather: everything it releases has a cost of moving, and can be made to spend it.*

**Playstyle.** Spend less than everything you fight. Evade, block cheaply, punish exhaustion, and be standing at the settlement, which is the whole of the craft.

**The road to Remainderman:**

1. Reach level 25 and outlast a gate-beast without striking it once; the clerks will record the bout as weather.
2. Walk the sand at dawn and price every scrap on it — chain, splinter, bone — before the rakes come.
3. Survive the afternoon they empty the menagerie on you, and lodge a complaint about the scheduling.
4. Inherit a dead man's kit, oil it, and retire it with honors the day you can afford better. Keep the whetstone.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Spend Them First** — Enemies attacking you burn stamina 30% faster; the pit pays patience better than fury.
- **Gait-Study** — After observing an enemy for six seconds, automatically evade the first use of their strongest attack.
- **The Cheap Parry** — Your blocks and parries cost no stamina against foes more tired than you.
- **Inheritance** — Each bout survived to the end grants a small permanent bonus to health; the estate accumulates.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Chantborne

**Role:** Communion tank — crowd-fed restoration, shared vigor · **Advances from:** Ovant

*Chantborne kneel to the stands before each bout, and mean it. Their doctrine holds the roar is the only honest god — ten thousand hearts agreeing at once — and it answers: wounds close at the chant's peak, and the fighter rises carried. Priests attend with notebooks and leave unhappy.*

**Playstyle.** Be the congregation's own. Build the chant, then stand inside it — healing, unbreakable, and generous — while it carries the whole bill.

**The road to Chantborne:**

1. Reach level 40 and kneel to the stands before a bout you may not survive, sincerely; the roar knows flattery from prayer.
2. Be healed of a wound the chirurgeons had priced as mortal, at the chant's peak, before witnesses including one furious priest.
3. Fill an arena in a plague year, and send every soul home well.
4. Answer the theologians' inquiry by inviting them to stand in the sand while ten thousand voices agree about you. None have accepted twice.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Roar Answers** — At peak chant, recover 25% health and shed every curse, fear, and poison; the stands do not permit them.
- **Carried on Voices** — While the chant sustains, you cannot be slowed or staggered, and you move fastest toward the greatest danger.
- **Give It Back** — Spend all Acclaim to grant every ally 20% damage and immunity to despair for 10 seconds; the love was never only yours.
- **Ten Thousand Hearts** — Enemies who strike you at peak chant are deafened and shaken by the crowd's fury for 4 seconds.

### Coupsmith

**Role:** Executioner — one rehearsed, perfect stroke · **Advances from:** Sandpainter

*Coupsmiths hold that the finishing stroke is the entire art and everything before it scaffolding. Each forges one named stroke and rehearses it for years — against posts, carcasses, weather — until cities book the stroke rather than the fighter. Their bouts are short. The queue for seats is not.*

**Playstyle.** Wind one spring all bout. Every feint and placed wound exists to set the named stroke, and the named stroke ends the conversation.

**The road to Coupsmith:**

1. Reach level 40 and forge your stroke: one motion, named, rehearsed ten thousand times against oak, carcass, and winter.
2. Deliver it before a full house on the first attempt; a Coupsmith who winds twice is a fencer with ambitions.
3. Decline to use it for an entire season of victories, so the name gathers weight while the queue gathers length.
4. Watch another fighter attempt your stroke and fail, and send the widow your fee for the lesson.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Named Stroke** — Your signature execution: damage scales with every round spent preparing it, and the crowd knows it is coming, which is the point.
- **Scaffolding** — Your other attacks deal 20% less but build Craft twice as fast; nothing you do is for its own sake.
- **Unveiling** — Delivered at peak Acclaim, the Named Stroke cannot be parried, dodged, or survived by anything below half health.
- **Once, Cleanly** — If the Named Stroke kills, all Craft is refunded and every enemy who saw it loses their next action.

### Quartergiver

**Role:** Capture controller — nonlethal mastery, living leverage · **Advances from:** Trammel

*Quartergiver doctrine is arithmetic with a pulse: a corpse fetches one purse, a captive three — ransom, rematch, and gratitude. They net beasts for menageries, fighters for ransom, and once a warlord for a city's peace. The Haltermen call the practice sentiment with rope. The Quartergivers call it Tuesday.*

**Playstyle.** Take everything alive. Subdue, bind, and hold; every captive is leverage, and a field emptied by capture pays three times over.

**The road to Quartergiver:**

1. Reach level 40 and take a famous killer alive while the stands screamed for death; collect the ransom, the rematch, and — eventually — the gratitude.
2. Net a gate-beast a menagerie can display, unscarred; scarred stock is a butcher's trade, and you are not one.
3. Hold a warlord three days against his own army's arguments, and price the peace politely.
4. Keep count of everyone you have taken alive; the day the count means nothing to you, the Haltermen were right, and you must stop.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Alive Is Worth More** — Your subduing strikes deal double effect; anything you reduce to helplessness is captured, not killed.
- **The Gentle Cast** — A net that pins without crushing; captives inside cannot act but take no harm from any source.
- **Custody** — Enemies will not strike through your captives; while you hold one, their formation must route around your position.
- **Terms of Release** — Release a captive under terms; they leave the field entirely, and their allies' resolve drops 15%.

### Reckoner

**Role:** Attrition tank — counted certainty, perfect economy · **Advances from:** Remainderman

*A Reckoner fights with a running count: swings the foe has left, breaths the beast spends per charge, blood a body can price before noon. They place no wagers — the creed holds that the only bet worth making is one already decided, which no odds-house will take. Bookmakers post their bouts as exhibitions.*

**Playstyle.** Count everything, risk nothing. Decline bad trades, tax enemy waste, and finish when the arithmetic says finished, not before.

**The road to Reckoner:**

1. Reach level 40 and call a bout aloud before it happens — every exchange, every wound, the ending — and be right before witnesses.
2. Be refused by every odds-house in the city; certainty is bad business, and yours is now notorious.
3. Fight the bout your count says to decline by declining it, publicly, at cost to your name. The count outranks the crowd.
4. Teach one young fighter to count, and watch them live. It is the only profit the craft shares.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Running Count** — You see every enemy's remaining stamina, uses of their strongest attacks, and roughly how long their courage lasts.
- **Declined** — Once per round, refuse an unfavorable exchange: automatically evade one attack that would cost you more than the counter earns.
- **Waste Tax** — Enemies who miss you lose 5% damage for the encounter, stacking; the count remembers.
- **Settled Early** — Against any foe whose count you have completed, your strikes deal 30% more; you are no longer fighting, only concluding.

### Impresario

**Role:** Deception fighter — staged openings, scripted bouts · **Advances from:** Ovant

*Where the Chantborne kneel to the crowd, the Impresarios seat it. Their heresy is simple: the roar is not a god but an instrument, and instruments are for playing. An Impresario's stumbles are choreography, their near-defeats are acts, and their opponents die on cue, having rehearsed without knowing it.*

**Playstyle.** Script the bout. Feed the crowd manufactured peril, bait enemies into the openings you wrote for them, and collect on every cue.

**The road to Impresario:**

1. Reach level 40 and stage a near-defeat so exact that three physicians certify the peril; recover at the scripted beat.
2. Fight a bout in which every wound you take was budgeted the night before, and finish under budget.
3. Watch a Chantborne kneel to the stands, and understand you will never again feel what they feel. This is the fee, and it is paid once.
4. Fill the arena for a fighter nobody has heard of, twice; the second time proves it was not luck.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Staged Stumble** — Feign a fatal opening; the first enemy to take it is countered for triple damage and the crowd's derision.
- **Rehearsed Without Knowing** — Study a foe for two exchanges; afterward, you may declare their next attack, and it is so.
- **House Lights** — Control what the crowd sees; gain Acclaim on your schedule rather than the fight's, one stack per second of theatre.
- **Die on Cue** — Your killing blows land at the exact peak of crowd noise, generating double Acclaim and staggering every enemy who watched the act.

### Showbleeder

**Role:** Blood-fueled bruiser — wounds as escalating theatre · **Advances from:** Sandpainter

*Showbleeders call the Coupsmiths' art half a picture: the opponent's death is a stranger's story, but your own blood is the only paint you ever really own. They open themselves deliberately — a line across the chest before the first exchange — and rage never touches it. Every drop is budgeted.*

**Playstyle.** Bleed on schedule. Each shown wound buys Acclaim and power; build to the crescendo where you are almost dead and entirely unstoppable.

**The road to Showbleeder:**

1. Reach level 40 and open your own cheek before a full house, smiling, and hear what silence sounds like the moment before a roar.
2. Map your body into what may be spent and what must be kept, and never once spend past the line.
3. Fight the crescendo bout: begin it whole, end it at the edge, win it there, and walk out unassisted.
4. Tell a Coupsmith, courteously, that his masterpiece dies with the other man; yours goes home with you every night.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Overture Cut** — Open the bout by wounding yourself; gain 20 Acclaim and 15% damage while the cut shows.
- **Budgeted Drop** — Bleeding never kills you and cannot be inflicted on you by enemies; your blood spends only on your instruction.
- **Red Crescendo** — Below 30% health, your damage, speed, and Acclaim gains double; the last act is the whole reason for the ticket.
- **Cannot Look Away** — Enemies within sight of your open wounds lose 15% accuracy; fascination, the chirurgeons write, presents like fear.

### Halterman

**Role:** Lockdown executioner — binds that finish · **Advances from:** Trammel

*The Haltermen's creed fits on a knife: what the chain takes, the chain keeps. A thing spared is a rematch on delay, and rematches kill netmen — they have counted. So the bind is the sentence and the tightening is the verdict, and no Halterman has ever met an escaped opponent twice.*

**Playstyle.** Bind to end. Your chains close like verdicts — no rescue, no ransom, no rematch — and everything they hold, they conclude.

**The road to Halterman:**

1. Reach level 40 and bind the thing that had escaped every pit in three provinces, then end it before the applause, not after.
2. Refuse a ransom that would have beggared a duke, with the rope already tight; the Quartergivers still tell it as a tragedy.
3. Keep the halter of everything you have concluded, knotted once each, and carry the weight without complaint.
4. Meet an opponent you spared in your netman days, survive the lesson, and never need it taught again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **What the Chain Takes** — Enemies you bind cannot be healed, freed, teleported, or rescued by any strength or spell their allies have brought.
- **The Tightening** — Bound foes take mounting damage each second, doubling every third second; struggle accelerates the schedule.
- **Halter-Cast** — Hurl a noose that silences and drags a foe to your feet; casters find the silence the worse half.
- **No Rematch** — Execute any bound enemy below half health instantly; the stroke cannot be interrupted, and their allies must check morale.

### Stakesman

**Role:** Gambler bruiser — all-or-nothing violence · **Advances from:** Remainderman

*Stakesmen call the Reckoners embalmed. A certainty pays even, they say, which is to say nothing; the pit only pays what you stake. So they post their own odds and fight under them — offhand only, blindfolded, two gates at once — and the stands love them the way they love a coin still in the air.*

**Playstyle.** Stake to win. Declare handicaps for multiplied returns, ride streaks, and treat your own blood as table money — feast or famine, nightly.

**The road to Stakesman:**

1. Reach level 40 and post your own death-odds at the gate, in numbers a child could check, then beat them before witnesses.
2. Wager your sword-hand on a bout and win; wear the unclaimed stake with more respect afterward.
3. Lose everything once — purse, kit, name on the bill — and rebuild it from one copper and an undercard slot.
4. Tell a Reckoner what the count leaves out, and fight the bout his arithmetic declined. Win or lose, that is the whole quarrel.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Posted Odds** — Declare a handicap at the bout's start — halved armor, wrong hand, no sight; win under it and all rewards triple.
- **Ride the Streak** — Each kill without taking a hit doubles your Acclaim gains; one clean hit against you clears the table.
- **Deadman's Flip** — When a blow would kill you, flip: heads, survive at 1 health with doubled damage; tails, it lands. Once per day.
- **Against the Line** — Gain 8% damage for every way the field outmatches you — numbers, size, reach, ground; hopeless is a stance.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Adored

**Role:** Mythic bulwark — love as fortress · **Advances from:** Chantborne

*Histories dispute whether the Adored is one fighter or a title the crowd re-bestows when needed. The accounts agree on the rest: cities riot when their bouts move elsewhere; wounds they take are felt, faintly, by everyone watching; and an army once refused battle rather than be hated by its own home crowd.*

**Playstyle.** Stand inside a love with structural properties. The chant absorbs what strikes you, turns crowds against your enemies, and rebuilds you mid-blow.

**The road to The Adored:**

1. Reach level 60 and be adored in a city you have never visited, in a tongue you do not speak.
2. Take a wound before a full house and hear ten thousand people gasp with the pain of it — shared, divided, survivable.
3. Have a war postponed because you were fighting that day, and never learn which side asked.
4. Walk through a riot held in your honor, untouched, touching everyone.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ten Thousand Take It** — While the chant holds, a third of all damage you suffer disperses into the crowd, who consider it fair.
- **Beloved Past Reason** — Enemies must overcome the chant to strike you at all; most swings falter mid-arc, and the crowd counts each one aloud.
- **The Chant Rebuilds** — At peak chant, regenerate 5% health per second; chirurgeons have stopped attending your bouts.
- **Turn the Stands** — Point at one enemy; the crowd turns on them, stripping 20% speed and resolve and feeding you Acclaim each time they act.

### The Immaculate Wound

**Role:** Mythic executioner — the one perfect stroke · **Advances from:** Coupsmith

*Anatomists dispute that the stroke is possible; the exhibits disagree. The Immaculate Wound's kills leave one mark, exact, and nothing else — no bruise, no fear on the face, no second try in living memory. Those they decline to finish describe the same thing: a stillness, and a decision they were not consulted on.*

**Playstyle.** End bouts at the moment you select, with the wound you designed. The fight is a formality the stroke permits to occur.

**The road to The Immaculate Wound:**

1. Reach level 60 and deliver the stroke through full plate, leaving the plate unmarked; the smith will exhibit it anyway.
2. Decline a kill an emperor commanded, in his own box, and leave the arena at your usual pace.
3. Let the anatomists' guild examine your work and publish that it is impossible; do not correct them.
4. Perform the stroke once with no audience but the opponent, and learn whether it is still art. Do not repeat the experiment.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Mark Only** — Your Named Stroke now kills any foe below half health, through any armor, leaving armor and cloth uncut.
- **The Consultation** — Choose not to finish a helpless foe; they yield permanently, and every enemy who sees the mercy loses the will to test you.
- **The Drawn Breath** — For the breath before your stroke, no witness — friend, foe, or crowd — can act; the moment is attended.
- **Beyond Anatomy** — Your critical strikes ignore resistances, immunities, and arguments; the anatomists have withdrawn their objections.

### The King's Ransom

**Role:** Mythic captor — anything taken alive · **Advances from:** Quartergiver

*The nets are famous: the king taken mid-charge through his own guard and priced politely; the plague-beast displayed alive to prove it could be; the ransom that bought every fighter in one pit their freedom. Chroniclers note the pattern with unease — nothing, so far, has proven too large to take alive.*

**Playstyle.** Capture at any scale. Giants, cavalry wedges, small armies — everything is stock, leverage, or ransom, and killing is for fighters with no imagination.

**The road to The King's Ransom:**

1. Reach level 60 and take a crowned king alive through his household guard, returning him unbruised at a rate history calls polite.
2. Empty a battlefield by capture alone; the chroniclers will check the casualty rolls twice and find them insulting.
3. Take alive the thing the menageries refused, and build the cage yourself.
4. Spend one legendary ransom buying every fighter in the pit that made you, and post no notice of it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Nothing Too Large** — Your casts bind creatures of any size for 6 seconds; siege beasts and giants count as merely awkward.
- **Priced Politely** — While you hold a captive, their allies must parley: enemies lose one action each round to hesitation and arithmetic.
- **Living Leverage** — Each captive held grants your party 10% damage reduction; formations cannot charge through their own.
- **The Great Haul** — Once per battle, cast the wide net: every enemy within 15 meters is captured at once, and the engagement is functionally concluded.

### The Unspent

**Role:** Mythic survivor — expenditure without return · **Advances from:** Reckoner

*Every currency the pit knows has been thrown at them — beasts, engines, other legends — and the count settles the same way: expenditure, no return. Odds-houses list their bouts as exhibitions, a wager requiring two possible outcomes. One school of clerks argues the Unspent died years ago and simply declined the deduction.*

**Playstyle.** Refuse to be spent. Perfect economy made mythic: attacks cost their makers more than they cost you, and the count only ever grows.

**The road to The Unspent:**

1. Reach level 60 and survive the menagerie emptied in one afternoon, spending less blood than a barber's nick.
2. Be delisted by every odds-house on the continent; frame the letters.
3. Outlast the arena itself — attend the demolition of the stone that first tried to spend you, and pocket a keepsake chip.
4. Answer the clerks' question — what would it cost — the only honest way: more than anyone has.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Two Possible Outcomes** — No single blow, spell, or fall may reduce you below 1 health; the second outcome has never been in stock.
- **No Sale** — Once per round, an attack against you simply does not profit: negate it entirely, and the attacker loses the stamina anyway.
- **The Count Only Grows** — Your Inheritance bonuses no longer cap, and half their value extends to every ally on your field.
- **Declined the Deduction** — The first death you suffer each day is refused as a clerical error; you continue from 30% health.

### The House

**Role:** Mythic orchestrator — bout, odds, and gate · **Advances from:** Impresario

*At some point the Impresario stopped appearing on the bill and became the bill. The House owns names, gates, odds, and seasons; entire rivalries are its compositions, including — the rumor runs — several wars, booked as sincerely as any undercard. Fighters discover their careers were programs. Kings discover the same, later.*

**Playstyle.** Run the field like a booked house. Enemies enter on your schedule, act on your cues, and lose at the hour printed on the ticket.

**The road to The House:**

1. Reach level 60 and own, quietly, the arena that once owned you — sand, gates, and the book of names.
2. Stage a bout every seat believes is fate, including both fighters.
3. Book a war's decisive battle, sell the seating, and be gone before either general understands whose show it was.
4. Keep one fight in every season honest, and tell no one which; a house that is all script forgets why crowds come.

**Abilities granted:**

- **On the Bill** — At battle's start, set the order in which enemies may act; improvisation against the program costs them 20% effect.
- **The Gate Is Yours** — Reinforcements, ambushes, and arrivals occur only through entrances you permit, at times you find dramatic.
- **A Word with the Odds** — Once per battle, declare one outcome — a miss, a crit, a stumble — and it occurs as declared.
- **The House Collects** — Whenever an enemy plan fails on your field, you gain Acclaim and 10% damage; failure is the house's percentage.

### The Encore

**Role:** Mythic showpiece — death as intermission · **Advances from:** Showbleeder

*Nine deaths are attested by licensed chirurgeons; the Encore disputes only the order. No one can prove which deaths were staged, and the profession has stopped trying — the boundary has worn thin on them, and death, whatever it once believed, now waits for the applause to decide the matter.*

**Playstyle.** Die as programmed, return for the bow. The final act is your home ground, and you have never once missed a curtain.

**The road to The Encore:**

1. Reach level 60 and die before a full house, certified thrice, then take your bow before the flowers land.
2. Buy the three chirurgeons dinner afterward and decline, kindly, to answer the only question they have.
3. Let connoisseurs travel to view the scars in order, and keep one — the first — covered, always; a body needs one privacy.
4. Miss your own funeral for a booking, and send regrets.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Attested Death** — Once per battle, die convincingly: untargetable while mourned, then return at the moment of your choosing with full Acclaim.
- **The Order Disputed** — When a blow kills you, it may be retroactively part of the act: survive at 15% health. Once per day.
- **Home Ground** — Below a quarter health, your abilities cost nothing and your critical strikes are guaranteed; this is where you live.
- **Worn Openly** — Your scars unsettle professionals: enemies who study you lose 20% resolve; chirurgeons charge you double and attend anyway.

### The Certainty

**Role:** Mythic warden — binds that conclude · **Advances from:** Halterman

*Kingdoms send for the Certainty when a thing must not merely die but end. Whatever the chain closes on is finished in every telling; nothing bound has returned, echoed, or been rumored since. Priesthoods object that certain deaths belong to gods. The Certainty attends anyway, and the objections have grown quieter.*

**Playstyle.** Be the conclusion. Your chain cannot be escaped, its verdicts cannot be appealed, and what you end stays ended, everywhere.

**The road to The Certainty:**

1. Reach level 60 and hold what legend says cannot be held, through the night it does everything the legends promised; end it at dawn.
2. Decline a god's petition for a prisoner's release — respectfully once, then finally.
3. Chain something that unmakes chains, with the one link forged from your own certainty; it is the only link that matters.
4. Retire the halters of your predecessors into the sea, all but one, and never explain the exception.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Closed Is Closed** — Your binds cannot be broken by strength, magic, or divinity; struggling is noted but changes nothing.
- **Ended, Not Slain** — Anything you execute cannot be raised, echoed, haunted back, or renegotiated; the matter is concluded in all jurisdictions.
- **The Chain Recalls** — Your cast cannot miss anything it has ever touched, at any range, through anything.
- **Counted Seconds** — A bound foe learns exactly how many seconds remain to them, as do you; the figure is precise and has never been revised.

### The Long Odds

**Role:** Mythic gambler — impossibility as fuel · **Advances from:** Stakesman

*The odds-men no longer post numbers for their bouts; the numbers embarrassed the profession. Every wager of their own life has come in, including the famous loss — the name staked and forfeited, the year fought as no one, the name won back at longer odds than it was lost at.*

**Playstyle.** Grow with the impossibility. The worse your position — numbers, wounds, prophecy — the stronger you stand, and you engineer worse on purpose.

**The road to The Long Odds:**

1. Reach level 60 having staked your name, lost it, fought a year as no one, and won it back at longer odds. The order is mandatory.
2. Post a wager at ten thousand to one, your life the stake, and collect.
3. Take the hopeless bet no one else would carry — a doomed line, strangers, no purse — and turn it.
4. Refuse one sure thing on principle, publicly; certainty is the only stake beneath you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Worse Is Better** — All your capabilities scale with how lost your cause is; at truly hopeless, they double, and the stands know the smell of it.
- **Stake Anything** — Wager health, weapons, senses, or Acclaim before an exchange; victory returns the stake threefold.
- **The Coin Remembers** — Once per battle, a killing blow becomes a flip you win; the coin knows whose it is.
- **Nameless Year** — Stripped of gear, allies, sight, or name, you fight at full capability; you have done this before, for longer.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Spectacle

**Role:** Apotheosis — the watched moment itself · **Advances from:** The Adored

*The fighter and the roar have finished separating. Now, wherever many hearts rise at once — coronations, executions, dawn charges — the Spectacle is in attendance, and things fighting inside it fight larger than themselves. Crowds have begun gathering ahead of anything happening. Something, the constables note grimly, always then happens.*

**Playstyle.** Be the occasion. Everything near you becomes larger than life; allies fight as the legends of themselves while a single eye remains open.

**The road to The Spectacle:**

1. Reach level 85 and walk out of your last bout mid-chant; the chant must continue without you. It has not yet stopped.
2. Attend, in the same hour, every full house in the world, and be felt in all of them.
3. Leave your name with the crowd on your way out; where you stand now, things do not have names, only audiences.
4. Grant one nobody, once a generation, the surge that made you; how they spend it is the show, and you no longer choose the ending.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Larger Than Life** — Any ally observed by anyone — friend, foe, crowd, you — gains 25% to everything; solitude is the only debuff you recognize.
- **The Roar Without Throats** — Summon the full-stands roar anywhere — crypt, field, empty hall; all crowd-fed powers on your side operate at capacity, always.
- **Every Eye** — Direct all attention to one ally's next action; it cannot miss, cannot be resisted, and will be retold badly for centuries.
- **Stage Fright** — Enemies act last while watched, and everything is watching; their finest abilities falter on the biggest night of their lives.

### The Better End

**Role:** Apotheosis — the clean ending, everywhere · **Advances from:** The Immaculate Wound

*Soldiers have always prayed for it; the prayer now has an address. Chaplains note that some endings arrive complete — instant, painless, at the right moment, witnessed — and that they cluster where they are most deserved. Executioners' guilds keep a feast-day. The dying, uniquely, file no complaints.*

**Playstyle.** Govern the quality of endings. On your field, death comes clean or not at all — and to those you attend, it comes as a courtesy.

**The road to The Better End:**

1. Reach level 85 and give ten thousand better ends and not one bad one; the audit is conducted by the dying, and they are strict.
2. Attend every ending on a battlefield in one night, personally, and leave none of them botched.
3. Be prayed to by soldiers you will never fight, and answer the only way the office answers: eventually, cleanly, once.
4. Decline to attend one death — your own — until the work is done. The work is not done.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Stroke, Everywhere** — Any mortally wounded creature on the field may be finished by you instantly, from any distance, painlessly; the stroke is already travelling.
- **Only the Clean Stroke** — On your field, agony fails: torture effects, lingering poisons, and cruel deaths simply do not function. Endings meet a standard.
- **The Attended End** — When a great foe dies well by your hand, every ally who witnessed it is steeled: immune to fear and despair for the battle.
- **Below Standard** — Once per day, refuse a death as below standard; the target survives at 10% health. The office reserves the right to decline.

### The Yield

**Role:** Apotheosis — surrender as natural law · **Advances from:** The King's Ransom

*The old soldiers insist battles once ended a single way. Now there is a moment in every hopeless fight — arms heavier, a knowing — when stopping becomes possible, and men take it. Generals call it a disease of morale. Mothers set a place for it at table. The word has become a proper noun.*

**Playstyle.** Make stopping real. Break the momentum of slaughter, shelter whoever lays arms down, and harvest strength from every life that walks away.

**The road to The Yield:**

1. Reach level 85 and accept the surrender of an army dispatched specifically to kill you — all of it, by name, before dusk.
2. Teach the word to a war that had none, in a tongue that had to borrow it.
3. Stand where routs happen and turn them into processions; the difference is whether anyone is trampled, and now no one is.
4. Refuse the surrender of one thing that offered it falsely, so the law keeps its edge; mercy with no teeth is only weather.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Offered Moment** — Extend the Yield to the field: every enemy below half resolve may lay down arms, and most discover they want to.
- **Sanctuary of the Yielded** — Anyone who has yielded cannot be harmed by anyone; the law enforces itself and entertains no exceptions.
- **Arms Grown Heavy** — Enemies striking the beaten, the fleeing, or the helpless find their weapons suddenly enormous: 50% slower, and ashamed.
- **Harvest of the Standing** — At battle's end, gain enduring strength for every combatant, either side, who leaves the field alive; the Yield pays in survivors.

### The Tally

**Role:** Apotheosis — the count that is never zero · **Advances from:** The Unspent

*After every slaughter, something counts the living, and the count comes up wrong: one more than the slaughter planned. Clerks of three empires have found the same impossible constant in the survivor rolls and blamed each other. The pit's old joke has hardened into law — something always remains, and remains standing.*

**Playstyle.** Be the remainder made law. While you stand, annihilation is an arithmetic error; you and whoever shelters in your count cannot be reduced to nothing.

**The road to The Tally:**

1. Reach level 85 and be the reason a massacre's count comes up wrong in forty provinces; sign nothing, correct nothing.
2. Survive the age's great slaughter carrying the full count of it, every name, and keep the count where no clerk will balance it.
3. Vanish from the records while never missing a battle; the constant works best unattributed.
4. Choose, at the end of something enormous, who the one-more-than-planned will be, and make it stick. This is the whole office, used once.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Never Zero** — While you fight, your side cannot be wiped out: the last three allies standing take 60% reduced damage, always.
- **Arithmetic Error** — Each round, the single most lethal blow aimed at your side is subtracted before it lands; the clerks never find it.
- **Audit the Field** — Know precisely what everything on the field has left — health, stamina, arrows, courage — at all times, to the unit.
- **Carried Forward** — Your permanent per-bout growth now extends to allies who survive battles at your side; the estate has heirs.

### The Gallery

**Role:** Apotheosis — the unseen audience of the world · **Advances from:** The House

*Duelists in empty halls have begun to feel it: a weight of attention, the sense of an aisle. The Gallery attends everything now — wars, oaths, small kindnesses at bad hours — and its regard has pressure. Somewhere behind the world there is seating, and the seating is never quite empty anymore.*

**Playstyle.** Provide the witness. Nothing on your field is hidden or unjudged; performances double, cowardice is reviewed, and history's bookings are yours.

**The road to The Gallery:**

1. Reach level 85 and quit the bill forever; discover, without surprise, that you cannot stop attending.
2. Sit, unseen, with a swordsman drilling alone at midnight, and let him feel the seat fill; his art was never for no one.
3. Book history's next great confrontation, attend it, and file the only review that will survive: the outcome.
4. Keep one seat empty in every house, always the same seat; who it is kept for is the Gallery's single secret.

**Abilities granted:**

- **No Empty Halls** — Everything on the field is witnessed: stealth, invisibility, and concealment fail entirely; every act is performed whether intended or not.
- **The Booking** — Once per round, defer one enemy's action to later in the program; they are simply not on yet.
- **Notices** — At each round's end the Gallery reviews: your best-performing ally gains 15% to everything, the worst-reviewed enemy loses the same.
- **Attendance Taken** — Enemies who flee your field are remembered; in every witnessed fight thereafter they falter, and every fight is witnessed now.

### The Curtain Call

**Role:** Apotheosis — endings completed, witnessed, bowed · **Advances from:** The Encore

*At the end of every magnificent thing there is now a beat — held, attended — in which the world waits for the bow. Great deaths get their final act; great lives get their exit walked, not dragged. Undertakers report the change without approving of it: endings, lately, have been arriving finished.*

**Playstyle.** Complete endings beautifully. The fallen rise for final acts, exits happen on your timing, and nothing magnificent dies unseen or unfinished.

**The road to The Curtain Call:**

1. Reach level 85, die your last attested death, and stay past it; the applause has not ended, and the office does not leave mid-ovation.
2. Give a dying stranger ten perfect final seconds, unwitnessed by anyone but you, and count yourself sufficient house.
3. Attend the age's worst ending — the one nobody could finish — and finish it: witnessed, bowed, complete.
4. Leave your own ending unscheduled, the only booking the office never makes; the curtain waits on you, and can.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Final Act** — Any ally who falls rises for ten unkillable seconds of brilliance before the curtain; many battles have ended inside them.
- **Transfixed** — Enemies witnessing a final act cannot intervene in it; interruptions are simply not taken.
- **Called Back for the Bow** — Once per battle, when any witness demands it, every downed ally rises at a third of their health; the demand is binding.
- **Strike the Set** — Once per day, declare the show over: enemies with no stake left accept the ending and withdraw from the field.

### The Great Chain

**Role:** Apotheosis — restraint as world order · **Advances from:** The Certainty

*Philosophers teach that creation hangs in order, link within link, each thing held to its station. They mean it as metaphor. Under the world, where the old monsters are kept, the metaphor is load-bearing: everything bound is bound to something, the something runs upward, and the far end is held. Occupied, in fact.*

**Playstyle.** Hold the far end. Bind anything at any scale, keep the world's kept things kept, and permit nothing on your field to exceed its station.

**The road to The Great Chain:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the far end of the chain; feel, arriving all at once, everything on it.
2. Walk the bottom of the world and tighten what your predecessors left slack, without waking what the slack was comforting.
3. Refuse to bind one thing that deserved binding, so the office remembers it has a hand and is not a mechanism.
4. Hold, on the day the oldest kept thing pulls, and be the reason that year has no history.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Link Within Link** — Chain every enemy on the field to each other; harm to one staggers all, and formations become arguments.
- **The Far End** — Reel anything bound — by you, by anyone, ever — to your hand from any distance; the chain runs through you now.
- **Held Below** — Bind one great foe down among the world's kept things until the battle ends; visiting hours are not observed.
- **Order of Links** — Nothing on your field may exceed its station: enemies cannot transform, enlarge, ascend, or become more than they arrived.

### The Turning Coin

**Role:** Apotheosis — the undecided, kept undecided · **Advances from:** The Long Odds

*Somewhere overhead a coin is still turning. Soldiers in hopeless lines report the same signs: a glint, a lightness, the sudden conviction that nothing is settled yet. Prophets find their surest pages hedging. The odds-men have begun paying out on battles arithmetic had closed, and have stopped asking why.*

**Playstyle.** Keep the outcome open. Certainties dissolve on your field, the doomed get their turn, and nothing is decided while the coin turns.

**The road to The Turning Coin:**

1. Reach level 85, stake the outcome itself — yours, everyone's — and toss; the coin has not landed, which is the office.
2. Attend every hopeless last stand in one night and leave each one merely possible; possible is enough, and all you deal in.
3. Pay out, personally, the wagers the arithmetic said were lost; the odds-men will learn not to ask, and you will not tell.
4. Never let it land. The day the coin comes down, the world is decided, and you have seen the world. Keep it turning.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Not Yet Landed** — Nothing on your field may be certain: guaranteed hits, fated deaths, and prophecies all become honest coin flips.
- **The Doomed Turn** — The most hopeless ally on the field acts twice each round; despair, it turns out, was a scheduling error.
- **Heads** — Once per battle, call the coin: one impossible thing, declared aloud, succeeds.
- **Stakes Returned** — Any ally who risks everything and falls gets the stake back: they survive at 1 health, once each per battle.
