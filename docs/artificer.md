# ⚙️ Artificer

> *Every miracle is a prototype; every prototype has a fuse.*

**Archetype:** Esoteric

Artificers treat magic as a material and disaster as data. Their miracles are drafted at a bench, prototyped in the field, and revised in blood, each failure logged in journals the guilds pretend not to copy. They build companions that outthink their plating and ordnance that keeps its appointments. Everything an artificer makes eventually explodes; the great ones decide precisely when.

## The Tree

```text
Artificer (Lv 1)
├── Golemwright (Lv 10)
│   ├── Ironshepherd (Lv 25) → Engine-Sire (Lv 40) → Thousandth Hand (Lv 60)
│   └── Plateheart (Lv 25) → Interred Engine (Lv 40) → Brazen Saint (Lv 60)
└── Volatile Chymist (Lv 10)
    ├── Petardier (Lv 25) → Cataclysmith (Lv 40) → Appointed Sun (Lv 60)
    └── Crucible-Drinker (Lv 25) → Panacea Heretic (Lv 40) → Azoth Incarnate (Lv 60)
```

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Artificer

**Role:** Hybrid utility caster — gadgets and preparation

*Every artificer begins as someone who asked why and was told to stop. They are journeymen of a guild that half distrusts them, carrying tool-rolls where knights carry swords and treating spellcraft as an engineering discipline with unusually strict tolerances. Villages hire them to fix mills; wars hire them to fix outcomes.*

**Playstyle.** Versatile toolbox play: deploy gadgets, exploit terrain, and trade raw power for preparation. Weak when caught without a plan, dangerous when given an evening to draft one.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Apprentice yourself to a guildless tinker who teaches that magic is a material with tolerances, and every tolerance can be exceeded once.
2. Build a device from scavenged parts and watch it fail. Record why in the journal you will keep until you die.
3. Present a working prototype to a guild examiner who expected a corpse, and collect your stamp without smiling.
4. Survive your first misfire. Leave the scar uncovered, so you remember what a misplaced decimal costs.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Jury-Rig** — Assemble a one-use device from scavenged parts; roll on your prototype table for its property, which is never quite nothing.
- **Copper Coil** — Hurl a charged coil that arcs lightning between the two nearest enemies, dealing shock damage to both.
- **Blueprint Ledger** — Once per rest, recall a spent gadget's schematic and rebuild it instantly at half cost.
- **Controlled Misfire** — When a device fails, choose the direction of the failure; enemies in the cone inherit the consequences.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Golemwright

**Role:** Summoner — construct companion control · **Advances from:** Artificer

*Golemwrights are the artificers who grew tired of working alone. They animate servitors, teach armor to think, and drag traveling forges through mud to the front lines. Soldiers mock the clanking retinue until it pulls them from under a dead horse; after that, they learn the constructs' names.*

**Playstyle.** Companion-driven midline play: position your servitor, repair between exchanges, and let thinking armor absorb what you cannot dodge. Your action economy is the weapon.

**The road to Golemwright:**

1. Reach level 10 and animate a servitor from your own schematics, borrowing no one's blueprint and blaming no one's math.
2. Teach it one word. When it obeys the third time without being watched, give it a name.
3. Carry your forge across a battlefield and rebuild your construct under arrow-fall, before you see to the wound in your arm.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Assembly** — Animate a clockwork servitor that fights, carries, and mends; it retains one learned behavior permanently between battles.
- **Thinking Plate** — Your armor studies each attacker, gaining stacking resistance to any damage type it has already survived this combat.
- **Traveling Forge** — Deploy your workshop as a field station that repairs constructs and reloads devices for allies within its glow.
- **Overwound Mainspring** — Wind your servitor past tolerance: it acts twice this round, then seizes for one.

### Volatile Chymist

**Role:** Ranged blaster — area denial · **Advances from:** Artificer

*Volatile Chymists treat the word unstable as a schedule rather than a warning. They brew ordnance the guild refuses to store indoors, sip their own prototypes to save time, and keep meticulous journals so that whoever inherits the laboratory knows exactly which shelf killed them.*

**Playstyle.** Area control through thrown chemistry: soften formations with corrosive clouds, spike damage with timed charges, and gamble on prototype elixirs when the arithmetic turns against you.

**The road to Volatile Chymist:**

1. Reach level 10 and stabilize a compound your instructors called unstabilizable. Then stabilize it a second time, in front of them.
2. Lose your eyebrows to a batch you were certain of, and write down the ratio anyway.
3. Demolish a target of the guild's choosing with exactly the yield you quoted — no more, and no apology.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Unstable Batch** — Your bombs each carry a random secondary element; you learn which one as it detonates.
- **Acrid Veil** — Shatter a flask into lingering vapor that corrodes armor, eating one point of protection each round.
- **Test Subject One** — Drink an unlabeled prototype: gain a strong random benefit and a documented, inconvenient side effect.
- **Fused Charge** — Place a charge with a timer of your choosing; its yield grows for every round it waits.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Ironshepherd

**Role:** Pet commander — sustained two-body pressure · **Advances from:** Golemwright

*An Ironshepherd builds one construct and stakes a lifetime on it. The machine is rebuilt after every battle until nothing original remains except the name, which is the part the shepherd actually maintains. Armies pay for the pair together; no offer has ever bought them separately.*

**Playstyle.** Two-body play: the construct holds the line and takes the wounds while you direct, repair, and strike into the openings it creates.

**The road to Ironshepherd:**

1. Reach level 25 and rebuild your construct so many times that none of the first metal remains, and it still answers its name.
2. Refuse the coin offered for it. Refuse the second, larger offer without pausing.
3. Sleep beside it for a season, until you know every sound it makes when something is wrong.
4. Let it take a blow meant for you, and repair it before you bind your own wound.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Naming** — Bond permanently with one construct; it gains a growth track, keeps its memories, and obeys no other voice.
- **Shepherd's Whistle** — Command your construct to intercept any attack aimed at an ally within its stride.
- **Graze on Ruin** — Your construct consumes battlefield scrap to repair itself mid-combat, healing more amid greater wreckage.
- **Iron Bellow** — Your construct roars from its furnace core, forcing nearby enemies to attack it for two rounds.

### Plateheart

**Role:** Armored bruiser — frontline attrition · **Advances from:** Golemwright

*Platehearts stop distinguishing between armor and anatomy. Plates are bolted to bone, a fabricated mind rides the harness, and the boiler in the chest is not a metaphor. Shield-walls part to let them plug a breach, and comrades learn to stand clear when the steam vents.*

**Playstyle.** Frontline attrition: wade in slowly, let the grafted armor learn each attacker, and win exchanges that would kill anything still made only of meat.

**The road to Plateheart:**

1. Reach level 25 and bolt the first plate to living bone. Do not cry out; the armor is listening.
2. Lend the harness-mind a portion of your memories, and forgive what it chooses to do with them.
3. Stand in a breach where flesh alone breaks, and hold until the wall is rebuilt behind you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Graft Protocol** — Bolt plating directly to bone: permanent armor that cannot be removed, disarmed, or stolen, only earned.
- **Second Opinion** — The armor's mind acts when yours cannot, parrying and stepping for you while you are stunned, sleeping, or afraid.
- **Boiler Vent** — Release chest-furnace steam in a scalding burst, driving melee attackers back and searing the persistent.
- **Deadbolt Stance** — Lock your frame: you cannot move or be moved, and attacks against adjacent allies redirect to you.

### Petardier

**Role:** Siege artillery — long-range timed burst · **Advances from:** Volatile Chymist

*Petardiers are siegecraft distilled into a single walking profession. They carry shaped charges up killing grounds, count fuses aloud while arrows fall, and are famous for dying in exactly the manner their title suggests. The ones who live become the reason fortress architects drink.*

**Playstyle.** Patient long-range devastation: mark targets, set staggered timers, and stack yields so that one exact moment does the work of a battery of trebuchets.

**The road to Petardier:**

1. Reach level 25 and bring down a fortress gate with a charge you carried up the killing ground on your own back.
2. Count a fuse aloud under arrow-fall and be right. Do this until the sappers stop checking your math.
3. Walk away from a detonation without turning to watch, because doubt is a thing you no longer carry.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Breaching Charge** — A shaped blast that ignores cover, walls, and shields, delivering its full yield to whatever hid behind them.
- **Ranging Shot** — Your first shell marks the impact zone; every later shell landing there strikes true automatically.
- **Fuse Discipline** — Set timers exact to the second; each round a charge waits adds another die of yield.
- **Powder Ledger** — Account for every grain: once per battle, combine all remaining charges into one consolidated detonation.

### Crucible-Drinker

**Role:** Mutagenic skirmisher — adaptive melee alchemy · **Advances from:** Volatile Chymist

*Crucible-Drinkers ran out of test subjects and volunteered. Each draught rewrites something — marrow, hide, the shape of their hands — and each dawn they catalogue what remains of the original. Guilds list them as equipment rather than members, which the Drinkers find flattering.*

**Playstyle.** Adaptive melee alchemy: drink into the fight you are facing, growing claws, plating, or gills as needed, and outlast whatever cannot adapt back.

**The road to Crucible-Drinker:**

1. Reach level 25 and drink a mutagen distilled from your own blood. Survive what it teaches your body about itself.
2. Catalogue every change at the mirror each dawn, until the catalogue's author is someone you used to be.
3. Hear a physician pronounce you an abomination, and correct only the terminology.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Mutagenic Draught** — Drink to grow the tool you need — claws, dermal plate, gills — lasting until you drink otherwise.
- **Purge Sweat** — Expel accumulated toxins through the skin as a caustic mist that sears everything adjacent.
- **Adaptive Marrow** — Your bones learn: gain resistance to the last damage type that hurt you until something new does.
- **Tolerance** — You are permanently immune to your own compounds, which is more useful than outsiders assume.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Engine-Sire

**Role:** Multi-summoner — battlefield overwhelm · **Advances from:** Ironshepherd

*An Engine-Sire's constructs build constructs. The craft has passed from hand to iron and from iron to iron again, and the foundry now marches under its own power with its maker walking at its head. Kingdoms describe an approaching Engine-Sire the way coastal towns describe weather.*

**Playstyle.** Overwhelming numbers with inherited discipline: field a family of constructs that share what each has learned, and replace losses faster than the enemy replaces courage.

**The road to Engine-Sire:**

1. Reach level 40 and forge a construct using your first as forge-hand, so the craft passes from your iron to its own.
2. March your foundry to a war it can end, and end it before the season turns.
3. Bury a frame that fell for you with full rites, and stand while the others grieve in gear-clatter.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Progeny Line** — Field multiple constructs drawing on a single pool of learned behaviors; what one masters, all inherit.
- **Inheritance Protocol** — When a construct falls, its memory core migrates to the nearest kin, carrying its buffs and its grudges.
- **Marching Foundry** — Your workshop walks to war as a siege platform, assembling replacement frames mid-battle.
- **Sire's Decree** — Speak one word; every construct you have ever built within earshot obeys it before any other order.

### Interred Engine

**Role:** Unkillable anchor — damage absorption · **Advances from:** Plateheart

*The Interred sealed the final seam years ago. Food, air, and sleep are administrative problems the armor has solved; the flesh inside is a rumor even to its owner. Fortresses request an Interred Engine by name when a gate must hold against something that should not exist.*

**Playstyle.** Immovable anchor play: occupy the one span of ground that matters, shrug off attrition that breaks sieges, and grow harder as the damage accumulates.

**The road to Interred Engine:**

1. Reach level 40 and seal the final seam. There is no removing the armor now; there is no you without it.
2. Endure a winter siege without food, air, or sleep, and decline to say which of you kept the watch.
3. Let a priest declare you dead, then enter the chapel and pay the funeral fee yourself.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Final Seam** — You cannot be extracted from your armor, and neither can fear, poison, drowning, or age reach inside.
- **Ossified Servos** — Below half health your plating fuses harder, and your damage reduction climbs as the damage accumulates.
- **Grave Pressure** — Your tread slows enemies in a radius, as if the ground itself had grown heavier.
- **Sepulcher Frame** — Once per day, entomb yourself in place: immune to all damage for a round while allies rally behind you.

### Cataclysmith

**Role:** Nuker — scheduled massive detonations · **Advances from:** Petardier

*Cataclysmiths hold the doctrine that any structure is a demolition site awaiting scheduling. War-colleges teach their fuse arithmetic; treaties name them individually. Each carries one sealed schematic — a yield they pray never to build — because a Cataclysmith without a worst case has simply not finished calculating.*

**Playstyle.** Setup-heavy nuking: spend turns in calculation and placement, then end the engagement, the fortification, and occasionally the local geography in a single scheduled instant.

**The road to Cataclysmith:**

1. Reach level 40 and erase a fortification so completely that cartographers dispute whether it was ever built.
2. Draft the yield you pray never to use, seal the schematic, and tell no one where it sleeps.
3. Lecture a war-college on fuse doctrine, and watch the generals go quiet when you begin to count.

**Abilities granted:**

- **City-Killer Shell** — Spend three rounds in assembly to fire ordnance that removes a structure from the battle and the map.
- **Synchronized Ruin** — Detonate up to five placed charges in exact sequence, walking a line of blasts across the field.
- **Overpressure** — Your detonations knock prone, deafen, and strip reactions from everything in the outer radius.
- **The Sealed Schematic** — Once in your life, unseal your worst calculation and build it; there is no second use, and no second fortress.

### Panacea Heretic

**Role:** Hybrid sustain — mutation and transfusion · **Advances from:** Crucible-Drinker

*Panacea Heretics cured what the temples called incurable and were excommunicated for the paperwork. Their bodies are running experiments — grafted limbs, borrowed lives, blood that heals whomever it touches with interesting side effects. The desperate seek them out; conclaves burn their formularies and quietly keep copies.*

**Playstyle.** Hybrid sustain and mutation: transfuse allies, bloom chimeric limbs for burst, and treat your own health as a flexible reagent.

**The road to Panacea Heretic:**

1. Reach level 40 and cure a plague the temples had already assigned to the will of the gods. Distribute the formula anyway.
2. Graft your first chimeric limb with your own steady hands, awake through every stitch of the surgery.
3. Stand trial before the conclave and leave excommunicated, with your formulary intact and your patients living.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Forbidden Formulary** — Brew elixirs the conclaves banned: borrowed lives, stolen years, and cures that outrage theology by working.
- **Chimeric Bloom** — Grow a grafted limb for one minute, gaining an additional attack or an additional pair of working hands.
- **Transfusion** — Pump your mutagenic blood into a dying ally, healing them fully and gifting them one random adaptation.
- **Excommunicate's Poise** — Curses, hexes, and consecrated ground no longer affect you; the church revoked its jurisdiction over your body.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### Thousandth Hand

**Role:** Army commander — total board control · **Advances from:** Engine-Sire

*Histories disagree on whether the Thousandth Hand is a person, an order, or an event. The truth is simpler and worse: a single will distributed through every frame it ever built, working in ten cities at once. Workshops wake when it passes. Nothing it has made has ever been truly destroyed.*

**Playstyle.** Total board presence: act through any construct on the field, raise the fallen ones into new frames, and make attrition a nonsense concept.

**The road to Thousandth Hand:**

1. Reach level 60 and pour your will into a thousand frames, until no single hammer in the world can find you.
2. Let your body sleep a full year while your hands keep building in ten cities.
3. Be referred to in the plural by frightened kings, and answer in the singular.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Distributed Will** — Your consciousness rides any construct you have built within a mile, acting through it as yourself.
- **Wake the Workshops** — Every dormant machine in the region rises and reports for assignment when you pass.
- **No Single Death** — If your body falls, your will migrates to the strongest surviving frame; you have not died, merely relocated.
- **Hands Beyond Counting** — Once per day, summon every construct bearing your maker's mark to a single battlefield.

### Brazen Saint

**Role:** Immortal bulwark — zone supremacy · **Advances from:** Interred Engine

*A Brazen Saint outlasted its own flesh and never abandoned the post. It has guarded one gate so long that the clergy added it to the litanies and pilgrims oil its feet. What remains inside the shell is debated; whether the gate has ever fallen is not.*

**Playstyle.** Zone supremacy: consecrate the ground you hold, armor the allies who share it, and continue fighting for a time even past your own death.

**The road to Brazen Saint:**

1. Reach level 60 and outlast the flesh entirely. When the last of you is brass, keep the vigil anyway.
2. Guard one gate for a generation, until pilgrims oil your feet and children swear oaths on your shadow.
3. Hear the clergy enter you into the litany of saints, and do not correct them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Canon of Brass** — The ground you guard becomes consecrated to endurance; allies standing on it gain your plating's resistances.
- **Vigil Past Death** — At zero health you keep fighting for ten rounds; what happens after the tenth has never been observed.
- **Pilgrim's Offering** — Oil, prayers, and relics left at your feet convert directly into repair and warding.
- **Litany Tread** — Your step tolls like a bell; enemies who hear three tolls fight at disadvantage against you.

### Appointed Sun

**Role:** Apocalypse burst — one perfect strike · **Advances from:** Cataclysmith

*The Appointed Sun announces its detonations in advance — sometimes by a year — and has never once been early, late, or stopped. Kings evacuate on its word alone. Historians divide certain wars into before and after an hour it chose, and the craters it left have names of their own.*

**Playstyle.** One perfect strike: telegraph an apocalypse, defend the countdown, and deliver a detonation that is less an attack than a calendar date.

**The road to Appointed Sun:**

1. Reach level 60 and announce a detonation a year in advance. Let kings evacuate. Fire it to the second.
2. Split the mountain armies broke against for a century, and end the war before its next winter.
3. Become the deadline. When historians write of the hour, ensure they mean you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Announcement** — Declare a detonation up to a year ahead; it cannot be defused, delayed, or hurried, including by you.
- **Solar Yield** — The declared blast detonates with the light and heat of a risen sun, exactly on schedule.
- **Perfect Fuse** — No charge of yours ever misfires, cooks off, or errs by even a second.
- **Afterglow** — Your craters remain hostile ground for a generation; enemies inside them burn, and allies do not.

### Azoth Incarnate

**Role:** Protean engine — every role, briefly · **Advances from:** Panacea Heretic

*The Azoth Incarnate solved alchemy's oldest problem by becoming the solution. Its body is first matter — every mutation it ever survived, wearable as a shape, holdable in a hand. It dissolves curses as demonstrations of principle and leaves drops of itself in sealed vials, each of which is also entirely it.*

**Playstyle.** Protean shapeshifting: flow between every form the line has learned, dissolve whatever binds, and answer each threat with the specific body that beats it.

**The road to Azoth Incarnate:**

1. Reach level 60 and complete the final distillation: reduce yourself to first matter, then reconstitute by will alone.
2. Dissolve a curse set by a god, in public, as a demonstration of principle.
3. Seal one drop of yourself in a vial for your successors, knowing the drop is also entirely you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Universal Solvent** — Your touch dissolves bindings — chains, wards, curses, contracts — reducing them to inert first matter.
- **Prima Materia** — Shift instantly between every mutation you have ever survived, wearing each body like a decanted shape.
- **Transmute Wound** — Convert incoming damage into a reagent pool, then spend it as healing, elixirs, or acid.
- **The Divided Vial** — Seal a drop of yourself in a vial; if your body is destroyed, you reconstitute from the drop, entire.
