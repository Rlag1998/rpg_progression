# 🌿 Druid

> *Speak for the green; wear what it lends you.*

**Archetype:** Spirit

Before the first wall there was an accord: the people would take only what the green could spare, and the green would remember them. Druids keep that fading contract. They speak for root and beast in the courts of men, and when words fail they borrow the forest's shapes and seasons — a bear's back, a winter's patience — and settle the account themselves.

## The Tree

```text
Druid (Lv 1)
├── Skinshifter (Lv 10)
│   ├── Clawsworn (Lv 25) → Bloodpelt (Lv 40) → First Predator (Lv 60)
│   └── Elderform (Lv 25) → Thousandskin (Lv 40) → The Unshaped (Lv 60)
└── Grovespeaker (Lv 10)
    ├── Thorn Shepherd (Lv 25) → Heartwood Regent (Lv 40) → Worldroot (Lv 60)
    └── Rotwright (Lv 25) → Carrion Gardener (Lv 40) → The Last Autumn (Lv 60)
```

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Druid

**Role:** Hybrid adept — spells, shapes, and support

*Druids are found, not recruited. A child who wanders home late with sap in her hair and a fox's patience in her eyes; a woodcutter who set the axe down mid-swing and never picked it up. The old accord chooses its keepers, and the circles teach the chosen only what they already half know.*

**Playstyle.** Versatile beginner: lash with thorns, mend with growth, and slip into small borrowed shapes. Weak at everything, present everywhere, learning which half of the accord calls loudest.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Answer the summons only you heard: leave the road at dusk and walk until the trees stop being scenery.
2. Sleep a full winter night against an elder oak and wake knowing one word of its slow language.
3. Keep a dying thing company until it finishes — no mercy stroke, no prayer — and understand why the circle watched you do it.
4. Swear the accord at the moot of three groves: take only what is spared, and answer when the green calls.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Thornlash** — A whip of living briar strikes the target and roots them in place for two seconds.
- **Second Skin** — Take a lean wolf-shape for ten seconds, trading spells for speed and a worrying bite.
- **Green Whisper** — Coax a wound closed with hastened growth, healing an ally over six seconds.
- **Read the Loam** — Learn everything that crossed this ground in the last day: numbers, weight, and wounds.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Skinshifter

**Role:** Frontline shapeshifter — adaptive melee brawler · **Advances from:** Druid

*The circle grants the second gift only to those who can come back from it. Skinshifters wear bear and wolf first, honest shapes with honest appetites, and learn the discipline of returning. Villages hire them against raiders and worse; the wise pay promptly, having seen what a patient person with a bear's arms can do.*

**Playstyle.** Adaptive frontline: open in bear to hold the line, break to wolf to run down the fleeing, and return to your own skin only when the work is done.

**The road to Skinshifter:**

1. Reach level 10 and run three nights with a wild pack, taking no kill and no shelter, until they stop watching you.
2. Wear the wolf until your own name sounds strange, then set it down and answer to that name anyway.
3. Let the circle sew your first pelt-cord and wear it always: a promise that the skin comes off.
4. Kill in a borrowed shape only once this year, for food, and eat all of it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ursa Mantle** — Assume bear-shape: doubled health, sweeping claws, and a taunt-roar that dares everything nearby to try.
- **Loping Shape** — Assume wolf-shape: gain sprint speed, and each bite leaves a bleed that stacks three times.
- **Marrow Memory** — Shifting shapes mends you; the body forgets a portion of its wounds with each change.
- **Hackles** — Sense hostile intent within thirty paces before it moves, even while wearing your own skin.

### Grovespeaker

**Role:** Zone controller — terrain, sustain, slow attrition · **Advances from:** Druid

*When a forest needs a voice at a treaty table, it grows one. Grovespeakers spend seasons in the moot-glades, learning the twin vocabularies of growth and rot, and emerge speaking for constituencies that measure debate in centuries. Loggers and land-hungry barons have learned that an ignored Grovespeaker's silence costs more than their words.*

**Playstyle.** Patient control: raise bracken walls, root the reckless, and let slow rot make your argument. You win fights the way forests do — by still being there.

**The road to Grovespeaker:**

1. Reach level 10 and sit a full session of the grove-moot without speaking; be the first human it does not adjourn for.
2. Plead a condemned grove's case before the men who hold the axes, and leave with the grove standing.
3. Tend one tree from seed through its first winter, and grieve honestly if you fail.
4. Learn the second vocabulary: find what the rot is saying beneath the loveliest glade, and repeat it without flinching.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Raise the Bracken** — Grow a wall of interwoven fern and thorn along a chosen line; it burns slowly and regrows quickly.
- **Root Verdict** — Roots erupt to bind up to three enemies for four seconds or until each is struck twice.
- **Sap Surge** — Flood an ally with rising sap: strong regeneration, and their next wound closes on its own.
- **First Rot** — Mark a foe for decay: armor softens and wounds fester, worsening over ten seconds.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Clawsworn

**Role:** Melee striker — bleeds and sustained pressure · **Advances from:** Skinshifter

*Clawsworn take a vow written in nothing but scars: the shape is a covenant, not a costume. They fight in fur more than skin, keep territories like wolves, and serve as their circles' answer to armored men. The vow's price is plain — kill only what you would eat, and mourn everything you kill.*

**Playstyle.** Committed melee: stalk, open with a hamstring, and stack bleeds until the target's options are kneeling or falling. Leaving beast-shape mid-fight feels like retreat.

**The road to Clawsworn:**

1. Reach level 25 and bring down a stag in wolf-shape using no magic but the shape itself.
2. Take a territory of your own and hold it one full season against everything that contests it, in fur, alone.
3. Fight a rival predator to submission and let it live, so the wild learns you kill only to eat.
4. Swear the claw-vow over your own spilled blood: the shape is covenant, not costume.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Half-Pelt** — Take a hybrid war-shape for twenty seconds: clawed hands, human cunning, and neither form's weaknesses.
- **Hamstring Rite** — A low, precise tear slows the target and deepens the bleeding of every later wound.
- **Scent of Openings** — Attacks against bleeding enemies gain critical chance for each bleed they carry.
- **Kill-Memory** — Executing a bleeding foe below one-quarter health refunds Half-Pelt and heals you for the remainder.

### Elderform

**Role:** Flex combatant — form-cycling answers to everything · **Advances from:** Skinshifter

*The earth keeps records in bone-seams and amber, and the Elderform learn to read them aloud. They petition extinct shapes out of deep time — river-things with too many jaws, horn-backed grazers like moving hills — and pay in years of their own. Scholars call the practice necromancy's cousin. Druids call it remembering on the land's behalf.*

**Playstyle.** Form-cycling flexibility: answer each threat with a shape the world forgot, spending remembered years as your resource. Never the same beast twice in one fight.

**The road to Elderform:**

1. Reach level 25 and descend to a bone-seam where the earth keeps its dead shapes; sleep among them until one dreams of you.
2. Carry a fossil a hundred leagues to where the creature last lived, and speak its extinction aloud there.
3. Wear an elder shape for one full day and pay its tithe of years without bargaining.
4. Open your bone-ledger: three shapes remembered, named, and promised they will walk again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Fossil Recall** — Assume an extinct form from your bone-ledger; each grants a body no living beast can match.
- **Antediluvian Hide** — While in an elder shape, ignore the first three blows from every new attacker; old skin has learned.
- **Shape Debt** — Swapping elder forms mid-combat refunds half the cost and staggers enemies who witness it.
- **Unwalked Gait** — Briefly take a shape without bones — spore-haze, eel-shadow — and pass through spaces bodies cannot.

### Thorn Shepherd

**Role:** Battlefield architect — walls, lanes, area denial · **Advances from:** Grovespeaker

*Thorn Shepherds herd bramble the way others herd sheep, driving hedge-flocks along old boundary lines that maps stopped honoring. Border villages leave them milk and iron nails; ambitious surveyors leave them alone. Where a Shepherd has walked, fields keep their old shapes, roads narrow politely, and fences grow back overnight with opinions.*

**Playstyle.** Living-terrain control: drive briar droves across the field, pen enemies into kill-lanes, and graft armor onto allies. The battlefield's geometry is yours to rewrite.

**The road to Thorn Shepherd:**

1. Reach level 25 and raise a hedge around a burning village faster than the fire can walk.
2. Drive a bramble-flock along a forgotten boundary for a month, sleeping inside it, losing not one cane.
3. Face a surveyor's armed crew at a marker stone and turn them back without a single death.
4. Learn each thorn's name for hunger, and feed your flock only what the accord allows.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Briar Drove** — Drive a moving wall of thorn across the field, carrying enemies with it and shredding those who resist.
- **Graft** — Bind living wood over an ally's wounds: temporary armor that blooms, then heals as it sheds.
- **Seed the Breach** — Hurl a seed that erupts instantly into full-grown hedge, sealing a gap, gate, or doorway.
- **Pollard** — Prune an enemy's momentum: their next three attacks are cut back to half damage.

### Rotwright

**Role:** Attrition caster — decay, links, anti-healing · **Advances from:** Grovespeaker

*Rotwrights work the accord's unglamorous half: everything owed back to the soil, collected on schedule. They read mold like scripture and keep the mycelial ledgers that link forest to forest underground. Grain-lords hate them; healers quietly consult them. A Rotwright at a deathbed is not an omen — it is a bookkeeping.*

**Playstyle.** Attrition and inevitability: seed decay early, thread your enemies through the ledger, and let time do its honest work while you stay patient.

**The road to Rotwright:**

1. Reach level 25 and sit with one felled oak from windfall to soil, keeping a ledger of everything that eats it.
2. Trace a mycelial thread between two forests a day's ride apart, on foot, never losing it.
3. Break a false embalming: give a hoarded corpse back to the ground over its family's objections, gently.
4. Eat only what the rot offers you for one season, and thrive.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wet Autumn** — Call a soft, spore-heavy rain over an area; everything hostile inside decays faster each second it stays.
- **Mycelial Ledger** — Thread enemies together underground; damage dealt to one is partly owed by all.
- **Sweet Rot** — Compost an active decay effect on an enemy into healing for a nearby ally.
- **Rust Sermon** — Preach to metal: armor, blades, and hinges within earshot begin to flake and fail.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Bloodpelt

**Role:** Apex melee — frenzy, pursuit, self-sustain · **Advances from:** Clawsworn

*Bloodpelts are what circles send when a monster starts hunting men. The pelt-cord has been re-sewn so many times it is mostly scar, and the shape comes off slower each year — a price accounted, not hidden. Lords who joke about leashing one are corrected exactly once, usually by their own huntsmen, quietly.*

**Playstyle.** Apex melee: enter the frenzy window, drink wounds as fuel, and refuse every disengage. Nothing you have marked escapes; little you catch survives.

**The road to Bloodpelt:**

1. Reach level 40 and take the hunt no bounty could close — the beast that eats hunters — wearing the shape it fears.
2. Let the circle re-sew your pelt-cord with your own sinew, and do not cry out.
3. Spend an entire winter as beast, and return in spring answering to your name on the first call.
4. Refuse a king's leash in his own hall, in your own skin, politely.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Season of Teeth** — For twelve seconds, attack speed climbs with every bleed you inflict, without ceiling.
- **Wound-Drinker** — Every bleed tick on every enemy you wounded returns a portion to you as health.
- **Broken Chase** — Nothing slows, roots, or frightens you while pursuing a marked quarry.
- **Red Quiet** — On a kill, terror settles over nearby foes; those who flee bleed as if bitten.

### Thousandskin

**Role:** Shape economy — evasion, decoys, total flexibility · **Advances from:** Elderform

*The Thousandskin keep bound ledgers of shapes the way misers keep coin. They can be the crows above a gallows, the eel beneath the ferry, the grey dog at a traitor's hearth. Circles deploy them when truth must be witnessed by something nobody lies in front of. Their own faces, folded away and rarely worn, stop aging.*

**Playstyle.** Shape economy: rotate impossible forms to answer every threat, scatter into swarms to shed damage, and bank shapes like currency for the decisive moment.

**The road to Thousandskin:**

1. Reach level 40 and hold nine shapes through a single hour of battle, ending in your own, unbled.
2. Complete a page of your ledger no other druid can read, and bind it in your own shed skin.
3. Live a month as something small and eaten-of — mouse, minnow, moth — and come back unbitter.
4. Witness a crime as three creatures at once, and give testimony the judges cannot dismiss.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Murmuration** — Scatter into a wheeling flock for six seconds; blows pass through, and you reform anywhere within it.
- **Borrowed Centuries** — Chain three elder forms in sequence without cost; the third arrives with doubled power.
- **Skinfall** — Step out of your current shape, leaving it fighting as a decoy for eight seconds.
- **The Unlisted Shape** — Once a day, become a shape you have never recorded; not even you know its limits until worn.

### Heartwood Regent

**Role:** Siege controller — living-terrain warfare at scale · **Advances from:** Thorn Shepherd

*A Heartwood Regent holds regency over standing forests the way stewards hold castles for absent kings — except the forest is awake, and consulted. They count rings the way clerks count taxes, wake heartwood for war only when the moot consents, and have marched whole treelines to new rivers. Two kingdoms keep treaties with individual Regents by name.*

**Playstyle.** Grand-scale control: wake the stand, give the march order, and fight from inside a moving forest. Your army was always standing there; now it agrees to walk.

**The road to Heartwood Regent:**

1. Reach level 40 and persuade a first-growth forest to walk one league and back without losing a single elder.
2. Sit regency through a moot that lasts a human year, and cast your one vote well.
3. Turn back fire, axe, and royal charter from your wardship in the same season.
4. Bury your predecessor among the roots and carry their ring-staff until the forest stops grieving.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wake the Stand** — Rouse every tree in the grove to fight; the elders swing hardest and remember grudges.
- **March Order** — The woken forest advances as a line, carrying allies in its canopy and grinding stonework beneath.
- **Ringcount** — Armor yourself in accumulated years: gain one ring of damage reduction per century of forest present.
- **Crown Verdict** — The canopy closes overhead: no flight, no sky-sight, and arrows lose their way in the dark.

### Carrion Gardener

**Role:** Endgame attrition — corpse-blooms and structural decay · **Advances from:** Rotwright

*Carrion Gardeners walk battlefields after the crows and before the historians. Their order holds a single charter: nothing wasted, no plague raised, no glory left standing long enough to fester. Generals petition to keep their dead; the Gardeners decline with terrible courtesy. Meadows across three provinces bloom where their worst arguments were won.*

**Playstyle.** Sanctified attrition: seed the field early, let every death feed your blooms, and grow stronger as the battle lengthens. Endings are your element.

**The road to Carrion Gardener:**

1. Reach level 40 and turn a fresh battlefield to meadow in one season, raising no plague and keeping no trophy.
2. Decline a weeping general's petition for his son's body, and be gentle enough that he thanks you within the year.
3. Take the charter-vow over an open grave: nothing wasted, nothing raised.
4. Garden the ruin of a plague-town until children can be raised on what grows there.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Field Tithe** — Each corpse on the field sprouts a spore-bloom that fights for you until it is spent.
- **Gentle Ruin** — Touch a wall, engine, or gate; its next decades of decay arrive over ten seconds.
- **Patient Cloud** — Loose a drifting spore-fog: enemies inside weaken steadily and cannot regain health.
- **Terrible Courtesy** — Offer one enemy a painless withering; refusal makes every decay effect on them permanent.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### First Predator

**Role:** Unstoppable hunter — infinite-scaling single-target pursuit · **Advances from:** Bloodpelt

*The First Predator wears the shape every prey animal remembers without being taught. Wolves lie down when it passes; nothing in the wild will meet its eye. Circles invoke it perhaps once a generation, when something has begun hunting the world itself, and afterward the histories go carefully quiet about what was seen.*

**Playstyle.** Ultimate pursuit: mark your quarry, and geography, armies, and time stop mattering. Everything else in the fight is scenery between you and the throat.

**The road to First Predator:**

1. At level 60, run the Long Hunt: pursue one quarry through three seasons without losing the trail for a single night.
2. Find the den where the first hunger dreams, and take its shape without waking it.
3. Spare something at the end of a perfect hunt, once, to prove the shape has not eaten the vow.
4. Let the wild rename you, and answer to nothing men call you afterward.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Original Hunger** — Your damage climbs the longer a hunt lasts, without cap, resetting only at the kill.
- **The Oldest Chase** — Mark one quarry; while you live it cannot become untrackable, unreachable, or unkillable by any means.
- **All Fangs Answer** — Every predator within a day's travel converges to hunt beside you until the quarry falls.
- **Prey's Memory** — Enemies who see you suffer the fear their ancestors learned; most kneel, some flee, none aim well.

### The Unshaped

**Role:** Omniform — any role at any moment · **Advances from:** Thousandskin

*The Unshaped gave their birth-shape back to the earth and did not replace it. What remains prefers forms the way weather prefers valleys — habitually, not necessarily. Two circles keep an empty chair for one; whether the chair has ever actually been empty is a question druids find funnier than anyone else does.*

**Playstyle.** Total fluidity: answer every threat by becoming its counter, flow between shapes without pause, and treat being a target as an optional condition.

**The road to The Unshaped:**

1. At level 60, give your birth-shape to the earth at the moot of three groves, and walk away as whatever comes next.
2. Spend a year borrowing a different shape each dawn, and want none of them back.
3. Pass through the eye of a needle, honestly, before witnesses.
4. Sit in the empty chair your circle keeps, and let them wonder ever after whether you were there.

**Abilities granted:**

- **No True Body** — Between shapes you are briefly nothing; blows, curses, and prophecies pass through.
- **Everything, Once** — Assume the exact form of any creature you can see, including its abilities, for twenty seconds.
- **Signature Absence** — Effects that target a creature fail to find you unless you consent.
- **Refused Finality** — The first killing blow each day finds you already being something it cannot kill.

### Worldroot

**Role:** Continental controller — the forest itself fights · **Advances from:** Heartwood Regent

*Histories disagree about whether the Worldroot is a person any longer. Their heart is planted in the oldest grove; their pulse comes up through taproots on three coasts. When a forest anywhere is put to the torch, somewhere the Worldroot's body burns too, and the reply arrives through the ground, patient and total.*

**Playstyle.** Continental presence: fight from every rooted thing at once, surfacing wherever the green touches. Battles are local affairs; you have stopped being local.

**The road to Worldroot:**

1. At level 60, plant your living heart beneath the oldest grove and learn to wake in every tree it touches.
2. Answer a burning on a coast you have never seen, through the ground, within a day.
3. Feed a famine from your own rings and let no one learn whom to thank.
4. Outgrow your name: when the histories argue whether you were ever one person, decline to settle it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Continental Grip** — Roots answer you anywhere soil exists, seizing armies a kingdom away from where you stand.
- **The Green Immensity** — For one minute, all terrain in sight becomes forest, and the forest is on your side.
- **Sap of Ages** — Every ally touching earth regenerates as though sleeping safe in high summer.
- **One Long Ring** — Your death is deferred to the end of the current century; damage is recorded, not suffered.

### The Last Autumn

**Role:** Inevitability aura — the fight ages around you · **Advances from:** Carrion Gardener

*Every few centuries the accord requires a final instrument, and a Rotwright's line produces one. The Last Autumn carries the year's fourth season with them; where they linger, harvests hurry, iron blushes, and empires feel their age. The histories record three. Each is remembered less as a person than as a very reasonable ending.*

**Playstyle.** Inevitability incarnate: your presence is the mechanic. Everything in the fight ripens toward its end; you decide only the order, and the mercy.

**The road to The Last Autumn:**

1. At level 60, ask the turning year for its last season and carry it ever afterward, so autumn arrives wherever you stand.
2. End something beloved that outlived its kindness — an orchard, an order, a king's long peace — and be right.
3. Walk unarmed through the empire that burned the moot-glades, and leave it a legend of gentle, total subsidence.
4. Refuse the winter: prove three times that you are the ending before the ending, and nothing further.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Everything Ripens** — Aura: all cooldowns, wounds, poisons, and lifespans within sight run measurably faster.
- **Kingdom Compost** — Declare a structure finished: it settles into fertile loam over one minute, regardless of stone or ward.
- **The Long Fall** — Leaves fall out of season across a chosen army; morale, armor, and certainty fall with them.
- **Mercy of Mulch** — A fallen ally is taken into the soil and returned at dusk, alive, smelling of rain.
