# 🎻 Bard

> *The right word, placed rightly, moves armies and ages.*

**Archetype:** Cunning

The Bard collects what others overlook — songs, secrets, debts of gratitude, the exact words a person cannot refuse. Some carry their voices to war and hold shield-walls together with a cadence; others whisper in archives and anterooms, ending wars before the first arrow. All of them understand the same truth: a word placed correctly moves more than any lever, and history belongs to whoever phrases it.

## The Tree

```text
Bard (Lv 1)
├── War-Skald (Lv 10)
│   ├── Shieldsinger (Lv 25) → Anthemwright (Lv 40) → Voice of Legions (Lv 60)
│   └── Dirgecaller (Lv 25) → Threnodist (Lv 40) → Knell of Empires (Lv 60)
└── Veiled Chronicler (Lv 10)
    ├── Namebinder (Lv 25) → Redactor (Lv 40) → The Unwritten (Lv 60)
    └── Silvertongue (Lv 25) → Kingmaker (Lv 40) → Author of Ages (Lv 60)
```

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Bard

**Role:** Support — versatile inspiration and control

*Every road-worn troupe carries one: the apprentice who learned that a crowd can be tuned like a lute. Bards trade in songs, secrets, and applause, and they know the market value of each. Most die as footnotes in someone else's ballad. A few learn to write the ballad first.*

**Playstyle.** Weave buffs and jeers between verses, patching allies' nerve and picking at enemies' composure while staying one clever step away from the front line.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Learn three hundred songs by ear, and be beaten once for singing the wrong one in the wrong hall.
2. Play for a crowd that came to jeer, and leave them silent.
3. Trade your first secret for supper, and understand what you have actually sold.
4. Bury your teacher with the song they hated, because it was the true one.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Opening Chord** — Begin combat with a flourish that grants nearby allies bonus initiative and a brief surge of morale.
- **Cutting Couplet** — Deliver a barbed verse that deals psychic damage and leaves the target flinching, weakening its next attack.
- **Ear for Trouble** — Passively sense lies and hidden hostility in conversation; act first against any ambush you overheard coming.
- **Borrowed Refrain** — Mimic the last ally ability you witnessed at reduced potency, once per rest.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### War-Skald

**Role:** Frontline support — formation buffs and morale · **Advances from:** Bard

*War-skalds march where the banners do, armored in nothing but nerve, keeping time for the shield-wall with drum and bellowed verse. Captains requisition them like rations. The soldiers know the arithmetic: a line that sings holds three heartbeats longer than a line that does not, and most battles are decided in two.*

**Playstyle.** Stand in the second rank and conduct the battle, layering marching cadences that harden allies and counter-verses that sap enemy charges. Your voice is the front line's spine.

**The road to War-Skald:**

1. Reach level 10, then enlist with a company that has lost its standard, and sing it a new one.
2. Keep the cadence through a full rout without once looking behind you.
3. Learn the death-songs of five peoples, so that no soldier under your voice dies to silence.
4. Carry the drum out of a defeat when stronger backs carried nothing.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Shield-Cadence** — Chant a marching meter that grants allies in formation bonus block and immunity to the first fear effect.
- **Drumfire** — Strike your war-drum to reset one ally's broken stance and pull them back into the line.
- **Skald's Bellow** — A shout that carries over battle noise; every ally who hears it regains a spent reaction.
- **Counter-Verse** — Answer an enemy warcry with practiced mockery, cancelling its morale bonus before it lands.

### Veiled Chronicler

**Role:** Control support — charm, debuffs, information warfare · **Advances from:** Bard

*Veiled Chroniclers keep the histories nobody commissioned: the bastard lines, the buried treaties, the words kings spoke before the witnesses died. They learn early that lore is a lever and a noose at once. Courts host them nervously, the way one hosts a fire — useful, warming, and watched.*

**Playstyle.** Fight before the fight begins: gather names, plant doubts, charm sentries. In combat, unmake enemy plans with bindings and misdirection rather than trading blows.

**The road to Veiled Chronicler:**

1. Reach level 10 and recite, unprompted, a history its owners paid to have forgotten.
2. Spend a season as a servant in a great house and leave knowing which floorboards listen.
3. End one feud with three sentences, spoken to the right person at the wrong hour.
4. Copy a banned chronicle by candlelight, then decide alone who is permitted to read it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Marginalia** — Recall one obscure fact about any named creature or place; gain advantage on your next action exploiting it.
- **Hushword** — Speak a syllable of old silence; one target cannot cast, command, or call for aid for a round.
- **False Provenance** — Forge a memory into a listener's recent past; they misremember one detail of the last hour.
- **Thread of Rumor** — Seed a rumor in a settlement; within a day it returns grown, carrying truths its carriers never meant to share.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Shieldsinger

**Role:** Defensive support — mitigation and battlefield anchoring · **Advances from:** War-Skald

*Shieldsingers are called wall-mortar by the infantry, and they take it as an honor. Their verses set into a standing line like cooling bronze, binding shield to shield until a hundred bodies breathe as one. Where a Shieldsinger plants their feet, generals redraw their maps around that point.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor a position and refuse the enemy. Layer sustained wards over allies who hold their ground, and punish anything that breaks formation — theirs or yours.

**The road to Shieldsinger:**

1. Reach level 25, then hold a breach with your voice while the shields behind you are still forming.
2. Sing an eight-hour siege watch without water, and let no one on the wall sleep afraid.
3. Take the wound meant for the line and finish the stanza before you bind it.
4. Refuse a general's order to retreat, and be right.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Mortar-Hymn** — Allies standing adjacent to one another share incoming damage and gain resistance while the hymn is held.
- **Planted Verse** — Choose ground; allies upon it cannot be moved against their will and mend slightly at each verse's turn.
- **Hold the Vowel** — Sustain a single note; while held, the first killing blow against each warded ally leaves them at one hit point instead.
- **Ring of Voices** — Once per battle, fallen allies within the song stand again with a portion of health, provided the line still holds.

### Dirgecaller

**Role:** Offensive support — fear, attrition, siege-breaking · **Advances from:** War-Skald

*Dirgecallers sing at armies the songs meant for their graves, and soldiers who hear their own funeral early tend to attend it. Besieged cities dread them more than engines; stone resists a ram, but a garrison's nerve is masonry of a softer kind. Their arrival at a wall is itself an ultimatum.*

**Playstyle.** Break the enemy before the charge does. Stack dread on packed formations, shatter morale thresholds, and turn organized resistance into a stampede pointed the wrong way.

**The road to Dirgecaller:**

1. Reach level 25 and end a siege without a single ladder touching the wall.
2. Learn what a city sounds like the night before it surrenders, and be the one who taught it.
3. Sing a veteran company into flight, then walk their empty line slowly.
4. Attend the funerals your songs began. All of them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Premature Requiem** — Name a target and sing their funeral; they take escalating psychic damage each round they can hear you.
- **Sapper's Threnody** — A low sustained tone that cracks mortar and works fortification stones loose over several minutes.
- **Rout-Note** — Enemies below half morale who hear this discord must flee toward their own rear lines, through their allies.
- **Grave-Hush** — Silence falls across a wide radius; enemy commands go unheard while your dirge alone carries.

### Namebinder

**Role:** Controller — single-target binding and compulsion · **Advances from:** Veiled Chronicler

*Namebinders collect what things are actually called: the syllables a river answers to, the word a demon signed before witnesses. To hold a true name is to hold a leash. They speak carefully in company, because they know precisely what a careless sentence can be made to do.*

**Playstyle.** Hunt the name, then own the fight. Slow to open, devastating once a target is named — command it, unmake it, or forbid it with a single word.

**The road to Namebinder:**

1. Reach level 25 and hear your own true name from a stranger's mouth, and survive the hearing.
2. Bind a lesser spirit by name alone, without circle, salt, or blade.
3. Trade a year of your singing voice for one syllable of a mountain's name.
4. Speak a true name in anger exactly once, and let what follows teach you never again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Utterance of Binding** — Speak a learned true name; the named creature obeys one one-word command with no saving throw.
- **Name-Sounding** — Probe a target with test-syllables over three rounds to uncover fragments of its true name.
- **Sealed Syllable** — Lock one word out of a target's mouth; they cannot speak it, cast with it, or write it.
- **Leash of Letters** — A creature you have named cannot move beyond thirty paces of you until dawn.

### Silvertongue

**Role:** Social controller — charm, leverage, war-avoidance · **Advances from:** Veiled Chronicler

*Silvertongues end wars in anterooms, over wine nobody quite remembers pouring. They are lent between courts like fine instruments, and every ruler who borrows one wonders, correctly, what tune was played upon them in return. The great treaties of the age bear their fingerprints and never their names.*

**Playstyle.** Win the encounter before initiative is rolled. Charm, leverage, and staged concessions turn enemies into hosts; in open combat, redirect aggression rather than absorbing it.

**The road to Silvertongue:**

1. Reach level 25, then talk your way out of an execution you deserved.
2. Broker peace between two houses while carrying a knife-scar from each.
3. Convince a mercenary company to march home unpaid, and have them thank you at the gate.
4. Sit across from a tyrant, speak for one hour, and leave the room owning the war's ending.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Perfect Concession** — Offer surrender terms so precisely fitted that the enemy commander accepts conditions you wrote for them.
- **Borrowed Loyalty** — Charm a target into treating you as a trusted advisor for an hour; afterward they rationalize everything you asked.
- **Turn the Blade** — An enemy who strikes at you redirects the blow to a target you nominate with a whispered suggestion.
- **Room Reading** — Instantly learn the strongest desire and sorest grievance of every person in a gathering.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Anthemwright

**Role:** Army-scale support — persistent zone anthems · **Advances from:** Shieldsinger

*Anthemwrights no longer sing songs; they build them, verse-engineered for a single army the way a smith fits a cuirass. A finished anthem outlives its commission — soldiers' grandchildren hum it in peacetime, and it still remembers how to close a shield-wall. Kingdoms count themselves fortunate to field three at once.*

**Playstyle.** Compose before battle, conduct during it. Deploy pre-built anthem structures that persist across the field, letting whole formations fight a measure above their nature.

**The road to Anthemwright:**

1. Reach level 40 and compose an anthem that holds a line after your death is reported. Correct the report later, in person.
2. Submit your masterwork to the circle of Anthemwrights and defend every syllable through three nights of hostile revision.
3. Retire a broken company's old anthem with full honors before you write them the new one.
4. Refuse a paying crown once, because the song they asked for was a lie.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Forged Anthem** — Spend a week composing for a specific company; while any ten of its members sing, all gain elite-tier discipline and defenses.
- **Bridge Verse** — Link two separated formations; they count as adjacent for morale and share the stronger unit's resolve.
- **Standing Coda** — Your anthem persists on the field for an hour after you fall silent, or after you fall.
- **Tempering Silence** — One measured pause; allies shed all fear, exhaustion, and one grievous condition as the anthem resumes.

### Threnodist

**Role:** Strategic debuffer — mass dread and unmaking · **Advances from:** Dirgecaller

*There is a short list of songs that are illegal to perform in three empires. Threnodists wrote all of them. Each carries a personal lament, years in the making and never yet sung whole; commanders have negotiated surrenders simply to keep it unfinished. Their gray robes empty streets faster than plague-bells.*

**Playstyle.** Escalate dread on a strategic scale. Long-form lament movements degrade an entire army's cohesion, health, and works, building toward a finale few commanders permit to arrive.

**The road to Threnodist:**

1. Reach level 40, then begin composing your own lament, knowing you may never be permitted to finish it.
2. Be named in an empire's law by your work and not your name.
3. Sing the second movement over a fortress, and count the gates that stand open by morning.
4. Grant mercy once when the finale was owed, and carry what that mercy costs.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Unfinished Lament** — Perform successive movements across rounds; each deepens army-wide penalties, and the final movement breaks any force still listening.
- **Toll for the Living** — Every enemy who has killed today hears a bell only they can hear; the guilty strike last and flinch first.
- **Ash Registry** — Name a fortification within the lament; its garrison abandons posts nightly until the walls stand unwatched.
- **Mercy Clause** — End any of your dirges instantly for all who ground arms; those who surrendered are immune to your songs until dawn.

### Redactor

**Role:** Existential controller — erasure and denial · **Advances from:** Namebinder

*Redactors unmake the written and the spoken. Where a Namebinder holds a leash, a Redactor cuts the collar out of the world: heresies un-taught, treaties un-signed, whole men reduced to a smudge in their mothers' memory. The great archives keep a lamp burning against them. It does not help.*

**Playstyle.** Delete the enemy's options. Strip abilities, spells, and identities from targets mid-fight; the strongest foes are simply those with the most to lose.

**The road to Redactor:**

1. Reach level 40 and erase a heresy so completely that its inquisitors forget their own occupation.
2. Un-write one page of your own history and live with the seam.
3. Stand in the Archive of Echoes and learn to hear which of its silences are yours.
4. Refuse to erase a name once, though the payment was a kingdom, because the record was true.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Strikethrough** — Erase one of a target's known abilities for the encounter; legendary creatures lose it for a full day.
- **Excision** — Remove a target's name from every mind within a mile; its allies stop taking its orders.
- **Blank Page** — Become unrecorded for ten minutes; scrying, memory, and written accounts cannot hold you.
- **Errata** — Rewrite one small fact declared aloud this scene — a password, an order, a price — and the world complies.

### Kingmaker

**Role:** Grand manipulator — favors, factions, proxy warfare · **Advances from:** Silvertongue

*Kingmakers have stopped attending wars; they attend successions. Each keeps a quiet ledger of debts owed by the crowned, collected in policy rather than coin. Three reigning monarchs are commonly agreed to be Kingmaker work, though the guilds of heralds dispute, very carefully, which three.*

**Playstyle.** Play the campaign, not the encounter. Call in owed favors — armies, pardons, poisonings — and enter combat only when a debt requires collecting in person.

**The road to Kingmaker:**

1. Reach level 40 and seat your first ruler, then decline the title they offer you.
2. Hold a debt for a decade without once mentioning it.
3. Unmake a tyrant with paperwork, witnesses, and one well-timed silence.
4. Survive being owed too much by someone who would rather cancel the debt with a knife.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ledger of Crowns** — Call in a stored favor from a ruler; receive troops, sanctuary, or a signature within a day.
- **Whispered Succession** — Over a week of court-work, redirect an inheritance, appointment, or command to a candidate of your choosing.
- **Diplomatic Poise** — Creatures that understand speech cannot strike you first in an encounter where you have not drawn a weapon.
- **The Long Toast** — During any shared meal, learn one secret each guest would trade their honor to protect.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### Voice of Legions

**Role:** Mythic army-wide aura — unbreakable formations · **Advances from:** Anthemwright

*The histories record perhaps five Voices of Legions, and argue about two of them. When one sings, an army becomes a single instrument: ten thousand shields tuned to one pitch, fear engineered out of the design. Old soldiers swear the Song at Vessa's Ford still holds the ford, decades after its singer fell.*

**Playstyle.** You are the battle's tempo. Every allied soul in earshot fights within your meter — unbreakable, synchronized, and, for as long as you sing, slightly more than human.

**The road to Voice of Legions:**

1. Reach level 60, then hold a field where every officer has fallen, conducting an army whose only commander is the song.
2. Have a truce called because both armies were singing your verse.
3. Teach the anthem to a child, a veteran, and an enemy, and hear all three keep it true.
4. Enter a history you did not commission, and find it needed no exaggeration.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ten Thousand, One Throat** — All allies who can hear you share the highest morale, discipline, and resolve found among them.
- **The Held Line** — Declare a line upon the earth; while you sing, no ally behind it can die, only fall unconscious.
- **Vessa's Measure** — Once per war, the enemy army hears its own marching song played back, perfected, and hesitates a full round.
- **Song Without Singer** — Should you fall mid-battle, your anthem continues from every allied mouth until the field is decided.

### Knell of Empires

**Role:** Mythic dread — campaign-ending finales · **Advances from:** Threnodist

*A Knell of Empires is less a person than a precedent. Chronicles credit the fall of Vhoria to eleven days of a single voice outside its walls, with no arrow loosed. Rulers keep the names of living Knells in sealed letters, opened only to learn what must never be provoked.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive as an ending. Your presence alone degrades enemy resolve at strategic range; your finished laments retire armies, dynasties, and the appetite for war itself.

**The road to Knell of Empires:**

1. Reach level 60, then finish the lament you began decades ago, and choose whom it is for.
2. End an empire's war without an army — yours or theirs — taking the field.
3. Hear your name written into a treaty as a clause: that you will not be sent.
4. Sing once, at the last, for the dead of your own making, and mean it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Finished Lament** — Complete your life's dirge over three days against one realm; its will to make war ends for a generation.
- **Eleven Days of Vhoria** — Besiege a fortification with voice alone; each dawn, a portion of its garrison lays down arms and walks out.
- **Funeral for the Future** — Show one commander their own succession dying within the song; they seek terms or break entirely.
- **Bell Unrung** — Any who have heard your finale and lived can never again be rallied against you.

### The Unwritten

**Role:** Mythic erasure — editing what is real · **Advances from:** Redactor

*The Unwritten cut their own names out of the world's record and stepped through the gap. Nothing remembers them longer than a breath, which makes them the only editors history cannot flatter or bribe. Scholars infer their existence from the seams: wars that stop mid-sentence, tyrants nobody recalls obeying.*

**Playstyle.** Exist in the margins. Unwrite enemies, events, and consequences at will; the fight itself is trivial — choosing what deserves to remain in the record is not.

**The road to The Unwritten:**

1. Reach level 60, then unwrite your own name and watch your mother's eyes pass over you.
2. Erase a war so cleanly that its widows wake merely sad, not knowing why.
3. Meet another Unwritten in the margins, and agree on nothing except the rules.
4. Choose one true, terrible thing and let it stand in the record forever, because someone must.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Total Redaction** — Remove a creature from the record entirely; it persists, but nothing can remember, follow, obey, or mourn it.
- **The Gap** — Step into an erased moment; you are untargetable and undetectable until you act upon the world.
- **Restored Passage** — Un-erase one thing you or any Redactor destroyed; the truth returns to every mind that once held it.
- **Authorial Doubt** — Force a legendary enemy to briefly disbelieve its own existence, losing all actions for a round.

### Author of Ages

**Role:** Mythic orchestrator — history as an instrument · **Advances from:** Kingmaker

*An Author of Ages no longer moves rulers; they move the idea of rule. Their work is measured in centuries: a philosophy seeded in a border town, a marriage arranged two generations early, a war scheduled like a harvest. Historians call certain eras well-written. They do not know how right they are.*

**Playstyle.** Operate at the scale of eras. Every faction is a character you are developing; combat is a plot device, deployed sparingly and always foreshadowed.

**The road to Author of Ages:**

1. Reach level 60, then complete a design your predecessor began before your grandmother was born.
2. Start a renaissance with a rumor and a scholarship.
3. Watch a war you scheduled forty years ago arrive on time, and feel nothing you did not plan to feel.
4. Write the age's ending in advance, seal it, and be right.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Draft of the Century** — Set a hundred-year design in motion; once per age, declare one historical outcome inevitable and watch events comply.
- **Recurring Character** — Any person you have spoken with for an hour can be activated as an ally decades later, unaware they were written.
- **Foreshadowing** — Declare an event aloud; within a year, circumstances bend until it occurs at a moment of your choosing.
- **Narrative Weight** — You cannot die in a way history would find meaningless; fatal blows in trivial scenes simply fail.
