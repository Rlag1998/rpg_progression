# 💀 Necromancer

> *Death is a resource. Spend it wisely.*

**Archetype:** Esoteric

Every library keeps one shelf chained, and the Necromancer has read it twice. They begin as scholars of the body's last hour and end as auditors of mortality itself — raising the fallen as labor and infantry, rendering souls into fuel and testimony, and filing their own death under pending. Feared not for cruelty, but for bookkeeping: nothing they love stays buried, and nothing they need stays dead.

## The Tree

```text
Necromancer (Lv 1)
├── Corpsebinder (Lv 10)
│   ├── Ossuary Captain (Lv 25) → Gravemarshal (Lv 40) → Sovereign of the Risen (Lv 60)
│   └── Gravetide Caller (Lv 25) → Charnel Shepherd (Lv 40) → The Unnumbered (Lv 60)
└── Pale Scholar (Lv 10)
    ├── Vesselwright (Lv 25) → Lich Ascendant (Lv 40) → Archlich (Lv 60)
    └── Soulchandler (Lv 25) → Usurer of Souls (Lv 40) → The Wakelight (Lv 60)
```

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Necromancer

**Role:** Summoner — attrition caster with expendable minions

*Most necromancers begin as failed physicians, grave clerks, or students who asked the anatomy lecturer one question too many. Universities expel them; cemeteries employ them. The rank means little more than survival — a scholar who has raised one servant, buried one mistake, and learned that the dead answer politely if the question is precise.*

**Playstyle.** Fight through a single raised servant while draining life from range; trade the minion for time, rebuild it from whatever the battle leaves behind.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Read the chained book to its last page, and let no librarian catch you weeping at the margins.
2. Sit a night's vigil with an unclaimed corpse and learn its name without being told.
3. Raise your first servant from a pauper's grave, and pay the grave back with honest coin.
4. Be expelled, disowned, or declared dead — whichever your city requires — and keep studying anyway.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Exhume** — Raise one corpse as a shambling servant that fights until destroyed or dawn, whichever comes first.
- **Last Breath** — Siphon vitality from a wounded enemy; the drain doubles against targets below a quarter of their health.
- **Chill of the Slab** — Numb a target with mortuary cold, slowing their attacks and dulling their pain into hesitation.
- **Grave-Speech** — Ask one question of a corpse dead less than a day; it must answer, though not kindly.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Corpsebinder

**Role:** Minion commander — sustained frontline pressure · **Advances from:** Necromancer

*Corpsebinders are the trade's first honest rank: practitioners who keep three or more dead in working order through a full campaign season. Mercenary companies hire them quietly to reduce burial costs and double as reinforcements. The binding is craft, not terror — knots of sinew, verses of ownership, and a ledger of every body owed.*

**Playstyle.** Command a small squad of durable undead from the second rank; repair them mid-fight, rotate the freshest forward, and let attrition do the arguing.

**The road to Corpsebinder:**

1. Reach level 10 and keep three risen servants standing through a full turning of the moon.
2. March your dead twenty leagues without losing one to rot, rivers, or wandering priests.
3. Bind a soldier who died mid-order, and see that the order is carried out.
4. Present your ledger of bodies to a Gravemarshal and have no entry questioned.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Muster of the Fallen** — Raise up to three corpses at once; each bound minion persists until destroyed rather than expiring at dawn.
- **Splint and Sinew** — Mend a damaged minion with grave-thread, restoring it to fighting order over six seconds.
- **Dead March** — Your minions advance in lockstep, gaining speed and immunity to fear while moving toward your marked target.
- **Tithe of Teeth** — Harvest a destroyed minion's remains to reduce the cost of your next raising.

### Pale Scholar

**Role:** Control caster — soul-siphon and denial · **Advances from:** Necromancer

*Pale Scholars have stopped studying death in bodies and started studying it in themselves. They fast until the pulse slows, copy forbidden folios by lantern-light, and learn to hold a departing soul the way other scholars hold a quotation. The pallor is not illness. It is the first negotiation with mortality, opened politely.*

**Playstyle.** Control the fight's tempo from range: slow hearts, sever escapes, and bank every kill as stored soul-light to fuel the next incantation.

**The road to Pale Scholar:**

1. Reach level 10 and copy the Sallow Concordance in your own hand without once misspelling a true name.
2. Distill your first soul into glass, and label the vessel honestly.
3. Let a physician pronounce you dead, and correct the record yourself the next morning.
4. Sleep one night in your own prepared grave, and rise before you are missed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Soul-Lantern** — Catch a soul at the moment of death in a glass vessel; spend it later as spell fuel.
- **Sever the Silver** — Cut the cord between body and soul on a target below one-fifth health, ending them instantly.
- **Deadman's Ledger** — Inscribe an enemy's name; if they die within the hour, you learn everything their soul carried.
- **Pallor** — Still your own heart for ten seconds; the dead ignore you and death-magic passes through.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Ossuary Captain

**Role:** Elite-minion commander — durable frontline core · **Advances from:** Corpsebinder

*Ossuary Captains reject the shambling horde as sloppy work. They raise soldiers who remember soldiering — veterans exhumed with their drill intact — and train them in silent yards until a company of bone stands parade-straight. Border lords hire them and pretend not to. A Captain's dozen outfights a warm hundred, and never breaks.*

**Playstyle.** Field a small elite of drilled undead veterans; hold formations, call disciplined maneuvers, and win by refusing to lose a single piece.

**The road to Ossuary Captain:**

1. Reach level 25 and raise a soldier of the old wars with every drill-command still in his marrow.
2. Drill your company a full winter in a yard where no living voice is permitted.
3. Defeat a living company in open field, then offer its fallen a place in your ranks.
4. Recover a banner the living abandoned, and let your dead carry it home.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Veteran's Memory** — Corpses of trained soldiers rise with one skill from life intact — shieldwork, archery, or siegecraft.
- **Bone Phalanx** — Your minions lock into a shield line, sharing damage between them and blocking passage entirely.
- **Sergeant's Knell** — Promote one undead to sergeant; nearby minions gain its discipline and strike in unison.
- **Silent Yard** — Drill your company between battles; each drilled minion enters the next fight with hardened bone and faster hands.

### Gravetide Caller

**Role:** Swarm summoner — overwhelming expendable numbers · **Advances from:** Corpsebinder

*Gravetide Callers follow wars the way gulls follow ploughs. They work the mass graves, the plague pits, the drowned levies — raising the cheap and countless dead in waves that need no drill, only direction. Fastidious colleagues call the work crude. The Callers reply that no wall was ever crude enough to stop the tide.*

**Playstyle.** Flood the field with short-lived shamblers, spend them as ammunition and cover, and replace losses from whatever the enemy was foolish enough to kill.

**The road to Gravetide Caller:**

1. Reach level 25 and raise a hundred dead in a single hour from a field you did not seed.
2. Walk the plague-cart circuit of three towns and leave no pit unworked.
3. Break a walled camp using no weapon but the bodies its defenders made for you.
4. Stand in the tide's center while it moves, and be the only stillness.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Carrion Surge** — Every corpse within forty paces rises at once as a shambler that lasts one minute.
- **Feed the Tide** — Enemies slain by your swarm rise immediately and join it.
- **Chaff of Bone** — Sacrifice nearby shamblers; each one absorbs a blow meant for you.
- **Grave Bloom** — Sow a patch of ground; anything that dies upon it stands back up under your call.

### Vesselwright

**Role:** Immortality engineer — self-sustaining attrition caster · **Advances from:** Pale Scholar

*Vesselwrights turn the scholar's pallor into craft. They fire phylactery-porcelain from bone ash, cut soul-anchors into gemstone, and apportion fragments of themselves into objects that cannot bleed. Each vessel is a masterwork and a hostage. Colleagues judge a Vesselwright's skill by how calmly they discuss their own murder.*

**Playstyle.** Fight like someone with nothing at stake: soak lethal blows into stored vessels, spend fragments of self for power, and rebuild between engagements.

**The road to Vesselwright:**

1. Reach level 25 and fire your first phylactery from bone-ash porcelain, glazed with a year of your own life.
2. Die once, deliberately, under controlled conditions, and take notes throughout.
3. Recover a colleague's shattered vessel and return what leaked out, asking nothing.
4. Hide your first vessel so well that you must trust it rather than check on it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Vessel** — On death, your soul retreats to a hidden phylactery and rebuilds your body over three days.
- **Apportioned Self** — Store a fifth of your soul in a carried vessel; soul-targeting magic finds only the remainder.
- **Kiln of Hours** — Transfer a wound's true cost to a prepared vessel, which cracks so you do not.
- **Hairline Death** — Once per battle, a killing blow leaves you at the edge of life and your newest vessel fractured.

### Soulchandler

**Role:** Resource caster — soul economy and control · **Advances from:** Pale Scholar

*Soulchandlers render the departing into light. Their lanterns hold souls in cold custody — some as fuel, some as witnesses, some as debts awaiting terms. Magistrates buy their testimony-candles; assassins buy their silence-wicks; the trade regulates itself through professional dread. A good chandler never lies about what a lantern holds.*

**Playstyle.** Fight over an economy of souls: bottle what dies, spend stored light for bursts of power, and threaten the one currency every enemy carries.

**The road to Soulchandler:**

1. Reach level 25 and bottle a soul mid-battle without spilling a single hour of it.
2. Keep a murdered witness lit until the trial ends, and let the verdict be theirs.
3. Refuse a fortune for a soul you promised to release, and release it.
4. Craft a lantern that burns for a year on one honest confession.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Render the Passing** — Bottle any soul that dies within thirty paces; each stored soul fuels one empowered casting.
- **Witness Light** — Burn a bottled soul as a lantern that replays its final hour for all to see.
- **Wick of Names** — Spend three stored souls at once to double a spell's effect and silence its casting.
- **Cold Custody** — Briefly pull a living enemy's soul into your lantern; the body stands vacant and defenseless.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Gravemarshal

**Role:** Legion commander — battlefield-scale undead warfare · **Advances from:** Ossuary Captain

*Gravemarshals command the standing legions of the dead — thousands strong, provisioned on nothing, deserting never. Perhaps six hold the rank in any age. Kingdoms map their borders around a Gravemarshal's silence, and generals study their battles with the lights on. Each carries a muster-roll of every soldier raised, owed, and honorably released.*

**Playstyle.** Direct whole formations rather than minions: raise fallen enemies mid-battle, promote undead officers on the field, and grind sieges without supply lines.

**The road to Gravemarshal:**

1. Reach level 40 and win a declared siege commanding no living soldier but yourself.
2. Take the surrender of a dead general on the field where he first fell.
3. Release a thousand veterans from your service in a single ceremony, and have them thank you.
4. Have a kingdom cede territory at the rumor of your march, before a single grave is opened.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Standard of the Risen** — Plant a bone standard; enemies who die within its shadow rise mid-battle, already sworn to your banner.
- **Field Promotion** — Raise a slain enemy officer as a bound commander whose former troops must resist the urge to flee.
- **Unbroken Line** — Your legions cannot rout, feel no attrition at night, and hold ground while a single bone remains.
- **Terms of Surrender** — Offer a besieged garrison honest terms; those who refuse join your muster-roll in advance.

### Charnel Shepherd

**Role:** Attrition engine — self-replenishing swarm control · **Advances from:** Gravetide Caller

*Charnel Shepherds tend the tide as a living thing — pasturing it on old battlefields, culling it, breeding its strongest strains of walking dead. The order is small and pastoral in its horrible way; shepherds trade shamblers at grave-markets and speak of good years. Villages empty at the sound of their bells, unhurriedly.*

**Playstyle.** Sustain a swarm that feeds itself: seed killing grounds, cull the weak for power, and outlast anything that cannot destroy bodies faster than you make them.

**The road to Charnel Shepherd:**

1. Reach level 40 and keep one swarm whole across three campaigns without returning to a city for bodies.
2. Breed a strain of shambler that other necromancers travel to buy, and set your own price.
3. Ring your bell in a fallen kingdom's capital and walk out with its garrison.
4. Lose the entire fold to holy fire, and rebuild it by the next full moon.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Shepherd's Bell** — Ring once; every unbound corpse within earshot rises and falls in behind you.
- **Cull and Keep** — Destroy your weakest shamblers to imbue the strongest, breeding a hardier strain each battle.
- **Rot Pasture** — Blight the ground; whatever dies upon it rises at once and at full strength.
- **Endless Fold** — Your swarm has no fixed size; each dawn it regrows to yesterday's count from local graves.

### Lich Ascendant

**Role:** Deathless duelist — attrition you cannot win · **Advances from:** Vesselwright

*A Lich Ascendant has died the first true death and filed the event away as procedure. The heartbeat is ceremonial now, maintained for company. They are feared less for power than for jurisdiction: killing one is a clerical error, promptly corrected. Most spend the rank in study, since time has stopped charging them for it.*

**Playstyle.** Treat every fight as a negotiation you cannot lose: absorb deaths as cooldowns, ignore mortal pressure entirely, and spend centuries-deep spellcraft with total patience.

**The road to Lich Ascendant:**

1. Reach level 40 and die your first true death with the ritual complete and your notes legible.
2. Return in time to attend your own funeral, and leave before you are recognized.
3. Destroy the record of your birth in every archive that holds it, replacing nothing.
4. Outstudy a mortal lifetime: read every book you were dying too fast to finish.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Filed Under Dead** — Dying is a ninety-second interruption; you reassemble at your anchor with full knowledge of your killer.
- **Cold Authority** — Lesser undead obey you without binding, recognizing something further along the road than themselves.
- **Anchor Chain** — Your soul is tethered across many vessels; banishment, possession, and severing simply fail.
- **A Century's Patience** — Channel a spell over minutes rather than seconds, its power scaling far beyond mortal limits.

### Usurer of Souls

**Role:** Soul broker — execution, denial, and leverage · **Advances from:** Soulchandler

*Usurers of Souls hold the dead in escrow and the living in terms. Their vaults lend soul-light against collateral no bank can seize back, and their foreclosures are attended by clergy hoping to learn something. The order is tiny, solvent, and untouched — everyone powerful enough to rob one owes one.*

**Playstyle.** Leverage the battlefield like a balance sheet: mark debts on enemies, hold allies' souls in safekeeping, and foreclose on anyone who cannot pay.

**The road to Usurer of Souls:**

1. Reach level 40 and hold one hundred souls in escrow with every contract witnessed and none disputed.
2. Foreclose on a tyrant in open court, and read the terms aloud first.
3. Forgive one debt entirely, and record why in a ledger no one will read.
4. Survive an audit by the courts of the dead, and be found exact.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Terms of Collection** — Mark an enemy; if they fall within the hour, their soul routes to your vault, bypassing any afterlife.
- **Compound Interest** — Each strike against a marked debtor drains a little more than the last, without ceiling.
- **Foreclosure** — Sever the soul of a marked enemy below one-quarter health; no resurrection honors the contract they broke.
- **Escrow** — Hold a willing ally's soul in your vault; their body cannot die while the deposit stands.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### Sovereign of the Risen

**Role:** Mythic commander — nation-scale deathless armies · **Advances from:** Gravemarshal

*Histories record perhaps three Sovereigns of the Risen, and dispute all of them. Each commanded every willing dead soldier of an era — armies that grew by exactly what they defeated. Nations now bury their dead in lead and salt, a custom whose origin children are not taught. The last Sovereign was never defeated, only asked, politely, to stop.*

**Playstyle.** Wage war at the scale of maps: every battle won enlarges the host, every enemy casualty is a recruitment, and retreat merely relocates the muster.

**The road to Sovereign of the Risen:**

1. Reach level 60 and break a crusade raised by seven kingdoms for the sole purpose of ending you.
2. Muster the dead of an entire forgotten war, and learn each name on the roll.
3. Refuse a crown offered by the living, and accept the one offered by the dead.
4. March your host past a city gate and leave it untouched, so the histories must call you something other than monster.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Grave Levy** — Call the willing dead of an entire province; they rise, form ranks, and await orders.
- **The Standing Muster** — All who fall in any battle you command rise at its end, offered a place rather than compelled.
- **One Order, Ten Thousand Hands** — Issue a single command that every soldier in your host executes simultaneously and without relay.
- **Peace of the Sovereign** — Declare a field at rest; no dead may be raised there by any hand, including yours.

### The Unnumbered

**Role:** Mythic swarm — inexhaustible tide of dead · **Advances from:** Charnel Shepherd

*Chroniclers stopped estimating the host of the Unnumbered after three censuses returned different figures and one returned no surveyors at all. Whether the title names a necromancer or the tide itself is a scholarly quarrel; the tide does not clarify. It has never been defeated, only outlasted, and never for long.*

**Playstyle.** Play as a weather system: the swarm arrives before you, regrows behind you, and victory means the enemy runs out of world before you run out of dead.

**The road to The Unnumbered:**

1. Reach level 60 and lift a siege by raising the besiegers' own casualties inside their camp each night.
2. Let three censuses of your host fail, and return one surveyor to tell of it.
3. Erase your name so thoroughly that scholars argue whether you exist, and attend the debate.
4. Become the reason a language has a word for the sound graves make when they open.

**Abilities granted:**

- **No Count Holds** — Your host cannot be reduced below half; destroyed shamblers reappear at the swarm's edge each minute.
- **The Graves Answer** — Every burial ground within a day's ride opens at your word, regardless of ward or consecration.
- **Undertow** — The swarm drags one enemy under; they emerge a minute later on your side of the line.
- **Season of the Tide** — For a day and a night, everything that dies in the region rises without being called.

### Archlich

**Role:** Mythic immortal — inevitability made arcane · **Advances from:** Lich Ascendant

*Histories disagree on which century produced the Archlich, and it declines to correct them. It has outlived its enemies, its allies, its language, and at least one calendar. Kingdoms no longer plot its destruction; they plot around its patience. Scholars note that everything it ever wanted has simply, eventually, happened.*

**Playstyle.** Win by being inevitable: no clock, no fear, no attrition applies to you, and every exchange trades the enemy's finite life against your surplus.

**The road to Archlich:**

1. Reach level 60 and let a kingdom execute you publicly, then resume your correspondence the same week.
2. Outlive every person who ever heard your living name, and keep the name anyway.
3. Complete the work: make your death not defeated, but optional, a formality you may yet schedule.
4. Be cited by historians in three eras, each certain you belonged to another.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Rescind** — Undo your own death the instant it occurs, anywhere, without travel to an anchor.
- **The Optional Grave** — Grant or revoke death itself for one creature in your presence, once a season.
- **Deep Time** — Your enchantments and curses do not expire; centuries-old workings answer when you call them due.
- **Terminal Lesson** — Show one enemy their complete death in advance; they fight the rest of the battle already grieving.

### The Wakelight

**Role:** Mythic arbiter — final passage of souls · **Advances from:** Usurer of Souls

*When every other light fails — gods distracted, ferries sunk, heavens shut — the dead walk toward the Wakelight. Histories place it at the end of every great war, unaffiliated, collecting. Whether it guides souls onward or simply keeps them is a theological crisis three churches have declined to investigate closely.*

**Playstyle.** Control where death leads: reroute every dying soul through your flame, extinguish the irredeemable permanently, and return the kept as the rarest mercy.

**The road to The Wakelight:**

1. Reach level 60 and stand at the end of a war so vast the heavens close their doors against the crowd.
2. Take custody of every soul the gods misplaced, and lose not one.
3. Turn away a god who comes to claim a soul in your keeping, and be obeyed.
4. Become the light the dying see, in every land, in every language.

**Abilities granted:**

- **All Roads End Here** — Every soul that dies in the region passes through your lantern first, and some are asked to stay.
- **Snuff** — Extinguish one soul utterly; no god, spell, or clause can retrieve it.
- **The Kept Flame** — Return a stored soul to its body whole — a true resurrection, granted rarely and priced honestly.
- **Hour of Moths** — Release a stormcloud of kept souls that swarm your enemies, each whispering its own death.
