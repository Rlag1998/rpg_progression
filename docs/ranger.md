# 🏹 Ranger

> *Where the maps end, the Ranger begins.*

**Archetype:** Cunning

Where the last road frays into deer-trail, the Ranger keeps walking. Reared on the wild borders, they read wolf-sign like scripture and trust a bowstring further than any oath. Villages hire them to find what wandered too far; kingdoms hire them to learn what is coming. They survive where maps end, and they bring back those who should not have gone.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Ranger

**Role:** Ranged skirmisher — traps and attrition

*Rangers begin as border children who noticed too much: the raven that meant a raiding party, the silence that meant wolves. An older warden takes them on without ceremony, teaches them tracks, wind, and hunger, then leaves them somewhere hard. The ones who walk back are Rangers. The others are lessons.*

**Playstyle.** Mark quarry and wear it down from range, laying snares along the retreat you predicted; win by knowing the ground better than anyone bleeding on it.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Walk past the last fence-post at dusk, alone, and come home with meat before anyone notices the knife is gone.
2. Apprentice yourself to a warden who has stopped counting winters, and carry their gear until they let you carry a bow.
3. Learn the forty signs of wolf, and the four that mean man.
4. Track a gut-shot stag for three days, and grant it a clean death you did not enjoy.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Quarry's Mark** — Mark one enemy; your attacks against it gain bonus damage and you sense its heading through any obstruction.
- **Wolf-Sign** — Read the ground to reveal every creature that crossed it within a day, with their number, weight, and wounds.
- **Thornfoot Snare** — Set a hidden snare that roots the first creature through it and leaves it bleeding.
- **Held Breath** — After a turn without moving, your next shot cannot be deflected and strikes a weak point.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Packbound

**Role:** Pair skirmisher — coordinated takedowns · **Advances from:** Ranger

*Some rangers stop hunting alone the day a beast decides not to leave. The Packbound swear the old blood-oath: meat shared, wounds shared, one mind wearing two skins. Border folk trust them over any patrol, because a wolf will not lie about what it smells.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as one creature in two bodies: your companion flanks, drags, and pins while you shoot, trading senses and wounds until nothing afield is faster.

**The road to Packbound:**

1. Reach level 10, then fast alone in the high pines until a beast chooses your fire over the dark.
2. Feed it from your own ration for a full season, and never eat first.
3. Take a wound that was meant for it, and let it lick the scar.
4. Hunt one whole winter without a spoken word between you, and come back fed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Blood-Oath** — Bind a sworn companion; you share wounds evenly and it acts on your instinct without command.
- **Two Throats** — You and your companion strike the same target within a heartbeat, and the stacked wounds bleed twice as long.
- **Borrowed Ears** — Perceive through your companion's senses at any distance, smelling fear and hearing pulses through walls.
- **Drag Down** — Your companion hamstrings a fleeing enemy and pins it prone until you arrive.

### Stillhunter

**Role:** Stealth sniper — single decisive shots · **Advances from:** Ranger

*The Stillhunter goes out alone and stops being a person for a while. They are the arrow that arrives from a hillside nobody watched, days after the target felt safe. Garrison soldiers speak of them the way sailors speak of weather: no malice in it, and no mercy either.*

**Playstyle.** Play the long game: choose your ground, vanish into it, and spend patience like coin until one perfect shot decides the encounter before it begins.

**The road to Stillhunter:**

1. Reach level 10 and lie three days in a blind of your own weaving, moving only your eyes.
2. Kill at two hundred paces something that would have killed you at two.
3. Let a search party pass within an arm's reach of you. Twice.
4. Come home, and let no one ask where you were.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Deadwood Stillness** — While motionless you cannot be detected by sight, sound, or scent, even in open ground.
- **Measured Wind** — Each turn spent aiming adds range and lethality to your next shot, without limit.
- **One Report** — Your first shot from concealment strikes for triple damage and does not reveal your position.
- **Cold Camp** — You leave no tracks, ash, or scent; nothing mortal can backtrail you.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Coursewarden

**Role:** Pursuit striker — anti-flight execution · **Advances from:** Packbound

*When something must not escape — a raider chief, a loosed horror, a deserter carrying plans — magistrates send for a Coursewarden. They and their beast do not fight quarry so much as spend it, running it hollow across days until it stands trembling and the ledger closes.*

**Playstyle.** Momentum is your resource: speed and damage build every turn the quarry flees, and the execution triggers when exhaustion, terror, and your companion converge.

**The road to Coursewarden:**

1. Reach level 25 and run a red hart to standing-still without loosing a single arrow.
2. Follow a raiding band over two rivers and a rockslide, then bring back their boots.
3. Refuse a bounty that asked you to stop at the border.
4. Let one quarry go, once, and understand why you never will again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Long Chase** — Each turn your quarry moves away, you and your companion gain speed and damage; the bonus never resets.
- **Hamstring Volley** — A spread of low arrows cripples the legs of everything running in a line.
- **Red Relay** — You and your companion alternate strikes without breaking stride, each hit extending the other's reach.
- **Run to Ground** — A fleeing quarry below half strength is dropped instantly when your companion reaches it.

### Findsman

**Role:** Scout-support — detection and rescue · **Advances from:** Packbound

*When a child vanishes past the tree line, villages do not pray; they send for a Findsman. Ranger and beast pool their senses into one wide attention the wood cannot lie to. They charge little, drink less, and remember every name they carried home, and each they did not.*

**Playstyle.** Recon and retrieval: ride your companion's senses to reveal what hides, hold a trail no sorcery can wash out, and shield whoever you are bringing home.

**The road to Findsman:**

1. Reach level 25, then find the child the wood took before third nightfall, and carry her out sleeping.
2. Learn the smell of every way a person can be lost.
3. Bring back a body when you had promised a boy, and say so plainly at the door.
4. Refuse payment from a mother, once, and never speak of it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Third Nose** — Merge senses with your companion to reveal every hidden creature, door, and grave within a bowshot.
- **Hair and Thread** — Once taken, a trail cannot be lost to water, snow, or sorcery.
- **Carry-Word** — Your companion bears a rescued creature to a spoken destination, unerring and unhindered.
- **Lantern Discipline** — Allies you escort cannot be frightened, charmed, or led astray while you watch.

### Longeye

**Role:** Extreme-range artillery — priority elimination · **Advances from:** Stillhunter

*Longeyes measure shots in hillsides. They loose at targets the eye alone cannot honestly claim, trusting wind-craft, terrain, and arithmetic older than the bow. Captains fear open windows in towns a Longeye is rumored to have passed through; the arrow arrives before the sound, and long after the grudge.*

**Playstyle.** Extreme-range elimination: spend the fight positioning and reading air, then delete the one target whose death unmakes the enemy's plan.

**The road to Longeye:**

1. Reach level 25 and cut a hanged man down at four hundred paces, in rain, with one arrow through the rope.
2. Spend a season loosing at nothing but wind-blown thistledown.
3. Miss once in front of someone who matters, and go back to the thistledown.
4. Put an arrow on a war-captain's map table, through the tent, as a letter of introduction.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Mile-Mark** — Strike a target beyond line of sight by memory of ground you have walked once.
- **Wind's Confession** — Read the air along the whole flight path; range and cover penalties do not apply to you.
- **Arcing Writ** — Loose over walls and ridgelines; the arrow descends on the target's shadow.
- **Second Shame** — If your first arrow kills, the next is nocked before the witnesses finish flinching.

### Lichenmask

**Role:** Ambush controller — concealed area denial · **Advances from:** Stillhunter

*A Lichenmask lies still long enough that the wood stops making an exception for them. Moss takes the cloak; beetles cross the bow hand. Whole armies file through passes under their count, and the tally reaches a distant desk with every button recorded and no watcher ever named.*

**Playstyle.** Area denial from perfect concealment: seed the ground with patient traps, strike only while unseen, and make the terrain itself into hostile bookkeeping.

**The road to Lichenmask:**

1. Reach level 25 and sit a fortnight in the pass while three columns march by; report every banner and every button.
2. Let moss claim your cloak before you claim a kill.
3. Go one full year without a stranger's eyes finding yours.
4. Bury your name under a flat stone, and visit it less each year.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Grown Over** — After a minute motionless, the terrain grows over you; you are undetectable until you choose otherwise.
- **Patient Root** — Your traps never expire and strengthen with each day they wait.
- **First and Only** — Attacks you make while unseen cannot be parried, dodged, or answered.
- **Swallowed Camp** — Hide a camp of six so thoroughly that hounds walk through it unknowing.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Wolf-Reeve

**Role:** Pack commander — encirclement and pursuit · **Advances from:** Coursewarden

*The Wolf-Reeve's warrant is older than most crowns and has no expiry. They command not one companion but a sworn pack, each wolf ranked and named, and the office answers to no magistrate once a quarry is declared. Kings have paid ransoms simply to be certain no warrant carried their name.*

**Playstyle.** Command a full pack across the whole map: encircle, cut every line of retreat, and grind a declared quarry down over days, not turns.

**The road to Wolf-Reeve:**

1. Reach level 40 and inherit a warrant from a Reeve who died mid-hunt; finish that hunt before your own name dries on the rolls.
2. Name each wolf of your pack for something you lost.
3. Refuse a king who wanted the warrant used on a debtor.
4. Close a hunt begun forty years before your birth, and burn the warrant after.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Warrant of Teeth** — Lead a pack of five sworn wolves; each can hold a separate mark.
- **Closing Ring** — Your pack forms a circle a bowshot wide; nothing inside leaves it without your leave.
- **Speech of the Old Pack** — Wild beasts within earshot obey you for one hunt, then owe you nothing.
- **No Second Winter** — A declared quarry cannot heal while any of your pack still draws breath.

### Finder of the Taken

**Role:** Warded rescuer — hostile-ground extraction · **Advances from:** Findsman

*Some places are struck from maps for good reasons, and the Finder walks into them anyway: barrow-fields that rearrange themselves, forests drowned but still growing. They are paid in advance, by custom. The feared part is not where they go; it is that the things there have started using their name.*

**Playstyle.** Endure what should not be entered: ward against lures and false voices, carry the fallen at full speed, and always know the way back out.

**The road to Finder of the Taken:**

1. Reach level 40 and enter a place struck from the maps; bring someone out still breathing.
2. Hear your true name spoken by something with no mouth, and keep walking.
3. Leave a marker at the deepest point you reached, so the next Finder aims past it.
4. Bring back one who was nine years gone, and stand quietly through the weeping.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Thread of Return** — You can always retrace your exact path, and allies holding your rope cannot be separated from it.
- **Name Under the Tongue** — You are immune to charms, lures, and voices imitating the loved or the dead.
- **Shouldered Burden** — Carry an incapacitated creature at full speed; while carried, both of you are warded from harm.
- **Salt and Iron Verge** — Draw a line the unnatural cannot cross for one night.

### Fatefletcher

**Role:** Declared executioner — inevitable single kills · **Advances from:** Longeye

*A Fatefletcher builds each arrow for one death only: the shaft cut on a named day, the fletching bound with the target's habits in mind. When the grey feather is found on a commander's pillow, sieges have lifted by breakfast. Scholars call the craft mathematics. The fletchers call it listening.*

**Playstyle.** Name the death in advance: spend turns fletching fate into a single arrow, then loose the one shot the battle has already agreed to.

**The road to Fatefletcher:**

1. Reach level 40 and fletch an arrow bearing your own name; carry it until you understand why.
2. End a siege with one shaft, and walk out through the celebrating.
3. Refuse a name you judged undeserved, and return the fee doubled.
4. Teach one apprentice the listening, then burn your notes.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Named Shaft** — Fletch an arrow against one named creature over an hour; against that creature, it cannot miss.
- **Already Loosed** — The killing shot lands before any witness registers the draw.
- **Grey Feather** — Leave a feather where a leader sleeps; their forces suffer dread and desertion until they withdraw.
- **Passes What It Must** — A named shaft ignores armor, cover, and wards standing between it and its purpose.

### Grey Vigil

**Role:** Invisible attrition — territorial denial · **Advances from:** Lichenmask

*No one has knowingly met a Grey Vigil. There are borders where warbands enter at full strength and thin, man by man, in weather and silence, with no arrow ever recovered. Other rangers find only cold blinds a decade old. The order acknowledges its dead, and no one living.*

**Playstyle.** Never be engaged: bank patience into power, remove intruders one by one when they stray, and let rumor finish what you started.

**The road to Grey Vigil:**

1. Reach level 40 and keep a border for a full year without being seen once, by enemy or friend.
2. Take the grey oath alone; if the order heard it, they will not say.
3. Turn back an army using only its own count of the missing.
4. Attend your predecessor's funeral, and leave before you are counted.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Thinning** — Each night, one intruder in your territory who strays from the others does not rejoin them.
- **No Such Watcher** — Within your sworn ground, no sense, spell, or record can establish that you exist.
- **Borrowed Weather** — Fog, rain, and dusk arrive when you need them, and side with you.
- **Quiet Ledger** — Every day you go unseen adds lethality to your first strike, without cap.

### Gazehound

**Role:** Sight-hunter — first-sighting burst execution · **Advances from:** Coursewarden

*The Gazehounds call the Reeves' warrant-hunts cruelty with paperwork: no chase should outlive its first sighting. They course the open marches with one gaze-hound or falcon, killing at full stretch, cleanly, before fear has time to season the quarry. The two orders share a parent, a border, and, by long arrangement, no table.*

**Playstyle.** Explosive pursuit in short windows: sight the quarry, slip the companion, and spend everything on the first pass; if the chase lasts past noon, you have already lost.

**The road to Gazehound:**

1. Reach level 40 and take a quarry between first sight and first shadow; the order times the trial by a dropped feather.
2. Refuse a Reeve's warrant aloud, once, on the grounds that paper outruns nothing.
3. Run beside your hound until your strides agree; you are admitted the day it stops checking where you are.
4. Kill nothing exhausted. If it stands trembling, walk away; you were too slow, and the shame is the lesson.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Slip** — Release your companion at first sighting; both of you triple your speed for ten heartbeats, and nothing outpaces you in open ground.
- **Sight Is Sentence** — Your first clean look at a marked quarry each hunt makes your next strike against it undodgeable and unblockable.
- **Gaze Unbroken** — Damage from you and your companion doubles against quarry that has never left your line of sight.
- **Clean at Speed** — A strike delivered at full sprint kills fleeing quarry below a quarter of its strength instantly and painlessly.

### Kithcaller

**Role:** Boundary warder — recall and lure-breaking · **Advances from:** Findsman

*The Kithcallers bury Finders respectfully and list them among the taken. Their doctrine: every rescuer who enters a taking-place cuts it a new path outward. So they stand at the last fence-post with the lost one's name said exactly right, and call, and the old boundary law makes the wood give back what is named.*

**Playstyle.** Hold the boundary and bring them to you: break lures, carry true names, and let your companion's long call walk where you refuse to.

**The road to Kithcaller:**

1. Reach level 40 and call the miller's twice-taken son out of the wood from the last fence-post, with both heels on the home side.
2. Learn the saying of names from the order's deaf caller, who has never yet heard herself be wrong.
3. Stand a Finder's funeral in silence, and say nothing about where the path they cut now leads.
4. Hold the call three nights against something answering in your mother's voice, and do not answer it back.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Said Right** — Speak a lost creature's true name correctly; it hears you wherever it is held and gains the will to walk toward you.
- **The Last Fence-Post** — Sanctify a boundary line; the lost who cross it shed every lure, hook, and follower at the crossing.
- **Turned Lure** — False voices and beckoning lights within earshot invert, drawing their makers stumbling to your side of the line.
- **The Long Call** — Your companion sustains the call for a night and a day; anything lost that hears it cannot be turned aside again.

### Toothdrawer

**Role:** Range controller — disarmament without death · **Advances from:** Longeye

*The Toothdrawers have ended eleven wars and no lives, a ratio the Fatefletchers call wasted listening. Their answer is doctrine: any archer can hit a heart; the shot worth mastering takes the sword, the rope, the candle. Commanders fear the midnight candle snuffed from four hundred paces, because there is never a second warning — there is never a need.*

**Playstyle.** Win by subtraction: strip weapons, cut mechanisms, pin the dangerous in place, and leave the field full of unhurt people with nothing left to fight with.

**The road to Toothdrawer:**

1. Reach level 40 and end a duel, a mutiny, and a siege in one season without a death on your count.
2. Snuff the candle on a tyrant's desk twice in one winter; by custom there is never a third.
3. Take the sword from a war-captain's hand at four hundred paces and let him keep the hand to remember you by.
4. Refuse the grey feather when the Fatefletchers offer it; the quarrel between the orders is conducted, naturally, in perfect silence.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Take the Blade** — Shoot the weapon from a hand at extreme range; it cannot be retrieved while you watch it.
- **The Snuffed Candle** — Extinguish a flame in a guarded room from outside; everyone within understands they were spared, and morale collapses accordingly.
- **Through the Rope** — Sever one rope, strap, chain, or mechanism within sight: gallows, gates, siege engines, and harnesses all come apart.
- **Pinned Sleeve** — Nail a target to timber or turf by its clothing; it is held fast, unharmed, until someone cuts it loose.

### Eyebright

**Role:** Hidden observer — exposure and marked intelligence · **Advances from:** Lichenmask

*Named for the meadow weed that grows facing whatever passes, the Eyebrights watch wars from inside them and never loose. Their counts have unseated dynasties that no arrow could reach. The Grey Vigil calls them empty quivers; the Eyebrights reply that a corpse is a signature, and they have never once signed.*

**Playstyle.** See everything, touch nothing: infiltrate unseen, learn the enemy entire, and hand your allies a battle already solved before the first arrow flies.

**The road to Eyebright:**

1. Reach level 40 and winter inside an enemy war-camp unarmed; deliver a count that ends the campaign faster than any arrow has.
2. Let a Grey Vigil cross your hedgerow unknowing, and leave their order a polite tally of the steps.
3. Unbury your name from beneath its flat stone; a witness, unlike a killer, must have one to swear by.
4. Watch one atrocity through to its end without loosing, and carry the count out with steady hands.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Every Button** — Study a force unseen for an hour; learn its numbers, routes, orders, and which officer is lying about them.
- **Burr-Mark** — Fix a clinging burr to a passerby; you perceive where its bearer goes and what is said around it for a week.
- **Harmless as Grass** — While you have harmed nothing for a day, no sense, hound, or sorcery anywhere can find you.
- **The Delivered Count** — Allies who hear your report gain unerring aim and foreknowledge against every unit and officer named in it.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### Pack Incarnate

**Role:** Mythic pursuit — many-bodied inescapable hunt · **Advances from:** Wolf-Reeve

*Histories from six kingdoms describe the same thing sideways: a hunt on the far borders that never quite ended, one will wearing many bodies. Wolves are part of it; so, the accounts insist, is something shaped like a ranger. When it takes a scent, sensible nations make peace to avoid being downwind.*

**Playstyle.** Act through every body at once: the pack is your reach, your senses, and your argument, and no distance or border interrupts it.

**The road to Pack Incarnate:**

1. Reach level 60 and run one quarry across three kingdoms and through the middle of a war, letting no border slow the pack.
2. Fall in the snow with the pack around you, and rise when they nose you up. Twice.
3. Give up your reflection; the pack sees you, and that is enough.
4. Let the histories name the hunt, and never the hunter.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Will, Many Throats** — Act through every pack member simultaneously; distance between bodies is irrelevant.
- **Scent Eternal** — A marked quarry cannot rest, heal, or hide until the hunt concludes.
- **The Hunt Arrives** — Your pack emerges from whatever cover exists at the quarry's destination, having always been ahead.
- **Winter Runs With You** — Cold, dark, and hunger afflict your quarry and feed your pack.

### Warden of the Way Back

**Role:** Mythic rescuer — retrieval across final borders · **Advances from:** Finder of the Taken

*Every land tells it differently and means the same story: someone taken past all finding, and years later a figure at the gate returning them unaged, declining supper. Grave-priests preach against the tale. Mothers go on telling it, and set a lamp in the window in case the Warden is passing.*

**Playstyle.** Anchor the living and reclaim the lost: open doors out of anywhere, hold names against death, and pay tolls no one else can carry.

**The road to Warden of the Way Back:**

1. Reach level 60 and walk into the country the dead walk out of; return leading someone by the hand.
2. Learn what the toll is, and pay it without telling anyone the price.
3. Return a child to the woman who buried her, and stay for the silence afterward.
4. Leave your own lamp lit. You will not be home for a while.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Door Where None Was** — Once a day, open a way out of any place, including places that are not places.
- **Kept Name** — Hold a dying ally's name under your tongue and lead them back before dawn.
- **The Long Carry** — Carry one soul any distance, across any border, at a cost you settle later.
- **Lamp in the Window** — Anyone lost who remembers you can always be found by you.

### The Last Arrow

**Role:** Mythic artillery — war-ending single shot · **Advances from:** Fatefletcher

*The chronicles disagree on the archer and agree on the arrow: a siege that lifted mid-assault, a tyrant dead at dinner behind three walls, a war that ended with one report no one heard. Scholars argue whether it is a single bowman or an office quietly passed down. The quivers stay one arrow light either way.*

**Playstyle.** Everything is windage for one shot: spend the battle unseen and calculating, then loose the arrow the war was always waiting for.

**The road to The Last Arrow:**

1. Reach level 60 and end a war with one arrow, then let history misname the archer.
2. Decline three thrones offered by people who guessed correctly.
3. Loose at something the priests say cannot die, and make them revise.
4. Pass the office on the way it came to you: an arrow, a heading, and no names.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Shot Foretold** — Declare a death anywhere beneath the same sky; the arrow keeps the appointment.
- **The Held Breath** — Time thins around your draw; nothing moves between anchor and loose.
- **No Wall Was Built** — The shaft ignores fortification, armor, ward, and weather standing between it and the declared.
- **Quiver of One** — You carry a single arrow; it is always the right one.

### The Absence

**Role:** Mythic ghost — unwitnessed erasure · **Advances from:** Grey Vigil

*Officially, there is no such ranger. Yet there are woods that veteran armies refuse to enter for reasons their generals cannot commit to paper, and border wars that ended because the winning side began, quietly, to be alone. Scholars call the Absence a superstition. The superstition has a body count.*

**Playstyle.** Win without ever appearing: you cannot be perceived or recorded, so enemies fight the terrain, the dark, and their own arithmetic instead.

**The road to The Absence:**

1. Reach level 60 and be declared dead by the order that trained you; attend the rite unnoticed.
2. Empty a fortress without breaching a gate or leaving an arrow.
3. Be disproven by a royal commission. Assist their research.
4. Become the reason the wood is quiet, and let quiet be the whole of your name.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Never There** — You cannot be perceived, remembered, or recorded by any means, mortal or otherwise.
- **The Unentered Wood** — Enemy forces refuse to enter ground you have claimed; orders to do so trigger desertion.
- **Death Without an Author** — Your kills present no wound, cause, or story that any investigation can hold.
- **Last Doubt** — Beings of fate and prophecy cannot include you in their accounts.

### The Cloudless

**Role:** Mythic sight-hunter — line-of-sight annihilation · **Advances from:** Gazehound

*Survivors of certain clear days describe it identically and are identically disbelieved: no arrow, no wolf, only a shadow crossing the sun once, and the column's captain dead mid-order. Veterans refuse open ground on cloudless mornings. The chronicles note a falcon seen twice in a century, stooping both times.*

**Playstyle.** Own everything you can see: perceive the whole march from above, strike at the speed of sight, and unmake the very idea of cover.

**The road to The Cloudless:**

1. Reach level 60 and take a quarry across a valley the moment you sight it, so its last echo arrives after its death.
2. Live a summer above the treeline until the high sight settles in: the whole march at once, every moving thing named.
3. Be seen twice in a hundred years, both times mid-stoop, and let the chronicles quarrel about the bird.
4. Stop looking at anything you do not intend to catch. That is the entire discipline, and it costs more than it sounds.

**Abilities granted:**

- **At the Speed of Sight** — Strike anything you can see the instant you see it; distance is not a factor and never was.
- **No Cover Was Ever There** — Concealment, cover, and armor of position do not apply against you; the ground declines to shelter your quarry.
- **The High Gaze** — Through the falcon you perceive everything moving under open sky within a day's ride, named and numbered.
- **One Pass** — Once per hunt, stoop from the sun: the dive cannot be seen, warded, or survived by a fleeing quarry.

### The Unlost

**Role:** Mythic beacon — mass recall and return · **Advances from:** Kithcaller

*The tale keeps arriving from different counties with the dates changed: a fire lit at a crossroads, and by morning the decade-lost walking out of the wood — unaged, unhurried, remembering a light. Nothing near it can stay lost, or stay taken. Whole taking-places have starved and closed for want of keeping.*

**Playstyle.** Be the point everything returns to: gather the scattered, unmake abductions and banishments, and turn the field's every displacement into an argument for your side.

**The road to The Unlost:**

1. Reach level 60 and keep a crossroads fire through one whole night while the decade-lost walk out of the dark, greeting each by name.
2. Attend a taking-place's closing: stand at its hem while it gives up its last, and let the wood fill the hollow by spring.
3. Lose nothing for nine years — no ally, no beast, no needle. The order stops testing when the needles stop being lost.
4. Let the tales keep your face wrong, so the lost look for the fire and not for you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First Hour** — When you arrive anywhere, everything lost within a day's walk — people, beasts, blades — begins moving toward your fire.
- **Cannot Be Scattered** — Allies sworn to your fire cannot be separated, banished, or misplaced; whatever tries delivers them to your side instead.
- **Starve the Taking** — Places and powers that keep the lost hold nothing new near you, and lose one keeping each dawn you remain.
- **Given Back** — Once per day, recall one creature taken beyond all finding — from mazes, mists, or memory — to the boundary you keep.

### The Averted

**Role:** Mythic interdictor — wars ended before beginning · **Advances from:** Toothdrawer

*The chronicles are strangest where nothing happened: full musters that dispersed at dawn, marches that turned home at the border, a tyrant's campaign map found pinned by a white-fletched arrow through its first objective. Every account blames different omens. Every quartermaster, pressed, mentions the arrow.*

**Playstyle.** Fight the war before it exists: unmake musters, hold whole fields in check at full draw, and finish campaigns that history will never get to start.

**The road to The Averted:**

1. Reach level 60 and empty a battlefield before the first horn: both armies home by winter, and the ballads left with nothing to rhyme.
2. Put your arrow through the war itself — the treaty page, the beacon, the drum. Never the drummer.
3. Watch one war you failed to avert, from first rank to last rite, so the craft's weight stays honest.
4. Be thanked by no one for the thing that did not happen, and learn to live well on that.

**Abilities granted:**

- **White Fletching** — Leave a white-fletched shaft on a war-table; the campaign planned there cannot keep its soldiers' loyalty and quietly disperses.
- **Nothing Loosed** — While you hold a full draw, no creature within sight of you can begin violence; the field waits with you.
- **One Falling Volley** — Disarm every combatant in a formation with a single arcing volley that harms none of them.
- **The Battle That Wasn't** — Once per day, declare an engagement over before it begins; every combatant loses the will and the means to start it for an hour.

### The Overstory

**Role:** Mythic witness — nothing goes unseen · **Advances from:** Eyebright

*Testimony keeps arriving that no living witness can account for: a tyrant's sealed order recited back a generation later, word for word; a massacre's every name recovered from an empty pass. Foresters swear the canopy pays attention now. Scholars posit a hidden office. Hedgewives say the wood remembers, and something reads it.*

**Playstyle.** Know the field completely and be nowhere on it: read every tell the wild keeps, strip all concealment, and make each enemy deed a matter of record.

**The road to The Overstory:**

1. Reach level 60 and recite, to the man who gave it, the sealed order that burned Cold Hollow, thirty years late and letter-perfect.
2. Let a kingdom fall on the strength of your count alone, and confirm nothing.
3. Refuse to look away once, at the worst of it, because a record with mercies in it is only gossip.
4. Deliver testimony to three courts that never find you, and let the guilty spend their lives wondering what else was seen.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Seen and Kept** — Perceive any deed done beneath leaf or hedge within the same march, as it happens, from wherever you sit.
- **Leafturn** — You read the wood's small tells; no concealment within the green holds against you, and you point every hidden thing out to allies.
- **Word for Word** — Recall perfectly anything you ever witnessed; creatures you accuse aloud cannot lie, hide, or profit from disguise while you speak.
- **Unblinking Season** — Hold a watch for a season without sleep, hunger, or discovery; each unbroken week deepens your allies' advantage over everything you have counted.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Hunting Moon

**Role:** Apotheosis — the hunt as celestial season · **Advances from:** Pack Incarnate

*Almanacs mark it now between harvest and frost: the Hunting Moon, under which no declared quarry may rest. On its nights every pack from the marches to the ice answers one rising; shepherds bar doors out of respect, not fear. Six kingdoms' histories agree the hunt never ended. It only set.*

**Playstyle.** Rise, declare, and run the world's packs as one body: no border, darkness, or distance shelters a quarry named beneath your light.

**The road to The Hunting Moon:**

1. Reach level 85 and let the pack outgrow the body: when every wolf from the marches to the ice answers one moonrise, the rising is you.
2. Keep the calendar honest: one quarry a moon, declared by the whole wild's need, and let kings petition like anyone else.
3. Give the histories their two true endings — the hunter never came back, the hunt never left — and sign neither.
4. Set once a year for a full night, so the world remembers that even the hunt keeps a sabbath.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Declared at Rising** — Name a quarry at moonrise; until moonset, every wild pack beneath your light is your body against it.
- **Bent Country** — A declared quarry finds every road, ford, and border bending back into the hunt; flight only changes the scenery.
- **Light to Hunt By** — While you are risen, allies tire not, lose no trail, and strike together as one pack wherever they hunt.
- **Between Moons** — Quarry that outlasts your setting heals nothing and forgets nothing until you rise again; few request a second month.

### The White Hart

**Role:** Apotheosis — living waymark out of anywhere · **Advances from:** Warden of the Way Back

*It appears only to the entirely lost: in barrow-fields, in the grey country past the last lamp. It carries no one and charges nothing; the Ferryman poles the one direction, and this walks the other. Follow, and the way home assembles underfoot. The toll, they say, was paid once, in full, by whoever it used to be.*

**Playstyle.** Walk ahead of the lost and the dying: appear where hope ends, make a road home of anything, and let nothing keep what follows you.

**The road to The White Hart:**

1. Reach level 85, walk past your own lit lamp a final time, and let the going take the shape it always wanted: four legs, white, unhurried.
2. Lead out someone who does not deserve it, because the way home was never a verdict.
3. Be hunted by a king who wants the way for his own, and lead him home too — the long way, until he understands.
4. Give the toll its remainder — the name, the face, the supper declined — and keep what is left: the direction home, wearing antlers.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Seen at the Worst Hour** — Appear beside any creature past all finding, wherever it is kept; the path behind you leads home, whatever lies between.
- **Nothing Keeps Them** — Hands, hungers, and old claims release whoever walks behind you; the grip that refuses is left holding nothing.
- **The Wound Waits** — A dying ally who rises to follow cannot die while walking; the wound is made to wait until home.
- **The Lamp Answered** — Every lamp set in a window against loss reaches you; once per night, step out of the dark beside one.

### The Appointment

**Role:** Apotheosis — death as kept schedule · **Advances from:** The Last Arrow

*Somewhere above every kingdom an arrow is falling, and has been for years. Deaths of consequence now keep hours, like courts: the arrow arrives on the appointed day, through weather, wards, and dynasties, never early, never twice. Chancellors have stopped asking whether. The remaining scholarship concerns when.*

**Playstyle.** Schedule the field's endings: call down shots loosed years ago, reserve enemies' deaths for their proper hour, and make time itself your quiver.

**The road to The Appointment:**

1. Reach level 85 and loose your last arrow straight up, into no weather at all; the world will hold it aloft, falling, everywhere, patiently.
2. Keep every appointment in memory alone: none written, none early, none ever missed. The office no longer has a holder; it has a schedule.
3. Grant one delay a decade, at your sole discretion, so that tyrants can never be certain which century is theirs.
4. Attend, unarmed and bareheaded, the one death you declined to keep, and let the calendar see what mercy costs it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Already in the Air** — Your arrow for any declared target has been falling for years; call it down at will, once per target.
- **The Hour Is Kept** — A creature you have appointed cannot be slain by any other hand; its hour belongs to you, and it knows.
- **Nothing Postpones** — Wards, prophecy, and time-bending magic cannot move an appointment; effects that try are spent against the calendar and lost.
- **The Hour Released** — Cancel an appointment publicly; the spared creature and all witnesses lose the will to oppose you for a season.

### The Hinterland

**Role:** Apotheosis — sovereign erasure, unmapped territory · **Advances from:** The Absence

*The surveys agree on the borders of what they cannot survey: a country's worth of ground that keeps no record and returns none. Companies enter on orders and arrive home out of step, fewer, certain they never marched. The recanted and the twice-buried go there to stop existing comfortably. Citizenship is conferred by disappearance.*

**Playstyle.** Carry sovereign silence onto any field: unmap ground, subtract intruders without authorship, and shelter whoever must not exist inside borders no one can cite.

**The road to The Hinterland:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop being somewhere: let the ground you keep slip off the maps with you inside, still keeping it.
2. Take in the ones who must not exist — the recanted, the twice-buried, the last of their kinds — and ask nothing. Especially names.
3. Turn back the royal cartographers with nothing at all: no arrow, no voice, only a morning on which their instruments agree.
4. Let the churches argue whether the Quiet Country is paradise or punishment, and keep the border either way; the argument is also a wall.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Borders of Nothing** — Claim ground; it leaves every map, memory, and record, and those you shelter inside cannot be found by anything.
- **Subtraction** — Companies that enter uninvited emerge days later at their own muster, fewer, with no account any interrogation can recover.
- **Conferred Citizenship** — Grant an ally residence; while they keep your silence they cannot be scried, tracked, prophesied, or named as anyone.
- **Struck From the Survey** — Once per battle, remove one enemy engine, banner, or champion from the field and from every record that it ever came.

### Noonfall

**Role:** Apotheosis — the shadowless hour hunts · **Advances from:** The Cloudless

*Armies march at dusk now, and generals pretend it is doctrine. At true noon under a clear sky the world holds one open instant in which nothing casts a shadow to hide in — and in that instant, on certain fields, something falls. Shepherds hood their hawks at midday, out of courtesy.*

**Playstyle.** Rule the open sky as a standing law: expose everything, strike from directly above, and make the hour itself the thing your enemies flee indoors from.

**The road to Noonfall:**

1. Reach level 85 and become the wrong side of the sun: when the shadowless instant begins hunting of itself, you are the instant.
2. Spare whatever looks up and shows its face; the hour is for what hides, and the bargain must stay legible.
3. Keep one field forever unwatched and never say which, so that hope retains an acre.
4. Accept that the falcons knew all of this first, and hold the watch in a manner they would not be ashamed of.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Shadowless Hour** — At your noon, stealth, illusion, cover, and veils fail everywhere under open sky; the field is shown entire to your allies.
- **Straight Down** — Strike any target under open sky that you or an ally has seen since dawn; the blow arrives from directly above.
- **Roofs Are Strategy** — Enemies under open sky in your hour cannot rally, hide, or aim upward; commanders now plan campaigns around awnings.
- **Bring Down the Noon** — Once per day, advance the sky to cloudless noon above one field; every shadow and shelter on it opens to the stoop.

### The Homing

**Role:** Apotheosis — the world's homeward pull · **Advances from:** The Unlost

*Losing has stopped working properly. Rings surface in the years' first furrow; strayed children arrive at supper vague about the missing month; invading armies wake, embarrassingly, at their own borders. Scholars measure a pull in things, faint and homeward, and cannot source it. Somewhere inside the pull, a fire is still kept.*

**Playstyle.** Bend every displacement homeward: recall allies mid-stride, send invaders bodily back to their hearths, and make territory itself a thing that cannot be taken from you.

**The road to The Homing:**

1. Reach level 85 and let the call outlive the caller: when lost things start home on their own in every county, take up keeping the pull instead.
2. Walk the world's boundaries once a generation, renewing the fence-posts, so everything remembers which direction is home.
3. Guard the exceptions hardest: what fled cruelty may stay lost, and the pull must never fetch it back.
4. Answer the philosophers who ask where home is for the homeless with a fire, built, lit, and left burning.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Nothing Stays Lost** — All things misplaced near you start the journey back: blades to hands, children to doorways, banners to the halls that hung them.
- **Sent Home** — Return one enemy bodily to the hearth that misses it most; it cannot find its way back for a day.
- **The Pull** — Your allies always know the way to you and the way home, and nothing — mist, malice, or magic — can turn them around.
- **Called Back** — Once per day, recall every ally on the field to your side unharmed, across any distance, arriving mid-stride.

### The Fair Warning

**Role:** Apotheosis — violence must announce itself · **Advances from:** The Averted

*Ambush has stopped working. Every raid finds the village armed and the livestock gone; every assassin's chosen night, the shutters already barred. Before each violence, somewhere, a white shaft stands in the ground with time enough to heed it. Jurists argue whether warning is mercy or jurisdiction. The unheeded stop arguing.*

**Playstyle.** Abolish surprise: your side is never ambushed, the enemy's every stroke arrives announced, and those who ignore your warning meet the one shot that follows it.

**The road to The Fair Warning:**

1. Reach level 85 and give the world its notice: one white shaft before every front rank on every field at dawn, and all of them yours.
2. Deliver fair warning to a god mustering against the living, and be heeded; the priests will file it under providence.
3. Keep the count of wars ended at the warning, and tell no one; the office works because it has never once been wrong.
4. Answer what the warned owe you: nothing. A warning with a price is only a threat arriving politely.

**Abilities granted:**

- **First the Feather** — No violence surprises your allies; each incoming stroke, snare, and treachery is announced to its target with time to answer.
- **Stand Aside** — Any creature that heeds your warning and withdraws cannot be harmed by the violence it was warned of.
- **Notice Served** — Serve notice on a commander: their orders are heard by their targets as they are given.
- **No Second Warning** — Against those who ignored the first, your shots cannot miss or be survived, and mercy is formally satisfied.

### The Green Witness

**Role:** Apotheosis — the land as sworn record · **Advances from:** The Overstory

*Courts adjourn to groves now. The custom began when denial stopped surviving contact with certain ground: stand where the deed was done, and those present see it done again, whole. The wild has become admissible. Somewhere its reader still walks, palm to the bark, keeping the world's longest deposition.*

**Playstyle.** Make truth a battlefield condition: show what any ground has seen, strip every lie-born protection, and let enemies fight on terrain that remembers them.

**The road to The Green Witness:**

1. Reach level 85 and take the land's oath: from this day, what the green sees, you have seen — all of it, always. The first night is the hardest.
2. Testify once against yourself, completely, for the record keeps no favorites or it keeps nothing.
3. Refuse every crown that asks to make the record convenient; a witness with a patron is a bard.
4. Answer the historians who ask who watches you by standing very still until they understand.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ground Truth** — Stand on any ground and see, whole, what was done there; all present witness it with you, once per place.
- **On the Green Record** — Every deed done under open green is kept; forgeries, false histories, and glamours wither when you produce the original.
- **Hostile Testimony** — Accuse an enemy with the land's memory; the guilty lose all protections born of lies — disguise, alibi, ward, and welcome.
- **Adjourned to the Grove** — Once per season, convene a dispute's parties in a grove where nothing false can be spoken; the judging stays theirs.
