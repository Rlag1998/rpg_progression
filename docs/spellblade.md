# 🔮 Spellblade

> *Spell and steel, sworn into a single edge.*

**Archetype:** Esoteric

Every generation, a few students fail correctly: too restless for the towers' silence, too curious for the drill-yard's rote. The Spellblade is what becomes of them, a heretic discipline that scribes casting into swordwork until neither can be separated. Both traditions disown the art; both quietly hire it. To take up this path is to belong nowhere, and to be needed everywhere.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Spellblade

**Role:** Hybrid melee caster — versatile skirmisher

*Expelled apprentices, drummed-out soldiers, hedge-duelists who taught themselves to cast between parries. The Spellblade begins as a rumor in both worlds: the swordsman who should not know that word, the student who should not hold a blade that well. No guild certifies them. Their credential is the survivors' account of what they did.*

**Playstyle.** Alternate cantrip and cut in a single rhythm; spend spell-charge to empower strikes, spend momentum to shorten casts. Weakest when forced to speak only one language.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Be refused twice: once by a tower that called your swordplay vulgar, once by a barracks that called your questions dangerous.
2. Steal, copy, or beg a single cantrip, and learn to speak it with a blade in your hand.
3. Survive a duel against a real swordsman and a real mage in the same season.
4. Scribe your first sigil into steel without cracking the blade, and keep what the failure cost you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Bladecant** — Weave a minor spell into a sword form; the strike deals bonus arcane damage and refunds part of the cast's cost.
- **Quicksilver Guard** — Parry with the inlaid edge to convert a blocked blow into one charge of stored spellforce.
- **Twin Grammar** — Casting after a melee hit halves the spell's cast time; striking after a spell adds arcane damage to the blow.
- **Sidestep Sigil** — Blink a short distance along a line you walked this turn, leaving a fading rune where you stood.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Runefencer

**Role:** Melee duelist — anti-caster pressure · **Advances from:** Spellblade

*Runefencers treat a hostile spell as one more blade to be read and answered. They scribe sigils along the fuller mid-swing, letting each pass rewrite the edge's purpose. Court mages hire them as seconds in arcane duels; the same mages cross the street when a Runefencer walks by uncontracted.*

**Playstyle.** Aggressive single-combat tempo: bait a cast, parry the spell itself, and punish through the gap. Rewrite the blade's rune between exchanges to match the opponent.

**The road to Runefencer:**

1. Reach level 10 and defeat a licensed duelist of either tradition using only what neither taught you.
2. Parry a living spell with bare steel and stay standing; the scar along the fuller is your certificate.
3. Learn to scribe a rune in the time a swing takes — first on parchment at a run, then on iron in the rain.
4. Stand as second in a mage's duel and be accepted, once by the challenger and once by the accused.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Parse the Weave** — Read an enemy's casting to learn the spell's school; your next parry against that school cannot fail.
- **Riposte in Kind** — Cut an incoming spell apart; a fragment of it returns along your counterthrust as arcane damage.
- **Wetscribe Edge** — Mid-swing, inscribe a fresh rune into the blade, changing its damage type before the blow lands.
- **Second Tongue** — While in single combat, your counterspells cost stamina instead of mana.

### Wardsworn

**Role:** Arcane defender — zone control, retaliation · **Advances from:** Spellblade

*Where Runefencers duel, the Wardsworn dig in. They layer spell-armor like laminate steel and let the wards do the answering, standing where mage-craft meets the shield-wall. Garrison captains request them by name for breach duty; abbots request them for vigils no one explains. Both pay in advance.*

**Playstyle.** Deliberate and immovable: stack wards before contact, anchor a zone, and let attackers spend themselves against retaliation. Wins by making ground too expensive to take.

**The road to Wardsworn:**

1. Reach level 10 and hold a doorway against a siege drill until the instructors, not you, call the halt.
2. Wear your first spell-armor for a full month; sleep in it, and learn what the wards whisper when they fail.
3. Take a blow meant for a stranger and let the ward, not the flesh, answer it.
4. Swear the Standing Oath before witnesses from both traditions, neither of whom will thank you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Laminate Aegis** — Maintain up to three concentric wards; each layer broken detonates outward, staggering adjacent attackers.
- **Oath of Standing** — While you have not moved this round, allies behind you take reduced damage and cannot be forcibly displaced.
- **Tollgate** — Inscribe a threshold on the ground; enemies crossing it are slowed and marked for your retaliation.
- **Answering Ward** — When a ward absorbs a blow, store its force; release it as an arc of returned violence on your next strike.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Nullblade

**Role:** Anti-mage hunter — spell denial · **Advances from:** Runefencer

*The Nullblade found the seam in the Runefencer's art: every weave, however grand, has a first thread. Their edges are scribed with unwriting runes that drink enchantment on contact. Towers keep registries of known Nullblades the way port cities keep plague maps, and quietly retain one or two regardless.*

**Playstyle.** Patient hunter's tempo: close the distance under dispelling cover, sever the enemy's opening weave, then kill the mage in the silence that follows.

**The road to Nullblade:**

1. Reach level 25 and cut a sanctioned spell out of the air before a tribunal that wanted you to fail.
2. Carry an unwriting rune against your skin for a year without letting it eat your own casting.
3. Hunt a rogue caster the towers disowned but could not stop; return the staff unbroken, as proof of restraint.
4. Refuse the first assassination contract you are offered. The second is your own affair.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sever the First Thread** — Interrupt any spell as it begins; the caster cannot attempt that spell again until they rest.
- **Drinking Edge** — Your strikes strip one active enchantment from the target and convert it into temporary vigor.
- **Field of Unwriting** — Project a short aura in which ongoing magical effects sputter and pay double to persist.
- **Nullstep** — Pass through a barrier or ward as if it were mist, once per engagement, unraveling it behind you.

### Edgescribe

**Role:** Melee striker — layered arcane detonation · **Advances from:** Runefencer

*Edgescribes turned the Runefencer's mid-swing sigils into full authorship: every cut leaves a rune in the wound, and the runes keep working after the blade moves on. Their duels read like manuscripts written on the loser. Universities forbid study of the technique, then cite it under other names.*

**Playstyle.** Layer marked wounds across one or many foes, then speak the closing word to make every inscription ignite at once. Builds toward a single authored moment.

**The road to Edgescribe:**

1. Reach level 25 and write a legible sentence in sword-cuts on a training post, at full speed, blindfolded.
2. Compose a rune of your own, one no tower archive holds, and survive its first casting from your edge.
3. Duel before a scribe who transcribes every stroke; when he reads it back, it must scan as verse.
4. Leave a marked man alive for a season, and learn what your runes do to a life that continues.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Wounding Script** — Each melee hit inscribes a rune in the target; runes deal arcane damage over time and stack to five.
- **The Closing Word** — Speak the phrase that finishes the sentence: all your runes on all enemies detonate simultaneously.
- **Errant Script** — Your missed swings scribe runes on the ground instead; enemies who step on them take the mark.
- **Author's Privilege** — Reading your own runes on a dying foe restores mana and shortens your cooldowns.

### Linewarden

**Role:** Formation anchor — ally warding, area denial · **Advances from:** Wardsworn

*Linewardens took the Wardsworn's oath and stretched it across a rank of soldiers. Their wards run shield to shield like mortar, and a line so bound does not break where a Linewarden stands. Marshals argue over them before battles; afterward, the soldiers they held do not argue at all.*

**Playstyle.** Positional and communal: bind your wards through allies' shields, walk the rank to reinforce failing sections, and make the formation itself your armor.

**The road to Linewarden:**

1. Reach level 25 and bind your wards through nine shields at once without a single soldier feeling the weight.
2. Hold a section of a real wall during a real breach; count the living afterward, and remember the count.
3. Teach a green company to trust the lattice enough to stand where veterans would run.
4. Refuse a marshal's order that would spend your line cheaply, and survive the court-martial with your oath intact.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Mortarwork** — Bind adjacent allies into your ward-lattice; damage they take is spread thin across the whole bound line.
- **Walk the Rank** — Move along your bound line without provoking attacks, repairing one broken ward per ally passed.
- **No Step Backward** — Bound allies cannot be feared, displaced, or routed while you remain standing.
- **Salient** — Project a ward-wall forward to claim ground; it holds until your line advances to occupy it.

### Spiteward

**Role:** Retaliation tank — punishment and attrition · **Advances from:** Wardsworn

*Spitewards read the Wardsworn's oath backwards: protection is fine, but debt is better. Their wards remember every blow taken and repay with interest — in scald, in seizure, in the slow ruin of the striker's own strength. Bandit companies teach recruits to recognize the plate. Recognizing it, they leave.*

**Playstyle.** Invite the hit: absorb punishment through vindictive wards, bank each blow as debt, then discharge the ledger onto whoever owes the most.

**The road to Spiteward:**

1. Reach level 25 and let a champion beat on your wards until his arms fail; then walk him home unharmed.
2. Scribe your first vindictive clause and test it on yourself, because you must know exactly what you charge.
3. Take a beating you could have prevented, and collect the debt a year later to the day.
4. Be named in a mercenary charter as a hazard, by companies you have never met.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Ledger of Blows** — Every hit you absorb is recorded as debt on the attacker; debt increases your damage against them.
- **Scald Clause** — Melee attackers take searing backlash through your wards, and their weapon hands blister, slowing their next swings.
- **Collections** — Discharge all recorded debt on one enemy as a single crushing invoice of arcane force.
- **Grudge Given Form** — While below half health, your wards regenerate from the pain of those who struck you.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Sword of Unmaking

**Role:** Elite anti-mage executioner — hard denial · **Advances from:** Nullblade

*A Sword of Unmaking is what the towers mean when they say containment. Perhaps a dozen exist in a generation, sworn to an order with no hall and one law: no weave stands that they have judged. Their edges no longer bear runes; the unwriting has soaked into the steel, and into them.*

**Playstyle.** Walk through the enemy's magic as if uninvited: strip wards on approach, execute casters through their own defenses, and leave the battlefield mundane.

**The road to Sword of Unmaking:**

1. Reach level 40 and be judged by three Swords in a room with no door you noticed on entering.
2. Unmake a named work — a tower's founding ward, a battlefield curse, a crowned man's geas — and file no claim.
3. Give up your own casting; the order requires that what you sever, you can no longer envy.
4. Carry the soaked edge for a year. If it still lets you sheathe it, you are a Sword.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Judgment of Silence** — Declare one enemy's magic void; for a full engagement they can cast nothing above a cantrip.
- **The Soaked Edge** — Your weapon permanently ignores magical armor, barriers, and displacement effects.
- **Mundane Ground** — Render a wide circle inert; within it, only your own blade remembers magic.
- **Unmake** — Strike a persistent enchantment, construct, or summoned being and end it outright; greater works take two blows.

### Scrivener of Scars

**Role:** Elite striker — engraved ruin, burst orchestration · **Advances from:** Edgescribe

*Scriveners of Scars write in the oldest medium. Their runes no longer fade when the wound closes; the scar itself remains a working script, dormant until read. Kings bearing a Scrivener's mark have signed treaties rather than test what the closing word might still do. The order keeps its library alive, and walking.*

**Playstyle.** Long-game authorship: seed engagements and years with dormant scars, then orchestrate detonations across every text you have ever written. Nothing marked is ever safe.

**The road to Scrivener of Scars:**

1. Reach level 40 and present the order with a scar you wrote ten years prior, still legible, still loaded.
2. Author a rune that outlives its bearer, and stand graveside to prove it.
3. Mark a king, a heresiarch, or an archon, and let the mark be enough; collect nothing.
4. Surrender your practice blades. From now on, everything you write is permanent.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Indelible Script** — Your runes persist through healing, dispelling, and death; only your own hand can strike them out.
- **Read Aloud** — Detonate every scar you have authored within sight, in any order you choose, over three heartbeats.
- **Palimpsest** — Overwrite an enemy's scar with a harsher edition; the old wound reopens as the new rune takes hold.
- **Dedication** — Scribe a protective epigraph on an ally's skin; the first fatal blow against them becomes a detonation instead.

### Warden of the Breach

**Role:** Elite bastion — breach denial, fortress warding · **Advances from:** Linewarden

*When a wall fails, the Warden of the Breach is sent to be the wall. The order maintains perhaps twenty at a time, rotated between fortresses the way relics are rotated between shrines. Chronicles record breaches by two names: where the stone broke, and which Warden stood in the gap.*

**Playstyle.** Become terrain: plant yourself in the gap, scale your lattice to fortress size, and convert every push against you into proof the gap is closed.

**The road to Warden of the Breach:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand a breach through a full assault after the garrison is ordered back behind you.
2. Ward a fortress so well that history records no attempt on it; accept that this is your only monument.
3. Learn the name of every stone in one wall. The order will know if you missed one.
4. Be requested, by name, by a commander who watched a different Warden die well.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Be the Wall** — Rooted in a breach, you gain massive damage reduction and count as fortification for every allied purpose.
- **Gatelaw** — Enemies entering your gap cannot target anyone else; those who attempt to slip past are hurled back.
- **Raise the Stones** — Draw rubble and ruin into your lattice, rebuilding physical cover from the wreck of the wall around you.
- **The Long Vigil** — Your wards no longer decay with time; a post once warded stays warded until you choose otherwise.

### Requiter

**Role:** Elite retaliator — debt-fueled devastation · **Advances from:** Spiteward

*Requiters are the Spiteward doctrine made institutional. The order keeps ledgers going back nine generations: blows owed, blows repaid, interest accrued in blood not yet spilled. A Requiter may arrive decades after the offense, wearing wards that remember what the offender has forgotten. Payment is always in the original currency.*

**Playstyle.** Escalating exchange: soak the opening violence, compound the debt through vindictive wards, and end fights with a repayment no armor was priced for.

**The road to Requiter:**

1. Reach level 40 and balance one ledger the order has carried for a hundred years, to the last blow.
2. Track a debtor across two borders and one war, and collect without touching anyone not named in the entry.
3. Master the forgiveness clause; the order trusts no collector who cannot decline.
4. Add your own name to a ledger for a wrong you committed, and let another Requiter come for you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Nine Ledgers** — Inherit stored debt from the order's ledgers; begin every engagement with retaliation already banked.
- **Mounting Debt** — Debt on an attacker doubles each round they continue striking you.
- **Original Currency** — Your repayment strike duplicates the exact wounds, curses, and breaks the target dealt to you, all at once.
- **Forgiveness Clause** — Void all debt on a target who yields; gain a lasting ward equal to what you declined to collect.

### Escheator

**Role:** Elite spell-thief — seizure and stolen arsenal · **Advances from:** Nullblade

*Escheators hold that unmaking is waste. When a caster is judged unfit, their magic is forfeit — severed whole, sealed into the Escheator's steel, catalogued, and kept. The order maintains the only armory of confiscated spells in the world. Swords of Unmaking call them hoarders. Escheators call the Swords arsonists in a library.*

**Playstyle.** Disarm rather than destroy: sever an enemy's spell, sheathe it in your blade, and answer with their own arsenal. Every fight restocks you.

**The road to Escheator:**

1. Reach level 40 and confiscate a living spell whole before the tribunal that came to watch it burn.
2. Catalogue every spell you have ever severed. The order audits, and the order does not forgive gaps.
3. Carry a seized storm for a season without loosing it, then surrender it to the armory unspent.
4. Decline the Swords' invitation once. They ask exactly once.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Writ of Seizure** — Sever a spell mid-cast and store it in your blade; you may cast it later as your own.
- **Forfeiture** — Your strikes strip enchantments from the target and add them to your blade's hold instead of destroying them.
- **Deep Scabbard** — Your blade holds up to five seized spells; casting one from steel costs no mana.
- **Prior Claim** — When an enemy casts a spell you have stored, yours resolves first and theirs fails.

### Illuminator

**Role:** Elite striker — self-inscribed detonation, sacrifice engine · **Advances from:** Edgescribe

*Illuminators broke with the Scriveners over a single question: whose skin is the page. Their answer is scriptural — they inscribe every rune into their own flesh, gilded and cross-referenced, and open the text in battle. A Scrivener writes ruin on kings. An Illuminator is the manuscript, and turns his own pages burning.*

**Playstyle.** Spend yourself as ammunition: open runes cut into your own skin, trading blood and pages for detonations no enemy scar could hold. Husband the text; it is finite.

**The road to Illuminator:**

1. Reach level 40 and present your body to the order open-shirted; the examiners read you for a day and must find no misprint.
2. Inscribe your first gilded rune over your own heart, awake, in one sitting, holding the stylus yourself.
3. Burn a page you spent ten years writing to save a stranger who will never know its price.
4. Refuse ever to mark another living body. The Scriveners will call it squeamishness; let them.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Gilt Wound** — Inscribe a rune into your own flesh; while it remains closed, it empowers your strikes with its school.
- **Open the Page** — Tear one of your inscribed runes open, releasing its full spell at triple potency and costing health.
- **Rubric** — While below half health, every rune inscribed in you reads one tier higher.
- **Bloodgilt** — Gild a fresh wound an enemy dealt you, converting it into a rune you did not have to pay for.

### Marchwright

**Role:** Elite mobile bastion — column warding on the march · **Advances from:** Linewarden

*Marchwrights answer the Wardens' rooted creed with heresy: ground is not holy, people are. Their lattices are built to walk — wards jointed like harness, moving with the column they shelter. They have brought retreats home intact through burning provinces. Wardens say the Marchwrights defend nothing. Marchwrights say the Wardens defend rubble.*

**Playstyle.** Keep everyone moving: bind marchers into a walking lattice, rebuild wards on the stride, and make the column's pace itself a defense no ambush can time.

**The road to Marchwright:**

1. Reach level 40 and walk a thousand refugees through a hostile province; arrive with a thousand.
2. Carry a warded column across a frozen river at night without once halting. The lattice must not notice the ice.
3. March your wards past a rooted Warden's post, and let the two doctrines regard each other in silence.
4. Sleep walking, mid-column, warded, and wake having lost no one. The order calls this ordinary. Make it so.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Jointed Lattice** — Your ward-bindings hold while allies move; the bound column keeps its full protection at any pace.
- **Cadence** — Set the column's pace; allies moving on your beat gain speed and cannot be slowed or rooted.
- **Rolling Rampart** — Project a ward-wall that walks with your front rank; enemies it passes over are shoved aside and slowed.
- **No One Straggles** — Stragglers and wounded are carried by the lattice itself, dragged gently along at the column's pace.

### Anvilsworn

**Role:** Elite attrition tank — absorption without reprisal · **Advances from:** Spiteward

*The Anvilsworn read the same vindictive wards as the Requiters and drew the opposite law: a blow returned is a blow multiplied. They take everything and answer nothing, wards drinking violence until the attacker's arm, weapon, and conviction fail in that order. Requiters call them a heresy of waste. Fewer people strike Anvilsworn twice.*

**Playstyle.** Stand and receive: convert every blow into hardness, dull the weapons and the will that deliver them, and win when the enemy cannot lift his arms.

**The road to Anvilsworn:**

1. Reach level 40 and stand a Requiter's full collection unanswered; when it is over, ask politely whether the order is satisfied.
2. Outlast a hundred hired strikers in a single day without lifting your blade; the order will count the broken hafts.
3. Strike the retaliation clauses from your own wards by your own hand, and learn how much of your strength was spite.
4. Take a blow from a friend sworn to test you, and charge them nothing for it, not even in your eyes.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Hammer's Ruin** — Weapons striking your wards dull and crack; each blow against you permanently weakens the arm behind it.
- **Hammered In** — Absorb a blow completely and grow harder; each swallowed strike raises your damage reduction for the rest of the fight.
- **Patience of Iron** — While you decline to attack, your wards regenerate and everyone striking you suffers growing exhaustion.
- **Broken on the Anvil** — An enemy whose weapon or stamina breaks against your wards collapses, spent, and cannot attack you again this battle.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Neverdrawn

**Role:** Mythic spell-execution — absolute magical denial · **Advances from:** Sword of Unmaking

*Histories disagree whether The Neverdrawn is a rank or one recurring person. Where they walk, prophecy stalls, scrying returns yesterday, and the towers' oldest grimoires develop blank pages. The blade is no longer distinguishable from the intention. What they cut has, in every sense that can be verified, never been cast.*

**Playstyle.** Erasure as movement: spells die at the thought-stage in your presence; you duel entire schools of magic and edit outcomes retroactively.

**The road to The Neverdrawn:**

1. Reach level 60 and unmake something a god maintains personally; survive the courtesy visit that follows.
2. Erase your own name from every archive, roll, and gravestone that holds it. What remains of you is the rank.
3. Stand where a prophecy says a hero dies, and see to it that the prophecy has never been written.
4. Sheathe the soaked edge for the last time. If the world still holds, you may draw it again.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Never Cast** — Undo a spell that already resolved this engagement; its effects, damage, and consequences are removed from record.
- **Never Written** — Erase one written work of magic permanently, everywhere — every copy, every memory of the wording.
- **The Quiet** — Your presence is a field where magic requires your consent; allies you name are exempt.
- **Final Parry** — Once, cut fate itself: cancel any single event you witness, at the cost of a memory of your choosing.

### The Final Stroke

**Role:** Mythic striker — continent-spanning authored ruin · **Advances from:** Scrivener of Scars

*The order speaks of a stroke that closes every sentence ever begun, and of the one who carries it unspent. The Final Stroke's runes are found on cliff faces, on the hulls of ships not yet sunk, in the flyleaves of books that later mattered. Nothing they have written has finished happening.*

**Playstyle.** Author at world scale: your marks accumulate across a lifetime and a continent, and combat is merely where you choose to punctuate.

**The road to The Final Stroke:**

1. Reach level 60 and detonate, in one ordered breath, marks you left in twelve kingdoms across forty years.
2. Write a rune that alters the outcome of a war you never attended.
3. Compose the terminal stroke. Show it to no one. Carrying it unspent is the rank.
4. Refuse to finish a killing sentence once, and let history see the ellipsis.

**Abilities granted:**

- **A Lifetime's Marginalia** — Every rune you have ever written, anywhere, becomes available for detonation or revision from where you stand.
- **The Unfinished Sentence** — Begin a cut and refuse to finish it; the wound waits, worsening, until you complete the motion — even years later.
- **Corrigendum** — Rewrite one rune's effect after it detonates, applying the corrected version as well.
- **End Mark** — Write a single terminal rune on the world itself; within its radius, nothing you have marked survives the period.

### The Held Gate

**Role:** Mythic bastion — absolute immovability, sanctuary · **Advances from:** Warden of the Breach

*Some fortresses are named for a Warden who never left. The Held Gate has stopped being stationed anywhere; places are stationed around them. Armies reroute rather than test the ground they stand on, and maps mark their vigil the way they mark mountains. No chronicle records one falling. Chronicles record everything else falling first.*

**Playstyle.** You are the objective, and the objective cannot be taken: anchor reality around your vigil, shelter thousands, and outlast whatever arrives.

**The road to The Held Gate:**

1. Reach level 60 and hold one gap through a year of continuous siege; let the besiegers starve first.
2. Have a fortress renamed for you while you still stand in it.
3. Turn away a god politely at your threshold. The politeness is the test.
4. Choose the place you will never leave. The rank begins when the choice does.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Terrain Entire** — You count as a fortress: allies inside your lattice gain walls, gates, and a garrison's worth of wards.
- **The Gate Holds** — Once per vigil, negate an army-scale attack completely — siege spell, dragonfire, tide, or god's regard.
- **Ground That Remembers** — Any ground you have defended before recognizes you; your wards there raise themselves at full strength instantly.
- **Open for No One** — While your vigil stands, nothing crosses your threshold without consent — including effects that ignore walls.

### The Iron Rebuke

**Role:** Mythic retaliator — inevitable reciprocal ruin · **Advances from:** Requiter

*The order's ledgers end in a symbol meaning the balance now keeps itself. The Iron Rebuke is that symbol given a body. Harm done in their presence returns unbidden, the way weather returns. Tyrants have abdicated on rumor of an approach. Wars have been settled by asking which side they would find more owed.*

**Playstyle.** Do nothing, and be lethal: reflection is passive, total, and lawful. Your art is choosing what to forgive before the world repays itself.

**The road to The Iron Rebuke:**

1. Reach level 60 and end a war by arriving; let both sides read their ledgers and reconsider.
2. Forgive the debt you have carried longest. The order says the rank cannot be held with it.
3. Stand in a massacre and let the verdict work; touch no one, and be the only thing survivors agree on.
4. Outlive the order. Keep the ledgers anyway.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Standing Verdict** — All harm dealt within your presence is automatically mirrored to its source; you no longer act to retaliate.
- **Debt Older Than You** — Invoke the full nine-generation ledger against one target whose line is named in it.
- **Amnesty** — Suspend the verdict for all within your sight; while it holds, no one can harm anyone.
- **Final Accounting** — Once in a lifetime, repay everything at once: every recorded debt of the order, discharged through your body in a single hour.

### The Crowded Sheath

**Role:** Mythic arsenal — an era's seized magic · **Advances from:** Escheator

*Archivists dispute the inventory; none dispute the weight. The Crowded Sheath carries every spell the order has seized in living memory — plagues of fire, unmakings, a god's stray sentence — all quiet in one blade. Treaties specify where they may stand. When the Sheath is drawn even an inch, weather changes allegiance.*

**Playstyle.** Fight with an era's confiscations: draw the right seizure for each moment, spend centuries of other people's power, and sheathe what the world cannot bear loose.

**The road to The Crowded Sheath:**

1. Reach level 60 and inherit the armory entire: every seizure since the founding, sworn into one sheath at your hip.
2. Carry it a year without drawing. Cities will learn your itinerary and reroute their wars accordingly.
3. Draw once. From ten thousand seizures, choose the one spell that ends the day gently, and spend it.
4. Refuse the king who asks what you carry; refuse the god who asks after him.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Inventory** — Every spell the order has ever seized is available to you; casting one consumes its only copy.
- **An Inch of Daylight** — Draw the blade a finger's width; the leakage alone dispels, deafens, and staggers everything nearby.
- **Deed of Release** — Loose a seized cataclysm whole — plague, storm, or siege-spell — exactly as it was cast the day it was taken.
- **Peacebond** — Bind your own blade into its sheath before witnesses; while the bond holds, no seized spell can escape, and your wards double.

### The Sole Edition

**Role:** Mythic striker — living archive, catastrophic recitation · **Advances from:** Illuminator

*When the Vermillion School was burned — shelves, masters, and memory — one Illuminator had already taken the whole curriculum into her skin. The Sole Edition is that office: a person who is the last copy of a dead school. Wars have been declared to read them, and longer wars to keep them shut.*

**Playstyle.** Your body is a dead school's canon: recite chapters as devastation, spend pages you can never reprint, and decide daily what knowledge is worth your skin.

**The road to The Sole Edition:**

1. Reach level 60 and be the last: outlive your school, its rivals, and everyone qualified to check your citations.
2. Recite one chapter over a battlefield, and let both armies see what the burned masters could do.
3. Choose the page you will never spend, whatever it costs. An edition is defined by what it keeps.
4. Write one worthy page of your own into the canon before naming a successor. A school that cannot grow is only a tomb.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Chapter and Verse** — Read a chapter of yourself aloud; the dead school's magic resolves at the strength of its founders.
- **Out of Print** — Spend a page permanently to cast its spell at triple effect; nothing in the world can restore it.
- **Index of Scars** — You know instantly which of your pages answers a given enemy; the correct rune warms first.
- **Closed Canon** — While your text stays shut, you take reduced damage from every school inscribed in you.

### The Sheltered Mile

**Role:** Mythic escort — moving sanctuary at nation scale · **Advances from:** Marchwright

*Somewhere on the roads there is a mile that cannot be attacked, and it moves. The Sheltered Mile has walked whole cities out of falling kingdoms — the column entering the pass is the column leaving it, to the child. Armies petition to know the route. The route, chroniclers note, declines to exist in advance.*

**Playstyle.** Everything within your mile is home ground: thousands move as one warded body, terrain cooperates, and pursuit arrives to find the road already over.

**The road to The Sheltered Mile:**

1. Reach level 60 and move a city that has already fallen: gates down, streets burning, every life still inside.
2. Outwalk a famine, a cavalry, and a winter in the same season, and deliver the column better fed than it left.
3. Let the Mile cross a battlefield mid-battle. Neither army must find anything to charge.
4. Never arrive. A Mile that ends somewhere is only a road; be mid-journey whenever the histories check.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Mile** — A mile of road centered on you counts as fortress interior for everyone you shelter.
- **The Route Declines** — Your path cannot be scried, predicted, or ambushed; pursuers' maps disagree about where you have been.
- **Waystones** — Ground your column rests on stays warded forever after; your route becomes a chain of sanctuaries.
- **Bring Them All** — No one sheltered in the Mile can be singled out; harm aimed at one is spread across your wards entire.

### The Quenched

**Role:** Mythic bulwark — violence-ending absorption · **Advances from:** Anvilsworn

*Smiths say a blade is finished when the quench stops singing. The Quenched has been struck by everything an age could raise — siege trains, hexes, a mountain, wronged gods — and answered none of it. Histories disagree whether they can still be harmed. The armies that tested the question are the reason histories disagree.*

**Playstyle.** Be the end of force: absorb anything once, harden against it forever, and stand until every weapon, spell, and intention raised against you dies of futility.

**The road to The Quenched:**

1. Reach level 60 and take a god's full blow standing, offering nothing in return but your continued attention.
2. Walk into the heart of a battle that has burned for three days, and stand until it goes out around you.
3. Outlast a siege alone as its sole objective; when the engines rust, walk out through the quiet.
4. Keep your blade. You will never draw it again, and it must be with you when you do not.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Struck Once** — Any specific attack that hits you cannot harm you again; your wards learn it permanently.
- **The Singing Stops** — Each round an enemy attacks you, their damage everywhere drops; at zero, they cannot raise arms against anyone today.
- **Mountain's Portion** — Take one army-scale blow per battle — siege engine, cataclysm, divine wrath — as though it were rain.
- **Nothing Left to Land** — Declare the violence spent: enemies whose every attack has failed against you this battle cannot attack again today.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Flyleaf

**Role:** Apotheosis — absence of magic given office · **Advances from:** The Neverdrawn

*Every grimoire holds pages that were never written on. Scholars once called this binding waste. Now they leave offerings in the margins. The Flyleaf is where the Neverdrawn went when the name ran out: a rank that exists as absence, held by someone, presumably, whom no record can confirm and no spell can precede.*

**Playstyle.** Play the gap in the world's text: unhappen magic wholesale, exist beneath every record, and be findable only by those who need nothing found.

**The road to The Flyleaf:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop being mentioned — not forgotten, never known. Your deeds must continue happening regardless.
2. Leave one page blank in the world's oldest grimoire by standing near it. The tower will re-shelve the book as scripture.
3. Let a historian prove you do not exist. Attend the lecture. Applaud.
4. Hold the rank the way the page holds nothing: entirely.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Margin of the World** — You exist in the unwritten parts of reality; effects that target names, fates, or records cannot find you.
- **Was Never** — Retire an entire school of magic from an engagement; for the battle's length, it has not been invented.
- **Nowhere Cited** — Allies walking beside you vanish from prophecy, scrying, and hostile memory until dawn.
- **The Courtesy Returned** — Once per age, a god who acts in your sight discovers the act has never been theirs.

### The Maker's Mark

**Role:** Apotheosis — endings authored as law · **Advances from:** The Final Stroke

*At the end of every finished manuscript, scribes set a small mark meaning the work is done and someone made it. The Maker's Mark is that mark, seated in a person. Wherever anything truly ends — a war, a dynasty, a long grief — their stroke is found at the bottom, in a hand archivists have stopped pretending not to recognize.*

**Playstyle.** Write conclusions directly: end effects, eras, and enemies by signing them finished, and ration yourself, because everything you complete stays completed.

**The road to The Maker's Mark:**

1. Reach level 85 and finish something the gods left running on purpose. Sign it.
2. End a war, a famine, and a story in the same year, each with a single stroke, each leaving no body behind.
3. Find every work you left unfinished across your life and finish each one kindly. The rank accepts no outstanding sentences.
4. Write the colophon of an age and date it. Historians will argue for centuries, using your date to do so.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Here Ends** — Sign any ongoing effect — spell, curse, plague, siege — as finished; it concludes as though it had run its full course.
- **A Familiar Hand** — Enemies who find your mark on their fallen falter; each must rally or quit the field.
- **Fair Copy** — Once per day, restore the field to how it stood three breaths ago, keeping only the endings you authored.
- **The Unspent Stroke** — The terminal stroke may finally land: end one thing of any size, permanently, once. Choose slowly.

### The Hinge of Heaven

**Role:** Apotheosis — immovability as cosmic law · **Advances from:** The Held Gate

*Astronomers note that the sky turns around a single stubborn point, and admit, when pressed, that the point was not always there. The Hinge of Heaven is the Gate's vigil made cosmological: the one fixed thing, around which weather wheels, wars orbit, and every door that has ever held has quietly been hanging.*

**Playstyle.** Be the fixed point: nothing moves you, everything defended by anyone anywhere borrows your stillness, and the world arranges its sieges around your convenience.

**The road to The Hinge of Heaven:**

1. Reach level 85 and outstay your fortress: let the stones retire, the capital move, and keep the vigil the geography abandoned.
2. Hold the night together where two heavens collide; astronomers will record a new star. Let them name it wrongly.
3. Be the reason a returning army finds home standing when nothing else on the map is.
4. Choose stillness so completely that the world turns around it. You will know it has worked when north moves.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Axis** — You cannot be moved, unmade, aged, or exiled; effects that attempt it rotate the world slightly instead.
- **Every Door Holds** — Bless all thresholds in a kingdom at once; while you stand vigil, none of them can be forced.
- **Borrowed Stillness** — Grant an ally your immovability for one blow; what strikes them finds you instead — and stops.
- **The Turning World** — Rather than approach an objective, remain: once per vigil, the world turns until the objective arrives before you.

### The Talion

**Role:** Apotheosis — reciprocity as standing world-law · **Advances from:** The Iron Rebuke

*Jurists in six kingdoms cite a law none of them passed: harm returns. The Talion is that statute, ambulatory. Where the Rebuke was a body the balance lived in, the Talion is the balance, wearing a body out of courtesy. Nothing owed escapes; nothing forgiven is reopened. Courts adjourn when they walk in.*

**Playstyle.** Preside rather than fight: harm within your jurisdiction returns at the moment of your choosing, mercy sets precedent, and your presence is due process.

**The road to The Talion:**

1. Reach level 85 and survive your own docket: every harm you ever dealt returns in one session, and you must preside.
2. Be cited as precedent by a court that does not believe you exist, and let the judgment stand.
3. Retire the ledgers you kept past the order's death. A law that still needs the written record is not yet you.
4. Hear the case brought against the world itself. Rule narrowly. Adjourn kindly.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Jurisdiction** — Your verdict covers a whole region; harm within it mirrors to its source at a moment you appoint.
- **Precedent** — Every mercy you grant becomes binding law; the forgiven act cannot be committed in your jurisdiction again.
- **Willful Breach** — Those who strike knowing the law take it back doubled; ignorance excuses the first blow only.
- **Standing Adjournment** — Suspend all violence in your presence indefinitely; battles in your jurisdiction must be argued instead.

### The Scabbard of the World

**Role:** Apotheosis — custodian of all ended magic · **Advances from:** The Crowded Sheath

*When a spell dies anywhere — dispelled, unmade, its last caster gone — scholars now agree it goes somewhere, and have stopped publishing where. The Scabbard of the World is the office every ended magic retires into. The holder walks lightly for someone carrying that. Thieves' guilds maintain a standing rule: no.*

**Playstyle.** Carry everything magic has ever lost: draw dead spells from any age, lend them briefly, and be the reason unmaking is never quite the end.

**The road to The Scabbard of the World:**

1. Reach level 85 and accept the escheat of ages: every spell that has ever ended, vested in your keeping.
2. Sheathe a falling cataclysm before witnesses, and file it correctly.
3. Be robbed by the finest thief alive, and let them find the scabbard holds only what consents to be held.
4. Answer, once, what you are keeping it all for. Answer honestly. Answer no one else.

**Abilities granted:**

- **All That Ended** — Draw any spell that has died from the world, cast it once, and return it to rest.
- **Brief Parole** — Release a dead spell into an ally's hands for one hour; it returns to you when spent.
- **Sheathe the Age** — Sheathe an active cataclysm as it happens; the storm, plague, or god-wrath simply retires into your keeping.
- **Nothing Is Lost** — Any magic destroyed in your presence vests in you instead; unmaking near you is merely a transfer of title.

### The Apocrypha

**Role:** Apotheosis — disputed canon, uncontainable technique · **Advances from:** The Sole Edition

*The Sole Edition can die; the argument about what was in her cannot. The Apocrypha is that argument, given rank. Their techniques are cited in books that deny they exist, taught by tutors no college employs, recognized on battlefields by scholars who then recant. Every forbidden library keeps one shelf empty, just in case.*

**Playstyle.** Fight as a text that survived its burning: cast from chapters that officially never existed, and be impossible to censor, copy, or disprove.

**The road to The Apocrypha:**

1. Reach level 85 and be burned in effigy, in archive, and in fact. Attend all three. Survive the reviews.
2. Have your finest technique condemned by a council that uses it in the field within the year.
3. Leave the last copy of yourself where no one will believe it: a child's primer, a laundry list, a treaty's margin.
4. Outlive your own refutation. The rank holds as long as the argument does; see that it continues.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Doubtful Attribution** — After any engagement, your presence in it becomes disputed; effects, curses, and vendettas anchored to your participation lapse.
- **Variant Reading** — Cast any spell of your dead school in a variant no counter was ever written for.
- **Burned and Extant** — When killed, you persist as the argument: return within a season, provably elsewhere the entire time.
- **One Empty Shelf** — Any library or archive you enter yields the forbidden chapter you need; it was always miscatalogued there.

### The Drover's Star

**Role:** Apotheosis — safe passage as celestial office · **Advances from:** The Sheltered Mile

*Refugee columns steer by a star the old charts do not show, and arrive. Chartmakers mark it reluctantly; it will not hold still between editions. The Drover's Star is the Mile raised into the sky's business: an office of safe passage, held by someone who still walks below it, at the head of everyone.*

**Playstyle.** Shepherd at world scale: every column moving under your star is warded, every road you have walked stays kind, and pursuit forgets its reasons.

**The road to The Drover's Star:**

1. Reach level 85 and rise: let the columns below elect your vigil into the sky, and keep walking anyway.
2. Bring home a people whose home is gone, and have the place they arrive at agree to be it.
3. Be the star a fleeing enemy column steers by, and let them arrive too. The office does not check banners.
4. Accept that roads credit no one. The office pays in arrivals; keep the count, and tell no one the total.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Under My Star** — Every column moving toward safety beneath your sky gains your lattice; distance raises no objection.
- **The Kind Roads** — Every road you have ever warded remains sanctuary forever; ambush, pursuit, and weather fail on them.
- **Setting Out** — However long the road, a column under your star reaches shelter by dawn; the miles simply behave.
- **No Pursuit** — Those hunting anyone under your protection lose the trail, then the orders, then the grievance.

### The Dead Calm

**Role:** Apotheosis — the weather that ends wars · **Advances from:** The Quenched

*Sailors know the weather where wind refuses; soldiers know its cousin. Battles end when it arrives — not won, not lost, over. The Dead Calm is the Quenched made climate: the reason old battlefields stay quiet, the stillness violence retires into. Veterans feel it come and start folding their banners without orders.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive as weather: violence within your calm loses conviction, armies remember appointments elsewhere, and whatever must still be fought is fought gently and last.

**The road to The Dead Calm:**

1. Reach level 85 and end a century's war by weathering over it, leaving both anthems unsung and both harvests standing.
2. Visit every field where you were ever struck, and leave each one quiet enough to farm.
3. Be prayed to by both sides on the eve of a battle that, come morning, declines to occur.
4. Still your own last violence. The Calm begins at home; let the smiths finally stop arguing.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Calm Comes In** — Settle over a battlefield; all damage everywhere on it decays each round until nothing lands at all.
- **No Wind for War** — Siege engines, war-spells, and cavalry charges fail to start within your calm; what needs momentum finds none.
- **Old Fields** — On any ground where a battle has ever ended, you cannot be harmed; most ground qualifies by now.
- **The Weather Clears** — Declare a war spent; combatants on all sides find themselves unable to continue it, today or ever.
