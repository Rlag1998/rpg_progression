# ⚓ Corsair

> *Violence with a tide table.*

**Archetype:** Cunning

The Corsair works the coasts the way a factor works a market: with a tide table, a powder budget, and no illusions about partners. The sea is a business associate with moods — generous, then murderous, then generous again. Corsairs have been rich twice and drowned once, and they hold both events against nobody. Their craft is violence, scheduled.

## Tier I — Initiate (Level 1)

### Corsair

**Role:** Melee skirmisher — opportunist burst and mobility

*Every harbor with bad lighting keeps a few: deckhands who noticed that cargo pays better before it clears customs. A first-rank corsair owns a knife, a coil of rope, and a working respect for the tide. The sea has not decided about them yet. Neither, officially, has the law.*

**Playstyle.** Hit fast, in daylight, where the cargo is. Swing on ropes, fight dirty, and leave before anyone agrees on what happened.

**Taking the first step:**

1. Sign aboard any ship that asks few questions, and learn the tide tables before you learn the crew's names.
2. Survive your first storm on deck, not below; the sea remembers who watched.
3. Take your first prize in daylight, flag up, and count the take honestly in front of the whole crew.
4. Be drowned once — briefly. What you say to the water while it holds you is between you and it.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Boarding Hook** — Hurl a hooked line 12 meters and haul yourself along it; arriving on an enemy staggers them and opens your next strike.
- **Powder Kiss** — Fire a point-blank pistol shot that blinds with smoke; deals double damage to targets already engaged with you.
- **Sea Legs** — Rolling terrain, rigging, and rails never slow you; you fight at full speed on any surface that moves.
- **Danger Money** — While outnumbered, every hit you land returns a little health and stamina; the rate improves with each additional enemy.

## Tier II — Adept (Level 10)

### Boardswain

**Role:** Melee assault — gap-close and opening burst · **Advances from:** Corsair

*Boardswains hold the rank the way a boatswain holds ropes: as the whole point. They are the first party over the rail, chosen for speed, nerve, and a documented indifference to falling. Merchant captains describe them to insurers in the past tense. The title is spoken with a straight face; the work isn't.*

**Playstyle.** Open every fight by arriving somewhere inconvenient. Chain grapples into strikes, keep momentum stacking, and end the boarding before the bell finds its rope.

**The road to Boardswain:**

1. Reach level 10 and be first over a defended rail three times; the crew counts, and the crew does not round up.
2. Board a ship whose rail stands higher than your mast, and be waiting at the wheel when your captain arrives.
3. Fall between two hulls in a fight and climb back aboard the enemy's. Which ship you climb is the examination.
4. Take a prize so fast the log cannot name the hour; let the enemy's own bell, unrung, stand witness.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Away Boarders** — Leap 15 meters to an enemy position; allies following within 3 seconds arrive with the same staggering impact.
- **Running Rigging** — Each grapple, swing, or vault grants momentum; spend 3 stacks to strike twice in one motion.
- **Before the Bell** — If no enemy has raised an alarm, your first strike on each target deals 40% bonus damage.
- **Teeth and Steel** — Clench a knife in your teeth while both hands work rope; you stay armed mid-swing, and swinging strikes cannot be parried.

### Saltwise

**Role:** Skirmish support — foresight, positioning, weather edges · **Advances from:** Corsair

*Saltwise is what a crew calls the hand who says shorten sail an hour before the captain does, and is right. They read water the way rangers read ground — and are cheerfully useless a mile inland. The rank is not granted. It accumulates, voyage by voyage, like salt in a rope.*

**Playstyle.** Fight the conditions, not the enemy. Call swells, gusts, and footing before they happen, so your side is always standing where the sea helps.

**The road to Saltwise:**

1. Reach level 10 and call the weather right thirty days running; the thirty-first day, the helmsman asks you first.
2. Bring a ship through a strait at night by sound and swell alone, hands never leaving the rail.
3. Taste bottom-mud from the lead and name the coast blindfolded. Any coast. The sea has a finite number of flavors, and you know them.
4. Refuse an easy landing you cannot explain your unease about, and be right by morning.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Read the Water** — Study the surface to reveal currents, shoals, and anything swimming; allies gain movement speed along routes you call.
- **Glass Falling** — Call an incoming squall before the sky admits it; when it lands, braced allies hold footing while enemies stagger and slide.
- **Mast Coin** — Nail a coin to the mast at battle's start; once, a killing blow against any ally lands as a graze instead.
- **Tide Table** — You always know the tide, the moon, and the hour; abilities that depend on timing recharge 20% faster.

## Tier III — Specialist (Level 25)

### Knifegrin

**Role:** Shock striker — fear-driven melee burst · **Advances from:** Boardswain

*The grin is literal: a knife held in the teeth leaves the mouth free to smile. Knifegrins study terror as a boarding tool — the scream timed to the swing, the pause on the rail in full view. Rogues work unseen; a Knifegrin's whole craft is being seen too late to matter.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive loudly and first. Feed on enemy fear: every foe who breaks or freezes fuels your next hit. Kill the brave ones early; the rest follow.

**The road to Knifegrin:**

1. Reach level 25 and take a ship where no shot is fired after yours; terror must finish what powder introduces.
2. Learn to smile with a knife in your teeth. Practice on a mirror until the mirror is the one unsettled.
3. Board alone, in daylight, announced. If the crew still fights, you were not ready. Recover, and be louder.
4. Have your grin described, inaccurately and worse, in three ports you have never visited.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Pause on the Rail** — Stand in full view for 2 seconds before engaging; enemies who watched suffer reduced accuracy against you all fight.
- **Blade-in-Teeth** — While your knife is clenched and visible, enemies below half health must check morale before attacking you.
- **Pick the Brave One** — Mark the steadiest enemy; killing your mark panics every foe who could see them fall.
- **Faster Than the Bell** — If you down an enemy within 6 seconds of engaging, the fight's alarm never sounds: reinforcements do not come.

### Slowmatch

**Role:** Demolitionist — timed charges, area burst · **Advances from:** Boardswain

*Slowmatches carry fire the way clerks carry ink: measured, budgeted, and always lit. They speak of hulls in ounces of powder and doors in seconds of cord. A good one boards with six charges and returns with one, having spent the other five like an apology — brief, loud, and final.*

**Playstyle.** Plan the fight in fuses. Place charges during the chaos your allies make, then collect the field in one rolling detonation, timed to the second.

**The road to Slowmatch:**

1. Reach level 25 and apprentice under a gunner with eight fingers; the missing two are the curriculum.
2. Cut a hundred fuses to a hundred counts and be wrong by less than a breath, total.
3. Open a strongroom through the hull beside it without waking the man asleep against the door.
4. Carry a lit slow-match through a powder magazine at a walk. The rank is the walk.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Measured Ounce** — Set a shaped charge sized exactly to its target; structures breach, doors open, and nothing behind them is scratched.
- **Grenado** — Lob a fused iron grenado; it detonates on your count, not on impact, and you are always right.
- **Rolling Fuse** — Link up to four charges on one cord; they fire in sequence, herding survivors toward the last one.
- **Burnt Fingers** — Snuff any explosive, yours or theirs, bare-handed; you take minor fire damage and always think it was worth it.

### Weathereye

**Role:** Forecaster support — vision, initiative, prediction · **Advances from:** Saltwise

*A Weathereye reads the sky like a bill of lading: what's arriving, what it's carrying, and for whom. They take no credit for storms — the storm was coming anyway; they merely booked the good seats. Navies keep one on retainer and pretend it is for morale.*

**Playstyle.** Play two minutes ahead. Reveal what the enemy will do, position allies for weather that hasn't arrived, and take polite credit afterward.

**The road to Weathereye:**

1. Reach level 25 and log a full year of dawns; the examiners read only the mornings you got wrong.
2. Predict the hour a hurricane makes landfall from three days out, and anchor your own ship on the strength of it.
3. Outsail a naval squadron using nothing but cloud, current, and their captain's impatience.
4. Refuse to explain how you knew, once, to someone important. The rank includes the silence.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Bill of Lading** — Read an enemy group like cargo: reveal their numbers, condition, and which one is carrying the thing worth taking.
- **Falling Glass** — Predict the field's next hazard aloud; allies gain evasion against it, and your accuracy rises while the prediction stands.
- **Weather Gauge** — Take the weather gauge: while your side holds the wind, your ranged attacks reach farther and theirs fall short.
- **Sailor's Warning** — Mark one enemy ability you have seen before; the next time it begins, everyone on your side knows.

### Caulborn

**Role:** Luck skirmisher — probability edges, death defiance · **Advances from:** Saltwise

*Born behind the caul, sailors say, and the sea may not drown you; Caulborn corsairs test the clause professionally. Dice fall kindly near them, ropes fray elsewhere, and musket balls arrive slightly used. None of it is magic. It is favor — earned, the Caulborn insist, though none can say when.*

**Playstyle.** Court variance. Your numbers wobble where others' hold steady — and the wobble loves you. Push every long shot; the odds are lying in your favor.

**The road to Caulborn:**

1. Reach level 25 and survive three deaths the crew has already mourned; attend your own wakes politely.
2. Win a coin toss eleven times running in front of witnesses, then decline the twelfth. Knowing when to decline is the rank.
3. Be the only survivor of something, once. Do not enjoy it. The sea checks.
4. Carry your caul, or buy one honestly from a midwife; the sea has never once checked the paperwork.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Kind Dice** — Any failed critical chance rerolls once per attack; the reroll improves the worse the fight is going.
- **Frayed Elsewhere** — Once per battle, an attack that should hit you finds a reason not to: a stumble, a gust, a misfire.
- **Caul Clause** — You cannot drown, and any death by falling into water becomes instead a rescue, eventually, by something.
- **Spilled Salt** — Enemies adjacent to you suffer small mishaps: jams, slips, dropped guards. Individually explicable. Statistically damning.

## Tier IV — Elite (Level 40)

### Noonflag

**Role:** Terror controller — mass surrender, bloodless victory · **Advances from:** Knifegrin

*The Noonflag order hoists its colors at midday, in plain sight, with hours of sea room left to run. This is arithmetic, not mercy: a crew given every chance to flee and choosing surrender empties no more powder. Their prizes arrive unbloodied and their reputation does the boarding. Quarterless captains call them soft, carefully.*

**Playstyle.** Win the fight before it files itself as one. Stack dread, offer exits, and punish only the ones who refuse both. Bodies mean you played badly.

**The road to Noonflag:**

1. Reach level 40 and take seven prizes in a season without a death on either ledger; the order audits both.
2. Sail into a harbor that has heard of you, flag up, and buy your supplies at fair prices from people who watch your hands.
3. Accept a surrender you wanted to refuse. The one you wanted to refuse is the examination.
4. Receive the noon colors from a captain who has never once fired first, and never once needed to.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Colors at Noon** — Announce yourself to the field; every enemy who can see your flag loses damage for each escape route left open.
- **Strike Your Colors** — Offer terms mid-battle; enemies below half morale lay down arms and are removed from the fight as surrendered, not slain.
- **Sea Room** — Open an escape corridor; enemies who take it cannot return, and each departure demoralizes those who stayed.
- **The Price of Refusing** — Your first strike against any enemy who declined surrender deals triple damage, in front of everyone.

### Powder Surgeon

**Role:** Precision demolitionist — surgical burst, structure removal · **Advances from:** Slowmatch

*Powder Surgeons open ships the way jewelers open lockets: to a diagram, ounce by ounce, sparing what must keep working. A prize sunk is a fee declined. They carry instruments in velvet rolls — drills, cords, brass scales — and despise the Hellburners as butchers who happen to own matches.*

**Playstyle.** Remove exactly what the fight requires: this mast, that gate, the third gun. Small charges, exact seconds, zero collateral. Your explosions have edges.

**The road to Powder Surgeon:**

1. Reach level 40 and present a taken ship to the order with every deck intact and the strongroom door on its hinges, open.
2. Sink nothing for a year. The year resets if you are seen enjoying an explosion beyond professional satisfaction.
3. Blow the chains off a prisoner without bruising the wrists. The order provides volunteers; the volunteers are all senior surgeons.
4. Decline, in writing, an invitation from the Hellburners. Keep the reply courteous; you will meet their work again on the water.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Charge to the Grain** — Place a charge weighed to the grain; it destroys its stated target completely and nothing else, ever.
- **Take It Intact** — Disable a mechanism, mount, or construct without destroying it; your side may use it once repaired.
- **Sounding Knock** — Rap a hull or wall to map its weak lines; your next charge there is half-cost and full-effect.
- **Ounce of Prevention** — Detonate an enemy's own powder store from a distance, having quietly under-fused it earlier in the fight.

### Stormbroker

**Role:** Weather tactician — forecast warfare, battlefield control · **Advances from:** Weathereye

*Stormbrokers do not raise weather; they schedule engagements inside it. A commission names the prize, and the Stormbroker names the hour the gale will hold the prize's harbor shut. They speak of hurricanes the way factors speak of difficult but reliable colleagues. No magic is involved. Somehow this is worse.*

**Playstyle.** Fight where the forecast says to. Arrive with the squall at your back, leave inside the rain, and make the sky read like your battle plan.

**The road to Stormbroker:**

1. Reach level 40 and deliver three fleets to three storms on time; the storms' punctuality will be credited to you. Accept the credit.
2. Sit out a season's richest prize because the sky said no. The order confirms every refusal against the wrecks of those who went.
3. Negotiate — that is the word the rank insists on — with a hurricane, by sailing its edge for six days and never once being surprised.
4. Hand a rival captain a true forecast and watch them distrust it into their own wreck. File this under instruction, not malice.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Scheduled Gale** — If battle begins in weather you forecast aloud beforehand, allies gain initiative and enemies begin the fight drenched, deafened, and late.
- **Lee Shore Appointment** — Herd an enemy toward the coast for 10 seconds; wind and current cooperate, having been consulted in advance.
- **Difficult Colleague** — Call in the squall line early; a wall of wind and rain crosses the field on your side's schedule.
- **Act of Weather** — Damage your enemies take from weather is recorded as no one's fault; it bypasses armor, wards, and litigation.

### Deepwager

**Role:** Gambler striker — staked power, all-or-nothing spikes · **Advances from:** Caulborn

*Deepwagers hold that luck unspent is luck insulted. They stake real things — blood, fingers, the ship, their own unfinished drowning — and play for outcomes no plan could purchase. Tithesmen call this robbing a patient partner. Deepwagers agree, and observe that the sea keeps dealing them in.*

**Playstyle.** Bet the fight on the fight. Ante health, gear, and position for doubled outcomes; fold early when the table turns. Never play sober odds — anyone can do that.

**The road to Deepwager:**

1. Reach level 40 and win a ship on a single cut of the cards, having staked your own; sail your winnings home.
2. Lose everything once, completely, and pay without a word. The order watches the paying, not the losing.
3. Stake your unfinished drowning against a storm's crossing and arrive dry. Do not ask what the sea would have won.
4. Teach a Tithesman to play cards. Take everything except the coin they nailed to the mast. Leave that; you are not a fool.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Blood Ante** — Stake a quarter of your health at combat's start; land the first blow and it returns doubled.
- **Double or Drown** — Declare double stakes on your next ability: twice the effect on success, and on failure the sea briefly collects you instead.
- **The Folded Hand** — Concede an exchange deliberately: take the hit, bank the insult, and gain guaranteed success on your next wager.
- **Drowned Man's Dice** — Roll bone dice mid-combat; on doubles, your cooldowns clear; on snake eyes, so do the enemy's. Roll anyway.

### Quarterless

**Role:** Executioner — kill-chain momentum, area dread · **Advances from:** Knifegrin

*The Quarterless fly red and mean it. Their doctrine is older than the order: mercy is a tax every future prize collects, and one annihilated crew buys a hundred bloodless surrenders — for other people. They believe they perform the Noonflag's arithmetic honestly, at the bloody end where it balances. Ports pay them to exist elsewhere.*

**Playstyle.** Momentum is the weapon. Every kill accelerates the next; hesitation resets you. Leave one witness per engagement — the doctrine is strict about the arithmetic.

**The road to Quarterless:**

1. Reach level 40 and take the red flag from a Quarterless captain's body; the order has no other ceremony of succession.
2. Erase a pirate-hunter crew to the last name but one, and choose the one for the story they will tell.
3. Refuse a surrender you wanted to accept. The one you wanted to accept is the examination.
4. Read the Noonflag ledgers of prizes taken without blood, and understand exactly whose blood pre-paid them. Do not argue. Sail.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Red Colors** — Hoist the no-quarter flag: your side's damage rises, and no enemy may surrender, flee, or be healed past half.
- **The Next Is Faster** — Each kill grants attack and movement speed for 8 seconds; the stacks cap where your conscience should be.
- **One Witness** — Designate one enemy who cannot be harmed by anyone; when all their allies are dead, they are released to talk.
- **No Quarter Asked** — While below half health you deal the same bonus damage you would take; the doctrine cuts both ways.

### Hellburner

**Role:** Area denial — walls of fire, zone control · **Advances from:** Slowmatch

*Hellburners hold that a ship is temporary and a lesson is not. Their craft is the fireship, the drifting magazine, the harbor that stops insuring itself. Where Powder Surgeons bill by the prize, Hellburners are paid by the absence: shipping lanes empty on rumor alone. Each carries their own pyre aboard, pre-rigged, as doctrine.*

**Playstyle.** Turn the field into a cost nobody will pay. Lay fire in walls and drifts, herd enemies with heat, and never plan a retreat you'd mourn.

**The road to Hellburner:**

1. Reach level 40 and ride your first fireship to the point of no return, then past it, then swim.
2. Cut loose a fortune in captured cargo and light it where its owners can watch. The lesson is the invoice.
3. Sleep aboard your own pre-rigged pyre for a season without once checking the fuses. Doctrine calls this faith. Insurers call it something else.
4. Watch the Powder Surgeons take a prize intact, and feel nothing but patience. Everything is kindling eventually; professionals can wait.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Fireship** — Convert any vessel, cart, or construct into a drifting bomb that steers itself toward the largest enemy cluster.
- **Herding Fire** — Ignite lines of fire that move: the burn advances a meter per second in the direction you swept your arm.
- **Drifting Magazine** — Set a slow-drifting powder cache alight; enemies must scatter or share its final opinion.
- **Pre-Rigged** — When reduced to zero health, you detonate everything you have placed, and are found later, somehow, singed and swimming.

### Graveyard Pilot

**Role:** Terrain controller — hazard mastery, pursuit denial · **Advances from:** Weathereye

*Every coast keeps a graveyard — the reef, the bar, the strait that eats keels — and every graveyard keeps a Pilot who uses it as a private road. Pursuit follows them in exactly once. Stormbrokers weaponize the sky; Pilots hold that the weapon is beneath them, and the road is not.*

**Playstyle.** Make geography pick sides. Lead allies through hazards at full speed, lure pursuers into water that finishes your arguments, and never fight where you stand — fight where they sink.

**The road to Graveyard Pilot:**

1. Reach level 40 and take a laden ship through the Teethwater at night, on a falling tide, and gain the harbor by dawn.
2. Chart nothing. Your graveyard lives in your hands and inner ear; paper is how other people's heirs find it.
3. Lead a warship in and come out alone. Answer the inquiry with the tide tables, and be acquitted by arithmetic.
4. Memorize the wrecks by name and cargo. You will be sailing over your predecessors; know whom to thank.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Private Road** — Allies you lead ignore terrain hazards and difficult ground while following within 10 meters of your path.
- **Follow Me In** — Mark a hazard as inviting; the next enemy to pursue you across it takes its full, undivided attention.
- **By the Mark** — Sound the depth aloud; for 8 seconds your side knows exactly where footing, cover, and drowning begin.
- **The Once** — Escape any engagement through terrain that closes behind you; each enemy may be taught this lesson only once, which suffices.

### Tithesman

**Role:** Fortune warden — luck insurance, paid guarantees · **Advances from:** Caulborn

*Tithesmen keep the old accounts: a share of every prize over the side, rum for the crossing, silence at the rail for the drowned. In return the sea is punctual with them, and lethal coincidence books elsewhere. Deepwagers sneer that favor bought is favor rented. Tithesmen pay that no mind, and pay everything else precisely.*

**Playstyle.** Spend resources to remove chance. Pay gold, gear, and your own share to guarantee crits, cancel enemy luck, and keep the crew inside the sea's goodwill.

**The road to Tithesman:**

1. Reach level 40 and tithe the sea a fortune in front of a crew that thinks you mad, then bring that crew home through what sinks the thrifty.
2. Learn the full rite: the share, the rum, the silence, the coin beneath the mast. Omit nothing. The sea audits.
3. Keep the observances a full year aboard a ship of scoffers; by year's end, let the scoffers be doing them too.
4. Bury a drowned stranger with his name spoken and his pockets full. What washes ashore afterward is yours by custom.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Sea's Share** — Cast a share of your gold into the water; the fight's next three attacks by your side cannot miss or misfire.
- **Paid in Full** — Cancel one enemy critical hit, dodge, or lucky escape per battle; a wet handprint marks where their luck used to stand.
- **Rum for the Crossing** — Pour a measure over the rail; allies gain resistance to drowning, despair, and cold for ten minutes.
- **Punctual Tide** — Once per day, the tide does exactly what your plan requires, at the minute your plan requires it.

## Tier V — Mythic (Level 60)

### The Black Sail

**Role:** Mythic dread — surrender at sight · **Advances from:** Noonflag

*Histories dispute whether the Black Sail is a ship, a captain, or a standing arrangement between the two. Sightings are better documented than battles, since there are no battles: convoys strike colors to a silhouette, and insurers pay out on testimony of weather. Forty years of prizes; the confirmed casualty list names one man, who fell down a hatch.*

**Playstyle.** Stop fighting; start appearing. Your presence is the ability. Engagements resolve in your favor when enemies can see you, and they can always see you.

**The road to The Black Sail:**

1. Reach level 60 and take a treasure fleet without a shot; let the escort's log read only 'sail sighted, colors struck, weather fine.'
2. Be reported in two oceans in the same week, and correct neither report.
3. Sail past a port you once spared, and watch it surrender anyway. Send back their keys with a tide table.
4. Retire, verifiably, in front of witnesses. When the sightings continue anyway, you hold the rank.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Silhouette** — Appear on any horizon the enemy checks; while visible, enemy morale drains and no enemy buff can be maintained.
- **Colors Answered** — Any enemy group that can see your flag must vote on surrender every 10 seconds; abstentions count in favor.
- **The Casualty List** — Battles you enter without a death stay that way: killing blows on either side land as capture instead.
- **Compound Reputation** — Prizes taken without blood add permanent dread to your silhouette; veterans surrender fastest, having heard the most.

### The Narrow Thunder

**Role:** Mythic precision — fortress-opening singular detonations · **Advances from:** Powder Surgeon

*Histories record the works and dispute the hand: the sea-fort of Carrent opened along one seam; a chain-boom parted at the link its maker doubted; a flagship surrendered to a bang no louder than a dropped book. The Narrow Thunder is what gunners call the idea that an explosion can be aimed like a needle. Somewhere, the idea has hands.*

**Playstyle.** One charge per problem, one problem per battle. Spend long setup for absolute results: what you detonate is deleted, and nothing beside it notices.

**The road to The Narrow Thunder:**

1. Reach level 60 and open a fortress the siege-engineers had priced at a year, before their invoice clears.
2. Detonate a charge in a crowded market that harms one lock and nothing else. The order calls this the graduation; magistrates call it impossible.
3. Be blamed for lightning twice and thunder once, in writing, by people who watched you do it.
4. Refuse to write the method down. Apprentices learn it from your hands or not at all; the world is not ready to be this openable.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Doubted Link** — Identify the one component holding an enemy formation, fortress, or plan together; your next charge is placed there.
- **No Louder Than a Book** — Your detonations make exactly as much sound as you allot them, including none; the damage is unrelated to the noise.
- **One Seam** — Destroy any single structure, gate, ward, or engine outright; everything within arm's reach of it survives untouched.
- **Grain Budget** — Your charges cost half powder against targets studied for 10 seconds; thrift, weaponized.

### Squallmonger

**Role:** Mythic forecaster — storms kept on retainer · **Advances from:** Stormbroker

*Histories argue whether the Squallmonger predicts weather or the weather consults them; both camps cite the same eleven engagements, each fought in impossible convenience. Gales arrive on their invoices. Fog lifts for their broadsides and closes for their exits. Admirals hire weather-clerks in self-defense; the clerks resign after reading the logs.*

**Playstyle.** Sell the sky's schedule to the fight. Every engagement opens in conditions you chose from a menu, and the menu keeps getting longer.

**The road to Squallmonger:**

1. Reach level 60 and win an engagement the almanac calls impossible, in weather that existed only over your half of the water.
2. Quote a hurricane's route to the day, publish it, and be wrong in exactly the ports that refused your fee.
3. Retire a naval squadron without firing: three appointments with three storms, all kept, none survived. The logs must call it weather; the admiralty won't.
4. Meet the Sorcerer's storm at sea — the one with blood in it — and tip your hat as it passes you by. It knows a colleague.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Menu of Skies** — Choose the weather any battle begins in from conditions you have personally survived; you have survived nearly everything.
- **Invoice the Gale** — Book a gale to arrive within the hour; it keeps the appointment, works the enemy flank, and leaves nothing in writing.
- **Convenient Fog** — Fog lifts where you aim and thickens where you retreat; enemies experience this as persecution, correctly.
- **Professional Courtesy** — Storms do not strike your ship, your crew, or your prizes; colleagues do not bill colleagues.

### All Hands

**Role:** Mythic gambler — impossible odds, total reversals · **Advances from:** Deepwager

*The phrase means two things at sea: every sailor to the ropes, or every sailor to the bottom. All Hands is the gambler who has been both meanings. Histories cite the wreck of the Gilded Gull, lost with all hands — and the fifty players of its final card game, ashore by dawn, rich, and unable to explain the boat.*

**Playstyle.** Go all-in as a lifestyle. Your worst positions hold your best outcomes: the closer your side stands to lost, the harder your reversals hit.

**The road to All Hands:**

1. Reach level 60 and stake everything you have ever won on one hand, against the sea, in a storm, and win.
2. Be lost with all hands. The step is written in the past tense for a reason nobody applying for the rank enjoys.
3. Give your winnings away entire, twice, to prove to the table that it was never about the money. The sea checks this one personally.
4. Play the Final Game against someone the histories say you cannot beat, and let fifty witnesses fail to agree afterward on what you wagered.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Both Meanings** — While any ally is downed, all your wagers succeed automatically; the sea respects a table with real stakes.
- **The Final Game** — Challenge a stronger enemy to stakes they cannot decline; winner takes the loser's next ten minutes.
- **Ashore by Dawn** — When your side would be lost with all hands, you are all instead ashore by dawn, once per campaign, minus the boat.
- **Dead Even** — Equalize: set both sides' chances to exactly even, ignoring numbers, wounds, and sense. Then play your luck.

### The Red Wake

**Role:** Mythic slaughter — unstoppable momentum, fleet execution · **Advances from:** Quarterless

*The histories agree a ship leaves a red wake; they disagree on when it began and why it never ends. Fleets that catch the Red Wake report nothing afterward, which is the report. One witness per engagement, the doctrine ran; the legend keeps the ledger current. Ports it passes declare mourning in advance, to save time.*

**Playstyle.** Never stop moving, never stop killing. Your momentum outlasts fleets: each engagement funds the next, and the wake behind you does half the work.

**The road to The Red Wake:**

1. Reach level 60 and break a punitive armada sent specifically for you; send its flag home folded, with the one witness carrying it.
2. Pass a coast at night and find every light already doused, every ship already beached. Accept the tribute of being believed.
3. Hear a false Red Wake is working your waters, and let the sea learn the difference. The difference should be legible from shore.
4. Spare, once, a ship full of children and choose to be seen doing it. Terror must have edges, or it is only weather.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Wake Runs Red** — Your kill-momentum no longer decays between fights, between days, or between wars.
- **Nothing Afterward** — Engagements you finish yield the enemy nothing: no logs, no lessons, no survivors past the one you chose.
- **Mourning in Advance** — Enemies who have heard of you begin the fight already suffering your first hit's morale cost.
- **Chosen Witness** — While your chosen witness lives, you cannot be slowed, silenced, or stopped; stories need endings, and you are theirs.

### The Ash Fleet

**Role:** Mythic conflagration — fleet-scale fire, denial incarnate · **Advances from:** Hellburner

*Port registries disagree on the Ash Fleet's size — one hull, or forty, or none since the night it burned itself as a demonstration. It anchors nowhere, takes nothing, and leaves shipping lanes the color of old campfires. Cities negotiate with its smoke on the horizon. The negotiations are short, and the Fleet's terms are always the same: elsewhere.*

**Playstyle.** Command fire as a formation. Your burns maneuver, screen, and flank like squadrons; the field is yours once everything flammable has picked a side.

**The road to The Ash Fleet:**

1. Reach level 60 and burn a blockade of forty hulls with three, at night, upwind, uninsured.
2. Anchor nowhere for a year. Fleets will report you in six seas; let the reports do the patrolling.
3. Accept a city's surrender delivered to your smoke, the actual ships being elsewhere. Sign as the Fleet. It is not lying if it burns.
4. Burn one of your own hulls as the demonstration, with your flag flying, and step ashore while it settles. The legend needs the silhouette.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Squadron of Flame** — Raise up to four autonomous fires that hold formation, obey signals, and pursue routed enemies with visible enthusiasm.
- **The Demonstration** — Burn something of your own, publicly; enemy morale breaks twice as fast for the rest of the engagement.
- **Smoke Negotiations** — Enemies inside your smoke may only retreat or surrender; both options are honored, which surprises them.
- **The Same Terms** — Designate a zone; anything that remains in it after 10 seconds is claimed by fire that arrives precisely on time.

### Fairwater

**Role:** Mythic pilot — lethal water made road · **Advances from:** Graveyard Pilot

*Survivors' accounts agree on the wrong details: the reef was there, the chart was right, and the water went flat as poured glass exactly one keel wide. Fairwater is the pilot the graveyards will not take — some say out of respect, some say out of patience. Wreckers hate them. Widows name boats after them.*

**Playstyle.** Carve safe lanes through lethal ground for your whole side. Where you lead, hazards abstain; where you have not led, your enemies are welcome to find out.

**The road to Fairwater:**

1. Reach level 60 and bring a dismasted hull through the worst water on the chart with the tide against you and the sea, for once, polite.
2. Walk away from a pilotage worth a fortune because the water felt proud that day. Be right. Be unable to prove it.
3. Take a hundred ships through your graveyard without a loss, until the underwriters stop pricing the strait and start pricing you.
4. Refuse to pilot a slaver through, once, in front of witnesses, and let the reef handle the paperwork.

**Abilities granted:**

- **One Keel Wide** — Flatten a corridor of calm through any hazard — reef, storm, battlefield — exactly wide enough for those you vouch for.
- **The Graveyards Abstain** — Environmental damage cannot touch you or allies within your wake; the sea is not done watching you work.
- **Poured Glass** — Still the water around an enemy vessel instead: becalmed, it drifts wherever the current you chose is going.
- **Wrecker's Grief** — False lights and lures fail within your sight; those who set them find their own marks moved by morning.

### The Undrowned

**Role:** Mythic constant — undrownable, favor made flesh · **Advances from:** Tithesman

*Everyone drowns who stays long enough; the sea is patient and the arithmetic is not close. The Undrowned is the standing exception — paid up, the tithesmen say, in full and in advance, though none can name the sum. Storms part bruisingly around them. Three navies have hanged them. The dates are engraved on the mugs they drink from.*

**Playstyle.** Be the certainty your crew steers by. You do not die at sea, and everything near you dies less; spend the difference loudly.

**The road to The Undrowned:**

1. Reach level 60 and be given up for drowned three times by three crews; interrupt each wake at the toast, sea-wet and thirsty.
2. Complete the great tithe. What it costs is between you and the water, and the water does not discuss its rates.
3. Go down with a ship, by choice, holding the manifest, and walk out of the surf a week later having delivered it.
4. Let the navies hang you once more, on a gallows in sight of the sea, and commission the third mug yourself.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Sum Unnamed** — You cannot die on, in, or under salt water; the sea declines to elaborate.
- **Bruising Courtesy** — Storms miss you narrowly and resentfully; whatever they meant for you lands on the nearest enemy instead.
- **Hanged Thrice** — Execution fails against you at sea or in sight of it; each failure permanently unnerves the executing authority.
- **Keep the Crew** — Allies aboard your vessel cannot drown while you live, a clause the sea honors and audits.

## Tier VI — Apotheosis (Level 85)

### The Second Sail

**Role:** Apotheosis — omnipresent dread, campaigns conceded unfought · **Advances from:** The Black Sail

*Every lookout knows the moment: a second sail, hull-down at dusk, exactly where the wake was. It does not close and it does not leave. The Black Sail was a ship one could surrender to; the Second Sail is the knowledge that one already has. Admiralties list it as weather. Crews list it in their prayers.*

**Playstyle.** Exist at the edge of every enemy's plan. You are sighted, never engaged; whole campaigns re-route around your possibility, and what re-routes, you own.

**The road to The Second Sail:**

1. Reach level 85 and be sighted, the same evening, from every masthead in a hemisphere; sign nothing, sink nothing, collect everything that turns back.
2. Let the Black Sail rot in a museum harbor while the sightings go on; attend the exhibit, unrecognized, and leave a good review.
3. Become a clause: when charters promise safe passage 'excepting weather,' be the exception both parties silently mean.
4. Once a generation, let one ship outrun you. Hope is the sail you fly now; keep it trimmed.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Hull-Down** — You occupy every horizon simultaneously; enemies anywhere at sea count as within your dread presence.
- **Already Surrendered** — Enemy plans made within sight of water begin at half conviction; their retreats are pre-plotted and known to you.
- **Listed as Weather** — You cannot be targeted, warded against, or negotiated with by anything that classifies you correctly; nothing does.
- **Sail on the Wake** — Appear behind any fleeing enemy at dusk, exactly once per dusk; whatever they abandon to run faster becomes yours.

### The Flaw

**Role:** Apotheosis — the weakness in made things · **Advances from:** The Narrow Thunder

*Masons whisper it, shipwrights curse it, armorers price it in: everything made has a flaw, and the Flaw has a favorite. It is the seam the builder dreamed about, the bolt tightened twice out of doubt. The Narrow Thunder aimed explosions; the Flaw no longer needs them. Attention suffices. Guilds post it apologies in advance.*

**Playstyle.** Look at things until they stop working. Armor, walls, fleets, plans — all of it was assembled, and you are the argument against assembly.

**The road to The Flaw:**

1. Reach level 85 and retire your instruments; from now on the velvet roll is ceremonial, and the ceremony is for other people's comfort.
2. Walk an arsenal's length and let nothing explode; restraint is the last calibration, and the loudest thing you will ever do.
3. Visit the shipyards annually, touching nothing. What the wrights reinforce after you leave was the flaw; they know you know.
4. Leave one perfect thing alone forever. The histories must record that it was possible to be spared.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Attention Suffices** — Regard a made thing for 3 seconds; it fails at its weakest point, which you may nominate.
- **The Builder's Dream** — Learn every structure's secret seam on sight, including those the builder successfully forgot.
- **Priced In** — Constructed enemies and equipment within your presence operate at 75% effectiveness; engineers can feel you in their teeth.
- **The Favorite** — Choose one enemy work — a flagship, a citadel, a siege train — per season; by season's end it has quietly come apart.

### The Hired Storm

**Role:** Apotheosis — weather under standing engagement · **Advances from:** Squallmonger

*Every sea has one storm the others defer to, and that storm keeps accounts. Cities have paid it off; wars have waited on its availability. Sailors say the Squallmonger finally closed the deal from the other side of the counter — no longer booking weather but taking the bookings. It has never once broken an engagement, and never once explained one.*

**Playstyle.** Be the weather your allies plan around. Arrive as front, fight as squall, depart as rumor; your terms are posted and your schedule is the battle.

**The road to The Hired Storm:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop traveling to weather; let weather admit it has been traveling to you. The admission is the ascension.
2. Take your first booking as a storm: one war, one coast, payment in advance. Keep the appointment so exactly that clocks are set by you afterward.
3. Refuse a tyrant's fee and rain gently on his fleet for a year, exactly enough to rot the rigging. Patience is also weather.
4. Keep one small harbor forever calm, unpaid and unexplained. Every storm needs somewhere it is only rain.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Open for Engagements** — Manifest as weather across the entire field: wind, rain, and pressure all answer to your intent while you preside.
- **Paid Off** — Honor payments: those who have paid you pass beneath you untouched; the receipts outlast their grandchildren.
- **Availability** — Wars within your waters cannot begin until you accept the booking; refusal has ended three wars unfought.
- **No Explanations** — You cannot be dispelled, forecast, or attributed; weather-clerks file you under 'seasonal' and say nothing further.

### The Fool's Wind

**Role:** Apotheosis — desperate luck made weather · **Advances from:** All Hands

*Old hands teach it grudgingly: there is a wind that favors idiots. It fills the sail nobody trimmed, misses the reef nobody watched for, and arrives precisely when deserving it would be impossible. Wise sailors never feel it. The Fool's Wind was a gambler once, the story runs, and stays fond of anyone playing badly enough to need it.*

**Playstyle.** Bless recklessness, yours and everyone's. The wilder the plan in your presence, the better it works; caution is the only thing you cannot save.

**The road to The Fool's Wind:**

1. Reach level 85 and stop rolling; the dice fall for other people now. Your stake is permanent, and the table is every sea at once.
2. Blow gently for a decade on the desperate and the doomed; take no credit, keep no tally, accept no worship you cannot deflect into a drinking song.
3. Refuse the wise. When the careful admiral begs for one favorable hour, be a flat calm. He chose arithmetic; let them keep each other.
4. Fill one trimmed sail, once a century, for no reason at all, so the scholars can keep disproving you.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Favors Idiots** — Any ally attempting something with under one-in-ten odds succeeds; the wind keeps its own arithmetic and shows nobody.
- **The Untrimmed Sail** — Plans abandoned as hopeless complete themselves in your presence: the fire lights, the rope holds, the door was never locked.
- **To Specification** — Cautious enemies receive no luck at all in your presence: every plan performs exactly to specification, which is never enough.
- **The Needed Gust** — Once per battle, when all is lost by any sane accounting, the wind arrives; what it does is not your decision anymore.

### The Closed Sea

**Role:** Apotheosis — maritime interdiction made law · **Advances from:** The Red Wake

*Some waters are closed. Not blockaded — closed, the way a court is closed: by an authority nobody elects and everybody obeys. Charts hatch the area in red and cite no statute. The Red Wake ended; the closure it patrolled did not. Insurers exclude it beside war and gods. Ships that enter are not sunk. They are simply not.*

**Playstyle.** Declare regions yours and enforce the declaration by existing. Within your waters you are jurisdiction itself: entry is consent, and consent is unwise.

**The road to The Closed Sea:**

1. Reach level 85 and close your first sea. Fire nothing. Sink nothing. Let the third ship that fails to return draw the boundary for you.
2. Outlive your ship, your crew, and your description; a law needs no hull. Keep the wake out of habit.
3. Permit one pilgrimage a year across your water, unharmed, so the closure is read as judgment and never as appetite.
4. When admiralties finally print the red hatching, sign nothing; boundaries that require signatures can be renegotiated, and yours cannot.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Waters Closed** — Close the field: for 30 seconds nothing enters or leaves, and everything inside answers to you.
- **Cite No Statute** — Your authority cannot be dispelled, appealed, or countered, having never been written down anywhere it could be.
- **Red Hatching** — Mark a zone on the world's charts; enemies who enter it are permanently weakened, beginning with their certainty.
- **Simply Not** — Once per war, a vessel, company, or engine that violates your waters ceases: no wreck, no record, no argument.

### The Ember Tide

**Role:** Apotheosis — fire as recurring weather · **Advances from:** The Ash Fleet

*Certain coasts know a second tide. It comes in on no moon anyone charts, glowing at the waterline, smelling of powder and hot tar. Harbormasters rake their moorings bare and call it maintenance. The Ash Fleet was ships; the Ember Tide is schedule — fire that returns the way water returns, to any shore that forgets it was warned.*

**Playstyle.** Set fires that come back. Your burns ebb, flood, and recur on timetables you publish or withhold; enemies fight the clock, and the clock is yours.

**The road to The Ember Tide:**

1. Reach level 85 and burn the same harbor twice, one year apart to the hour; punctuality is the point, and the warning.
2. Ebb. Let a decade pass without fire, so the coasts learn the difference between ash and low tide.
3. Come in glowing on a moonless flood against a fleet at anchor, and leave the anchors. Anchors are how they will remember the ships.
4. Accept the harbormasters' quiet arrangement: raked moorings, cleared berths, and no questions. Fire that keeps a schedule is due the courtesies of a tide.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Flood Fire** — Send fire in as a rising tide across the field; it ebbs on your mark and returns stronger each cycle.
- **No Moon Charted** — Your recurring burns cannot be predicted or warded against; almanacs decline to cover you, citing precedent.
- **Waterline Glow** — The sea itself carries your embers: any water an enemy crosses may ignite, having been warned first.
- **Rake the Moorings** — Coasts you have burned once obey you forever: their harbors close, open, or burn again at your low whistle.

### The Landfall

**Role:** Apotheosis — arrival, personified and enforced · **Advances from:** Fairwater

*Sailors do not pray for good voyages; they pray for landfall, and something has started answering. Ships past hope raise coasts that were not there at sunset. Harbor bells ring for hulls the harbor never saw. Fairwater stopped taking ships through, the pilots say, and became the through. Every chart's last line now means someone.*

**Playstyle.** End journeys on your terms. Allies always arrive — somewhere, intact, together; enemies discover that arrival is a privilege you administer.

**The road to The Landfall:**

1. Reach level 85 and be prayed to by name, correctly, by a crew you have never met; answer by existing where they needed a coast.
2. Bring home a ship the sea had fairly won. Do it once, apologize to the water, and do not make a habit of theft.
3. Refuse a landfall, once, to a hull full of plague, and hold it at sea gently until it is only a hull. Arrival is a judgment; render it.
4. Let the Ferryman keep the far crossings; yours is the near shore, the bell, and the breakfast after. Guard the difference jealously.

**Abilities granted:**

- **Raise the Coast** — Grant a desperate ally instant safe arrival: they are removed from danger and set down at the nearest defensible shore.
- **Bells for the Unseen** — Allies who would die at sea instead wash ashore alive, eventually, with a story they decline to tell.
- **No Arrival** — Declare an enemy voyage incomplete: their fleet remains at sea, provisioned but shoreless, until they abandon the campaign.
- **Chart's End** — Wherever you stand becomes, for one hour, a friendly port: repairs, rest, and resupply attend your side.

### The Seventh Wave

**Role:** Apotheosis — the sea settling accounts · **Advances from:** The Undrowned

*Count the swells: old hands swear the seventh runs largest, and older hands swear it chooses. It lifts the tithed hull over the bar and opens beneath the one that never paid — same water, same minute, ancient distinction. The Undrowned went under at last, the story runs, and came up as the wave itself: the sea, settling accounts by hand.*

**Playstyle.** Arrive seventh. Judge every hull, crew, and cause by what it has paid the water, then deliver the sea's answer in person, at scale.

**The road to The Seventh Wave:**

1. Reach level 85 and go under for the last time, mid-count, with the whole tithe paid; what surfaces on the seventh is the rank.
2. Learn the sea's list. Not its grievances — its accounts. You will find your own name there, marked settled, in your own hand.
3. Break over one harbor that cheated its sailors for a generation, once, at the height of market day, and recede without a second stroke.
4. Lift more than you drown. The old partnership holds even now: the sea is a business, and business needs the boats coming back.

**Abilities granted:**

- **The Count** — Combat runs in counts of seven around you; the seventh beat of any exchange belongs entirely to your side.
- **Over the Bar** — Lift an allied vessel, party, or cause past an impassable obstacle on one great swell, arriving intact and slightly blessed.
- **Opens Beneath** — Open the water under an enemy who has wronged the sea; the sea keeps its own list and shares it with you.
- **The Weighing** — Once per age, weigh a fleet against its conduct: the deserving float; the rest the sea keeps. Survivors say the wave read names.
